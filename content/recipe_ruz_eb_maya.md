# Ruz eb Maya (Gaza Chickpeas and Rice) / رز ع ميّة

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Gaza Strip: refugee camps and towns (Jabalia, Shati, Khan Younis, Rafah).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice and legumes. A humble pot of rice and chickpeas cooked in onion-browned water or broth, then lifted with a pounded paste of garlic, dill seed and red pepper. The grains stay soft and separate and the chickpeas tender.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A single pot on a gas burner or wood fire, with a mortar and pestle for the dill and garlic.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*The Gaza Kitchen* (Laila El-Haddad and Maggie Schmitt) includes *Ruz eb Maya* as "one of the many simple dishes that emerged in the years following the 1948 exodus". The name means "rice with water", a recipe born of hardship that turned UN emergency-ration ingredients into filling meals ([ckbk – The Gaza Kitchen, Chickpeas and Rice](https://app.ckbk.com/recipe/gaza70968c06s001ss001r003/chickpeas-and-rice)). Reviewers single it out as a dish tied to Gaza's modern history ([AramcoWorld review](https://www.aramcoworld.com/en/resources/reviews/2017/the-gaza-kitchen-a-palestinian-culinary-journey)). UNRWA's quarterly food packages still usually contain rice, lentils and oil, and [mujaddara](recipe_mujaddara.md) has become a household staple for the same reason ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/world/mena/a-cuisine-under-siege-how-israel-and-hamas-have-left-gazan-food-on-the-chopping-board-1.895192)).

The dill seed and red pepper are Gaza's signature: the "holy trinity" of dill, garlic and chili runs through Gazan cooking. Compare the Orihuela vigil rice [tres puñaos](recipe_arroz_de_los_tres_punaos.md), another rice and legume dish of scarcity.

**Culinary science:** browning the onion deeply before adding the water gives the broth body and sweetness without meat. Canned or pre-cooked chickpeas add starch, so the rice cooks a little creamier than plain *ruz mfalfal*.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Gaza refugee-camp version (documented):** Water rather than stock, with dill seed, garlic and red pepper flakes.
*   **With broth (better times):** Chicken or meat broth instead of water, as the recipe allows ("water or broth").
*   ⚠️ Differences between camps or towns are not documented.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: The Gaza Kitchen structure. Quantities are reconstructed because the page is truncated.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 4 tbsp |
| Onion, chopped | 1 large |
| Garlic | 4 cloves |
| Dill seeds | 1 tbsp |
| Red pepper flakes | ½–1 tsp |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short- or medium-grain rice, rinsed | 300 g |
| Cooked chickpeas (or 1 can, drained) | 250 g |
| Water or broth | 700 ml (≈ 2.3:1 to the rice) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Pound the dill seeds and pepper flakes with ½ tsp of the salt until fragrant, add the garlic and mash to a paste.
2. Fry the onion in the oil over medium heat until well browned (**12–15 min**).
3. Add the water or broth and the chickpeas and bring to a boil.
4. Stir in the rice and the remaining salt, then cover and cook on low for **18 min**.
5. Stir in the dill-garlic paste, cover again and rest for **10 min** off the heat.
6. Fluff and serve with a tomato-cucumber salad or yogurt.
