# Muhallabiyeh / مهلبية

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** muhallabiyeh is a living, pan-Arab milk pudding; this file covers the **Shami (Syrian) everyday form** thickened with rice flour, from Arabic recipe media. The medieval ancestor (*muhallabiyya* in 10th–13th-century Arabic cookbooks) is treated in the historical section of the atlas, not here. Modern recipes often use cornflour instead of rice flour; town-level differences are lightly documented.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Damascus and Aleppo (sweet shops and homes), and the wider Levant.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sweet rice (rice-flour milk pudding): smooth, silky and set, served chilled, scented with **orange-blossom or rose water** and topped with **Aleppo pistachios**.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy saucepan, stirred continuously over low heat; set in small bowls or a large shallow dish.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Unlike [riz bi haleeb](recipe_riz_bi_haleeb.md), which keeps whole grains, *muhallabiyeh* is made with **ground rice** (rice flour), giving a smooth custard-like set without eggs. In Bilad al-Sham it is flavoured with milk, sugar and flower water and finished with crushed pistachios, the Levantine signature, while Egyptian and Gulf versions vary the scent (vanilla, cardamom, saffron). It is served to guests, at Ramadan, and as a light everyday sweet.

The name links it to a much older dish in medieval Arabic cookery; that history, and whether the medieval dish was the same thing, is discussed in the atlas's historical recipes.

**Culinary science:** rice-flour starch gelatinises around 70–80 °C; continuous stirring prevents lumps and scorching on the base, and cooking a few minutes past the boil removes the raw flour taste. Flower water is added **off the heat**, because its volatile aromas fade with boiling.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascus:** Orange-blossom water; pistachio topping; sometimes a thin layer of syrup ⚠️.
*   **Aleppo:** Rose water and abundant Aleppo pistachios; layered fruit versions are Aleppo's [mbattan](recipe_riz_bi_haleeb.md) family ⚠️.
*   **Lebanon / Palestine:** Rose or orange blossom, mastic optional; coconut or crushed nuts on top.
*   **Modern home shortcut:** Cornflour instead of rice flour; milk powder in some recipes.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Shami rice-flour muhallabiyeh** with orange-blossom water and pistachios.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Full-fat milk | 1 L |
| Rice flour | 75 g |
| Sugar | 120 g |
| Orange-blossom water (or rose water) | 1½ tbsp |
| Mastic, ground with a pinch of sugar (optional) | 2 small tears |
| Pistachios, crushed | 40 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Milk : rice flour | ≈ 13 : 1 by weight |
| Portions | 4 bowls (≈ 250 ml) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Slurry:** Whisk the rice flour with 250 ml of the cold milk until smooth.
2. **Heat:** Warm the rest of the milk with the sugar (and mastic) until steaming.
3. **Thicken:** Pour in the slurry, stirring constantly over low heat, until it thickens and bubbles; keep stirring 3–4 minutes more.
4. **Scent:** Off the heat, stir in the orange-blossom water.
5. **Set:** Pour into bowls, cool, then chill at least 3 hours.
6. **Serve:** Cold, topped with crushed pistachios.
