# Jaew Bong / แจ่วบอง (Pla Ra Bong / ปลาร้าบอง)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan and Laos (Luang Prabang's *jaew bong* is a Lao classic).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a thick, dark, smoky relish of pounded roasted chillies, shallot, garlic, galangal and lemongrass bound with cooked fermented fish (*pla ra*); pinched up with sticky rice or used as a dip for grilled meat and vegetables.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Embers or a dry pan to roast the aromatics, a stone mortar (*khrok hin*); sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Silpa-Wattanatham notes that "bong" is commonly explained as coming from *bong*, a bamboo tube, i.e. a relish packed to keep for a long time, and that today's *pla ra bong* / *jaew bong* has shifted in meaning toward a seasoned relish rather than the old long fermentation ([Silpa-mag – ปลาร้าบอง](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=13721)). A Khaosod recipe pounds roasted shallot, garlic, mature galangal, lemongrass and dried chilli in a stone mortar, then pounds in cooked minced pla ra, finishing with fried makrut lime leaves and dried chilli ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/technologychaoban/?p=96102)). Street grills serve it with grilled pork, sticky rice and raw vegetables ([The Standard](https://thestandard.co/lifestyle-restaurant-and-bar-street-bite-plara-pork/)). Jaew belongs to the dry, *pla ra*-based family of Isan relishes ([Silpa-mag – แจ่วกับป่น](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=162504)).

**Culinary science:** roasting aromatics on embers caramelises their sugars and tames raw pungency; the fermented fish supplies salt and glutamates, so a small amount seasons a large mound of rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Isan home style:** coarse, dark, very hot ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/technologychaoban/?p=96102)).
*   **Luang Prabang style (Laos):** sometimes with buffalo skin; ⚠️ not documented in the sources here.
*   **Commercial jars:** sold nationwide as a pantry relish.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Isan jaew bong (Khaosod method; quantities reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried chillies, roasted | 15 |
| Shallots and garlic, roasted | 6 / 1 head |
| Galangal and lemongrass, roasted | 30 g / 2 stalks |
| Pla ra fish flesh, minced and cooked | 100 g |
| Makrut lime leaves and dried chillies, fried | to finish |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Jaew per person | ≈ 2 tbsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Roast:** roast chillies, shallot, garlic, galangal and lemongrass on embers or in a dry pan until charred in spots.
3. **Pound:** pound the aromatics fine in a stone mortar, then pound in the cooked pla ra until thick.
4. **Serve:** top with fried lime leaves and chilli; eat with sticky rice, grilled meat and raw vegetables.
