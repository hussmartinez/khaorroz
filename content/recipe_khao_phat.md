# Khao Phat / ข้าวผัด

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand and Bangkok; now the default one-plate meal nationwide.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Wok-fried rice: **day-old jasmine rice tossed over high heat** with egg, garlic, a protein and fish sauce. The grains are separate, lightly smoky (*klin kratha*), slightly chewy, and served with lime, cucumber and *phrik nam pla*.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A carbon-steel wok (*kratha*) and ladle-spatula (*tahliw*) over a roaring gas or charcoal flame.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao phat ("stir-fried rice") belongs to the Chinese fried-rice family, brought by Chinese migrants and turned Thai. It is, in Wikipedia's words, a variety of fried rice typical of **central Thai cuisine** ([Wikipedia – Fried rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fried_rice)). What makes it Thai is the seasoning and the garnishes: **fish sauce** instead of soy, a lime wedge, cucumber, spring onion, and a saucer of *phrik nam pla* (bird's-eye chilies in fish sauce). Palace cooks also had their own refined version, **khao phat chao wang**, attributed to Khunying Klib Mahitthon, a palace-raised cook of Rama V's era ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=161093)). ⚠️ Details of that recipe were not accessible. The Ayutthaya-era origin sometimes claimed for khao phat is not documented in the sources found.

**Culinary science:** cooked rice that has rested and cooled (retrograded starch, drier surface) fries into separate grains instead of a paste. A very hot wok gives Maillard browning and a faint smoke, the *wok hei* (Thai *klin kratha*). Fish sauce added at the end seasons without steaming the rice soggy.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Everyday Bangkok:** *khao phat kai / mu / kung / pu* (chicken, pork, prawn, crab). Crab fried rice is the premium shophouse version.
*   **Khao phat sapparot:** with pineapple, cashews and curry powder, sometimes served in a pineapple shell.
*   **Khao phat kapi:** fried with shrimp paste, a cousin of [khao kluk kapi](recipe_khao_kluk_kapi.md) ([Phol Food Mafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=46016)).
*   **Khao phat Amerikan:** the 1960s–70s ketchup version, see [khao phat Amerikan](recipe_khao_phat_amerikan.md).
*   **Khao phat chao wang (palace style):** ⚠️ attributed to Khunying Klib Mahitthon ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=161093)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **khao phat kung (prawn)**, cooked in two batches.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Day-old cooked jasmine rice | 800 g |
| Prawns, peeled | 300 g |
| Eggs | 4 |
| Garlic, chopped | 6 cloves |
| Onion, sliced; spring onions | 1; 4 |
| Fish sauce / sugar / light soy | 3 tbsp / 2 tsp / 1 tbsp |
| Oil | 60 ml |
| Lime wedges, cucumber, *phrik nam pla* to serve | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice per batch (2 batches) | 400 g |
| Rice : egg | ≈ 200 g : 1 egg |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Prep:** break up the cold rice. Mix the fish sauce, sugar and soy.
2. **Heat:** get the wok smoking hot, add half the oil and fry half the garlic until golden.
3. **Prawns and egg:** sear half the prawns, push them aside, crack in 2 eggs and scramble until just set.
4. **Rice:** add half the rice and toss hard for 2 min. Add half the onion and season with half the sauce mix. Toss 1 min more, then add the spring onion.
5. **Repeat** for the second batch. Serve with lime, cucumber and *phrik nam pla*.
