# Arroz con Conejo y Caracoles / Arròs amb Conill i Caragols (Arroz con Conejo y Serranas)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Alicante's interior: El Vinalopó Mitjà (El Pinós/Pinoso, Monòver, Novelda, Petrer), L'Alt Vinalopó (Villena, Biar), L'Alcoià (Castalla), and La Vega Baja del Segura (Redován, Callosa, Orihuela, Almoradí). The dish continues across the border into the Murcian Altiplano.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). A very thin layer, often only 1–1.5 cm, with a pronounced socarrat.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A thin carbon-steel paella over **sarmientos** (vine-prunings from the Monastrell vineyards). This is a very hot, very fast fire that dies quickly, which defines the dish.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
This is the rice of the dry, vine-and-almond interior of Alicante. It was a hunter's and shepherd's dish built from what the *monte* gave: wild rabbit, mountain snails, thyme and rosemary, all cooked over the vine prunings left from winter pruning of the local Monastrell vineyards. In Pinoso it is the town's signature dish, served at the **Mostra de la Cuina del Pinós** alongside gazpachos, gachamiga and fasegures. In La Vega Baja it is known as **arroz con conejo y serranas**: *serranas* are small white mountain snails (*Sphincterochila candidissima*), sometimes replaced by larger *vaquetas* (*Iberus*). These snails have usually fed on wild thyme, which perfumes them.

> **The serrana, a south-eastern signature.** The *serrana* (Sphincterochila candidissima) is a chalk-white snail of dry, sunny limestone hills. It aestivates sealed to rocks and shrubs through the summer, and it is gathered after the autumn and spring rains. The **species itself is not exclusive to Alicante**: zoological sources record it in other dry Mediterranean areas, including the Ebro steppes of Aragón (where it is called *caracol gitano*), southern France, Italy and Malta. What **is** distinctive is its **name and culinary role** in Alicante and Murcia. There, "serranas" are the prized snail of the rice, sold by that name and named in the dish itself (*arroz con conejo y serranas*). Pinoso guides speak of rices with "serrano" snails. Most of Spain uses other species instead: *vaquetas*/*Iberus* in Valencia, *bovers* in Catalonia, *cabrillas* in Andalusia. ⚠️ Whether "serrana" always denotes *S. candidissima*, or loosely any wild hill snail, varies by seller. Sources: [Wikipedia – Sphincterochila candidissima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphincterochila_candidissima); [Aranzadi – Fauna de Pina de Ebro (PDF)](https://monteriza.aranzadi.eus/wp-content/uploads/invertebrados/025.sphincterochila-candidissima.pdf); [Directo al Paladar – Arroz con conejo y serranas](https://www.directoalpaladar.com/recetas-de-arroces/arroz-con-conejo-y-caracoles-receta-tradicional-murciana).

**Culinary science:** sarmiento burns at a very high temperature but for a short time. The cook therefore uses a **thin rice layer**, about 300–320 g of rice in a 50–55 cm pan for four, with **less broth** than a Valencian paella. The rice cooks in about 15–16 minutes, and the fire's intensity at the end produces a heavy socarrat. The flavour comes from **long browning of the rabbit**, from **ñora** (a dried round sweet pepper typical of the Segura basin) fried and pounded into the base, and from herbs added to the broth.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **The Pinoso (El Pinós) Style:** The benchmark. **Sarmiento fire is non-negotiable.** Rabbit is browned very slowly (20+ min) with garlic. Tomato is fried down to a dark paste. **Serranas** or vaquetas are pre-cooked with **thyme** (*tomillo*), and a sprig of thyme or rosemary goes into the broth. Saffron, not colourant. Very thin layer and a strong socarrat. Often a little **ñora** or pimentón.
*   **The Villena / Alto Vinalopó Style:** Similar to Pinoso, but some cooks add **red pepper strips** fried in the base and **garbanzos** or *pisto*. Rosemary is preferred over thyme. Snails are sometimes replaced by **conejo + pollo de corral**.
*   **The Redován / Callosa (Vega Baja, sierra side) Style:** Uses **serranas** gathered in the Sierra de Callosa–Orihuela. Snails are **purged** for several days (fasting, then flour or thyme), blanched and **pre-fried** in the oil before the rabbit, so they absorb fat and garlic. The broth is lighter than in the interior, and lemon wedges are served alongside. *Ñora* is pounded with garlic into a **picada** that goes in with the water.
*   **The Almoradí / Vega Baja huerta Style:** More huerta than monte. In season, **alcachofa de la Vega Baja** (artichoke) is added, plus **pimiento rojo** and sometimes **habas**. The rice is cooked slightly longer, closer to 18 min, in a deeper layer. Snails are often smaller and fewer. **Pava borracha** (Almoradí's wine-fattened turkey) sometimes stands in for part of the rabbit at festivals; see [recipe_arroz_con_pava.md](recipe_arroz_con_pava.md).
*   **The Elche / Baix Vinalopó Style:** Conejo + caracoles are often combined with **pimiento rojo** and **pimentón** and a stronger tomato base. Cooked on gas or orange wood rather than sarmiento.
*   **The Castalla (Alcoià) Style:** A wood-fired restaurant tradition. Pine wood is common. Some cooks include **conejo + pollo + caracoles + bajoqueta** (a bridge to the Valencian paella).

|---|---|---|---|---|

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Pinoso style** (sarmiento, thin layer, serranas, thyme). The Redován and Almoradí modifications are noted inline.*

**Pan:** 55 cm thin carbon-steel paella. **Fire:** 4–5 bundles of dry sarmiento.

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 100 ml |
| Rabbit (preferably *de monte*), in small pieces | 1 (≈ 1.1 kg) |
| Garlic | 4 cloves (2 whole, 2 sliced) |
| Ñora, seeded (or 4 g pimentón dulce) | 1–2 |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Serranas / vaquetas, purged & blanched | 300 g (≈ 40–50 snails) |
| Wild thyme (tomillo) or rosemary | 2 sprigs |
| Water | 1.2 L |
| Saffron | 0.3 g |
| Salt | to taste |

**Snail preparation (2–5 days ahead):**
1. Purge the live snails: fast them for 3–5 days in a ventilated box, optionally on a bed of thyme or flour.
2. Wash in salted water and vinegar until there is no more foam. Start them in **cold** water so they come out of their shells, bring to a boil with a thyme sprig, and cook for **10 min**. Drain.

**Extraction:**
1. Heat the oil and fry the ñora **for 10–15 seconds only**, then remove it. Pound it in a mortar with the 2 whole garlic cloves, salt and a splash of water to make a *picada*.
2. Brown the rabbit with salt **slowly** for **20–25 min**, turning, until it is deep golden. [**Redován:** fry the blanched snails in the oil for 3 min *before* the rabbit, set them aside, and return them with the water.]
3. Add the sliced garlic, then the tomato. Fry until jammy and dark (**6–8 min**).
4. Add the water, the ñora picada, the snails, the thyme and the saffron. Simmer for **20 min**. The liquid will reduce to ≈ 950 ml. Correct the salt.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice (Bahía/Senia, or Bomba de Calasparra in Vega Baja) | 320 g |
| Broth in the pan | ≈ 950 ml (≈ 3:1 by volume; thin layer) |
| Rabbit | ≈ 1.1 kg |
| Snails | 300 g (in shell) |
| Lemon (Redován / Vega Baja) | 1, in wedges |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Snails purged and blanched, rabbit jointed, ñora seeded, fire laid but not lit until the base is ready (or start with a modest fire for browning).
2. **Searing / Fondo:** As above. Keep a moderate fire during browning.
3. **Liquid Addition:** Bring to a hard boil, add the rice in a line and spread it evenly. Remove the thyme sprig. [**Almoradí:** add 4 quartered artichokes rubbed with lemon and 100 g habas with the water.]
4. **Fire Manipulation (sarmiento):**
    *   Min 0–7: feed **fresh sarmiento** and keep a roaring fire under the whole pan.
    *   Min 7–13: let the flames die down to embers. Don't add more wood.
    *   Min 13–15: add **one last small bundle** of sarmiento for a final burst. This is the *socarrat* moment. Listen for crackling.
5. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Remove from the fire and rest **3–5 min**. Serve from the pan, with lemon wedges in the Vega Baja.
