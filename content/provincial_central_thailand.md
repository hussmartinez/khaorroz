# Central Thailand / ภาคกลาง

> ⬅ [README](README.md)

## 1. Overview
The Central Plains, the floodplain of the **Chao Phraya** and its tributaries from Chainat to the Gulf, are Thailand's historic rice bowl and the seat of every capital since Ayutthaya (1351). Here rice means **khao suai** ("beautiful rice"), non-glutinous rice steamed or boiled until separate and fluffy, eaten with a spread of curries, relishes and soups. That sets the centre apart from the sticky-rice cultures of the North (Lanna) and Isan. The fragrant **Khao Dawk Mali 105** ("jasmine rice") was selected from one of 199 panicles collected in **Chachoengsao** (Bang Khla district) in 1950–51 by the Rice Department official Sunthorn Sihanern. It was tested at Khok Samrong (Lopburi) and certified on 25 May 1959, and later found its home in the dry fields of Isan's Thung Kula Rong Hai ([Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/education/news_342342); [Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=51247)). ⚠️ Ban Pho villagers claim the variety came from their district rather than Bang Khla ([Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/news_163253)).

Four communities shape the region's rice dishes:
*   **The Thai court.** The palace kitchens of Ayutthaya and later Rattanakosin refined dishes such as [khao chae](recipe_khao_chae.md) and [khao tang na tang](recipe_khao_tang_na_tang.md).
*   **Chinese-Thai migrants.** Teochew, Hainanese and Cantonese migrants gave Bangkok its one-plate rices: [khao man kai](recipe_khao_man_kai.md), [khao kha mu](recipe_khao_kha_mu.md), [khao mu daeng](recipe_khao_mu_daeng.md), [khao tom](recipe_khao_tom.md), [jok](recipe_jok.md) and [khao phat](recipe_khao_phat.md).
*   **Thai Muslims.** Bangkok's Thai-Muslim communities brought the biryani-like [khao mok kai](recipe_khao_mok_kai.md).
*   **The Mon.** The Mon of Pathum Thani, Pak Kret (Ko Kret), Phra Pradaeng and Ratchaburi are the source of the Songkran rice that became khao chae.

**Elsewhere in the Thai section:** sticky-rice dishes of the North and Isan, southern rices (e.g. khao yam), rice desserts and ritual rices, and the Sukhothai/Ayutthaya historical reconstructions each have their own pages.

## 2. Cultural Sub-Regions
| Sub-region | Rice personality | Key places |
|---|---|---|
| **Bangkok / Rattanakosin** | Court cuisine; Chinese-Thai one-plate rices; curry-over-rice shops; Thai-Muslim khao mok | Rattanakosin island, Yaowarat/Talat Noi, Bang Rak, Nang Loeng, Banglamphu |
| **Ayutthaya** | The old capital's cosmopolitan court and Muslim quarters | Ayutthaya, Bang Pa-in |
| **Suphanburi / Chainat / Ang Thong** | The deep rice plain; everyday khao suai with curries and relishes | Suphanburi, Chainat |
| **Phetchaburi** | Palace-linked sweets and khao chae (Phra Nakhon Khiri, Tha Yang) | Phetchaburi town, Tha Yang |
| **Ratchaburi / Pathum Thani / Nonthaburi (Mon)** | Mon Songkran rice (khao chae) | Ko Kret, Pathum Thani, Phra Pradaeng |
| **Chachoengsao** | Birthplace of Khao Dawk Mali 105 | Bang Khla, Ban Pho |
| **Chonburi / east coast / Chanthaburi** | Seafood and Chinese-Thai coastal rices | Chonburi, Chanthaburi |
