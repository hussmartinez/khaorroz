# Khanom Bueang Thai / ขนมเบื้องไทย

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the story of the name "Thai" being added to distinguish it from a Vietnamese version comes from a single article. An Ayutthaya origin is popular but I found no documentation for it.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand (Bangkok, Ayutthaya), sold at markets and street stalls nationwide.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice-flour snack: a paper-thin, crisp crepe of rice and mung-bean flour spread on a hot iron plate, filled with meringue-like cream and either sweet (golden egg threads, *foi thong*) or savoury (shrimp, coconut, coriander) toppings, then folded like a taco.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy flat iron plate over a charcoal stove; the batter is spread with a round brass spatula.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
A Bangkok Biz News article argues that the word *Thai* was added in the Rattanakosin period. Captives brought back from campaigns in Vietnam under Rama III made a similar folded crepe, which people called *khanom bueang yuan* (Vietnamese khanom bueang), so the local one became *khanom bueang Thai* ([Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/700463)). Silpa Watthanatham magazine has traced Thai sweets back to the Sukhothai period ([Silpa-mag – Thai sweets of the Sukhothai era](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=163301)). Ayutthaya markets still sell khanom bueang as a local speciality ([Michelin Guide – Ayutthaya](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/travel/what-not-to-miss-in-ayutthaya-from-cafes-to-street-food-to-gifts)). The savoury shrimp filling was traditionally eaten in the cool-season month of *duean ai* (December), when shrimp are plentiful ([Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/789945)).

**Culinary science:** rice flour gives crispness; roasted mung-bean flour adds a nutty flavour and helps the very thin film brown evenly; and lime water (*nam pun sai*) firms the batter.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Sweet (*khanom bueang wan*):** meringue-style cream with *foi thong* and shredded coconut.
*   **Savoury (*khanom bueang khem*):** cream with chopped shrimp, coconut, coriander and pepper; traditional in December ([Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/789945)).
*   **Ayutthaya market stalls** ([Michelin](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/travel/what-not-to-miss-in-ayutthaya-from-cafes-to-street-food-to-gifts)).
*   **Khanom bueang yuan:** the soft Vietnamese-style crepe, a separate dish ⚠️.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice flour | 100 g |
| Roasted mung-bean flour | 40 g |
| Wheat flour | 20 g |
| Sugar | 60 g |
| Egg yolk | 1 |
| Lime water (nam pun sai) | 60 ml |
| Salt | pinch |
| Cream: egg whites + sugar (meringue) or coconut cream | 2 whites + 100 g |
| Sweet topping: foi thong / shredded coconut | 80 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice flour in the batter | ≈ 60 % of the flour |
| Crepes | about 30 small |
| Batter per crepe | 1 tsp, spread very thin |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Batter:** Mix the flours, sugar, egg yolk, salt and lime water into a thick, smooth batter; rest 30 min.
2. **Cream:** Whip the egg whites with sugar to a stiff, glossy meringue (or reduce coconut cream).
3. **Cook:** On a hot iron plate, spread 1 tsp of batter into a very thin disc and cook until crisp and golden at the edges.
4. **Fill and fold:** Spread the cream, add the topping and fold in half. Serve at once.
