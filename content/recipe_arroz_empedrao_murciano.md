# Arroz Empedrao (Empedrado) Murciano

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Región de Murcia](provincial_murcia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The highland villages of the Región de Murcia ("pueblos de altura"): Noroeste and Altiplano.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). A thick guiso (stew) of rice and beans that is rested until it sets.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A **clay cazuela** (cazuela de barro), served straight from the pot.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria* includes this dish in its 2022 survey of **forgotten Spanish dishes**. It describes the *empedrado* as a traditional recipe of the Murcian highland villages, still cooked in many homes, especially in winter, but **"not as popular as other rice dishes"**. It is made **only with vegetable ingredients**: red beans, rice, potato, a whole head of garlic, onion, green pepper, tomato, pimentón, bay leaf and saffron. The name means "cobbled", because the beans dot the rice like paving stones. It is the Murcian counterpart of the Valencian Lenten [arròs empedrat](recipe_arroz_empedrado.md), which uses salt cod and white beans.

**Culinary science:** the pre-cooked **red beans** release starch and a slightly sweet, earthy colour into the water. Combined with rice cooked at a 2:1 ratio and a resting period in clay, this gives a solid, spoonable guiso. The journal paper describes it as "a solid stew of moderate flavour". Traditionally it is eaten with home-cured split olives, whose sharpness balances it, and a glass of local wine.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
> ⚠️ The RENC paper documents only one generic highland version. The variations below are reconstructed and should be verified locally.

*   **Highland base (RENC):** Red beans, whole garlic head, bay leaf, potato, green pepper and *ajos tiernos* (spring garlic) in the sofrito. Purely vegetable.
*   **Noroeste (Caravaca / Moratalla):** ⚠️ Some households may add salt cod in Lent, which brings it closer to the Valencian empedrat.
*   **Altiplano:** ⚠️ Cooked slightly looser, and sometimes with turnip.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **RENC 2022 highland recipe**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried red beans (soaked 12 h) | 250 g |
| Whole head of garlic | 1 |
| Bay leaf | 1 |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |
| Potato, small cubes or strips | 1 (200 g) |
| Onion, chopped | 1 |
| Green pepper, chopped | 1 |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 1 large (200 g) |
| Pimentón | 3 g |
| Black peppercorns | 6 |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |

**Extraction:** Cook the beans until tender, then drain and rinse them. Put them in the clay cazuela with the garlic head, saffron and bay leaf, cover with water and bring to a boil. In a frying pan, brown the potato, then fry the onion and pepper until soft, then add the tomato and pimentón.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice | 400 g |
| Water | ≈ 800 ml (2:1 to the rice, "as in a paella") |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. When the water in the bean pot boils, add the rice, salt, peppercorns, and more saffron if needed.
2. Pour in the sofrito.
3. Simmer about **20 min**, until the rice and potato are cooked.
4. Turn off the heat and **rest** so that the stew sets and the clay cools slightly.
5. Serve from the cazuela with split olives.
