# Mahshi Kousa wa Batinjan / محشي كوسا وباذنجان

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the stuffed-vegetable technique is well documented across the Levant. The **Hebron yogurt version** is documented by a chef in UAE press. The **Gaza** profile (dill, chillies) rests on the general description of Gazan cooking in *The Gaza Kitchen*, not on a specific mahshi recipe I could read.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Palestine (especially the agricultural heartlands, where summer squash is abundant), and across Lebanon, Syria and Jordan.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vegetables (*mahashi*). The rice is cooked *inside* the zucchini and eggplant until soft and juicy, and served in its tomato (or yogurt) cooking liquid.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide, heavy pot on the hob. The vegetables are packed upright or in rows and weighed down with an inverted plate. A long thin corer, the ***manakra***, is used to hollow them.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Mahshi* means "stuffed". It is the summer and Sunday dish of Palestinian homes: small pale-green **kousa** squash and small **eggplants** (*batinjan*) are hollowed, filled with **raw rice and minced meat**, and simmered in a tomato broth. Cooks often core and prepare the filling the day before for family gatherings. The cored flesh is not wasted: it is fried with eggs or onions. Raw rice is the key. It cooks inside the vegetable on the vegetable's own juices and the surrounding broth, so the shells must be filled loosely (about 1 cm of headroom) or they burst. Sister dishes include *warak dawali* (vine leaves), *malfuf* (cabbage), stuffed peppers and onions, and the meat-only, yogurt-sauced ***sheikh al-mahshi***.

**Culinary science:** the plate on top keeps the vegetables submerged and stops them floating and splitting. The acidity of tomato (or yogurt) keeps the squash skins firm during 30–40 minutes of simmering. Cooked yogurt sauces are stabilised with starch or flour so they don't curdle. With the rice cooked inside a vegetable "vessel", this is a distant cousin of the Valencian [baked rices](recipe_arroz_al_horno.md): a sealed, steamed rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **General Palestinian (tomato):** Raw rice, minced beef and/or lamb (half and half is popular), **allspice and a touch of cardamom**, ghee or olive oil. Simmered in **blended fresh tomato**. Vegetarian versions use rice, tomato, onion and chickpeas.
*   **al-Khalil / Hebron — *kousa bi-laban*:** Zucchini stuffed with rice, meat, garlic and **dried mint**, finished in a **cooked yogurt sauce** stabilised with starch, topped with fried pine nuts, fried garlic and fresh mint. Hebron is known for rice-and-yogurt cooking (*laban m'aqqad*, jameed, Hebron mansaf).
*   **Gaza:** ⚠️ The Gazan palate described in *The Gaza Kitchen* is "more herby, spicy, peppery, lemony", with dill and green/red chillies, and the book has a "stuffed summer vegetables" chapter. The exact Gaza filling was not verifiable.
*   **Sheikh al-mahshi (Levant-wide):** **No rice**. Eggplants or zucchini are slit lengthwise, filled with minced meat and cooked in yogurt. It is listed here because it is often confused with mahshi.

| Variable | Tomato mahshi | Hebron kousa bi-laban | Sheikh al-mahshi |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filling | Rice + meat | Rice + meat + garlic + mint | Meat only |
| Sauce | Fresh tomato | Cooked yogurt (+ starch) | Yogurt |
| Herb | — | Dried + fresh mint | Mint |

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **general Palestinian tomato mahshi** (eggplant + kousa). The Hebron yogurt finish is given as an alternative.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo) (the cooking liquid)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ripe tomatoes, peeled, blended with 120 ml water | 5 (≈ 600 g) |
| Allspice | ½ tsp |
| Salt | to taste |
| [Hebron alternative] Full-fat yogurt + 2 tbsp cornflour + 2 garlic cloves + 1 tbsp dried mint | 1 kg |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Small eggplants (or Italian/Indian eggplants) | 8–12 |
| Kousa (pale summer squash) | 8 |
| Rice (Egyptian or Calrose), raw, washed | 200 g |
| Minced beef/lamb (half and half) | 340 g |
| Ghee or olive oil | 2 tbsp |
| Allspice, cardamom, salt, black pepper (filling) | ½ tsp / ¼ tsp / 1 tsp / ½ tsp |
| Filling headroom in each vegetable | ≈ 1 cm |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Trim the stems. Core each vegetable with the *manakra*, rotating gently and leaving a sturdy shell. Don't pierce the bottom. Keep the cored vegetables in water.
2. **Filling:** Mix the raw rice, meat, ghee and spices by hand.
3. **Stuffing:** Fill each vegetable **loosely**, leaving about 1 cm at the top, because the rice swells.
4. **Packing:** Arrange them snugly in the pot. Pour in the seasoned tomato liquid until they are almost covered. Weigh them down with an inverted plate.
5. **Fire Manipulation:** Bring to a boil, then cover and simmer on low for **30–40 min**. Test one: the rice inside must be fully tender.
6. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Remove the plate and rest covered for **10–15 min**. [Hebron: cook the stuffed zucchini in water or stock first. Separately whisk the yogurt with the cornflour and salt, bring it to a boil while stirring, add the garlic and mint, return the zucchini and simmer gently for 5 min. Garnish with pine nuts and fresh mint.]
