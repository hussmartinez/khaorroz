# Arroz a la Zaragozana / Arroz a la Aragonesa

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Aragón, Navarra & La Rioja](provincial_aragon_navarra_rioja.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** "arroz a la zaragozana" appears as a dish name in older Spanish cookbook tradition, but my searches found **no documented recipe or history**. Zaragoza food guides list ternasco, borrajas, chilindrón and *cazuela aragonesa* (a stew in which rice is added at the end) rather than this rice. The version below is built from Aragonese pantry staples (pork, longaniza de Graus, chilindrón peppers) and is a **reconstruction**. Verify it against regional cookbooks before treating it as canonical.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Zaragoza and the middle Ebro (Ribera Alta del Ebro), with Graus (Huesca) as the source of the longaniza.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay *cazuela* over a wood or coal stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Aragonese home cooking adds rice to stews (*cazuela aragonesa*, *colas de cordero* with rice, *conejo en salmorejo* with rice) rather than cooking it in a flat pan. A rice "a la zaragozana" would follow that logic. It would use the **chilindrón** flavour base (roasted red pepper, tomato, onion, ham) that defines Zaragoza's poultry and lamb stews, plus **longaniza de Graus** or pork rib. Red pepper strips and ham are the usual "a la aragonesa" markers in Spanish culinary naming.

**Culinary science:** the chilindrón base relies on slow-cooked sweet red pepper (rich in sugars and carotenoids) and cured ham fat, giving a sweet-savoury, deeply coloured sauce. Rice cooked in it absorbs the pepper sugars and turns brick-red and glossy.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — reconstructed)
*   **Zaragoza city:** Pork rib + **chilindrón** base + peas.
*   **Graus / Ribagorza (Huesca):** **Longaniza de Graus** as the protein.
*   **Ribera del Ebro:** Garden vegetables (**borraja**, artichoke) in season; see [arroz con borrajas](recipe_arroz_con_borrajas_y_almejas.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Zaragoza chilindrón rice** (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pork ribs, short pieces | 400 g |
| Longaniza de Graus, in chunks | 200 g |
| Serrano ham, diced | 60 g |
| Olive oil | 70 ml |
| Onion | 1 |
| Red peppers (roasted or fresh), strips | 2 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 250 g |
| White wine | 100 ml |
| Meat stock | 1.6 L |

**Extraction:** Brown the ribs and longaniza (12 min). Add the ham, onion and peppers and cook slowly (**20 min**). Add the garlic, then the tomato (8 min), then the wine.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 340 g |
| Stock | ≈ 1.4 L (≈ 4:1) |
| Peas | 100 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toasting:** Rice in, 1 min.
2. **Liquid:** Add the boiling stock. Simmer **17 min**, adding the peas at min 12.
3. **Finish:** Rest 2 min.
