# Zarb / زرب

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** zarb is widely described, but **mostly by travel writers and Wadi Rum camps**, which are promotional sources, plus one Jordanian translator's home recipe. None of them give quantities, and none document differences between tribes or towns. The method below follows those descriptions: a sealed pit, a tiered rack, meat on top, rice and vegetables below. The quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Bedouin south of Jordan: Wadi Rum, the Hisma desert and the Ma'an–Aqaba hinterland. It is now cooked for guests at desert camps across the south.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Bedouin pit-oven feast. Spiced rice steamed in a covered tray beneath the meat, catching its juices; fluffy, never wet.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A **pit dug in the sand** (or a buried metal drum), lined with glowing **wood coals**, holding a multi-tiered metal rack. The pit is sealed with a lid or tin sheet, blankets and sand, and the food cooks for hours.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Zarb* is the Bedouin underground oven of southern Jordan and Sinai, and the name of the feast cooked in it. Meat, chicken, lamb or goat, rests on the top tier of a rack, with trays of **rice**, potatoes, onions, tomatoes and carrots below. The rack is lowered onto hot coals at the bottom of a sand pit, which is then sealed so nothing escapes. Hours later the pit is opened in front of the guests, a moment of hospitality theatre that Wadi Rum camps have made famous with visitors. The food is turned out onto a large tray with the rice as the bed, as in a mansaf, and eaten with bread and salads.

It is a desert solution to cooking without an oven or much fuel. A small amount of wood, burned down to coals and buried with the food, is enough to cook for a crowd. Sand insulates, so there is no flame to tend and no wind.

**Culinary science:** the sealed pit works like a low-temperature, high-humidity oven. Steam from the meat and vegetables can't escape, so collagen-rich cuts **braise in their own vapour** and turn tender without drying out. Fat and juices **drip from the upper rack onto the rice below**, so the rice is seasoned by the meat as it steams. The heat source slowly decays, so cooking starts hot and finishes gently, which suits both meat and rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Wadi Rum desert camps:** The documented modern standard: chicken and/or lamb on the top tier; rice, potatoes, onions and tomatoes below; served on a tray with the rice under the meat, with bread and salads.
*   **Home zarb (urban Jordan):** One Jordanian translator's recipe uses a **buried barrel**. The chicken is marinated in **yogurt, garlic, lemon and seven-spice**. The rice is pre-seasoned with turmeric, anise, cumin, cinnamon, cardamom, bay leaves and **dried lime**, plus carrot and tomato juice, and sits on a lower rack with tomatoes and onions.
*   **Tribal or regional variants:** ⚠️ Not documented. Lamb vs. goat vs. chicken, and the spice blends, vary by family and camp.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **home / camp zarb** with lamb and chicken, following the documented layering. Quantities are reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb, bone-in pieces (shoulder) | 800 g |
| Chicken, in quarters | 800 g |
| Marinade: plain yogurt / garlic / lemon juice / olive oil | 150 g / 4 cloves / 1 lemon / 30 ml |
| Seven-spice (*baharat*) + paprika + salt | 2 tsp + 1 tsp + 2 tsp |
| Potatoes, thick slices | 4 (600 g) |
| Onions, quartered / tomatoes, halved | 3 / 4 |
| Carrots, grated (for the rice) | 2 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Long-grain rice (basmati), rinsed and soaked 20 min | 400 g |
| Water + tomato juice for the rice (≈ 1.25 : 1 by volume; it also steams) | 450 ml + 100 ml |
| Oil / samn for the rice | 40 ml |
| Turmeric / cumin seed / anise seed | ½ tsp / ½ tsp / ¼ tsp |
| Cinnamon stick / cardamom pods / bay leaves / dried lime (pierced) | 1 / 4 / 2 / 1 |
| Coals | a pit or drum fire burned down to a deep bed of embers (≈ 1 h) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Marinate (4 h or overnight):** Coat the lamb and chicken in the yogurt marinade and spices.
2. **Fire:** Light a wood fire in the pit or buried drum and burn it **≈ 1 hour** to a thick bed of glowing coals.
3. **Rice tray:** Fry the onion with the whole spices and turmeric in the oil. Add the carrot, the tomato juice, salt, bay and dried lime, then stir in the drained rice and pour over the water. Put it in a deep tray and cover tightly with foil.
4. **Load the rack:** Rice tray and potatoes, onions and tomatoes on the **lower** tiers, meat on the **top** tier so its juices drip down.
5. **Bury:** Lower the rack into the pit over the coals. Cover with a lid or metal sheet, then blankets or sacking, then **sand**, sealing every gap so no steam escapes.
6. **Cook:** Leave **2.5–3.5 hours** undisturbed.
7. **Open and serve:** Dig out the rack in front of the guests. Turn the rice onto a large tray, lay the vegetables and meat on top, and serve with bread, yogurt and salad.
