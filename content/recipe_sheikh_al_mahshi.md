# Sheikh al-Mahshi / شيخ المحشي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is well documented, and sources commonly trace it to Syria. The Damascus attribution and the town variants below come from Arabic recipe sites and encyclopedic sources rather than historical documents. Note that, unlike most *mahashi*, the filling has **no rice**; it belongs in this atlas because it is traditionally **served with rice**, which completes it.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Syria (Damascus is the usual attribution), with versions across Palestine (Hebron), Jordan, Iraq and the Gulf; Lebanon prefers eggplant.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** "The chief of stuffed dishes": small zucchini stuffed with lamb, onion and pine nuts, lightly browned, then simmered in a **cooked yogurt sauce**, ladled over plain white or vermicelli rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A frying pan for the zucchini, then a heavy pot for the yogurt sauce, which must be stirred constantly over gentle heat.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Sheikh al-mahshi* means "the chief (sheikh) of stuffed things". It is a dish of prestige: the zucchini are hollowed, stuffed with meat and nuts rather than cheap rice, fried in *samneh* (clarified butter) and finished in yogurt. Serving it is considered an honour for guests because of the work involved. The original sauce is **yogurt** stabilised with starch or flour (and sometimes an egg) so it does not split; tomato versions are later derivatives. In Hebron the sauce is made with *jameed*, the dried yogurt of [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md).

Syrian recipe sites describe it as served hot with white rice, or with rice and chicken, and accompanied by pickles. It belongs to the same yogurt-sauce family as [shakriyeh](recipe_shakriyeh.md) and *kibbeh labaniyeh*: meat cooked in *laban* and spooned over rice.

**Culinary science:** yogurt curdles above ~80 °C because its proteins (mainly casein) clump in the acid. Whisking in cornflour or an egg before heating, stirring in one direction until it boils, and never covering the pot keeps the proteins dispersed. Frying the zucchini first adds Maillard flavour and stops them releasing water into the sauce.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascus (yogurt, the "original"):** Zucchini (*kousa*) with lamb, onion, pine nuts, cinnamon and pepper; yogurt sauce finished with garlic fried with dried mint; white rice alongside.
*   **Hebron (Palestine):** Sauce made from *jameed* (dried yogurt) instead of fresh yogurt; heavier and saltier.
*   **Lebanon:** More often eggplant (*sheikh al-mahshi batinjan*), sometimes in tomato.
*   **Tomato version (modern, pan-Levantine):** Fried stuffed zucchini simmered in tomato sauce; still served with rice.
*   **Iraq & the Gulf:** Spread widely under the same name; zucchini preferred.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Damascus, yogurt sauce, with white rice**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Small zucchini (kousa), cored | 12 (≈ 1.2 kg) |
| Minced lamb | 400 g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 1 large |
| Pine nuts | 40 g |
| Samneh or butter (frying) | 60 g |
| Cinnamon, allspice, black pepper, salt | ½ tsp each, salt to taste |
| Full-fat yogurt | 1.25 kg |
| Cornflour (or 1 egg + 1 tbsp flour) | 2 tbsp |
| Garlic, crushed | 4 cloves |
| Dried mint | 1 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| White rice (Egyptian short-grain), to serve | 300 g |
| Rice cooking water | 450 ml (≈ 1.5 : 1) |
| Yogurt : zucchini | ≈ 1 : 1 by weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Filling:** Fry the onion in half the samneh, add the lamb and spices, cook until browned; stir in the toasted pine nuts.
2. **Stuff and fry:** Fill the cored zucchini (not too tightly) and fry them in the rest of the samneh until golden on all sides.
3. **Stabilise the yogurt:** Whisk the yogurt with the cornflour (or egg) and a cup of water in the pot until smooth.
4. **Cook the sauce:** Heat gently, stirring constantly in one direction, until it boils and thickens. Do not cover.
5. **Simmer:** Add the zucchini and simmer 15–20 minutes, until tender.
6. **Rice:** Meanwhile cook the rice: rinse, toast briefly in butter, add boiling salted water, cover and cook 15 minutes on low; rest 5 minutes.
7. **Finish:** Fry the garlic with the dried mint in a little butter and pour over the pot. Serve the zucchini and sauce with the rice.
