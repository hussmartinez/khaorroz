# Ruz w Lahm al-ʿUrs (Village Wedding Rice and Meat) / رز ولحم العرس

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a **historical village feast** reconstructed from ethnography, not from a written recipe. The Finnish anthropologist Hilma Granqvist recorded wedding customs in the village of **Artas** (near Bethlehem) in the 1920s–30s. As analysed in *Jerusalem Quarterly* (2024), she describes men slaughtering goats, sheep or a calf and cooking "the meat and rice on a big fire", then placing "the rice on big wooden trays" with "pieces of meat on the top". The quantities and seasoning below are a modern adaptation.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Artas and the villages of the Bethlehem hills; by extension the fellahi villages of the West Bank highlands.
*   **Historical Source:** Hilma Granqvist, *Marriage Conditions in a Palestinian Village* (Artas, fieldwork 1925–1931; published Helsinki 1931–35), as discussed in *Jerusalem Quarterly* 99 (2024).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Feast rice (*walima*). Plain rice cooked in the meat broth, butter-rich and fluffy, heaped on wooden trays under boiled mutton or goat.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Huge copper cauldrons over an open wood fire; large round **wooden trays** for serving.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Granqvist's Artas, rice was a purchased luxury and the defining food of the wedding. The women's songs praise the groom's man as "father of the overflowing food tray, full of **rice from the sea** and piled up with mutton". "Rice from the sea" means rice imported by ship, chiefly Egyptian rice landed at Jaffa. Rice was also part of the **bride price**: one recorded settlement lists "£100 minus 5 piasters… two sacks of rice and one sack of wheat and 9 goats and melted butter (*samne*)". The bride herself was sent home to her parents with "a basin with meat and rice on her head", a sign that she was now a provider.

Cooking was men's work at these feasts: "Near men, relatives to the bridegroom, slaughter the animals for the feast – goats, sheep, or sometimes a calf or a cow – and cook the meat and rice on a big fire". The guests were served on wooden trays. Hospitality was competitive: villagers vied to host guests (*mughalata*), and the winner "prepares the food needed for the feast, such as rice and sheep". This undressed rice-and-meat tray is the ancestor of the more elaborate [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md), [qidreh](recipe_qidreh.md) and [ruz bi-djaj with hashweh](recipe_ruz_bi_djaj_hashweh.md) of later Palestinian weddings. It closely parallels the communal feast rices of Spain's matanzas and village fiestas (see [arroz al horno](recipe_arroz_al_horno.md)).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Artas, 1920s–30s (Granqvist):** Goat or sheep (sometimes calf or cow) boiled; rice cooked "on a big fire"; wooden trays; *samne* (clarified butter) as part of the provisions.
*   **Later village weddings (⚠️):** Rice tinted with turmeric and topped with fried almonds and pine nuts. In the south, *jameed* sauce made it mansaf; in Hebron, the sealed qidreh pot.
*   **Gaza and coastal weddings:** Qidreh in clay urns and maqluba ([qidreh](recipe_qidreh.md)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: modern adaptation of the Artas wedding tray (scaled from feast to family size).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Mutton or goat on the bone (shoulder, neck) | 1.5 kg |
| Water | 3 L |
| Onion, halved | 1 |
| Salt | 2 tsp |
| Bay leaf, cardamom, allspice berries (optional, later practice) | 2 / 4 / 6 |
| Samneh (clarified butter, sheep's if possible) | 60 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Egyptian medium-grain rice, rinsed | 400 g |
| Meat broth for the rice | 700 ml |
| Samneh for the rice | 40 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Boil the meat:** Cover the meat with the water, bring to a boil and skim thoroughly. Add the onion, salt and spices and simmer for 2–2.5 hours until tender.
2. **Rice:** Melt the samneh in a heavy pot, stir in the drained rice for 2 minutes, add 700 ml of hot broth, cover and cook on low for 18–20 minutes.
3. **Brown the meat (optional):** Brush the meat with the remaining samneh and brown it briefly in a hot oven or over embers.
4. **Tray:** Heap the rice on a large wooden or metal tray and lay the meat on top.
5. **Serve:** Serve the rest of the broth in bowls, and eat from the shared tray with the right hand or with bread.
