# Keftes (Syrian-Jewish Meatballs in Tamarind Sauce, over Rice) / كفتة بالتمر الهندي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented by Jewish food media (My Jewish Learning, Forward, Kosher.com) for the Aleppo-Jewish community in Brooklyn, where it is a holiday dish served with rice. Families vary the tamarind, lemon and tomato ratios. The Aleppine town of origin of specific variants is not documented. Hebrew name: קפטס.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Aleppo's Jewish community and its diaspora (Brooklyn, Deal NJ, Mexico City, Israel).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: herb-and-pine-nut beef meatballs, browned then simmered in a sweet-sour sauce of tamarind (*ou*), tomato and Aleppo pepper until glossy, spooned over plain white rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A frying pan to brown the meatballs and a heavy pot for the sauce; a lidded pot for the rice.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the Aleppo-Jewish kitchen, **tamarind concentrate** (*ou*, *temerhindi*) plays the role pomegranate molasses plays elsewhere in Syria. Poopa Dweck calls it "the sour secret to Syrian cooking" ([Tablet – Taking on Tamarind](https://www.tabletmag.com/podcasts/vox-tablet/taking-on-tamarind)). *Keftes* are its signature meatballs. My Jewish Learning's recipe, learned from a Syrian-Italian family in Brooklyn and described as "a holiday favorite", simmers the meatballs in tamarind, tomato paste and lemon and ends with "serve with rice" ([My Jewish Learning – Syrian meatballs with tamarind sauce](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/the-nosher/syrian-meatballs-with-tamarind-sauce-recipe/)). The best-known variant, ***keftes garaz***, adds **sour cherries**, a Jewish echo of Aleppo's famous *kebab bil-karaz* ([Forward – Keftes garaz](https://forward.com/food/recipes/382890/syrian-meatballs-keftes-garaz-with-cherries-and-tamarind)). At Passover, matzah meal replaces breadcrumbs.

**Culinary science:** tamarind supplies tartaric acid, and sugar balances it. As the sauce reduces, the meatballs absorb it and plump slightly. Browning first (Maillard) gives the sauce depth and keeps the egg-bound meatballs from falling apart.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Brooklyn Aleppo-Jewish (documented):** Beef, eggs, pine nuts, matzah meal, herbs, baharat, Aleppo pepper; tamarind-tomato-lemon sauce.
*   **Keftes garaz:** Sour cherries (fresh, frozen or dried) added to the tamarind sauce.
*   **Family ratios:** The amount of tamarind, lemon and tomato varies by family; cooks are told to taste as they go.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Brooklyn Aleppo-Jewish keftes** (My Jewish Learning, about one-third), **with white rice**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ground beef | 1 kg |
| Eggs | 2 |
| Pine nuts, toasted | ½ cup |
| Matzah meal or breadcrumbs (+ extra for rolling) | ¼ cup |
| Fresh herbs (parsley, coriander, mint), chopped | ⅓ cup |
| Baharat or allspice / Aleppo pepper / salt | 1 tsp / 1 tsp / 1½ tsp |
| Olive oil + Aleppo pepper + allspice (sauce) | 2 tbsp + ½ tsp + ½ tsp |
| Water | 1.3 L |
| Tamarind concentrate | 3 tbsp |
| Tomato paste | 3 tbsp |
| Lemon juice; sugar (optional) | 1 tbsp; to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short- or medium-grain white rice, washed | 320 g |
| Water for the rice | ≈ 480 ml |
| Oil or butter for the rice | 1 tbsp |
| Meatballs per person | 6–8 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meatballs:** Mix the beef, eggs, pine nuts, matzah meal, herbs and spices; roll walnut-sized balls and coat lightly in matzah meal.
2. **Brown:** Fry in batches in a little oil until browned on all sides.
3. **Sauce:** Warm the olive oil with Aleppo pepper until red, add allspice, then the water, tamarind, tomato paste, lemon and salt; simmer a few minutes.
4. **Simmer:** Add the meatballs and cook about 30 min until the sauce thickens; taste and adjust sour-sweet balance.
5. **Rice:** Toast the rice 1 min in oil, add boiling water and salt, cover and cook on very low heat 15–18 min; rest 5 min.
6. **Serve:** Meatballs and sauce over the rice.
