# Galayet Bandora with Rice / قلاية بندورة مع الرز

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** galayet (qalayet) bandora is a well-documented Jordanian and Palestinian dish, but it is **more often eaten with bread** than with rice. Wikipedia and several Jordanian food guides record the rice version ("galayet bandora rice", served over rice and eaten with a spoon); none localise it town by town. The Jordan Valley origin story is reported as one opinion among several.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Jordan and Palestine, especially the farming villages and the Jordan Valley (al-Ghor), where tomatoes are grown; eaten everywhere from Amman to Irbid and Karak.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice with a quick stew (*ruz w yakhneh* family). Plain white or vermicelli rice under a thick, glossy pan-fry of tomatoes, onion, chili and olive oil, sometimes with a little minced meat; saucy, never soupy.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A shallow frying pan (*meqlaye*) or small clay dish for the tomatoes, a pot for the rice; historically over a wood fire in the fields during the olive harvest.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Galayeh* (Levantine *qalayeh*, "a frying") is the simplest Levantine stew: ripe tomatoes fried down in olive oil with onion or garlic and hot green chili. Wikipedia describes it as popular across the Levant "but especially in Jordan and Palestine", and notes that Jordanians and Palestinians traditionally make it **during the olive harvest**, when families eat in the groves with fresh oil. One Arabic account traces it to the **Jordan Valley and Palestine**, where tomatoes grow well; another stresses that tomatoes only entered Levantine cooking around the **19th century**, although they reached the Mediterranean in the 16th. So this is a young dish by Levantine standards, and the rice pairing is younger still.

In Jordanian homes it is eaten two ways: scooped with *taboon* or *shrak* bread, or ladled over rice for a cheap, filling meal. Food guides list **"galayet bandora rice"** among Jordan's rice dishes, describing a sweet-tangy tomato sauce with garlic, olive oil and sometimes beef over rice. A Jordanian touch reported in recipe sites is **sumac**, and serving it with fried eggs in the style of shakshuka. Its Spanish cousin is the tomato-and-pepper *sofrito* that underpins [arroz a la cubana](recipe_arroz_a_la_cubana_canario.md).

**Culinary science:** frying tomato in plenty of olive oil drives off water and concentrates sugars and glutamates; the oil emulsifies with the tomato pulp into a glossy sauce that clings to rice grains. Hot chili's capsaicin dissolves in the oil, so the heat spreads through the dish.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Jordan Valley / al-Ghor (⚠️ reported origin):** Tomatoes straight from the field, olive oil and green chili; meatless.
*   **Amman and the cities:** Often with a little minced beef or lamb, and served over vermicelli rice; sometimes garnished with toasted pine nuts in restaurants.
*   **Jordanian sumac version:** Sumac stirred in at the end for extra sourness (recipe sites).
*   **With eggs:** Eggs cracked into the pan at the end, shakshuka-style; then usually eaten with bread rather than rice.
*   **Palestinian villages:** The same dish at olive-harvest time, usually with bread; garlic rather than onion in some families.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Amman household version over rice**, with optional meat.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ripe tomatoes, diced | 1 kg |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Onion, finely chopped (or 4 garlic cloves) | 1 |
| Hot green chilies, sliced | 2 |
| Minced beef or lamb (optional) | 250 g |
| Salt | 1 tsp |
| Sumac (optional) | 1 tsp |
| Toasted pine nuts (optional garnish) | 30 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice (Egyptian or medium grain) | 320 g |
| Vermicelli (*sh'ariyyeh*, optional) | 40 g |
| Water for the rice | ≈ 600 ml (≈ 1.9 : 1) |
| Butter or oil for the rice | 20 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Toast the vermicelli in the butter until golden, add the rinsed rice and salt, stir 1 min, add the boiling water and cook covered on low **15 min**; rest 5 min (see [riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md)).
2. **Base:** Heat the olive oil and soften the onion and chilies (**5 min**). If using meat, add it now and brown it (**5 min**).
3. **Tomatoes:** Add the tomatoes and salt and fry over medium-high heat, stirring, for **15–20 min**, until the water has cooked off and the oil separates.
4. **Finish:** Stir in the sumac if using. Taste for salt.
5. **Serve:** Spoon the galayeh over the rice, scatter pine nuts on top, and serve hot with green chilies on the side.
