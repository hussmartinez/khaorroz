# Ghammeh / غمّة

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a disappearing offal dish documented mainly by Anissa Helou (*Offal: The Fifth Quarter*, *Cooking the Foods of My Homeland*) and by diaspora family recipes. Its regional spread inside Lebanon is not documented; the forum and diaspora variants below are reported as family practice.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lebanon (Helou's Beirut family tradition) and the Lebanese diaspora (the US *ghammi* of Madelain Farah). Closely related dishes are cooked in Syria and Libya.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed offal. Lamb's stomach (tripe) sewn into pouches and intestines filled with rice, chickpeas, minced lamb and tomato, simmered for hours with lamb's trotters. The rice turns soft and rich in gelatin, served in a deep, garlicky broth.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large stockpot over a long, slow flame. The stomach pouches are sewn shut with needle and thread.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Ghammeh* is the most ambitious rice dish of the Lebanese "fifth quarter": the stomach, intestines and trotters of the lamb, which a household that had slaughtered an animal would never waste. Anissa Helou remembers her mother making it and compares preparing the tripe to "dressmaking": the stomach is cut into pieces, sewn into pouches, filled three-quarters full (the rice swells) and sewn shut. The finished dish, she writes, is delicious even if it is not pretty. The same rice-and-meat stuffing in sheep casings is [fawaregh](recipe_fawaregh.md), and the two are often cooked together.

**Culinary science:** the lamb's trotters are simmered first. Their collagen dissolves into gelatin, giving the broth the body that the rice absorbs inside the pouches. The tripe needs repeated washing and scraping to remove its smell. Chickpeas are soaked and rubbed with bicarbonate of soda so they soften within the long cooking time instead of staying hard.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Anissa Helou's family version (Beirut):** Short-grain (Egyptian) rice, chickpeas, canned tomatoes, onion, minced lamb neck, seven-spice and cinnamon. Cooked with 4 lamb trotters; the broth is optionally finished with crushed garlic and lemon.
*   **Lebanese-American *ghammi* (Madelain Farah):** Seasoned with saffron and allspice; commenters note that beef tripe and casings can replace lamb.
*   **Grandmother's version (diaspora forum):** 2 cups rice, 1 cup soaked chickpeas and a pound of minced lamb shoulder, the tripe boiled after stuffing.
*   **Libyan *osban*:** A related North African stuffed tripe, noted for comparison only.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Anissa Helou's method, scaled from 6–8 to 4.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb's trotters, singed and washed | 2–3 |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Lamb's stomach (tripe), thoroughly cleaned, in 4–5 pieces | ½–1 small |
| Lamb intestine, cleaned (optional) | 1 length (≈ 1 m) |
| Short-grain rice (Egyptian or Calasparra), rinsed | 300 g |
| Dried chickpeas, soaked overnight | 130 g |
| Bicarbonate of soda (for the chickpeas) | 1 tsp |
| Canned tomatoes, drained and chopped | 1 × 800 g can |
| Onion, finely chopped | 1 large |
| Minced lamb (neck) | 330 g |
| Seven-spice (or allspice) | 1½ tsp |
| Ground cinnamon, black pepper, sea salt | ½ tsp each, salt to taste |
| Garlic and lemon juice (to finish) | 3 cloves, 1 lemon |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Fill each pouch | ≈ ¾ full (the rice swells) |
| Water | to cover the trotters and pouches generously (≈ 3 L) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Chickpeas:** Rub the drained chickpeas with bicarbonate of soda, leave 15–20 min, then rinse well.
2. **Broth:** Bring the trotters to a near-boil in a large pot of water, skim, add the cinnamon, cover and simmer **1½ h**.
3. **Stuffing:** Mix the rice, chickpeas, tomatoes, onion, minced lamb, spices and salt.
4. **Sew and fill:** Sew the tripe pieces into pouches with one side open, fill each three-quarters full and sew it shut. Fill the intestine loosely and tie both ends.
5. **Simmer:** Add the parcels to the pot with the trotters, top up with water, salt, and cook **≈ 2½ h** until tender.
6. **Serve:** Serve very hot with the broth, seasoned if you like with crushed garlic and lemon juice.
