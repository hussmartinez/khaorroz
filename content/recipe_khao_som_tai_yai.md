# Khao Som (Tai Yai Tomato Rice) / ข้าวส้ม (ไทใหญ่)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented in Thai press and Thai PBS's "one province, one dish" series, but the full recipe article (Khaosod) could not be read. Quantities are reconstructed from the published description: rice kneaded with sour tomatoes, fish or pork, garlic and turmeric oil. The name "khao som" is also used for unrelated dishes elsewhere, so this file covers only the Tai Yai rice ball.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Mae Hong Son and other Tai Yai (Shan) communities of north-western Thailand.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Hand-kneaded rice balls. Warm non-glutinous rice is mashed with soft sour tomato and river fish (or pork) into a tangy, orange-red rice ball, sometimes with mashed potato.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok for the garlic-turmeric oil and tomato, a rice pot, and a wide bowl for kneading by hand.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khao som* ("sour rice") is the Tai Yai rice ball. Cooked non-glutinous rice is kneaded with *makhuea som*, a small, sourer tomato, then enriched with flaked river fish or mashed potato and shaped by hand. Thai press calls it "the Tai Yai version of a rice ball" ([Khaosod – Sentang Sedtee](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/featured/article_37037)), and Thai PBS lists it among strangely named provincial dishes ([Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/331274)). Like [khao kan chin](recipe_khao_kan_chin.md), it reflects the Tai Yai preference for ordinary rice in a sticky-rice region.

**Culinary science:** acid from the tomato and the gelatinised starch of warm rice combine into a cohesive, mouldable paste. Turmeric fried in garlic oil fixes the colour in fat, and the fish adds protein and savoury depth.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Mae Hong Son (fish):** Flaked river fish fried with tomato in garlic-turmeric oil ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/featured/article_37037)).
*   **Pork version:** Minced pork instead of fish.
*   **Pantry version:** Pounded dried shrimp instead of fresh fish ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/featured/article_37037)).
*   **Vegetarian:** Tomato, potato and garlic oil only.
*   ⚠️ Accompaniments such as fried garlic, dried chilli and herbs are general Tai Yai practice, not documented for this dish specifically.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Mae Hong Son fish khao som (reconstructed quantities).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Cooked jasmine rice, warm | 600 g |
| Small sour tomatoes (*makhuea som*) or cherry tomatoes | 300 g |
| River fish, steamed and flaked (or minced pork / 40 g dried shrimp) | 200 g |
| Garlic, sliced | 6 cloves |
| Ground turmeric | 1 tsp |
| Vegetable oil / salt | 4 tbsp / 1 tsp |
| Boiled potato, mashed (optional) | 150 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice : tomato | 2 : 1 by weight |
| Balls | 8, about 120 g each |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Garlic oil:** Fry the garlic in the oil until golden, then stir in the turmeric for a few seconds. Lift the garlic out and keep it.
2. **Tomato and fish:** Fry the tomatoes in the turmeric oil until they collapse, then add the fish (or pork / dried shrimp) and salt. Cook until thick.
3. **Knead:** Tip the warm rice into a bowl, add the tomato mixture (and potato), and knead by hand until evenly orange and cohesive.
4. **Shape:** Form into balls or flattened mounds.
5. **Serve:** Top with the fried garlic. Eat warm or at room temperature.
