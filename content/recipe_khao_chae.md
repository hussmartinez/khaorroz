# Khao Chae / ข้าวแช่

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the Mon origin and the Songkran link are agreed by all sources. **When** the dish entered the Thai court is contested: sources variously say Rama II (1809–1824), Rama IV (through Chao Chom Manda Sonklin, a royal consort of Mon descent) or Rama V (via the royal visits to Phetchaburi). The side-dish set below follows Bangkok court-style restaurants. Quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Mon communities of the Central Plains (Pak Kret / Ko Kret, Pathum Thani, Phra Pradaeng, Ratchaburi), the Bangkok court, and Phetchaburi (Phra Nakhon Khiri, Tha Yang).
*   **Historical Source:** Mon Songkran tradition (*Thingyan* rice), adopted by the Rattanakosin court in the 19th century; oral and court tradition, no single written recipe.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Court rice served in **scented iced water**: firm, starch-free grains that stay separate in jasmine-perfumed cold water, eaten with a set of intensely sweet-salty fried and stuffed side dishes.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay pot (*mo din*) and a steamer (*lang thawa*, *huat*) over a charcoal stove (*tao than*); a glazed bowl (*chamkhrop*) for serving; an earthen jar for perfuming the water with jasmine and candle smoke.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao chae ("soaked rice") began as the **Mon Songkran rice**, which the Mon call *poeng dachk*. It was prepared at the April New Year, at the height of the hot season, as an offering to the deities and to monks ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/news/story-of-khao-chae-chao-wang); [Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=48456)). Thai accounts credit **Chao Chom Manda Sonklin**, a royal consort of Mon descent, with bringing it to King Mongkut's table (Rama IV). It then travelled with the court to the hilltop palace of Phra Nakhon Khiri in **Phetchaburi**, where the town adopted it. Other accounts say it reached the public through palace cooks in Rama V's reign ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2690075); [Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/lifestyle/news_2462527)). English-language sources place its adoption earlier, under Rama II. Chef McDang called it "the only Thai dish that can truly be considered royal Thai cuisine" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_chae)). The Tourism Authority now promotes it as the dish of Songkran ([TAT News](https://www.tatnews.org/?p=81686)).

**Culinary science:** the grains are cooked firm, then **rinsed under running water** to strip surface starch, so the rice stays crisp and separate in cold water instead of turning to gruel. Sources say ordinary jasmine rice is too soft, and the firmer *khao ta haeng* is preferred. The water is perfumed in stages: jasmine flowers are steeped overnight, and the jar is smoked with a scented candle (*thian op*). The aromatics are volatile, so the water is served chilled with ice and flowers. The side dishes are deliberately **sweet and salty**, to balance the bland cold rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Mon communities (Ko Kret, Pathum Thani, Phra Pradaeng) — the original Songkran rice:** prepared for the New Year as an offering, then shared. ⚠️ The differences in Mon side dishes are not documented in the sources found.
*   **Bangkok court style (khao chae chao wang):** the full set of side dishes: **luk kapi** (shrimp-paste balls with wild ginger, shallot and palm sugar), **hom daeng yat sai** (stuffed, battered shallots), **phrik yuak sot sai** (stuffed sweet peppers wrapped in egg net), **mu/nuea foi** (sweet shredded pork or beef), **chai po phat khai** (sweet pickled radish with egg), with fresh mango, cucumber and krachai ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_chae)). Served in Banglamphu, Dinso Road and hotel restaurants every April ([Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/life/food/2024/04/15/refreshing-with-khao-chae-at-benjarong-baan-dusit/)).
*   **Phetchaburi (Tha Yang, town shops):** the town treats khao chae as its emblem. Shops stress **luk kapi, pla wan** (sweet dried fish) and **chai po phat wan**, and serve set plates year-round ([KTC](https://www.ktc.co.th/article/dining/restaurant/kao-chae-phetburi); [Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/food/1224748)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok court style**, simplified to two side dishes (luk kapi, chai po phat khai).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Firm jasmine or *khao ta haeng* rice | 300 g |
| Drinking water for perfuming | 2 L |
| Fresh jasmine flowers (unsprayed) | 2 handfuls |
| Thai scented candle (*thian op*), optional | 1 |
| Shrimp paste (*kapi*), roasted | 60 g |
| Krachai (fingerroot), shallot, garlic, pounded | 30 g each |
| Palm sugar | 60 g |
| Sweet pickled radish (*chai po wan*), chopped | 120 g |
| Eggs | 2 |
| Fresh mango, cucumber, krachai, chilies to serve | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Cooked rice | ≈ 750 g |
| Scented iced water per bowl | ≈ 300 ml |
| Side dishes | 2–6 kinds, small portions |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Perfume the water (the night before):** float the jasmine flowers on cold boiled water in a covered jar overnight. Optionally smoke the jar with a lit scented candle under the lid. Strain and chill.
2. **Cook the rice firm:** boil or steam the rice until just tender. Rinse it repeatedly in cold water, rubbing gently, until the water runs clear and the grains are firm and separate. Drain.
3. **Steam:** sprinkle the rice with a little scented water, wrap it in muslin and steam it for 5–8 min to set the grains. Cool.
4. **Luk kapi:** fry the pounded krachai, shallot and garlic, add the kapi and palm sugar, and cook down to a stiff paste. Roll it into small balls.
5. **Chai po phat khai:** stir-fry the radish with a little sugar until glossy, then scramble in the eggs.
6. **Serve:** put rice in each bowl, pour iced scented water over it, and add a few ice cubes and a jasmine flower. Eat a spoonful of rice and water, then a bite of a side dish.
