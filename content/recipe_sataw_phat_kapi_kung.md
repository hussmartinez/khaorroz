# Sataw Phat Kapi Kung / สะตอผัดกะปิกุ้ง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the sources are home recipes (Wongnai user recipes) and a Thai PBS cooking segment. There is no historical source.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Thai south (sataw-growing provinces: Surat Thani, Nakhon Si Thammarat, Trang, Krabi), now cooked nationwide.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a quick, glossy stir-fry of bitter-green *sataw* beans (stink beans) with prawns in a pounded shrimp-paste and chili sauce. It is spooned over hot rice with a boiled egg and fresh vegetables.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok (*kratha*) over a strong flame; the paste is pounded in a mortar.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Sataw* (*Parkia speciosa*) is the southern bean par excellence: pungent, slightly bitter, and eaten raw with *nam phrik* or cooked with shrimp paste. Every published recipe follows the same pattern: fry shrimp paste with pounded chili and garlic until fragrant, then add sataw and prawns, and season with sugar and fish sauce or salt ([Wongnai – home recipe](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/b2e2a6231e264f228b6f17e266b8e708); [Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/051043cfbcfc4e51bb4f758d7094a791)). The southern way to eat it is with hot steamed rice, a boiled egg and fresh vegetables ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=63005)).

**Culinary science:** shrimp paste is very rich in glutamates, and frying it in oil releases toasty pyrazines. The sweetness of the sugar and prawns balances the sataw's sulphurous bitterness.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Dried-shrimp version:** dried shrimp, red jinda chilies and single-clove garlic ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/b2e2a6231e264f228b6f17e266b8e708)).
*   **Grilled-kapi version:** shrimp paste grilled in a banana leaf, then pounded with garlic and shallot; finished with lemon basil and lime ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/051043cfbcfc4e51bb4f758d7094a791)).
*   **East-coast fusion:** an Ang Sila (Chonburi) prawn and sataw stir-fry ([Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/Foodwork/watch/185265)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sataw beans, shelled | 200 g |
| Prawns, peeled | 300 g |
| Shrimp paste (kapi) | 1½ tbsp |
| Garlic | 6 cloves |
| Red chilies | 5 |
| Oil | 2 tbsp |
| Palm sugar | 1 tbsp |
| Fish sauce | 1 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 400 g raw, steamed |
| Stir-fry per plate | ¼ of the batch |
| Sides | boiled egg, cucumber, herbs |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Steam the jasmine rice (≈ 18 min) and rest.
2. **Paste:** Pound the garlic, chilies and shrimp paste coarsely.
3. **Fry:** Fry the paste in hot oil until fragrant (1 min).
4. **Prawns and sataw:** Add the prawns until they turn pink, then the sataw, and toss 1–2 min.
5. **Season and serve:** Add sugar, fish sauce and a splash of water. Serve over hot rice.
