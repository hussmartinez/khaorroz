# Jazar Mahshi bil-Tamr Hindi (Stuffed Red Carrots in Tamarind) / جزر محشي بالتمر الهندي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the main source is the Palestinian-recipe section of the Jordan-based blog *Chef in Disguise* (Sawsan Abu Farha). Claims that particular towns (e.g. Jaffa) specialise in it could not be verified. An Egyptian Ramadan version also exists.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Palestinian (and Palestinian-Jordanian) home cooking, in winter when long red carrots are in season.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vegetables (*mahashi*). Cored red carrots are filled with rice and minced meat, lightly fried, then simmered in a sweet-sour sauce of tamarind and pomegranate molasses until soft. The rice turns tender and stained mahogany.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A long coring tool (*qawara*), a frying pan, and a wide heavy pot on the stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Palestinian cooks stuff carrots, eggplant, potatoes, zucchini, cucumbers, tomatoes and more, and pair each with its own sauce: tomato, lemon and garlic, stock, tamarind, or yogurt and jameed. Long red (purple-red) carrots go with the sweet-sour tamarind sauce, which "balances the mint, garlic and sweetness of the carrots". The cook also suggests cucumbers as a substitute ([Chef in Disguise – Stuffed red carrots with tamarind sauce](https://chefindisguise.com/2020/04/21/stuffed-red-carrots-with-tamarind-sauce-jazar-mahshi-bil-tamr-el-hindi/)). In Egypt a Ramadan version of rice-stuffed carrots with tamarind circulates as a modern recipe ([Sada El-Balad](https://www.elbalad.news/6889292)). Compare the Aleppine [mehshi basal](recipe_mehshi_basal.md) and the Aleppo-Jewish [yebra](recipe_yebra.md), which also use tamarind.

**Culinary science:** soaking the carrots for hours in lemon water softens them for coring. Frying before braising caramelises the surface. Tamarind and pomegranate molasses give an acid that keeps the carrots' shape during the long simmer while the rice inside cooks through.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Palestinian home style (documented):** Red carrots, rice and beef, dried mint, tamarind and pomegranate sauce, finished with a garlic-herb *tasheh*. Served with yogurt or extra *hashweh* rice.
*   **Cucumber version:** Stuffed cucumbers in the same sauce with a shorter cooking time, suggested by the same source. ⚠️ Jerusalem attributions are unverified.
*   **Egyptian Ramadan version:** Rice-and-nut stuffing with tamarind on top. A modern viral recipe.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Chef in Disguise proportions (Palestinian section).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Tamarind block simmered in 4 cups water, strained | 200 g → 1 cup concentrate used |
| Pomegranate molasses | ½ cup |
| Onion, chopped + dried mint + carrot cores | 1 small / 1 tbsp / all |
| Water | 6 cups |
| Garlic *tasheh*: olive oil + garlic + parsley + fresh mint | ¼ cup / 1 head / ¼ cup / ¼ cup |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Red carrots, peeled and cored | 16 |
| Rice, soaked 15 min | 1 cup (≈ 200 g) |
| Minced beef | 125 g |
| Allspice, cinnamon, dried mint, salt | ½ tsp / ½ tsp / 1 tsp / ½ tsp |
| Oil for frying | ½ cup |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Soak the peeled carrots for 6 h in water with ¼ cup of lemon juice, then core them, keeping the cores.
2. Mix the drained rice, beef, spices and 1 tbsp of oil. Fill the carrots, leaving 2 cm empty at the top.
3. Fry the carrots in batches for 3–5 min per side.
4. Soften the onion and the carrot cores in oil (8–10 min). Add the mint, tamarind, molasses, salt and water, and simmer for 10–15 min.
5. Add the carrots, cover and cook on medium for **30–40 min** until soft and the rice is cooked.
6. Fry the garlic, parsley and mint for 5 min, stir them into the pot, rest covered for 15 min and serve with yogurt.
