# Shakriyeh with Vermicelli Rice / شاكرية مع رز بشعيرية

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Damascus, Hama and Homs; shared with Lebanon (*laban immo*) and Palestine.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stew over rice (*yakhneh w ruz*). A hot yogurt-and-lamb stew spooned over a fluffy vermicelli pilaf.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot for the meat, a heavy saucepan for the yogurt (stirred constantly), and a separate pot for the rice; stovetop.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Shakriyeh is lamb (or beef, or chicken) simmered with whole spices and then finished in **hot cooked yogurt**. It is one of the Levant's festive dishes, cooked for **Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, the first day of Ramadan and Christmas**, and most common in spring. As early as 1867 the Lebanese scholar Butrus al-Bustani defined it as "a dish of meat cooked in yogurt". The name's origin is disputed: theories link it to *shukr* ("thanks"), to a personal name Shakir, or to Aramaic. In Lebanon the version with small onions is called ***laban immo*** ("his mother's milk"). It is always eaten with **vermicelli rice** (*ruz bi-sh'ariyyeh*), and the white sauce and the buttery rice are inseparable on the plate.

The dish sits in a family of yogurt-cooked meats across al-Sham. Jordan's [mansaf](provincial_jordan.md) uses dried *jameed* instead of fresh yogurt; Palestinian names include *al-safwa*; some areas call it *arman bi-laban*.

**Culinary science:** yogurt **curdles** when boiled, because its acid-set proteins tighten and squeeze out whey. Levantine cooks stabilise it in three ways: **starch** (cornflour) whisked in cold, an **egg** (in some recipes), and **constant stirring in one direction** while it heats, adding the hot meat broth gradually. Once it has thickened and come to a simmer it no longer splits, and the meat can finish in it.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascus:** **No mint** in the yogurt, and it is often made with **cow's-milk** yogurt. Served with vermicelli rice and toasted pine nuts.
*   **Hama:** Known for the **quality of the "Arabic" yogurt** used; finished with **mint** on top.
*   **Homs:** **Safflower** (*'usfur*) added, with or without mint; some cooks add **garlic**.
*   **Lebanon — laban immo:** Lamb with **small whole onions** in the yogurt; garlic and dried mint fried in butter on top.
*   **Chicken versions:** Common in modern home cooking (cardamom, bay, cinnamon in the broth), served with vermicelli rice.
*   **Hama and Homs (round 3):** Al-Ittihad notes that the dish's preparation "differs from one Syrian city to another": Hama is prized for the quality of its *laban ʿarabi* (local yogurt) and mint, while Homs sometimes adds **safflower** (*ʿusfur*); it is "usually served with rice". ([Al-Ittihad](https://www.aletihad.ae/articleamp/16671/2011/الكبّة-اللبنية-أشهى-الأكلات-الشامية-وأكثرها-شهرة))
*   **Damascene name story (round 3):** Damascenes tell that a mother who first cooked it named it after her beloved son Shakir; other accounts give the name a Turkish origin. An-Nahar recommends serving it with "rice fluffy with vermicelli or cooked bulgur". ([An-Nahar – الشاكرية باللحم على الطريقة السورية](https://www.annahar.com/lifestyle/cooking/216712/الشاكرية-باللحم-على-الطريقة-السورية-وسبب-اسمها-الطريف))

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Damascus** (no mint in the yogurt), with the Hama and Homs finishes noted.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb shoulder (or beef), 3 cm cubes | 800 g |
| Water | 1.5 L |
| Cinnamon stick, 3 cloves, 2 bay leaves, 4 cardamom pods | 1 set |
| Pearl onions (small whole), peeled | 12 |
| Plain full-fat yogurt (cow's milk in Damascus) | 1 kg |
| Cornflour, dissolved in 60 ml cold water | 2 tbsp |
| Egg (optional stabiliser) | 1 |
| Salt | to taste |
| [Homs] Safflower | 1 pinch |
| [Hama] Dried mint, to finish | 1 tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice (Egyptian short-grain or basmati) | 300 g |
| Fine vermicelli (*sh'ariyyeh*) | 60 g |
| Ghee or butter | 40 g |
| Water for the rice | ≈ 1.5× the rice volume (short-grain) |
| Yogurt sauce : meat broth | ≈ 1 kg : 250 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meat:** Cover the meat with the water and whole spices, bring to a boil, skim, and simmer **75 min** (45 min in a pressure cooker). Strain and keep the broth.
2. **Onions:** Fry the pearl onions in a little oil until golden, then simmer them in a ladle of broth for 10 min.
3. **Yogurt:** Whisk the cold yogurt with salt, the cornflour slurry (and the egg, if using). Heat it over medium heat, **stirring constantly in one direction**, until it thickens and simmers (≈ 10–12 min). Add ≈ 250 ml of hot broth gradually.
4. **Combine:** Add the meat and onions and simmer gently for **10 min**. [Homs: add the safflower.]
5. **Vermicelli rice:** Brown the vermicelli in ghee until deep golden. Add the drained rice and stir for 1 min. Add hot water and salt, cover, and cook on low **15–18 min**. Rest 10 min and fluff.
6. **Serve:** Rice on the plate with the shakriyeh ladled over or beside it, topped with toasted pine nuts. [Hama: sprinkle dried mint.]
