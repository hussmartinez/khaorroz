# Ouzi / أوزي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the Damascene filo *ouzi* is well documented in recipes and food media. Its **history is not**: no source traces a dated origin, and one popular account (filo as "packaging" for Bedouin travellers) is anecdotal. The Jordanian and Gulf versions below are described from press accounts; town-level variants inside Syria are not documented.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Damascus and southern Syria; also cooked in Jordan, Lebanon and the Gulf under the same name in different forms.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry): separate, spiced grains, baked inside crisp pastry.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot for the rice, then individual bowls lined with filo (or a large tray) baked in a household or bakery oven.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Ouzi is Damascus's **celebration rice**. Basmati or short-grain rice is cooked with minced lamb or beef, peas (often with carrots), whole spices and the Levantine seven-spice mix (*baharat*). It is then wrapped in thin pastry (filo, a home-rolled dough, or, in modern kitchens, puff pastry) and baked into golden domes or a single great pie, studded with fried almonds, pine nuts or pistachios. Sources describe it as a luxury dish served at **weddings and happy occasions**, but also at **funerals and moments of grief**: it is the dish of gatherings, whatever the reason.

The name travels across the Arab world with different meanings. In **Jordan and the Gulf**, *ouzi* usually means **a whole roast lamb served on a mound of spiced rice** (a feast dish, without pastry). In the UAE and Oman a related dish, *shuwa*, is cooked in an underground oven wrapped in palm leaves. Dubai restaurants serve *ouzi surar* ("ouzi parcels"), the filo-wrapped Damascene form. Arabic recipe sites trace the name to Turkish *kuzu* ("lamb") and call the pastry form *quzi al-Sham*, "the Damascus ouzi".

**Culinary science:** the rice is cooked **just to absorption** before wrapping. The pastry then acts as a sealed oven, so the grains finish in their own steam while the fat in the pastry (ghee or butter brushed between filo sheets) crisps. Under-hydrated rice would stay chalky; over-wet rice would make the pastry soggy from inside.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascus (ouzi bil-ajin / ouzi surar):** Rice + minced meat + peas (± carrots) + nuts, wrapped in filo or puff pastry as individual parcels or one large pie and baked. Served with yogurt or salad.
*   **"Quzi al-Sham" with home-made *ruqaq* dough:** Thin home-rolled sheets line a soup bowl, are filled and folded, then inverted onto a tray and baked.
*   **Jordan / the Gulf — ouzi of whole lamb:** Whole or half lamb roasted (or slow-cooked) and served over spiced rice with nuts; no pastry. ⚠️ Press-level sources only.
*   **Lebanon:** ⚠️ Usually the unwrapped form: rice with lamb, peas and nuts on a platter (close to the Syrian filling served as a pilaf). Not documented in detail.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Damascene filo parcels**, quantities adapted from a published Syrian ouzi recipe (rice and filling) plus standard filo technique.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ghee | 50 g (+ 80 g melted, for the filo) |
| Minced lamb (or 85% lean beef) | 450 g |
| Onions, diced | 2 (≈ 250 g) |
| Garlic | 4 cloves |
| Seven-spice (*baharat*) | 2 tbsp (split between meat and rice) |
| Aleppo pepper | 2 tsp |
| Whole spices: bay leaf, cinnamon stick, 4 cardamom, 2 cloves | 1 set |
| Turmeric | ¼ tsp |
| Chicken stock, warm | 720 ml |
| Peas and diced carrots | 200 g |
| Almonds / pine nuts, fried in ghee | 60 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Basmati rice, soaked 10 min | 300 g (1½ cups) |
| Stock : rice | 2 : 1 by volume |
| Filo sheets | 12–16 (3–4 per parcel) |
| Oven | 190 °C |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meat:** Brown the minced meat with one onion over high heat (8–10 min). Add the garlic, half the spices and salt. Set aside.
2. **Rice:** Soften the second onion in ghee (5 min). Toast the drained rice for 1 min, add the whole spices (30 s), then the peas, carrots, the remaining spices and the turmeric.
3. **Cook:** Add the warm stock, stir once, cover and cook on low for **20 min**. Rest covered for 10 min. Fold in the meat and half the nuts.
4. **Wrap:** Brush a bowl with ghee and lay 3–4 filo sheets crosswise, brushing each with ghee and leaving the edges overhanging. Put a few nuts in the bottom, fill with rice, fold the edges over, and invert onto a tray.
5. **Bake:** Brush the tops with ghee and bake at **190 °C for 25–30 min** until deep golden.
6. **Finish:** Rest 5 min. Serve with plain yogurt or a chopped salad.
