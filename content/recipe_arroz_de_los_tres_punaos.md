# Arroz de los Tres Puñaos / Arroz de Vigilia (Arroz Clarico de Vigilia)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Vega Baja del Segura: Orihuela, Almoradí, Callosa de Segura, Bigastro. Each family and town has its own version.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy). A legume-and-vegetable *potaje* with the rice cooked in it, eaten with a spoon.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large earthenware *cazuela* or *olla* on a wood or gas hob, simmered slowly for 2–3 hours.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
This is the Vega Baja's great **Lent (Cuaresma) and vigil** rice: a meatless dish of the poor huerta. Its name comes from the measuring system. Into the pot went **three handfuls** (*tres puñaos*): one of beans (*habichuelas* or *alubias*), one of lentils, and one of rice. In some families the three handfuls are all dried legumes (*habichuelas, lentejas, frisuelos*). The Bigastro town council describes it as a dish of hard times, when "mothers and grandmothers filled a pot with whatever they could gather nearby". Local press lists it alongside the *olla viuda*, *bacalao meneao* and *hartabellacos* as the Vega Baja's Lenten repertoire.

Its naming is a perfect case of hyper-local nomenclature. (For Orihuela's meat *arroz clarico* with mint, see [Arroz Clarico](recipe_arroz_clarico.md).) A commenter from Almoradí reports that **Almoradí** calls it *arroz clarico de vigilia*, which distinguishes it from the meat version. An Orihuela commenter on the same blog insists that in **Orihuela** *arroz clarico* is a **different dish altogether**: a meat-based, cocido-style broth with few chickpeas and compulsory *hierbabuena* (mint). Orihuela keeps the name *tres puñaos* for the vigil version. Bloggers note that the name "tres puñaos" is **not** used across the whole comarca.

**Culinary science:** the three legumes cook at different speeds. Beans take about 60 min after soaking, lentils about 45 min, and rice 15–20 min. So the dish is built in **staggered layers** rather than all at once. Starch from all three, plus potato, binds the broth without any meat gelatin. The flavour comes from a garlic, sweet-pimentón and tomato sofrito fried and then deglazed with bean water.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **The Orihuela Style ("de los tres puñaos"):** Beans + lentils + rice in equal handfuls. Chard, potato, and either turnip or artichoke. Sofrito of *ajetes* (young garlic), garlic, pimentón and tomato.
*   **The Almoradí Style ("arroz clarico de vigilia"):** Same structure under a different name. The Almoradí artichoke is the natural seasonal addition in March.
*   **The Bigastro Style (five legumes):** Bigastro's municipal recipe uses **habichuelas, frijuelos** (black-eyed peas), **lentils and broad beans** (habas): 250 g rice, 50 g habichuelas, 200 g frijuelos, 125 g lentils and 50 g habas, plus 3 artichokes, 1 turnip, 3 chard leaves, 1 celery stalk and 100 g green beans. The legumes are pre-boiled and their water is changed. The vegetables cook for about 30 min, chard last, then the rice for about 20 min. The council describes it as having "no added fat", although its sofrito uses oil.
*   **The Callosa de Segura Style:** Published by the town council as *arroz de vigilia o de los tres puñaos*. Search summaries mention chard, potato and turnip or artichoke. ⚠️ The council page blocked automated access, so these details aren't independently verified.
*   **The Sierra (upland) Variant:** Cooks in the hills replace the rice with ***trigo picado*** (cracked wheat), becoming a cousin of the *olla de blat*.
*   **Seasonal additions (all towns):** cardoon (*cardo*) in winter, artichoke in March, parsnip (*chirivía*), squash, peas (*pesoles*), broad beans.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Orihuela**. Proportions are adapted from a Vega Baja home cook's 6-person "three handfuls" recipe, with one handful ≈ 80–90 g.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried beans (alubias, habichuelas or *carillas*), soaked 12 h | 170 g |
| Brown lentils | 170 g |
| Water | 2.5 L |
| Olive oil | 60 ml |
| Ajetes (young garlic), chopped (or 3 garlic cloves) | 1 small bunch |
| Pimentón dulce | 8 g (1 tbsp) |
| Ripe tomatoes, grated | 2 small (150 g) |
| Potatoes, in chunks | 2 medium (300 g) |
| Chard (*acelgas*), chopped | 300 g |
| Cardoon (winter) or 3 artichokes (spring) | 200 g / 3 |
| Turnip | 1 small |
| Salt | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 170 g (the third "puñao") |
| Legume broth at the moment the rice goes in | ≈ 1.6–1.8 L |
| Legumes : rice | 2 : 1 by weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation (night before):** Soak the beans. Lentils don't need soaking.
2. **Beans:** Cover with about four fingers of water (≈ 2.5 L) and simmer **unsalted, 60 min**, over very low heat.
3. **Sofrito:** Fry the ajetes and garlic with a pinch of salt until golden. Off the heat, add the pimentón (5 s), then the tomato, and cook it down (5 min). Deglaze the pan with two ladles of bean water and pour everything into the pot.
4. **Lentils:** Add them with a little salt, cover and simmer **45 min**.
5. **Vegetables:** Add the potato, turnip, cardoon or artichoke, and the chard last. Simmer **15 min**.
6. **Rice:** Add the rice and cook **15–18 min** at a steady simmer, stirring occasionally. Add hot water if it gets too thick: it should stay soupy.
7. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Rest 2 min and serve in deep plates. It is even better the next day (thin it with water when reheating).
