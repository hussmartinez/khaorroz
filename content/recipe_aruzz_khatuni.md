# Aruzz Khatuni (The Lady's Rice) / أرز خاتوني

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The dish is attested in the 13th-century Syrian cookbook *Kitab al-Wusla ila al-Habib*, where it is the "tenth dish" of the rice section, "which is wonderful". The steps below follow the recipe as paraphrased from Charles Perry's English translation (*Scents and Flavors*, 2017, p. 121) by a Boston University gastronomy blog. The original gives no quantities, so all amounts are a modern reconstruction. Musk is part of the original but is omitted here.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Ayyubid Syria, most likely Aleppo and Damascus, the courts where the *Wusla* was compiled and copied.
*   **Historical Source:** *Kitab al-Wusla ila al-Habib fi Wasf al-Tayyibat wa-l-Tib*, anonymous (attributed in some copies to Ibn al-'Adim of Aleppo), mid-13th century, Ayyubid Syria
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sweet court rice. Separate grains glossed with fat, folded through with pistachios toasted in sesame oil and pounded with sugar, and scented with rose water.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A tinned copper pot (*qidr*) over a charcoal brazier.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khatun* is a Turkic title for a noblewoman or queen, so *aruzz khatuni* is "the lady's rice", a name that fits the Turkic-Ayyubid elite who ruled Syria in the 13th century. The *Wusla* was that era's bestseller. It is a 635-recipe compendium compiled in Syria around the middle of the century, and in some manuscripts it is credited to the Aleppine historian Kamal al-Din Ibn al-'Adim (d. 1262). Its rice chapter, per the published table of contents, lists a *muhallabiyya*, white rice *bardawili*, *rukhamiyya* ("marble rice"), plain and yellow pilafs, rice with cornelian cherries, *fa'iziyya*, a servants' *kashkiyya*, and finally this *khatuni*.

Following the translation as paraphrased by the BU gastronomy blog: water is boiled with melted, unsalted sheep's tail fat and chicken fat. The rice is added and boiled down until only the fat remains with it. Pistachios are toasted in sesame oil, crushed and mixed with pounded sugar, then added to the rice generously, sprinkled with rose water and "a little musk". The author's verdict: "It is outstanding."

**Culinary logic:** this is a true pilaf technique, an "absorption" rice, in which the grains finish in fat rather than water. Sweet pistachio-and-sugar rices like this one are the ancestors of the Levant's nut-strewn festive rices (see [riz a djej](recipe_riz_a_djej.md)) and of Ottoman sweet pilafs. The medieval kitchen saw no hard line between sweet and savoury.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Wusla rice chapter (13th c.):** Sister dishes in the same chapter are yellow (saffron) pilaf, "marble" *rukhamiyya*, and rice with cornelian cherries. The khatuni is the sweetest, most perfumed member.
*   **Modern descendants:** Nut-crowned festive rices such as [riz a djej](recipe_riz_a_djej.md) and [ruz bi-djaj with hashweh](recipe_ruz_bi_djaj_hashweh.md) keep the toasted-nut finish, now on savoury rice. The sweet side survives in [riz bi haleeb](recipe_riz_bi_haleeb.md).
*   **A modern cook's adaptation (BU blog):** rendered leaf lard instead of tail and chicken fat, orange-blossom water instead of rose water, musk omitted. On a second attempt the fat was infused with the aromatics the *Wusla* prescribes for tail fat.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern adaptation; quantities reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ghee or rendered lamb fat (for tail fat) | 50 g |
| Chicken fat or butter | 30 g |
| Water | 700 ml |
| Shelled unsalted pistachios | 100 g |
| Sesame oil (untoasted) | 2 tbsp |
| Sugar, pounded fine | 60 g |
| Rose water | 1–2 tbsp |
| Salt | a pinch |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short- or medium-grain white rice, soaked 20 min | 300 g |
| Water : rice | ≈ 2.3 : 1 by weight, boiled down to fat |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Fat broth:** Bring the water to a boil with the lamb fat, chicken fat and a pinch of salt.
2. **Rice:** Add the drained rice, cover and cook on low heat until the water is absorbed and the grains are glossy with fat (≈ 15–18 min). Rest 5 min off the heat.
3. **Pistachios:** Toast the pistachios in the sesame oil until fragrant, cool, crush coarsely and mix with the pounded sugar.
4. **Finish:** Fold most of the pistachio sugar through the rice, mound it on a platter, scatter the rest on top and sprinkle with rose water.
