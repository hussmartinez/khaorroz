# Qidreh / قدرة (al-Qidra al-Khaliliyya)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** al-Khalil / Hebron, its home and signature dish. A distinct version is made in Gaza.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Feast rice baked in a sealed pot (*qidra*). Buttery grains that have absorbed all the broth, under browned lamb and whole chickpeas.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** The *qidra* itself, which gives the dish its name. In Hebron it is a **copper or brass pot**; in Gaza an **unglazed clay urn**. Either way it is carried to a **neighbourhood wood-fired bakery oven**, with each pot marked with its family's name.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Qidra* simply means "pot", sometimes specifically a clay vessel, and the dish is named after it. It is Hebron's festive rice: lamb (or chicken) and rice cooked together in a sealed pot in the baker's oven for hours, then finished with local ghee (*samneh baladi*). It appears at weddings, *'aqiqa* birth celebrations, Fridays, Ramadan and both Eids, and Palestinian Christians make it at Christmas. It is usually ordered from a bakery rather than made at home. A 2015 Al Jazeera report describes qidra gradually displacing mansaf at Hebron feasts. It records one family's origin account: the Jordanian army brought meat and rice during Ramadan to the bakery of the late Hajj Ata Fnoun al-Tamimi and asked him to cook them together in his oven. His grandson Jalal says the baker went on to refine the dish with spices and **safflower** (*'usfur*) for colour. Other sources credit other Hebron families or trace it to the Ottoman period. ⚠️ The origin is disputed.

**Culinary science:** the oven surrounds the sealed pot with steady dry heat. Rice cooks gently in measured, defatted broth while the meat browns on top, much like the Valencian [arroz al horno](recipe_arroz_al_horno.md), which was also cooked in the village baker's oven in marked pots. Ghee brushed on at the end gives gloss and a nutty, lactic aroma. Safflower or turmeric colours the rice yellow without adding much flavour.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **The Hebron (al-Khalil) Style:** **Plain, with few spices**. The aim is the flavour of meat, rice and ghee. Lamb or beef, or chicken. Rice pre-cooked in spiced, safflower-coloured water, then layered with the browned meat and its defatted broth in a **copper pot**. Wood-fired oven. **At least 3 hours** in all. Some Hebron cooks first brown **whole garlic cloves in samneh** in the pot. Served with yogurt and cucumber.
*   **The Gaza Style:** Cooked in an **unglazed clay urn**, which is cracked open at the table so the rice spills out. It adds **chickpeas, onions, whole garlic cloves** and more spices, typically **turmeric, cumin, cardamom, allspice and cinnamon** (⚠️ the exact mix varies by family). It is documented in *The Gaza Kitchen* (El-Haddad & Schmitt) as a dish for weddings, births and funerals.
*   **Home versions everywhere:** A Dutch oven at about 180 °C replaces both the copper pot and the bakery oven. Freekeh sometimes replaces part of the rice.

| Variable | Hebron | Gaza |
|---|---|---|
| Pot | Copper / brass | Unglazed clay urn |
| Spicing | Minimal; safflower for colour | Turmeric, cumin, cardamom, allspice… |
| Additions | (Garlic in samneh) | Chickpeas, onions, whole garlic |
| Serving | Turned into a tray | Urn cracked open at the table |

**Hebron "qidra with yogurt" (⚠️):** The UAE newspaper *Al-Ittihad* describes Hebron's best-known dish as "القدرة الخليلية أي الأرز مع اللبن ولحم الخروف والسمن البلدي" (*qidra khaliliyya*, rice with yogurt, mutton and village *samneh*, sometimes with bread). It adds that "rice with cooked yogurt" is a Hebron custom. This may describe a yogurt-served qidreh, or the city's mansaf-like rice. It is recorded here as a Hebron variant to verify ([Al-Ittihad](https://www.aletihad.ae/articleamp/23164/2014/الكوسا-باللبن-طبق-من-تراث-مدينة-الخليل-الفلسطينية)). See also [kousa bi-laban](recipe_kousa_bi_laban.md).

**Village feast ancestor:** Before the copper *qidra* became a bakery dish, Bethlehem-hill weddings served plain broth-cooked rice and mutton on wooden trays; see [ruz w lahm al-ʿurs](recipe_ruz_w_lahm_al_urs.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **home oven version of the Hebron qidra**, with the optional Gaza chickpeas and garlic.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo) (the broth / *maraq*)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb shoulder or neck, on the bone, large pieces | 1.2 kg |
| Water | 1.8 L |
| Onion, halved; bay leaf; cardamom pods; cinnamon stick | 1 / 2 / 4 / 1 |
| Samneh (ghee) | 40 g |
| Garlic, whole peeled cloves | 1 head |
| Spice for the meat: allspice, black pepper, cinnamon | 1 tsp each |
| Salt | to taste |
| [Gaza] Chickpeas, soaked overnight and part-cooked | 200 g |

Simmer the lamb with the onion and whole spices for **75–90 min**, skimming, until nearly tender. Strain and skim off the fat. Keep **≈ 900 ml** of broth.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice (Egyptian medium grain or basmati), washed, soaked 30 min | 400 g |
| Defatted broth | ≈ 800–850 ml (≈ 2 : 1 by volume) |
| Safflower (*'usfur*) or turmeric | 1 tsp / ½ tsp |
| Samneh to finish | 30 g |
| Oven | 180 °C |
| Garnish: fried almonds or pine nuts, parsley; serve with yogurt and cucumber | — |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meat:** Rub the poached lamb with the allspice, pepper and cinnamon. Brown it in the oven at 200 °C (**20 min**) or in the pot.
2. **Garlic base:** In a heavy oven-safe pot, melt 40 g of samneh and fry the whole garlic cloves until golden (**3 min**). [Gaza: add the chickpeas and a sliced onion.]
3. **Rice:** Stir in the drained rice and the safflower or turmeric and coat it in the fat (**2 min**). Lay the meat on top.
4. **Liquid Addition:** Pour on the hot broth (≈ 2 : 1) and correct the salt. Seal with a lid, plus foil if the lid is loose.
5. **Oven:** Bake at **180 °C for 60–75 min** until the rice has absorbed all the broth and the meat is browned. (The bakery version goes in and out of the wood oven over 3 hours.)
6. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Brush the meat and rice with the remaining samneh. Rest **10 min** covered. Turn out onto a tray or serve from the pot. Scatter nuts on top and serve with yogurt.
