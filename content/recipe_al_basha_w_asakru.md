# Al-Basha wa ʿAsakru (The Pasha and His Soldiers) / الباشا وعساكره

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented as a Damascene dish by An-Nahar (served "alongside fluffy rice") and by a Mawdoo3 recipe of old Damascene dishes (which serves it in yogurt without mentioning rice). Its Ottoman origin is reported as "accounts", not documented. It combines two pan-Levantine components ([kibbeh labanieh](recipe_kibbeh_labanieh.md) and shishbarak); this file covers only the named Damascene combination.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Damascus.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: fried kibbeh (the "pasha") and small meat dumplings, *shishbarak* (the "soldiers"), cooked together in a hot garlic-coriander yogurt sauce, served beside fluffy white rice (*ruz mfalfal*).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot for the yogurt sauce, stirred constantly in one direction; a lidded pot for the rice; stovetop.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
An-Nahar's report explains that the warlike name is simply the label of a **Damascene dish**: it brings together two dishes, *kibbeh labaniyeh* and *shishbarak*, in "one yogurt". According to the accounts it cites, the dish has Turkish or Ottoman roots, reached Damascus under Ottoman rule, and was developed by Damascenes, who became the most famous for it. It is served garnished with coriander "alongside fluffy rice" ([An-Nahar – الباشا وعساكره](https://www.annahar.com/arabic/article/1213841-الباشا-وعساكره-أكلة-شعبية-أم-هجوم-مسلح)). Mawdoo3 includes it among old Damascene dishes, with a meat filling, a kibbeh layer and a yogurt sauce with garlic and tarragon ([Mawdoo3 – أكلات دمشقية قديمة](https://mawdoo3.com/أكلات_دمشقية_قديمة)).

The joke of the name is visual: the big kibbeh are the pasha, the many little dumplings his troops, all marching in white yogurt. Like [shakriyeh](recipe_shakriyeh.md), it belongs to the Damascene repertoire of **cooked-yogurt (*labaniyeh*) dishes**, which are always paired with plain or vermicelli rice to soak up the sauce.

**Culinary science:** yogurt curdles when boiled because its proteins clump. Damascene cooks stabilise it with starch (cornflour) and sometimes egg, and stir it constantly in one direction until it boils, which keeps the sauce smooth.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascus (An-Nahar):** Fried kibbeh (bulgur, meat, a little semolina) and shishbarak in yogurt thickened with cornflour and milk powder, lots of garlic and coriander; fluffy rice on the side.
*   **Old Damascene recipe (Mawdoo3):** The dough is filled and cut into pieces before boiling, with egg and tarragon in the yogurt.
*   **Elsewhere:** The two components are cooked separately across the Levant ([kibbeh labanieh](recipe_kibbeh_labanieh.md)); the combined "pasha and soldiers" name is Damascene.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Damascene version with rice** (An-Nahar), quantities reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ready kibbeh (bulgur shell, meat-pine nut filling), fried | 8 pieces |
| Shishbarak (meat-filled dough dumplings), baked or fried briefly | 30 pieces |
| Plain yogurt (full fat) | 1.2 kg |
| Water | 300 ml |
| Cornflour | 2 tbsp |
| Egg white (optional stabiliser) | 1 |
| Garlic, crushed with salt | 6 cloves |
| Fresh coriander, chopped | 1 bunch |
| Butter or ghee | 2 tbsp |
| Salt | 1 tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short- or medium-grain rice, washed and soaked 20 min | 320 g |
| Water for the rice | ≈ 480 ml |
| Ghee for the rice | 1 tbsp |
| Yogurt sauce : dumplings | enough to just cover |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Stabilise the yogurt:** Whisk the yogurt with the water, cornflour, egg white and salt in a heavy pot.
2. **Heat slowly:** Bring to a boil over medium heat, stirring constantly in one direction, so it does not split.
3. **Rice (meanwhile):** Fry the drained rice 1 min in ghee, add boiling water and salt, cover and cook on very low heat 15–18 min; rest.
4. **Dumplings:** Add the shishbarak to the boiling yogurt and simmer 10 min; add the fried kibbeh for the last 5 min.
5. **Garlic-coriander:** Fry the garlic and coriander in butter for 30 seconds and stir into the pot.
6. **Serve:** In deep plates, with the fluffy rice alongside to take the sauce.
