# Andalucía / Andalucía

> ⬅ [README](README.md)

## 1. Overview
Andalucía is **Spain's largest rice producer**. The **marismas del Guadalquivir** (Isla Mayor, La Puebla del Río, Villafranco) were put under rice in the 20th century, partly by Valencian settlers. Andalusian rice cooking, however, follows its own logic. On the coast it is predominantly **caldoso** (soupy): fishermen's pots of fish, shellfish and cuttlefish (*choco*), built on an onion-and-pepper sofrito, sometimes thickened with a fried-bread **majado**, and often scented with **fino** or **manzanilla**. Inland, rice goes with game from the sierras (partridge, hare, rabbit) and with the mixed "**arroz con cosas**" of Sevilla's ventas. The region's own Andalusi heritage (rice was introduced to al-Andalus) means rice desserts and spices have deep roots here too.

## 2. Cultural Sub-Regions
| Sub-region | Rice personality | Key towns |
|---|---|---|
| **Marismas del Guadalquivir** | Paddy country: duck and crayfish rices, arroz con cosas | Isla Mayor, La Puebla del Río, Villafranco |
| **Costa de Huelva** | Choco and clam caldosos | Huelva, Punta Umbría, Isla Cristina, Ayamonte |
| **Bahía de Cádiz & Sanlúcar** | Seafood rices with fino or manzanilla, langostino | Cádiz, Sanlúcar, El Puerto |
| **Costa de Málaga** | Arroz caldoso marinero, beach merenderos | Málaga (El Palo, Pedregalejo) |
| **Sierras (Morena, Cazorla, Los Pedroches)** | Game rices: partridge, hare, rabbit | La Carolina, Andújar, Cazorla |
| **Sevilla city & campiña** | Arroz a la sevillana / con cosas, arroz campero | Sevilla, Dos Hermanas |
