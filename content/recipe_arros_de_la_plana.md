# Arroz de la Plana / Arròs de la Plana (Paella de l'Horta Castellonenca)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província de Castelló](provincial_castellon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** "Arròs de la Plana" is **not a documented named dish**. Searches in Spanish and Valencian found no recipe under that name. The emblematic dish of La Plana is the **olla de la Plana**, a bean-and-vegetable stew with no rice. What the Plana cooks is the **huerta paella**: the Valencian paella frame adapted to the orange-and-artichoke huerta around Castelló, Vila-real, Burriana and Nules. This file documents that paella as commonly cooked in the comarca. The variations are **reconstructed from general comarca practice and should be verified locally**, for example during Castelló's *Mes de l'Arròs* or the Magdalena festivals.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Plana Alta and La Plana Baixa: Castelló de la Plana, Vila-real, Burriana, Nules, Almassora.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A steel paella over **orange-tree prunings** (the Plana is citrus country).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
La Plana is Castellón's irrigated coastal plain, transformed in the 19th and 20th centuries into a sea of orange groves, with vegetable huertas in between. Its paella culture is closely tied to the **Magdalena** festival in Castelló: during the *Romeria de les Canyes* and the *Festes de la Magdalena*, *colles* (festival groups) cook paellas in the open. Castelló de la Plana devotes an annual **Mes de l'Arròs** to the city's rices. Valencia's tourism board traces how paella moved in the early 20th century from rural farmhouses to the beach eating houses of **El Grau de Castelló**, among other places. The marinera branch of that history is covered in [arrocito del Grau](recipe_arrocito_del_grau.md).

**Culinary science:** this is the same seco logic as the [paella valenciana](recipe_paella_valenciana.md). Orange-wood fire gives a steady, aromatic heat, and the comarca's winter artichoke adds its characteristic sweetness and darkening.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — ⚠️ reconstructed)

*   **Castelló de la Plana (Magdalena paella):** Chicken and rabbit, sometimes with **pork ribs**. Bajoqueta and garrofó in season. Cooked in large festival pans.
*   **Vila-real / Burriana:** In winter, **artichoke** and sometimes **habas**, close to the Valencian *paella d'hivern* (see [paella de verduras](recipe_paella_de_verduras.md)).
*   **Coastal Plana (Grau, Burriana port):** It shifts toward sepia and prawns, and becomes a marinera rice rather than a huerta one.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Plana winter huerta paella** ⚠️ (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Chicken, jointed | 600 g |
| Rabbit, jointed | 400 g |
| Pork ribs, short pieces | 200 g |
| Artichokes, quartered | 6 |
| Garrofó (dried, soaked) | 80 g |
| Olive oil | 90 ml |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| Water | 1.7 L |
| Saffron | 0.3 g |

**Extraction:** Brown the meats slowly (20 min). Fry the artichokes and garrofó (5 min). Add the pimentón (5 s), then the tomato (5 min). Add the water and saffron, and simmer for 30 min, reducing to about 1.2 L.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Broth | ≈ 1.2 L |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Scatter the rice and level it. Don't stir after this.
2. **Fire Manipulation (orange wood):** High heat for 8 min, then let the fire burn down to embers for 8–9 min, then a final flare for the socarrat.
3. **Resting Phase:** Rest 5 min.
