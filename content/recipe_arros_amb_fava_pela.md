# Arroz con Habas Peladas / Arròs amb Fava Pelà (Fava Pelada)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is well attested in the Marina Alta (Benitatxell's fava festival, Jesús Pobre's rice meeting, Pedreguer, Xàbia). However, I found **no published quantified recipe**. The blueprint is reconstructed from the documented features: dried peeled broad beans, thick and creamy texture, long cooking.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Marina Alta: El Poble Nou de Benitatxell (signature dish), Xàbia, Jesús Pobre, Pedreguer.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). It is described as thick and creamy, with body from the bean starch.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** An olla or caldero over wood, historically left on the fire for half a day.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Fava pelà* is the **dried broad bean with its skin removed**. Once peeled it collapses into a purée-like body when cooked. In the dry-farmed Marina Alta it was a winter staple. Benitatxell sources say it was traditionally cooked for **half a day**, and a portion was kept for the farm workers' evening meal. Today **El Poble Nou de Benitatxell** holds annual **Jornades Gastronòmiques de la Fava** around *arròs amb fava*. Jesús Pobre's rice meeting dedicated an edition to it, and Moraira's Mañet restaurant served it in its traditional-food days. A Valencia Plaza column lists *arròs amb fava pelada* as Pedreguer's local rice.

**Culinary science:** without its skin, the bean's cotyledons break down and release amylose and protein into the broth. This creates a velvety, almost gravy-like body, so no stirring technique is needed. Any meat or pork added (common in winter versions) is optional.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: reconstructed Marina Alta base.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried peeled broad beans (*fava pelada*), soaked 12 h | 300 g |
| Pork ribs or belly (optional, winter) | 300 g |
| Olive oil | 60 ml |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 100 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 4 g |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |
| Water | 2.5 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice | 300 g |
| Bean broth at rice-in | ≈ 1.3–1.5 L (meloso) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Beans:** Simmer the soaked beans (with the pork, if using) in 2.5 L of water for **1.5–2 h**, until they collapse.
2. **Sofrito:** Fry the garlic, then off the heat the pimentón, then the tomato. Add this to the pot.
3. **Rice:** Add the rice and saffron and simmer **17 min**, stirring now and then. The beans will thicken it.
4. **Finish:** Rest 2 min and serve thick.
