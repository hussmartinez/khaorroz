# Khao Mak / ข้าวหมาก

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the method and microbiology are well sourced (Thai university papers, a Thai patent), but its history is undocumented beyond "made since ancient times". Proportions follow common household practice.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Nationwide village sweet, popular in the cool season. Every region makes it, using its own *luk paeng* starter cakes.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** A fermented sweet rice. Steamed glutinous rice, fermented for 2–3 days with ***luk paeng*** (dried starter cakes of rice flour, moulds, yeasts and herbs), turns soft and juicy, sweet, faintly sour and lightly alcoholic.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A huad or steamer for the rice, a winnowing tray or cloth to cool it, and earthenware jars or banana-leaf cones for the fermentation.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao mak ("fermented rice") is described by Thai researchers as a folk dessert "made since ancient times": sticky rice fermented with **luk paeng**, starter cakes carrying the moulds and yeasts that turn starch into sugar, alcohol and lactic acid. Traditional starters include herbs such as ginger, galangal, licorice and garlic. Starter cakes are now hard to buy because they are classed as intoxicant-producing, a regulatory twist that threatens the home tradition. It belongs to the same Asian family as Chinese *jiuniang* and Indonesian *tapai*. Thais eat it mainly in the cool months, often in small leaf cones.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Central Thailand:** White sticky rice, eaten as a sweet or with ice.
*   **Isan and Lanna:** Also made with local heirloom sticky rices. Black sticky rice versions are eaten as a snack.
*   **Herbal luk paeng:** Different villages' starter cakes (with ginger, galangal, licorice, garlic) give different aromas.
*   **Probiotic research versions:** Thai universities have developed starters from heirloom rice (e.g. *khao niao luem phua*).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **household white khao mak**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Luk paeng (starter cakes), finely ground | 2 cakes (about 10 g) |
| Clean cool water (for rinsing) | as needed |
| Banana leaves (for cones), optional | 10 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice, soaked overnight | 500 g |
| Starter : rice | about 2 % by weight |
| Fermentation | 48–72 h at warm room temperature |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Steam:** Steam the soaked rice for **30 minutes**, until fully cooked.
2. **Rinse:** Rinse the hot rice in cool clean water to separate the grains and remove sticky starch, then drain well and cool to body temperature.
3. **Inoculate:** Sprinkle the ground starter over the rice and toss gently so every grain is coated.
4. **Ferment:** Pack it loosely into a clean jar (or leaf cones), cover and keep warm for **2–3 days**, until it is juicy and sweet with a light alcoholic aroma.
5. **Serve:** Chill. Eat with its juice, plain or over ice.
