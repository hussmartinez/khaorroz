# Daoud Basha / داود باشا

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is pan-Levantine and its origin is contested: Lebanon, Syria and Iraq all claim it, and one source places the story in Egypt. The Lebanese attribution of the name to Daoud Pasha, first governor of the Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate, comes from Lebanese popular history (Libnanews) and is legend rather than documented fact.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lebanon (claimed especially around Deir el-Qamar and the Chouf), Syria, Palestine and Jordan, where it is a common home dish.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stew over rice. Spiced meatballs simmered in tomato sauce (often with onions, sometimes potatoes, pine nuts and pomegranate molasses), served over white or vermicelli rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A frying pan to brown the meatballs and a stew pot for the sauce, on the stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The name honours a pasha. The best-known Lebanese story connects it to **Daoud Pasha**, an Armenian Catholic Ottoman official born in Istanbul in 1816. After the 1860 conflict and the French expedition he became the first Christian governor of the new **Mount Lebanon Mutasarrifate**, based at Deir el-Qamar, until 1868. He was unpopular for his heavy taxes, but supposedly his favourite dish kept his name. Other versions credit an Egyptian or Iraqi Daoud Pasha, and food writers call the story "speculated and rumoured history with no substantial proof".

**Culinary science:** the meatballs are browned first so they hold together and give a Maillard crust to the sauce. Grated onion and a little bread or rice in the mince keep them tender. The tomato sauce is often sharpened with pomegranate molasses, whose acidity balances the fatty meat. Unlike Western meatballs it is served over rice, never pasta.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Lebanese home version:** Small meatballs with pine nuts in a tomato sauce with baby onions; minced beef with cinnamon and paprika (Libnanews author's version).
*   **With pomegranate molasses:** A tangier tomato-pomegranate sauce, cited by several Levantine recipe writers.
*   **Palestinian/Jordanian "kufta and potato stew":** Potatoes are added to the stew (Falasteeni Foodie).
*   **Iraqi version:** The dish also has an Iraqi claim, mentioned for comparison only.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Lebanese version with pine nuts and baby onions.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Minced beef or lamb | 600 g |
| Onion, grated (for the meatballs) | ½ |
| Seven-spice, cinnamon, salt, pepper | 1 tsp, ½ tsp, to taste |
| Pine nuts | 40 g |
| Baby onions (or 1 onion, sliced) | 250 g |
| Olive oil | 4 tbsp |
| Tomato paste | 2 tbsp |
| Crushed tomatoes | 500 g |
| Pomegranate molasses (optional) | 1 tbsp |
| Water | ≈ 400 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Meatballs | ≈ 30, walnut-sized |
| Rice (white or vermicelli) | 300 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meatballs:** Mix the meat with the grated onion and spices and roll into walnut-sized balls.
2. **Brown:** Brown the meatballs in the oil and set aside. Toast the pine nuts in the same pan, then soften the baby onions.
3. **Sauce:** Add the tomato paste, tomatoes and water, and season.
4. **Simmer:** Return the meatballs and simmer **20–25 min** until the sauce thickens. Stir in the pomegranate molasses at the end, if using.
5. **Serve:** Serve over rice, scattered with the pine nuts.
