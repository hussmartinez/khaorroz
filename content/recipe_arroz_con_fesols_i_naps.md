# Arroz con Alubias y Nabos / Arròs amb (en) Fesols i Naps

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** L'Horta de València, La Ribera, La Safor, and the inland comarques (Camp de Túria, Los Serranos). Variants exist in La Marina Alta (Dénia) and the Alcoià.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy). Eaten with a spoon. The broth should be *trabado* (lightly bound by starch).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A tall clay **olla** or iron *caldera*, started early on a wood stove (*foc a terra*). Pressure cookers are the modern shortcut.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
This is the great **winter rice** of the Valencian countryside. It is also known as *caldera*, *caldera de l'horta*, *olla pobre* or *arròs junt*. It comes from the pig-slaughter season (*la matança*), using pork trotter, ear, belly and fresh *morcilla* or *botifarra*, combined with dried white beans and the **yellow turnip** (*nap groc*, the Valencian heirloom "nap de pebre"). The turnip gives the broth its earthy-sweet, mustardy note and pale amber colour. The dish is effectively an *olla* in which the rice is cooked directly at the end, instead of a separate soup and *pilota*. The chef Héctor Medina argues it should **not rest**: it is moved so that the rice releases starch and the broth binds.

**Culinary science:** pork trotter and ear are rich in **collagen**, which converts to gelatin over 1.5–2 hours of simmering. Combined with starch from the beans and rice, this gives the broth its glossy, lip-sticking body. Yellow turnip contains glucosinolates, which soften into sweet-bitter notes with long cooking. *Pimentón* is added to hot oil and then quenched, so it releases its colour without burning.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **The L'Horta / Valencia City Style:** Pork trotter (*peu*), ear (*orella*), belly, **morcilla de ceba**, sometimes beef. White beans (*fesol blanc*) + **nap groc**. Pimentón + saffron. Rice cooked in the broth for **15–17 min**.
*   **The La Safor / Ribera Style:** Adds **cardo** (cardoon) or **penca** (chard stalks) in winter, and sometimes **carlota** (carrot). Some Ribera households fry the rice briefly first.
*   **The Interior (Camp de Túria / Serranos) Style:** Adds **blanquet** and **chorizo** from the matança, heavier on pimentón. Cooked in a deep caldera over wood.
*   **The Dénia / Marina Alta Style:** Known locally as *arròs amb fesols i naps*, with chicken added. In Dénia's caldoso rice tradition it is lighter, with a clearer broth.
*   **The Alcoi / Alicante interior echo:** Close to the *olleta* family. White beans, turnip and pork, but the cereal is often wheat or rice.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **L'Horta de València** (pork matança cuts + nap groc + white beans).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried white beans (*fesols blancs*), soaked 12 h | 250 g |
| Pork trotter (*peu de porc*), split | 1 (≈ 400 g) |
| Pork ear (*orella*), optional | 1 (≈ 200 g) |
| Pork belly (*cansalada*) | 250 g |
| Beef shin (optional) | 200 g |
| Yellow turnip (*nap groc*), peeled, in chunks | 2 (≈ 400 g) |
| Morcilla de ceba | 2 (≈ 200 g) |
| Olive oil | 50 ml |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |
| Pimentón dulce de la Vera | 4 g |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |
| Water | 3 L |
| Salt | to taste |

**Extraction:**
1. Blanch the trotter and ear for 5 min, drain and rinse (this gives a cleaner broth).
2. In the olla, combine the beans, trotter, ear, belly, beef and 3 L of cold water. Bring slowly to a boil and **skim**. Simmer gently for **1 h 15 min** (or 25 min in a pressure cooker).
3. Add the turnip and cook for **20 min** more.
4. In a small pan, warm the oil, fry the sliced garlic, then **off the heat** add the pimentón for 5 s and pour it straight into the olla. Add the saffron and the **morcilla** (whole). Simmer **10 min**.
5. Lift out the meats, bone the trotter, and chop everything into bite-size pieces. Return them to the pot. You should have ≈ **2 L** of broth. Correct the salt.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 320–350 g |
| Broth | ≈ 2 L (≈ 6:1, caldoso) |
| Beans (cooked) | ≈ 550 g |
| Meats | ≈ 1.2 kg gross |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Soak the beans the day before.
2. **Fondo:** As above, about 1 h 45 min total.
3. **Rice Addition:** Bring the broth to a lively boil and add the rice.
4. **Fire Manipulation:** Strong simmer for **15–17 min**. **Stir gently** every few minutes to release starch and bind the broth.
5. **Finish/Resting Phase:** **Serve immediately.** Don't rest it, because the rice keeps drinking the broth. Serve in deep bowls with a piece of each meat and a slice of morcilla.
