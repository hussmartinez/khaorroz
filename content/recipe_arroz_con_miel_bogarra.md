# Arroz con Miel de Bogarra / Arroz con Miel Navideño (Sierra de Albacete)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Castilla-La Mancha & Extremadura](provincial_castilla_la_mancha_extremadura.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a **forgotten dish**, documented in the *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria* supplement on Castilla-La Mancha's forgotten dishes, which reports it in **Bogarra** (Comarca de la Sierra, Albacete) as a Christmas dessert. The source gives ingredients and method but **no quantities**. The quantities below are my blueprint, and no other town variants are documented.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bogarra, Sierra de Alcaraz y Segura (Albacete).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy) (dessert).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** An *olla* or pot over a wood stove. It is served cold from a large dish (*fuente*).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In the remote mountain villages of the Albacete Sierra, **honey** from rosemary and thyme hives was the traditional sweetener. The RENC records an **arroz con miel** eaten at **Christmas** in Bogarra. The rice is cooked with **orange and lemon peel** and **cinnamon**, then sweetened with honey or sugar plus a separately made **caramel**, and coloured **yellow** with colourant (formerly saffron). It is served **cold** on a platter and decorated with cinnamon and the citrus peels. The source notes that the traditional ratio, **twice as much sugar or honey as rice**, is "too cloying; better less", and that cane sugar is the most common variant today. Unusually, this rice pudding contains **no milk**, which links it to older Andalusi-style sweet rices.

**Culinary science:** without milk, the creaminess comes entirely from rice starch and the syrup. Caramel adds bitter-sweet depth and colour. Cooling sets the starch-sugar gel so the pudding can be portioned.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Bogarra (documented):** Honey or sugar + caramel + orange and lemon peel + cinnamon stick + yellow colour. Served cold.
*   **Documented variant:** **Cane sugar** instead of honey (now most common).
*   *No other town variants documented. Compare the milk-based [arroz con leche](recipe_arroz_con_leche.md).*

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bogarra** (reduced-sweetness quantities).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Water | 1.2 L |
| Orange peel (no pith) | 1 orange |
| Lemon peel | 1 lemon |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Saffron (or a pinch of colourant) | 0.1 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 200 g |
| Honey (rosemary/thyme) | 200 g (1:1; the source's 2:1 is "demasiado empalagoso") |
| Sugar for caramel | 60 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Infuse:** Bring the water to a boil with the peels and cinnamon. Add the rice.
2. **Cook:** Simmer **20 min**. Remove the cinnamon after about 10 min so it doesn't break up.
3. **Sweeten:** Stir in the honey and saffron and cook **5 min** more.
4. **Caramel:** Melt the sugar in a pan to an amber caramel and stir it into the rice.
5. **Finish:** Pour onto a serving dish, decorate with cinnamon and the peels, and serve **cold**.
