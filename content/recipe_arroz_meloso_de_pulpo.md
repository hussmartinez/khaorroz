# Arroz Meloso de Pulpo / Arròs Melós de Polp (i Arròs amb Polp Sec)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** arroz meloso de pulpo is common in Alicante's coastal restaurants (for example in Santa Pola), but I found no canonical published recipe tied to a specific town. The **polp sec** (dried octopus) tradition of Dénia and Xàbia is well documented as an ingredient. However, **I found no published recipe for an "arròs amb polp sec"**. The polp-sec variant below is reconstructed from how dried octopus is used locally, and should be checked with Marina Alta cooks.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Alicante coast: Santa Pola, Alicante city, and La Marina Alta (Dénia, Xàbia) for the polp sec variant.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep paella or *cassola* over gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Octopus (*polp*, *Octopus vulgaris*) is caught with pots and traps by the artisanal fleets of Santa Pola, Dénia and Xàbia. In the **Marina Alta** a centuries-old preserve survives: **polp sec**, octopus hung to dry in the sea wind on lines or racks between autumn and spring (the summer heat spoils it). Xàbia has made it part of its identity. Dried octopus is usually grilled, but local cooks also use it to give a deep, smoky-iodine flavour to rices and *putxeros* (Alicante Plaza mentions a *putxero de polp* among Marina dishes). Fresh-octopus meloso rice is the modern restaurant form that grew out of these traditions.

**Culinary science:** octopus muscle is rich in collagen and tough when raw. It is tenderised either by **"scaring" it** (*asustar*: dipping it three times into boiling water before simmering), by freezing, or by long simmering. Its cooking water, full of gelatin, becomes the best base for the rice. For a meloso, the rice is stirred gently so that it releases surface starch, which binds with that gelatin.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **Santa Pola / Alicante:** Fresh octopus, simmered and cut up. Salmorreta base. Its own cooking liquid is mixed with fumet. Often with **almejas** or **langostinos**.
*   **Marina Alta, polp sec ⚠️ (reconstructed):** Dried octopus is rehydrated, toasted briefly over embers, and cut into small pieces that go into the sofrito. The broth is lighter, because the dried octopus brings concentrated flavour. A smaller amount of octopus is used.
*   **Dénia restaurants:** "Paella with octopus and dried tomato" is listed locally. This is a seco variant.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Alicante fresh-octopus meloso**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Octopus (fresh or previously frozen) | 1 × 1.2 kg |
| Water for cooking it | 3 L |
| Rockfish fumet | 0.8 L |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Salmorreta | 60 g |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |
| [Polp sec variant: dried octopus instead of fresh] | 150–200 g |

**Extraction:** "Scare" the octopus three times in boiling water, then simmer it for **40–45 min** until a skewer goes in easily. Rest it 10 min in the water, then cut it into 2 cm pieces. Keep **0.8 L** of the cooking water. Make the sofrito: garlic, then salmorreta (1 min), then tomato (5 min). Add the octopus pieces for 2 min. Mix the octopus water with the fumet.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 350 g |
| Broth (octopus water + fumet) | ≈ 1.4–1.5 L (≈ 4:1 by volume) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toasting:** Add the rice to the sofrito and toast for 1 min.
2. **Liquid Addition:** Add the boiling broth and saffron, and check the salt.
3. **Fire Manipulation:** A strong boil for 8 min, then medium for 8–9 min, stirring gently 3–4 times.
4. **Resting Phase:** Rest 1–2 min. It should ripple when you shake the pan. Serve with allioli.
