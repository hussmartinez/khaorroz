# Lsaineh (Stuffed Ox-Tongue Leaves) / اللسينة – محشي لسان الثور

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish and its Palestinian name are attested in Arabic recipe media (Sada El-Balad, Just Food). I found no ethnographic source tying it to particular towns, and the identity of the "ox-tongue" leaf (a wild or cultivated broad leaf) varies by source.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Palestinian villages and towns. Spring and early summer, when the leaves are tender.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed leaves (*mahashi*). Broad, slightly rough leaves called *lisan al-thawr* ("ox tongue"), known in Palestine as *lsaineh* or *waraq al-lisan*, are rolled around rice and minced meat. They are cooked on a bed of tomato and onion until the rice is soft and the rolls are juicy.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot, its base lined with tomato and onion rings, on a gas or wood fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Palestinian cooks stuff almost any leaf or vegetable that can be hollowed or rolled. Alongside vine leaves ([warak enab](recipe_warak_enab.md)), cabbage ([malfouf](recipe_malfouf_mehshi.md)) and chard ([selek](recipe_mehshi_selek.md)), they use the long tongue-shaped leaf called *lisan al-thawr*. Arabic recipe media record that "in Palestine it is called *al-lsaineh* or *waraq al-lisan*". The leaves are washed several times to remove grit, filled with rice and minced meat, and arranged over rings of tomato and onion ([Sada El-Balad](https://elbalad.news/5921857); [Just Food – لسان الثور](https://www.justfood.tv/وصفات/28978/طريقة-عمل-لسان-الثور)).

**Culinary science:** the leaf's roughness and slight bitterness soften with cooking, and its large surface makes fat, chunky rolls with a high ratio of filling to leaf. The tomato and onion base keeps the rolls off direct heat and becomes the sauce.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Meat version:** Rice + minced meat (the documented recipe).
*   **Vegetarian / Lenten version:** Rice, tomato, onion and herbs in olive oil, as for [yalanji](recipe_yalanji.md). ⚠️ Inferred from Palestinian Lenten practice, not documented specifically for lsaineh.
*   ⚠️ Town-level differences are undocumented.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: meat lsaineh (Arabic recipe-media proportions, scaled down).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lisan al-thawr leaves, washed, stems removed | 250 g |
| Tomatoes, sliced into rings (pot base) | 2 |
| Onion, sliced into rings (pot base) | 1 |
| Olive oil | 3 tbsp |
| Lemon juice + crushed garlic (finish) | 3 tbsp + 3 cloves |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Egyptian short-grain rice, washed | 300 g |
| Minced meat | 250 g |
| Grated onion | ½ |
| Seven-spice, salt | 1 tsp / to taste |
| Water or broth | ≈ 700 ml (to just cover the rolls) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Wash the leaves several times and blanch them for 30 s to make them pliable.
2. Mix the raw rice, meat, grated onion, spices and 1 tbsp of oil.
3. Put a spoonful on each leaf, fold in the sides and roll it into a finger.
4. Line the pot with the tomato and onion rings and pack the rolls tightly on top.
5. Weigh them down with a plate, add water to just cover, bring to a boil, then simmer for **40–45 min** until the rice is tender.
6. Pour in the lemon and garlic, rest for 10 min, then flip onto a tray or serve from the pot.
