# Muhallabiyya of the Caliphs (Rice with Chicken, Milk and Honey) / مهلبية

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The rice-and-chicken *muhallabiyya* is attested in Ibn Sayyar al-Warraq's 10th-century Baghdadi cookbook. The details here come from Nawal Nasrallah's translation as excerpted in the press, and from the food historian Peter Heine. It is an **Abbasid Iraqi** source. It belongs here because the *muhallabiyya* name and lineage run straight to the Levant's modern milk puddings and to the 13th-century Syrian *Wusla*, whose rice chapter opens with a *muhallabiyya*. The story naming the dish after the general al-Muhallab ibn Abi Sufra is a **legend**. Quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Abbasid Baghdad (source). The *muhallabiyya* family was shared across Syria, Egypt and al-Andalus, and later the Ottoman world.
*   **Historical Source:** *Kitab al-Tabikh*, Ibn Sayyar al-Warraq, 10th century, Baghdad (tr. Nawal Nasrallah, *Annals of the Caliphs' Kitchens*, 2007)
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sweet-savoury milk rice. Whole rice grains and jointed chicken cooked in sheep's or goat's milk, glazed with honey until "the rice grains show through the honey", perfumed with rose water and saffron.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A stone or copper pot over embers.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Al-Warraq's 10th-century cookbook, the oldest surviving Arabic cookbook, gives several *muhallabiyya* versions: with eggs, with dry noodles, with rice, and with rice and chicken. Peter Heine calls the last "the most interesting". As excerpted from Nasrallah's translation, a chicken is jointed and put into a pot of boiling water ("1 ratl (2 cups)"). It is cooked until the water evaporates and the chicken fries in its own fat, then seasoned with coriander, cumin, cassia and saffron. Milk is boiled, washed rice is added with the chicken, and near the end a large amount of honey is stirred in gently, so the grains stay whole. The dish is finished with rose water and saffron.

The meat-and-milk version survived for centuries. Al-Baghdadi (1226) and a 13th-century Andalusian cookbook give mutton variations. Ottoman records describe a muhallebi with shredded chicken (*tavuk göğsü*) in the time of Mehmed II, and a meatless rose-water version from 1530. The modern Levantine **muhallabiyya**, a rice-flour or rice milk pudding, and [riz bi haleeb](recipe_riz_bi_haleeb.md) are its sweet descendants. Spanish [arroz con leche](recipe_arroz_con_leche.md) belongs to the same wider family of rice cooked in milk; that is a parallel, not proven descent.

**Culinary logic:** frying the chicken in its own fat before adding the milk builds flavour, and late, gentle addition of the honey keeps the grains intact.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Baghdad, 10th c. (al-Warraq):** Egg, noodle, rice, and rice-with-chicken versions. A basic pudding version soaks rice overnight, grinds it and stirs it into boiled milk, sweetened with *tabarzad* sugar.
*   **Syria, 13th c. (*Wusla*):** The rice chapter opens with a *muhallabiyya* (recipe text not consulted).
*   **Al-Andalus, 13th c. (anonymous cookbook, tr. Perry):** A savoury baked lamb version with milk, sugar, flatbread and eggs.
*   **Ottoman Istanbul (15th–16th c.):** Chicken-breast *tavuk göğsü*; rose-water muhallebi from 1530.
*   **Modern Levant:** Meatless *muhallabiyya* with rose or orange-blossom water and pistachios; [riz bi haleeb](recipe_riz_bi_haleeb.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern adaptation of the rice-and-chicken version; quantities reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Chicken, jointed (thighs and drumsticks) | 800 g |
| Water | 500 ml |
| Ground coriander | 1 tsp |
| Ground cumin | ½ tsp |
| Cassia or cinnamon, ground | ½ tsp |
| Saffron threads | a pinch |
| Whole milk (sheep's or goat's if available) | 1 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice, washed | 200 g |
| Honey | 150 g |
| Rose water | 1 tbsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Chicken:** Simmer the chicken in the water until the water has evaporated and the pieces fry lightly in their own fat (≈ 35 min). Season with the coriander, cumin, cassia and half the saffron.
2. **Milk:** In another pot bring the milk to a boil. Add the rice and the chicken and simmer gently, stirring often, until the rice is soft (≈ 25 min).
3. **Honey:** Stir in the honey gently in two additions so the grains stay whole. Cook 5 min more, until glossy.
4. **Finish:** Off the heat, add the rose water and the remaining saffron (bloomed in a spoonful of warm milk). Serve warm.
