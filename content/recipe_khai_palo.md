# Khai Palo / ไข่พะโล้

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Origin claims come from recipe blogs; the Chinese soy-braise ancestry is widely repeated but not academically documented here.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok and Central Thailand (Teochew and Hokkien Chinese-Thai communities); Phuket has its own *mu hong*.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: hard-boiled eggs and pork belly braised in a sweet-salty five-spice soy broth, ladled over rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot or clay pot (*mo din*) for the slow braise.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khai palo is a Chinese-Thai braise brought by Teochew and Hokkien migrants and adapted to Thai tables, sweetened with palm or brown sugar and perfumed with cinnamon and star anise ([Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/kai-palo/print/7198); [Temple of Thai](https://www.templeofthai.com/recipes/pa-loh-thai-five-spice-pork-egg.php)). Chef Chumpol Jangprai, in a Michelin Guide feature, notes the more you eat it with rice, the better it becomes ([Michelin Guide](https://guide.michelin.com/th/en/article/dining-in/recipe-chef-chumpol-jangprai-and-his-five-spiced-eggs-ufarm)). In Phuket, the related *mu hong* is the Hokkien-Peranakan braise ([Oh Happy Bear](https://www.ohhappybear.com/2021/12/12/phukets-moo-hong-and-chaiyas-salted-eggs-หมูฮ๊องภูเก็ตใส่ไ/)).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Khai palo with pork belly (*mu sam chan*):** the standard Bangkok home version.
*   **With tofu puffs:** a cheaper egg-and-tofu version.
*   **Phuket *mu hong*:** darker, pepperier, a separate Peranakan dish.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Eggs, hard-boiled and peeled | 6 |
| Pork belly, in chunks | 400 g |
| Garlic, coriander root, white peppercorns (pounded) | 5 cloves, 3, 1 tsp |
| Five-spice powder, star anise, cinnamon stick | 1 tsp, 2, 1 |
| Palm sugar | 3 tbsp |
| Dark soy, light soy, oyster sauce | 2 tbsp, 3 tbsp, 1 tbsp |
| Water | 1.2 L |
| Fried tofu puffs (optional) | 8 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) | 400 g, rinsed (+ 520 ml water, absorption method) |
| Per plate | 1–2 eggs + pork + broth |
| Rice per plate | ≈ 180 g cooked |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Fry the pounded garlic–coriander root–pepper paste, then caramelise the palm sugar with it.
2. Add the pork and spices, coat, then add the soy sauces and water.
3. Add the eggs; simmer gently 1–1½ h, turning the eggs so they colour evenly.
4. Add tofu puffs for the last 15 min; adjust sweet-salty balance.
5. Cook the rice: rinse 400 g jasmine rice until the water runs nearly clear, add 520 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 min, then rest 10 min covered (or use an electric rice cooker / *mo hung khao*).
6. Serve with rice and the broth spooned over.
