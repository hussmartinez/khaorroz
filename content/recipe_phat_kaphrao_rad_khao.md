# Phat Kaphrao Rad Khao / ผัดกะเพราราดข้าว

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok and the Central Plains; now the default lunch order across Thailand.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** One-plate rice: jasmine rice under a fiery, glossy stir-fry of minced pork or chicken with holy basil (*kaphrao*), usually crowned with a crisp-edged fried egg (*khai dao*).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A carbon-steel wok (*kratha*) over a high gas or charcoal flame; a stone mortar (*khrok*) for the garlic-chili paste.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Phat kaphrao* ("stir-fried holy basil") is a 20th-century dish. One account places its first restaurant sales among Chinese-Thai cooks around the reign of King Rama VII (1925–1935); others date its popularity to the 1940s–50s. Before it, the nearest dish was beef stir-fried with Thai basil (*phat bai horapha*) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phat_kaphrao); [Uwajimaya](https://www.uwajimaya.com/uwajipedia/pad-krapow/)). ⚠️ The dates are contested; a 1687 mention of holy basil by La Loubère concerns the herb, not the dish. It is the archetypal *ahan tam sang* (cooked-to-order) plate, almost always eaten over rice with a fried egg ([Splendid Table](https://www.splendidtable.org/story/2014/01/23/stir-fried-chicken-with-hot-basil)).

**Culinary science:** holy basil's clove-like eugenol is volatile, so the leaves go in at the very end, off the hottest heat; the wok's searing heat reduces the sauce to a glaze that clings to the meat and seeps into the rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Bangkok shophouse (*kaphrao mu sap khai dao*):** minced pork, bird's-eye chili, garlic, a little long bean or onion (purists object) and a fried egg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phat_kaphrao)).
*   **Kaphrao kai / nuea / talay:** chicken, beef or mixed seafood versions sold at every rice-and-stir-fry stall.
*   **Kaphrao krop:** with deep-fried crisp holy-basil leaves on top. ⚠️ Regional differences are anecdotal; this is a pan-Thai dish.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok minced pork with fried egg**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Minced pork (or chicken) | 400 g |
| Holy basil leaves (*bai kaphrao*) | 2 packed cups |
| Garlic + bird's-eye chilies, pounded | 8 cloves + 6–10 |
| Oyster sauce / fish sauce / light soy | 2 tbsp / 1 tbsp / 1 tbsp |
| Sugar | 1 tsp |
| Eggs, for khai dao | 4 |
| Neutral oil | 4 tbsp + oil for frying eggs |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 400 g raw (≈ 1.1 kg cooked) |
| Stir-fry per plate | ≈ 120 g |
| Fried egg per plate | 1 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Cook 400 g jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) with 520 ml water; rest 10 min covered.
2. **Eggs:** fry the eggs in plenty of very hot oil, basting until the whites puff and the edges are lacy and crisp; keep the yolks runny.
3. **Paste:** pound garlic and chilies coarsely in the *khrok*.
4. **Stir-fry:** in a smoking wok, fry the paste in oil for 10 s, add the meat and break it up until cooked (2–3 min).
5. **Season:** add the sauces and sugar and toss until glossy; add a splash of water if dry.
6. **Finish:** off the heat fold in the holy basil until just wilted; spoon over rice and top with the egg.
