# Arroz Caldoso de Isla Cristina / Arroz Caldoso con Almejas y Marisco Onubense

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Andalucía](provincial_andalucia.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** I found **no published traditional recipe** specific to Isla Cristina. Local restaurant listings do show arroz caldoso with clams and seafood as a fixture of the town. Everything below is a reconstruction based on Huelva coastal practice and should be verified with local cooks.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isla Cristina, La Redondela, Islantilla, and the Ayamonte estuary (Huelva).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep *cazuela* or pot over gas, in the port's taverns.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Isla Cristina is one of Spain's major fishing ports. It was founded after the 1755 Lisbon earthquake by Catalan and Valencian salt-fish (*salazón*) traders, and it still has a strong canning, *mojama* and fish-auction culture. Its taverns serve **arroz caldoso** made with the clams of the Huelva coast (*chirla*, *coquina*) and **gamba blanca de Huelva**. The Catalan-Valencian founding families are sometimes cited as the reason rice is so present in this Andalusian port, but treat that link as anecdotal.

**Culinary science:** *gamba blanca* has a very sweet, delicate flesh. Its heads hold most of the flavour, so they are crushed into the sofrito. Bivalves are added at the end so they open in the broth and release their liquor.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — reconstructed)
*   **Isla Cristina:** Gamba blanca + chirlas/almejas + monkfish. Pimentón and saffron.
*   **Ayamonte:** Portuguese-border influence: *coentros* (cilantro) sometimes appears.
*   **Punta Umbría:** More **choco**; see [arroz con choco](recipe_arroz_con_choco.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Isla Cristina tavern style** (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Gamba blanca de Huelva | 400 g |
| Fish fumet | 2 L |
| Olive oil | 70 ml |
| Onion | 1 |
| Green pepper | 1 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:** Peel the gambas, keeping the tails. Fry the heads in the oil, pressing them (3 min), then strain them out. Build the sofrito in the same oil (12 min).

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 300 g |
| Fumet | ≈ 1.8 L (6:1) |
| Clams (chirlas/almejas), purged | 500 g |
| Monkfish | 250 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Add the rice to the sofrito, then the boiling fumet and saffron.
2. **Fire:** Simmer **13 min**. Add the monkfish and clams and cook **3 min**. Add the gamba tails for **1–2 min**.
3. **Finish:** Serve immediately.
