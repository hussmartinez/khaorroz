# Arroz Clarico (con Hierbabuena) / Arroz Clarico de la Vega Baja

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md) · [Región de Murcia](provincial_murcia.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a living home dish that is almost **undocumented online**. Written traces: (1) Directo al Paladar's survey of Orihuela's cooking lists *arroz clarico* among the huerta dishes, next to potajes and arroz con verduras; (2) an Orihuela reader on a Vega Baja recipe blog describes it as **"a meat stew, cocido-style, with few chickpeas and compulsory *hierbabuena* (mint)"**, distinct from the Lenten *arroz clarico de vigilia* of Almoradí (see [tres puñaos](recipe_arroz_de_los_tres_punaos.md)); (3) the user of this atlas reports it as a **commonly cooked caldoso of the Vega Baja with a yellowish broth and mint**. The Vega Baja comarca cookbook (CONVEGA) does **not** include it. The atlas user has also seen **all the household variants** listed in §3 cooked first-hand. Quantities below remain a reconstruction from comarca cocido practice.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Vega Baja del Segura: Orihuela and its huerta, with the same name heard in Almoradí (for the meatless vigil version). It probably extends into the Murcian huerta ⚠️.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy). A "clear" (*clarico*, diminutive of *claro*) yellow broth with the rice swimming in it, eaten with a spoon.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A tall *olla* or *cazuela* on the hob, the same pot as the cocido.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The name says it all: ***clarico*** is the affectionate Vega Baja and Murcian diminutive of *claro* ("thin, clear"). It describes a rice cooked in an **abundant, clear broth**, as opposed to the thick legume potajes or the dry paellas. It belongs to the comarca's **cocido culture**: the same kitchens that make the Christmas *cocido con pelotas* and the *olla gitana* make an everyday clarico. Meat and bones are simmered with a **small handful of chickpeas**, and the broth is coloured **yellow** with saffron or, very commonly in modern homes, *colorante*. Rice is cooked directly in it. The defining aromatic is ***hierbabuena*** (spearmint). This is a Murcian-Vega Baja signature that links the dish to the mint-scented *olla gitana* and to the mint used in Vega Baja *pelotas*. It ultimately echoes the Andalusi use of fresh mint in broths.

The **name collision** is a textbook hyper-local case. In **Orihuela**, "arroz clarico" is this meat broth with mint. In **Almoradí**, "arroz clarico de vigilia" is the meatless Lent rice that Orihuela calls *de los tres puñaos*. The shared word, *clarico*, refers to the texture (a clear, soupy rice), not to any particular ingredient.

**Culinary science:** mint's aroma (carvone and limonene) is **volatile and heat-sensitive**. Long boiling flattens it into a dull herbal note, while adding it at the end keeps it bright. That's why most mint-scented Murcian stews add the *hierbabuena* in the last minutes, or leave a sprig in each bowl. Saffron's crocin is water-soluble and gives the "yellowish" broth. Colorante (tartrazine-type) reproduces the colour but not saffron's aroma.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **The Orihuela Style (documented by an Orihuela reader):** Meat broth "tipo cocido", **few chickpeas**, **compulsory hierbabuena**. Caldoso.
*   **The Almoradí "clarico de vigilia" (Lent):** Same name and texture, but **no meat**: beans, lentils, vegetables and rice. Documented separately as [arroz de los tres puñaos](recipe_arroz_de_los_tres_punaos.md).
*   **Household variants (first-hand, atlas user's field observation in the Vega Baja):** every one of the following is **cooked in practice**. The dish is a family of house styles rather than one fixed recipe:

    | Variable | Option A | Option B |
    |---|---|---|
    | Meat | Chicken or hen (*gallina*) | Pork bones (*espinazo*) + *magro* or ribs (or both together) |
    | Potato | With potato (thicker, more filling) | Without potato (clearer broth, truer to the *clarico* name) |
    | Yellow colour | Saffron threads | *Colorante* (the everyday modern choice) |
    | Hierbabuena | Added in the last minutes (bright, fresh mint) | Simmered with the broth (softer, rounder herbal note) |

    The written sources don't say which combination goes with which town. ⚠️ Whether *pelotas* go in on special days is still unconfirmed.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Orihuela meat clarico**, reconstructed. Swap freely between the §3 options: pork for hen, no potato, colorante for saffron, mint simmered from the start instead of at the end.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Free-range chicken or hen (*gallina*), in pieces | 600 g |
| Pork bone (*espinazo*) + a piece of *magro* or rib | 300 g + 200 g |
| Chickpeas, soaked 12 h ("few") | 80–100 g |
| Potato, in chunks | 1 large (300 g) |
| Ripe tomato, halved (or grated) | 1 |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |
| Olive oil | 40 ml |
| Saffron threads (or colorante) | 0.2–0.3 g |
| Water | 3 L |
| **Hierbabuena** (fresh spearmint) | 1 good bunch (≈ 15 g) |
| Salt | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice (Calasparra or Senia/Bahía) | 250–280 g |
| Broth at rice-in | ≈ 2 L (≈ 7–8 : 1, a clear caldoso) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Cocido base:** Put the chicken, pork bones, magro and soaked chickpeas into 3 L of cold water. Bring slowly to a boil and **skim** carefully, because a clear broth is the point. Simmer **1 h 15 min** (or 30 min in a pressure cooker).
2. **Sofrito:** In a pan, fry the garlic in the oil, then the tomato, for 5 min. Add it to the pot with the saffron and the potato. Simmer **15 min**. The broth should now be golden-yellow.
3. **Meat:** Lift out the bones, pick off the meat and return the meat to the pot. Correct the salt. You should have ≈ 2 L of broth.
4. **Rice:** Bring to a lively simmer, add the rice and cook **16–17 min**. Stir only gently, because the broth should stay *clarico* rather than starchy.
5. **Mint:** In the **last 2–3 minutes**, add half the hierbabuena (whole sprigs). Remove it when serving, or leave it in if you like the stronger note.
6. **Finish:** Serve immediately in deep plates with a fresh mint leaf or two in each. A caldoso doesn't rest.
