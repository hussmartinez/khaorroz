# Paella Mixta / Paella Mixta (de Carn i Marisc)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** paella mixta is widely described as an outside, tourist-driven hybrid that is rarely cooked in Valencian homes. However, **I found no academic or primary historical source documenting a specific tourist origin** (for example a date or restaurant). The origin account below summarises popular and press sources and should be read as received opinion, not established history.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Tourist coasts throughout Spain (Benidorm, Costa del Sol, Barcelona, Madrid) and abroad. It is rarely cooked in Valencian households.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A steel paella over gas. Industrial versions are often pre-cooked.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
**Why it is not "paella valenciana":** it combines **meat** (chicken and/or pork, sometimes chorizo) with **seafood** (prawns, mussels, squid) and often adds onion, peas and red pepper strips. By Valencian convention a paella is named after what is in it, and the *valenciana* is a fixed recipe of chicken, rabbit and huerta legumes. Mixing land and sea in one pan and adding onion breaks that recipe, so in Valencia the mixta is seen as a dish for outsiders. Wikipedia sums up the Valencian position: only the paella valenciana is the "true" paella. Press sources note that the mixta is the most popular paella **internationally** but is hardly eaten in Valencia.

The usual account links it to the 1960s–70s tourism boom. On that account, restaurants on the Spanish coasts served a single "paella" that put the meat and seafood versions in one pan, to suit visitors who expected Spain's "national dish" to contain everything. Catalan and inland Spanish *arroz mar y montaña* traditions also mix land and sea, but they are separate, legitimate dishes with their own logic and are not the same as the tourist mixta.

**Culinary science:** the mixta's main technical problem is timing. Chicken needs 20+ min of browning and simmering, while prawns overcook in 3 min. It therefore needs a two-stage build: meat fondo first, and shellfish laid on top late.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

> ⚠️ Not a town-rooted dish, so the variations below are typologies (by type of restaurant), not municipal schools.

*   **The Costa Blanca tourist-restaurant style:** Chicken, pork, prawns, mussels, squid, peas, red pepper, yellow colourant (*colorante*).
*   **The Madrid "paella de los jueves" style:** The Thursday paella tradition of Madrid bars, often mixta and often pre-cooked.
*   **The Catalan *mar i muntanya* (legitimate cousin):** Chicken or rabbit with prawns or cuttlefish, with a deliberate *picada*. A dish of its own and not the tourist mixta.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **the best-practice mixta**: a two-stage build that respects the timing of both proteins.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, in pieces | 500 g |
| Pork loin, cubed | 200 g |
| Squid, in rings | 250 g |
| Prawns | 8 |
| Mussels | 300 g |
| Red pepper, in strips | 1 |
| Onion, finely chopped (optional, non-Valencian) | ½ |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Pimentón | 3 g |
| Peas | 100 g |
| Olive oil | 90 ml |
| Chicken stock + mussel liquor | 1.4 L |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:** Brown the pepper strips and set them aside. Brown the chicken and pork slowly (15 min). Sear the squid (4 min). Add the onion (6 min), then the garlic, then the pimentón (5 s), then the tomato (5 min). Open the mussels separately and add their strained liquor to the stock.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Stock | 1.2–1.3 L |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toasting:** Add the rice and peas and toast for 1 min.
2. **Liquid Addition:** Add the boiling stock and saffron. Level the rice and check the salt.
3. **Fire Manipulation:** High heat for 8 min, then medium-low for 6 min. At minute 14, lay the prawns, mussels and pepper strips on top. Give it 3–4 min more, then 1 min on high heat.
4. **Resting Phase:** Rest 4 min.
