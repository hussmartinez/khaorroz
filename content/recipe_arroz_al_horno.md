# Arroz al Horno / Arròs al Forn

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Costera (Xàtiva), La Vall d'Albaida (Ontinyent), L'Horta Sud (Torrent), and La Safor. It is made across almost the whole territory, and in Alicante and Castellón as well.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). The grains are loose and fully absorbed, with a slightly crisp top and edges.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A shallow, wide glazed earthenware *cassola de fang*. It was traditionally carried to the village **baker's wood-fired oven** (*forn de llenya*), because most homes had no oven of their own.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Arròs al forn is the rice of the *olla*. It was invented to use the broth, chickpeas and meat left over from Saturday's *cocido* (*olla* or *putxero*). On Monday the household fried leftover pork, sausage and potato in the *cassola*, added rice, chickpeas and the *cocido* broth, and sent it to the bakery. The baker cooked dozens of cassoles at once, timing them by position in the oven. Each family marked its pot with a scratched initial or a coloured string. This is why the dish has as many versions as households, and why Valencian cookbooks call it "the most democratic rice".

In **Xàtiva** the tradition is ritualised as **"arròs al forn en tanda"**: on the Thursdays before Carnestoltes (Carnival), families queue their cassoles at the bakers' ovens in strict order (*tanda*). The Xàtiva version uses *panceta* (belly) instead of the pig's snout and ear found elsewhere.

**Culinary science:** oven heat surrounds the pot instead of coming only from below, so no socarrat forms at the base. Instead the heat creates a gentle **evaporation crust on top**. The whole head of garlic in the centre steam-roasts and softens. The potato slices on the surface act as a lid that protects the rice from drying out. Because the clay holds heat for a long time, cooking continues after the pot leaves the oven, so arròs al forn is undercooked by about two minutes on purpose and finished on the rest.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **The Xàtiva Style (La Costera):** Belly (*cansalada*) instead of snout and ear. *Pilotes* (meatballs from the *olla*: minced pork, breadcrumbs, pine nuts, parsley, egg) are common. Chickpeas are always included. Broth comes from the cocido. The Carnestoltes *en tanda* ritual belongs here.
*   **The Ontinyent / Vall d'Albaida Style:** Heavier on *botifarra de ceba* (onion blood-sausage) and *blanquet* (white sausage with pine nuts). Some families stud the surface with **raisins** (*panses*), and the result is then called *arròs passejat* or *arròs amb panses*.
*   **The Torrent / Horta Sud Style:** Pork ribs (*costella*) and *morcilla* dominate. Tomato is halved and laid face-up on top rather than grated. Some cooks add the snout and ear (*morro i orella*).
*   **The Alicante-province echo (Alcoi / Comtat):** *Arròs al forn amb pilotes* sometimes gets an egg finish. That technique is the bridge to [Arroz con costra](recipe_arroz_con_costra.md), which is essentially an arròs al forn sealed with egg.

| Element | Xàtiva | Ontinyent | Torrent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pork cut | Belly | Ribs + belly | Ribs, snout/ear |
| Sausages | Morcilla | Botifarra de ceba + blanquet | Morcilla |
| Pilotes | Common | Occasional | Rare |
| Raisins | No | Optional | No |
| Tomato | Grated + halves | Halves | Halves on top |

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Xàtiva-style** (belly, chickpeas, morcilla, optional pilotes), based on the Generalitat Valenciana's tourism recipe card.*

**Vessel:** A 32–34 cm *cassola de fang*. Season it in water before first use.

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
The broth should be **olla broth** (left over from cocido). If you don't have any, make it:

| Broth ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Water | 2 L |
| Beef shin / *jarrete* | 300 g |
| Chicken carcass or thigh | 300 g |
| Pork bone (*espinazo*) | 200 g |
| Dried chickpeas, soaked 12 h | 200 g |
| Potato, carrot, celery, turnip | 1 each |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

Simmer for **2.5 h**, skimming, then strain. Keep the chickpeas. You need **800 ml** of broth at the end.

**Sofrito in the cassola:**
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Pork belly, 2 cm slices | 250 g |
| Pork ribs, cut short | 250 g |
| Morcilla de cebolla (blood sausage) | 2 pieces (≈200 g) |
| Potato, 0.5 cm rounds | 1 medium (200 g) |
| Ripe tomatoes | 2 (one grated, one halved) |
| Whole head of garlic | 1 |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| Pilotes (optional, Xàtiva) | 4 × 60 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Broth (very hot) | 800 ml (exactly **2:1** by volume to the rice) |
| Cooked chickpeas | 250 g |
| Oven | 220–250 °C, conventional heat, top and bottom |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Preheat the oven to **240 °C** for at least 20 min. Heat the broth to boiling.
2. **Frying:** Heat the oil in the cassola on a hob over a heat diffuser. Fry the potato rounds until golden and set them aside. Fry the halved tomato (cut-side down) and the whole garlic head, then set them aside. Brown the ribs and belly (≈8 min). Briefly fry the morcilla, 1 min only, so it doesn't burst. Set it aside.
3. **Toasting:** Add the grated tomato and cook it for 3 min. Add the pimentón for 5 s, then the rice. Stir and toast for **2 min** so that every grain is coated in fat.
4. **Assembly:** Add the chickpeas and spread everything in an even layer. Put the garlic head in the **centre** and arrange the tomato halves, morcilla, pilotes and potato rounds on the surface like a mosaic.
5. **Liquid Addition:** Pour in the boiling broth with the saffron. It should just cover the rice. Taste for salt.
6. **Fire Manipulation (Oven):** Move the cassola straight into the oven. Bake **18–20 min** at 240 °C until the surface is dry and the potatoes are golden. Don't open the oven during the first 12 minutes.
7. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Rest **5 min** out of the oven. The clay keeps cooking the rice. Serve straight from the cassola.
