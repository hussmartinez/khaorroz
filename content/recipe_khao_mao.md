# Khao Mao / ข้าวเม่า

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the night-time pounding custom is documented as fading; quantities for *khao mao khluk* were not found and are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan (e.g. Amnat Charoen) and the rest of the rice-growing north and centre; made at the end of the rainy season, just before harvest.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Young-rice snack: unripe "milk-stage" glutinous rice grains roasted and pounded flat into soft, green, chewy flakes, then tossed with grated coconut, sugar and a pinch of salt.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay pan or wok over a wood fire to roast the grains, a large wooden foot- or hand-pounder (*khrok mong*); served on banana leaf.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao mao is made from glutinous rice whose grains are not yet ripe: farmers soak, roast and pound them, then mix them with coconut, sugar and a little salt as a sweet snack ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=41202)). It is made while waiting for the harvest, from "milk-stage" grains ([MGR Online – Amnat Charoen khao mao](https://mgronline.com/smes/detail/9630000085669)). The Department of Cultural Promotion is documenting khao mao with the aim of registering it as national intangible cultural heritage ([Bangkok Biznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/judprakai/1208646)). Fried *khao mao thot* is a sweet for Kathin and Pha Pa merit-making, and *khao mao phok* is sold at Wat Hat Mun Krabue ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=109668); [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2959610)). The communal night pounding by young farmers after harvest is fading ([GotoKnow](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/422914)).

**Culinary science:** milk-stage grains are still soft and sugary; roasting partly gelatinises their starch and pounding flattens them while hot, so they set into tender, green, slightly sweet flakes.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Khao mao khluk:** tossed with coconut and sugar ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=41202)).
*   **Khao mao thot:** fried, for Kathin and Pha Pa ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=109668)).
*   **Khao mao phok:** coated and fried, Wat Hat Mun Krabue ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2959610)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: khao mao khluk (reconstructed quantities).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Khao mao (young pounded glutinous rice flakes) | 200 g |
| Freshly grated young coconut | 120 g |
| Sugar | 40–60 g |
| Salt | a pinch |
| Warm water (to soften) | 50 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice flakes : coconut | ≈ 5 : 3 |
| Rest after mixing | 10 min |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Prepare the rice:** if dry, sprinkle the khao mao flakes with warm water and leave 10 min to soften (fresh flakes need no water).
2. **Mix:** toss the flakes with coconut, sugar and salt.
3. **Rest:** leave 10 min so the sugar dissolves into the coconut moisture.
4. **Serve:** on banana leaf, as a seasonal snack.
