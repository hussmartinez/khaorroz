# Yakhnet Batata / يخنة البطاطا

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is very well documented as a household stew served over rice, but almost all sources are recipe sites and family blogs. Town-level differences are only lightly attested and are marked below.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The whole Levant: Lebanese, Syrian, Palestinian and Jordanian home kitchens. Arabic recipe sites describe yakhneh as an old dish "still cooked in Bilad al-Sham, especially Palestine and Jordan".
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: cubes of potato and beef, lamb or chicken simmered in a garlicky tomato sauce until the potato just starts to break, ladled over white rice or *riz bi sh'ariyyeh* (rice with toasted vermicelli).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep stew pot (*tanjara* or *qidr*) on the hob for the stew, and a second lidded pot for the rice.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Yakhneh* (Persian *yakhni*, "stew") is the backbone of the everyday Levantine table: one vegetable, a little meat, tomato and rice. Lebanese sources describe the format exactly: "a vegetable and protein (usually beef cubes or chicken) simmered in a flavored garlic tomato sauce, and served on a bed of white rice with vermicelli noodles". Potato is the most common, cheapest and most child-friendly of these stews. Some Lebanese families call it ***ousiyeh***, and "the name varies depending on whom you ask".

Rice is not a side here; it defines the meal. Stew and rice are cooked together as a pair, and the stew is ladled over the rice on each plate. See the companion [riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md) and the sister stews [bazella w ruz](recipe_bazella_w_ruz.md), [fasoulia w riz](recipe_fasoulia_w_riz.md) and [loubieh bi-lahmeh](recipe_loubieh_bi_lahmeh.md).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Lebanese style (beef or lamb):** Beef cubes browned with garlic and coriander, tomato paste, a cinnamon stick, and potatoes fried or added raw. Served over vermicelli rice. Slow version about 2.5 hours.
*   **Lebanese chicken version (*yakhnet batata wa djaj*):** Chicken thighs in garlic-tomato sauce, about 35 minutes in total.
*   **Palestine / Jordan (⚠️ household practice from Arabic recipe sites):** Potatoes **fried golden first**, meat simmered about an hour with onion, bay leaf and cardamom, tomato added; served with white rice.
*   **Syria (⚠️):** Syrian *yakhneh* recipes add carrots or peas alongside the potato.
*   **Vegan / Lenten version:** Butter beans instead of meat (Lebanese Christian fasting adaptation).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Lebanese beef yakhnet batata with vermicelli rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Beef or lamb, 3 cm cubes | 600 g |
| Potatoes, 3 cm cubes | 800 g |
| Onion, chopped | 1 |
| Garlic, crushed with salt | 5 cloves |
| Fresh coriander, chopped | 1 small bunch |
| Tomato paste | 2 tbsp |
| Crushed tomatoes | 400 g |
| Cinnamon stick + 7-spice (*baharat*) | 1 + 1 tsp |
| Olive oil or ghee | 40 ml |
| Water | 1 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium/short-grain rice (Egyptian or Calrose) | 300 g |
| Vermicelli (*sh'ariyyeh*) | 50 g |
| Water for the rice | 450 ml |
| Ghee or butter for the rice | 30 g |
| Stew : rice per plate | about 1 : 1 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Brown the meat:** Sear the cubes in the oil with the onion, then add the cinnamon, baharat, salt and 1 L of water. Simmer covered for **75–90 min** until tender.
2. **Sauce:** Fry the garlic and coriander for 1 minute, stir in the tomato paste, add it to the pot with the crushed tomatoes.
3. **Potatoes:** Add the potato cubes (or fry them golden first, Palestinian-Jordanian style). Simmer **25 min** until soft at the edges.
4. **Rice:** Rinse and soak the rice 15 min. Toast the vermicelli in the ghee until deep golden, add the rice, stir, add salted boiling water. Cover and cook **15 min** on low, then rest 10 min.
5. **Serve:** Mound the rice on each plate and ladle the stew over it. A squeeze of lemon is optional.
