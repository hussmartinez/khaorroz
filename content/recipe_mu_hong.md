# Mu Hong / หมูฮ้อง (หมูหอง)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the published sources document it as a Phuket local dish of the Hokkien-descended community. The Baba-Peranakan attribution is common, but those sources do not state it directly.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Phuket (Old Town and the island's Hokkien-descended families), extending to Phang Nga and Takua Pa.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: large pieces of pork belly braised for about two hours in a salty-sweet soy, black-pepper and garlic liquor until the meat is tender and the skin glossy. It is served over steamed rice with a fermented-chili dip.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot (often a clay pot) over a low charcoal fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Phuket families traditionally cooked *mu hong* in large quantities for major festivals, both auspicious ones (weddings, Chinese New Year) and funerary rites ([Silpa-mag – the origin of mu hong](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=355)). Large slabs of belly are marinated and then simmered gently in a salty-sweet seasoning until the meat is tender and the skin has absorbed the colour. The dish needs about two hours over low heat and is eaten with a chili-and-vinegar dip ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=355)). It is one of the signatures of Phuket's local food, sold alongside dim sum, *chiao kuai* and *o-aew* in the island's food halls ([Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/food/1097145); [Wongnai – mu hong in Phuket](https://www.wongnai.com/listings/moo-hong-phuket)).

**Culinary science:** long, gentle braising converts the belly's collagen into gelatin, and the sugar and soy reduce into a glaze. Black pepper, the defining Hokkien seasoning here, stays aromatic because it is crushed coarse.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Phuket Old Town:** Black pepper and garlic, dark and glossy, served with steamed rice and a dip ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=355)).
*   **Festival batches:** Very large pots for weddings and funerals; the leftovers improve the next day ⚠️.
*   **Restaurant versions:** Phuket heritage restaurants list it as a signature dish ([Wongnai listings](https://www.wongnai.com/listings/moo-hong-phuket)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Skin-on pork belly, in large pieces (5 cm) | 800 g |
| Garlic, crushed | 1 head |
| Black peppercorns, coarsely crushed | 1 tbsp |
| Light soy sauce | 3 tbsp |
| Dark soy sauce | 1 tbsp |
| Palm or brown sugar | 3 tbsp |
| Water | 700 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 400 g raw, steamed |
| Mu hong per plate | 3–4 pieces with sauce |
| Dip | fermented chili with vinegar |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Marinate:** Rub the pork with garlic, pepper and soy sauces; leave 1 h.
2. **Brown:** Caramelise the sugar in the clay pot, then add the pork and turn it until coloured.
3. **Braise:** Add the water and simmer over a very low fire, covered, for **about 2 h**, turning occasionally, until tender and glossy.
4. **Rice:** In the last 30 min, steam the jasmine rice.
5. **Serve:** Pork over rice, with sauce spooned on and the chili-vinegar dip.
