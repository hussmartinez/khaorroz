# Mahshi Lift (Stuffed Turnips) / محشي لفت

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is documented as Palestinian (West Bank) and Jordanian winter food, stuffed with rice, meat and sumac, in tomato sauce or in a tahini or tamarind version. The cookbook *Falastin* (Tamimi & Wigley) carries a "Mahshi Lift" recipe. Hebron's association with the tamarind version comes from general lists and wasn't confirmed by a dedicated source. Quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The West Bank hills, especially the al-Khalil (Hebron) area, and Jordan.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vegetables (*mahashi*). Hollowed turnips filled with rice, minced meat and sumac, cooked until soft and sweet in a tangy tomato or tamarind sauce.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide pot on a gas ring or wood stove. The turnips are lightly fried first.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Turnips (*lift*) are a cold-season crop of the Palestinian hills. Stuffing them is a **winter** custom, alongside cabbage ([malfuf mahshi](recipe_malfouf_mehshi.md)) and the summer zucchini and eggplant of [mahshi](recipe_mahshi.md). What sets *mahshi lift* apart is its **sour profile**: local **sumac** goes into the filling, and the cooking sauce is either tomato or, in the older style, **tamarind** (*tamr hindi*), sometimes with tahini. Tamarind-soured stuffed vegetables are a Jerusalem and Hebron taste: Jerusalem also has stuffed cucumbers with tamarind (*mahshi khiyar ma'a tamr hindi*), and Jaffa families stuffed carrots with tamarind. Heritage cooks such as Mirna Bamieh's "Palestine Hosting Society" record these dishes as at risk of disappearing.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Tomato version (West Bank, Jordan):** Filling of rice, minced lamb, spices and sumac; turnips fried, then simmered in tomato sauce.
*   **Tamarind / tahini version (Hebron and Jerusalem tradition) (⚠️):** The sauce is soured with tamarind pulp, sometimes enriched with tahini.
*   **Jaffa cousin:** Carrots stuffed and cooked with tamarind (Abu Haibeh family, documented by Mirna Bamieh).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: tomato-and-tamarind (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium turnips | 10–12 (≈ 1.2 kg) |
| Minced lamb or beef | 250 g |
| Short-grain rice, rinsed | 150 g |
| Sumac | 2 tsp |
| Allspice, cinnamon, salt | 1 tsp / ½ tsp / 1 tsp |
| Oil for frying | 4 tbsp |
| Tomato paste | 2 tbsp |
| Tamarind pulp (or 2 tbsp concentrate) | 40 g |
| Garlic, crushed | 3 cloves |
| Water | 600 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Filling per turnip | ≈ 2 tbsp (¾ full) |
| Sauce | to cover ¾ of the turnips |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Hollow:** Peel the turnips, trim the tops and hollow them out with a corer, leaving 5 mm walls.
2. **Fill:** Mix the rice, meat, sumac, spices and salt, and fill the turnips three-quarters full.
3. **Fry:** Fry the stuffed turnips in the oil, turning, until lightly golden all over.
4. **Sauce:** Dissolve the tomato paste and tamarind in the water with the garlic and salt.
5. **Simmer:** Pack the turnips upright in a pot, pour the sauce over, cover and simmer on low for 50–60 minutes until the turnips are tender and the rice is cooked.
