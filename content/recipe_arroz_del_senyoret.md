# Arroz del Señorito / Arròs del Senyoret

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Alicante city (L'Alacantí) and the Valencia city coast (El Cabanyal, La Malva-rosa). It is a restaurant standard throughout the coast.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A thin steel paella over gas or wood.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
"Senyoret" means "little gentleman" or "young master". Everything in this rice comes **already peeled, cleaned and boned**, so the diner never has to touch a shell. One story credits household cooks who prepared it for the *señorito* of a well-off family. Another says it was a restaurant invention for urban visitors who didn't want to peel prawns with their fingers. Neither story is solidly documented. Structurally it is an [arroz a banda](recipe_arroz_a_banda.md) enriched with peeled shellfish, and Alicante guides describe it in exactly those terms.

**Culinary science:** with no shells in the pan, flavour from the shellfish has to be **transferred into the stock** beforehand. The heads and shells are fried and simmered into the fumet, and in some kitchens their fat is also pressed out into the oil. Because peeled prawns overcook in seconds, they go in **raw at minute 12–14** or are laid on top for the last minutes, while the cuttlefish is seared at the start.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **The Alicante City Style:** **Salmorreta** base. Diced **sepia** + peeled **gamba roja** + sometimes **rape** (monkfish) cubes. Rockfish fumet fortified with prawn heads. Very thin layer, socarrat, allioli. Recipes stress a single stir when the stock goes in, then none.
*   **The Valencia City Style:** Garlic + tomato + pebre roig sofrito (no salmorreta). Sepia, **chipirones**, peeled **gambas**, and sometimes **mussels out of their shells**. Saffron is prominent. Rice varieties such as *Tartana* or Bomba are used (e.g. Adolfo Cuquerella's version).
*   **The Murcia / southern Vega Baja echo:** Locals sometimes call a similar rice "a banda" and serve it with **shell-on** chunks, contradicting the senyoret principle. Don't confuse the two.
*   **Modern meat "senyoret":** Restaurants such as Hermanos Torres have created a **meat** senyoret with boned chicken, rabbit and pork. Note this as a contemporary riff, not tradition.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Alicante city** (salmorreta + sepia + gamba roja + monkfish).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Red prawns (*gamba roja*) | 400 g whole (≈ 16 pcs) → peeled tails reserved |
| Rockfish fumet (see [arroz a banda](recipe_arroz_a_banda.md)) | 1.5 L |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Salmorreta | 60 g |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |

**Shell extraction:** Peel the prawns. Fry the heads and shells in 30 ml of the oil, **pressing the heads** to release their coral, for **3 min**. Add the fumet and simmer for **15 min**, then strain. You should have ≈1.3 L of fortified stock.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Fortified fumet | 1.2–1.3 L |
| Sepia, diced 1 cm | 250 g |
| Monkfish, 2 cm cubes | 200 g |
| Peeled prawn tails | 16 |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Make the stock as above.
2. **Sear:** Sear the sepia in the rest of the oil until caramelised (**8 min**). Sear the monkfish cubes for **1 min** and remove them. Add the garlic, then the salmorreta (1 min).
3. **Toasting:** Rice in, toast **2 min**.
4. **Liquid Addition:** Boiling stock and saffron. Stir once. Salt.
5. **Fire Manipulation:** High for 8 min → medium for 4 min → **put the monkfish back and lay the prawn tails on the surface** at about min 12 → medium-low for 4 min → high for 1–2 min (socarrat).
6. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Rest **3 min**. Serve with allioli. Everything is eaten with a spoon and no fingers are needed.
