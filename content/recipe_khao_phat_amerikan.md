# Khao Phat Amerikan / ข้าวผัดอเมริกัน

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is Thai, not American, and is linked to the Vietnam War period, when US servicemen were based in Thailand. Several origin stories compete. One credits **Khunying Suriphan Manivat** (pen name "Nittaya Natayasunthorn"), based on an interview in *Sakul Thai*. None is settled.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok and towns near the former US airbases (Udon Thani, Korat, U-Tapao). Now on menus nationwide.
*   **Historical Source:** 1960s–70s Thai restaurant invention for US servicemen (Vietnam War era); attribution disputed.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Wok-fried rice: **ketchup-reddened fried rice with raisins**, served as a platter with fried chicken drumstick, ham or hot dog, and a fried egg.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Wok (*kratha*) over gas, and a separate pan or deep fryer for the chicken and egg.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
"American fried rice" was invented in Thailand. It is usually dated to the **Vietnam War years**, when Thai cooks fed American airmen and marines stationed on Thai bases. It became a fixture of Thai menus while remaining rare in Thai restaurants abroad, and Malaysia has a halal cousin, *nasi goreng USA* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_fried_rice)). Thai press reports name Khunying Suriphan Manivat as its creator, but say no account is certain ([Thansettakij](https://www.thansettakij.com/general-news/533517); [Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=279807)).

**Culinary science:** ketchup's sugars caramelise in the hot wok, which gives the rice its red-orange colour and a sweet-sour note that the raisins echo. The plate is built as a Western-style breakfast platter around the rice, Thai cooks' idea of American food.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Bangkok restaurant style:** ketchup rice with raisins, fried chicken drumstick, ham, hot dog and fried egg.
*   **Home and kids' versions:** sausages only, less sweet ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/american-fried-rice-for-kids)).
*   **Malaysia (*nasi goreng USA*):** chicken replaces pork for halal requirements ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_fried_rice)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok restaurant platter**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Day-old jasmine rice | 800 g |
| Tomato ketchup | 8 tbsp |
| Raisins | 60 g |
| Onion, diced; mixed peas and carrot | 1; 150 g |
| Butter or oil | 40 g |
| Light soy, sugar, salt | 1 tbsp, 1 tsp, pinch |
| Chicken drumsticks (fried), ham, hot dogs | 4, 4 slices, 4 |
| Eggs, fried | 4 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice : ketchup | ≈ 100 g : 1 tbsp |
| Platter per person | rice + 1 drumstick + 1 egg + ham and hot dog |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Fry the sides:** deep-fry the drumsticks (or use pre-cooked ones) and pan-fry the ham, hot dogs and eggs. Keep warm.
2. **Base:** melt the butter in a hot wok and fry the onion, peas and carrot 2 min.
3. **Rice:** add the rice and toss until hot. Add the ketchup, soy, sugar and raisins and toss until evenly red and lightly caramelised.
4. **Serve:** pack the rice into a bowl and turn it out on a platter. Arrange the chicken, ham, hot dog and fried egg around it, with extra ketchup on the side.
