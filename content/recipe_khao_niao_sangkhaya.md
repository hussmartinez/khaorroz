# Khao Niao Sangkhaya / ข้าวเหนียวสังขยา

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** recipes are well sourced (Le Cordon Bleu Dusit, Wongnai, a Thai academic study of the standard recipe). The historical claim rests on secondary reading of King Rama II's *Kap He Chom Khrueang Khao Wan*, as reported by Wongnai.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand and Bangkok markets; nationwide.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** A sticky-rice sweet. Coconut-soaked sticky rice (*khao niao moon*) is topped with **sangkhaya**, a steamed custard of duck eggs, palm sugar and coconut milk, either set in slices or spooned soft.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A huad or steamer for the rice and a steaming tray for the custard. In the old style the custard is steamed in a hollowed pumpkin (*sangkhaya fak thong*).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Sangkhaya* is the Thai cousin of the Portuguese-influenced egg sweets of the Ayutthaya court (*thong yip*, *foi thong*). Here the egg is set with palm sugar and coconut rather than syrup. Pairing it with sticky rice is one of the oldest *khao niao na tang tang* ("sticky rice with various toppings"). Wongnai reads King Rama II's poem praising sweet dishes as evidence that sticky rice and sangkhaya were known by the early Rattanakosin period (⚠️ second-hand). Old-town shops still sell two rices (white and black) and two toppings (sangkhaya and *na kung*, a sweet-savoury shrimp floss).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Bangkok market shops:** White or black rice with sangkhaya or *na kung*.
*   **Pumpkin sangkhaya (*sangkhaya fak thong*):** The custard steamed inside a pumpkin and sliced on top of or beside the rice.
*   **Soft spooned custard:** Some shops serve a pourable sangkhaya with pandan.
*   **Reduced-sugar:** A Thai university study found tasters accepted a 60 % sugar cut in the standard recipe.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok market style**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Duck eggs | 4 |
| Palm sugar | 120 g |
| Coconut cream | 200 ml |
| Pandan leaves (juice or knotted) | 3 |
| Salt | pinch |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice, soaked overnight | 300 g |
| Coconut milk + sugar + salt (for the *moon*) | 300 ml + 80 g + 1 tsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Steam the soaked rice for **25–30 minutes**. Soak it in the warm sweetened coconut milk, covered, for **20 minutes**.
2. **Custard:** Rub the palm sugar with the eggs and pandan by hand until dissolved. Stir in the coconut cream and salt, then strain.
3. **Steam:** Pour the custard into a tray (or a hollowed pumpkin) and steam over medium heat for **25–35 minutes**, until just set.
4. **Cool:** Let it cool, then cut into squares.
5. **Serve:** Put a mound of sticky rice on banana leaf with sangkhaya on top.
