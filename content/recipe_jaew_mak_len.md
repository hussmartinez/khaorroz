# Jaew Mak Len / แจ่วหมากเล่น (Jaew Makhuea Thet / แจ่วมะเขือเทศ)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** tomato jaew is documented as one of the most popular jaew, but no source gave quantities or the roasting method; the recipe is reconstructed from the general jaew method.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan and Laos (*mak len* is the Lao/Isan word for tomato).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a smoky, tangy relish of roasted tomatoes, chillies, shallot and garlic pounded with fermented fish, loose and juicy; scooped with sticky rice and vegetables.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Embers or a charcoal grill to roast the tomatoes, a clay mortar; sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Jaew are the dry, *pla ra*-based relishes of Isan, as opposed to *pon*, which add fish or meat, and tomato jaew is singled out as one of the most popular ([Silpa-mag – แจ่วกับป่น](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=162504)). Classic jaew technique toasts dried chilli, pounds galangal and often adds toasted rice powder, balancing salt and sour ([SheSimmers – How to make jaew](https://shesimmers.com/2009/06/how-to-make-jaew-thai-dried-chilli.html)).

**Culinary science:** charring tomatoes over embers collapses their cells and concentrates sugars, giving a sweet-smoky pulp that the pounded chilli and fermented fish season.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **With pla ra:** the Isan default ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=162504)).
*   **With khao kua:** some families add toasted rice powder ([SheSimmers](https://shesimmers.com/2009/06/how-to-make-jaew-thai-dried-chilli.html)).
*   **With fish (*pon makhuea thet*):** becomes a *pon* when fish flesh is added. ⚠️

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: reconstructed Isan roasted-tomato jaew.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Small tomatoes | 300 g |
| Fresh bird's-eye chillies | 6–10 |
| Shallots and garlic | 4 / 5 cloves |
| Pla ra (boiled) or fish sauce | 2 tbsp |
| Lime juice | 1 tbsp |
| Spring onion and coriander | 1 handful |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Tomato : chilli | ≈ 30 : 1 by weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Roast:** grill the tomatoes, chillies, shallots and garlic over embers until blistered; peel the shallots and garlic.
3. **Pound:** pound chillies, shallot and garlic, then add the tomatoes and pound to a juicy pulp; season with pla ra and lime.
4. **Serve:** finish with herbs; eat with sticky rice and boiled or raw vegetables.
