# Kaeng Om / แกงอ่อม

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan (Nong Khai, Udon Thani, Nong Bua Lamphu) and Lanna; of Lao heritage.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a "little-water" herb stew (*om* = cooked with just enough liquid) of chicken, pork, beef or fish with many vegetables, dill, lemongrass and fermented fish; green, herbal, slightly thickened; eaten with sticky rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay or aluminium pot over charcoal; sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Kaeng om gathers many vegetables in one pot and is said to come from Lao culture, eaten by Isan people and northern Thais; the name comes from cooking with little water ("om") and many vegetables ([Wongnai – om mu](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/bd0c33fa2a8d472684d63a920b93722d)). Typical ingredients are lemongrass, pla ra, spring onion, dill (*phak chi lao*), baby corn, Thai eggplant and lemon basil ([Cookpad – kaeng om kai](https://cookpad.com/th/recipes/15422641)). A university study testing kaeng om recipes from Nong Khai, Nong Bua Lamphu and Udon Thani found tasters preferred the Nong Khai version, based on pla ra, dill and spring onion ([TCI journal (RMUTP)](https://so09.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/hecrmutp/article/download/80/65)). Some cooks add toasted ground sticky rice to thicken it, a cousin of [khao buea](recipe_khao_buea.md) ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/bd0c33fa2a8d472684d63a920b93722d)).

**Culinary science:** cooking with little water keeps the broth concentrated and lets the vegetables steam in their own moisture; dill's volatile aroma is added at the end.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Nong Khai:** pla ra, dill, spring onion; the preferred version in the RMUTP study ([TCI](https://so09.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/hecrmutp/article/download/80/65)).
*   **Nong Bua Lamphu / Udon Thani:** variants tested in the same study ([TCI](https://so09.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/hecrmutp/article/download/80/65)).
*   **Om mu with khao kua:** an Isan–northern hybrid with toasted rice ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/bd0c33fa2a8d472684d63a920b93722d)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Nong Khai-style kaeng om kai (chicken).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Chicken pieces | 500 g |
| Lemongrass, sliced / shallots / dried chillies (pounded) | 2 stalks / 4 / 5 |
| Pla ra | 3 tbsp |
| Thai eggplant, baby corn, long beans | 300 g |
| Dill, spring onion, lemon basil | 1 bunch each |
| Water | 500 ml |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Liquid : solids | ≈ 1 : 1.6 ("om", little water) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Base:** stir-fry the pounded aromatics briefly, add chicken and pla ra, then the water; simmer 15 min.
3. **Vegetables:** add eggplant, corn and beans; cook 5 min.
4. **Finish:** add dill, spring onion and basil off the heat; serve with sticky rice.
