# Ruz Hamid (Ma'an Sour Rice) / الرز الحامض المعاني

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented by the Jordanian newspaper *Ad-Dustour* (2020 and later articles) and by Jo24 (2013), which agree on the core: rice, often with lentils, cooked in sour fermented yogurt (*laban al-mureis* / *jameed*), finished with local ghee and fried onion. Two points are uncertain. (1) The **Armenian origin** is a single oral account (Sheikh Mohammad al-Muflih al-Qutaishat, in *Ad-Dustour*); the article gives no date or evidence. (2) The **Arabic Wikipedia article** "الرز الحامض" describes a *different* dish, a lemon-and-chard rice that matches the Palestinian-Lebanese *rizz bi-hamod*. That article is tagged as needing maintenance, and its sourcing does not mention Ma'an's yogurt preparation, so it is not used here except to note the confusion. Quantities below are reconstructed; neither news source gives them.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Ma'an, southern Jordan. The newspaper sources call it a dish "found only in Ma'an" (*Ad-Dustour*).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Festive rice and lentils cooked in sour fermented yogurt. The grains turn soft, pale and tangy; the dish is spread on a flat tray, glossed with ghee and covered with crisp fried onion and pine nuts.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large pot for cooking the rice in the simmering yogurt, and a flat metal tray (*sedr*) for serving, as in the tribal guesthouses (diwans) on Eid mornings.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Al-ruz al-hamid* ("the sour rice") is Ma'an's Eid dish. Jo24 (2013) describes families meeting in the tribal diwans and at relatives' homes over the three days of Eid al-Fitr, with sour rice and *mujallala* (bread crumbled into hot tomato and ghee) on the trays. Both are also served at graduation and Tawjihi-success celebrations. The sourness comes from the dairy: the rice and lentils are cooked in *laban al-rabi'* or *laban al-mureis*, the locally made sour yogurt or dried yogurt (*jameed*) of the south. This ties the dish to the same dairy culture as [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md).

*Ad-Dustour*'s columnist Mahmoud Krishan calls it "a quintessential popular dish" (*akla sha'biyya bi-imtiyaz*). He quotes Sheikh Mohammad al-Muflih al-Qutaishat's view that it is "a culture that crept into Ma'an with the arriving Armenian migrants". That is plausible for a town on the Hejaz railway that took in refugees in the early 20th century, but it is undocumented beyond his account ⚠️. His recipe uses thin-grained American rice and advises serving it on its own, without salads or pickles, so its flavour stays clear.

Cooking rice in fermented yogurt is unusual in the Levant, where yogurt is usually added after cooking (as in [shakriyeh](recipe_shakriyeh.md) or [kousa bi-laban](recipe_kousa_bi_laban.md)). The sour rice belongs with the rice-and-lentil dishes of the region ([mujaddara](recipe_mujaddara.md), [musluʿa](recipe_musluaa.md)). Its dairy cooking liquid makes it a southern Jordanian relative of mansaf's jameed sauce.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Ma'an, with lentils (Jo24, 2013):** Rice and lentils cooked together in *laban al-rabi'*/jameed, finished with local ghee, fried onion and fried pine nuts.
*   **Ma'an, rice only (*Ad-Dustour*, 2020):** Thin-grained rice soaked 15 minutes, added to boiling *laban al-mureis* and cooked over medium heat. It is spread on a tray and topped with onion fried in ghee and more ghee, with optional almonds and pine nuts fried in ghee.
*   **Not the same dish: *rizz bi-hamod* (Palestine, Lebanon):** A lemon-sour rice with chard and chickpeas, sometimes soupy. The Arabic Wikipedia article conflates it with the Ma'an dish.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Ma'an with lentils** (Jo24), using the method from *Ad-Dustour*. Quantities are reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jameed (dried yogurt), soaked and blended, or thick sour laban | 250 g jameed (or 1 L sour laban) |
| Water (to loosen the jameed) | 1.2 L |
| Brown or green lentils | 150 g |
| Onions, thinly sliced | 3 large |
| Local ghee (*samn baladi*) | 100 g |
| Pine nuts (and/or almonds) | 50 g |
| Salt | to taste (jameed is salty) |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice (short or thin-grained, as in the sources) | 400 g, soaked 15 min |
| Jameed liquid for cooking the rice | ≈ 1.2 L, kept at a simmer |
| Lentils (pre-cooked) | 150 g dry weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Jameed:** Break the jameed into pieces, soak it in warm water for a few hours, then blend with the water until smooth. Bring it to a simmer slowly, stirring constantly in one direction so it doesn't split.
2. **Lentils:** Boil the lentils in water for 15–20 min until just tender, then drain.
3. **Rice:** Soak the rice for 15 min, drain, then add it with the lentils to the simmering jameed. Cook over medium heat, stirring gently now and then, until the rice is tender and the liquid is absorbed (≈ 20 min). Taste before adding salt.
4. **Onions:** Fry the onions slowly in half the ghee until deep golden and crisp. Fry the pine nuts in a spoon of ghee until golden.
5. **Serve:** Spread the rice on a flat tray, drizzle with the remaining warm ghee, and cover with the fried onion and pine nuts. Serve warm, on its own.
