# Khao Kua (Toasted Rice Powder) / ข้าวคั่ว

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Isan and the north, and Laos. It is the signature seasoning of *larb* and *nam tok*.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice used as a seasoning. Raw sticky rice is dry-toasted golden, often with galangal, lemongrass and kaffir lime leaf, then pounded into a coarse, nutty, sandy powder that thickens and perfumes meat salads.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A dry wok or clay pan over low charcoal, then a stone or clay mortar (*khrok*).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Isan cooking rice is not only eaten: it is also a seasoning. Larb, once the dish served to guests at merit-making, weddings and funerals, gets its scent from *khao kua* and dried chilli ([Michelin Guide – larb](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/what-is-larb-the-unsung-hero-of-thailand-s-isan-cuisine)). Isan recipes for pork, chicken and catfish larb all call for it, at about 1–4 tablespoons per 300–500 g of meat ([Shopee Blog – Isan menu](https://shopee.co.th/blog/northeastern-food-menu-recipes); [Wongnai – larb catfish](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/minced-catfish-spicy-salad-with-herbs); [Wongnai – spicy minced pork](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/spicy-minced-pork)). Lanna cooks use the same low-heat roasting and pounding for their spice mixes ([Payap University – Lanna food document](https://library.payap.ac.th/webin/NTIC/scan%20lanna%20food/word/ph000088.doc)).

**Culinary science:** dry-toasting raw rice browns the grain's surface sugars and proteins, the Maillard reaction, without gelatinising the starch. Pounding the rice coarse leaves crunchy bits that soak up lime juice and fish sauce, slightly thickening the dressing and binding it to the meat.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Isan:** Sticky rice toasted dark gold, often with galangal and lemongrass, pounded fairly coarse for larb and *nam tok*.
*   **Lanna:** Used in *larb mueang* alongside the north's dried-spice blend (*phrik larb*).
*   **Plain vs aromatic:** ⚠️ Toasting the herbs with the rice is common family practice, but no source documents it specifically.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: aromatic Isan khao kua (makes enough for several larbs).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Raw glutinous rice (unsoaked) | 100 g |
| Galangal, thin slices (optional) | 3 slices |
| Lemongrass, sliced (optional) | 1 stalk |
| Kaffir lime leaves (optional) | 3 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Toasting | 12–15 min, low heat |
| Use | 2 tbsp per 300 g meat for larb |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toast:** Put the dry rice in a dry wok over low heat and stir constantly until evenly deep golden and fragrant (**12–15 min**). Add the herbs for the last 3–4 minutes.
2. **Cool:** Spread on a plate to cool completely, which keeps the powder crisp.
3. **Pound:** Pound or grind to a coarse powder, discarding any fibrous herb pieces.
4. **Store:** Keep airtight and use within a week or two.
5. **Use:** Fold into larb or *nam tok* just before serving, with chilli powder, lime, fish sauce, shallots and herbs.
