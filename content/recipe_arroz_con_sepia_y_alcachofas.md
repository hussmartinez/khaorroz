# Arroz con Sepia y Alcachofas / Arròs amb Sépia i Carxofa

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md) · [Província de Castelló](provincial_castellon.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Valldigna and La Safor (Tavernes de la Valldigna, Gandia), the Valencia city coast, and the Castellón coast (Benicarló artichokes).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry) in the common paella form. The Valldigna *calder* tradition cooks it more caldoso (see §3).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A steel paella, or an **iron calder** in La Valldigna, over wood or gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
This is the classic **winter mar i muntanya of the Valencian coast**. Cuttlefish from the inshore boats is paired with artichoke from the huerta, whose season (November to April) overlaps with cuttlefish's best months. The Generalitat notes that the Benicarló artichoke "fits very well" into rice dishes, including seafood rices. A tourism booklet from the Diputació de València on **Tavernes de la Valldigna** documents a local version: sepia, squid and monkfish fried until golden, a separate sofrito of onion, tomato and garlic with salt and *pebre de nyora*, and about 20 min of cooking in hot broth. It also specifies that the **pot must be iron** so the rice takes on its characteristic **greenish colour**, which comes from the artichoke's polyphenols reacting with the iron.

**Culinary science:** artichoke polyphenols (caffeoylquinic acids) oxidise and react with metal ions. In stainless steel they turn the rice grey-brown. In an iron calder they bind with iron and give a dark green tint, which Valldigna cooks prize and cooks elsewhere avoid by rubbing the artichokes with lemon. Cuttlefish is seared hard first, because its juices and caramelised surface are the backbone of the fondo.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **Tavernes de la Valldigna:** Sepia, **squid** and **monkfish**. A separate onion, tomato and garlic sofrito with *pebre de nyora*. An **iron calder** for the green colour. About 20 min in hot broth.
*   **Valencia city / La Safor:** Seco in the paella. Garlic, tomato and pebre roig, **no onion**. Artichokes in quarters or eighths, fried until golden. Rockfish fumet.
*   **Castellón (Benicarló / Vinaròs):** **D.O. Benicarló artichoke**, sometimes with **galeres** (see [galeras y alcachofas](recipe_arroz_con_galeras_y_alcachofas.md)) or as *arròs negre amb carxofa*, with ink.
*   **Alicante:** A salmorreta base, and the colour is redder.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Valencia/Safor seco**. The Valldigna changes are given in brackets.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sepia, diced 1.5 cm | 400 g |
| Artichokes, cleaned, in eighths (lemon-rubbed unless using iron) | 6 |
| Olive oil | 100 ml |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Pebre roig (or pebre de nyora) | 3 g |
| Rockfish fumet | 1.3 L |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |
| [Valldigna: + 150 g squid + 200 g monkfish; + ½ onion in the sofrito] | — |

**Extraction:** Fry the artichokes until golden (6 min) and set them aside. Sear the sepia until all its liquid has evaporated and it browns (8–10 min). Add the garlic, then the pebre (5 s), then the tomato (5 min). Put the artichokes back in.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Fumet | 1.2–1.3 L |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toasting:** Add the rice and toast for 2 min.
2. **Liquid Addition:** Add the boiling fumet and saffron, and check the salt.
3. **Fire Manipulation:** High heat for 8 min, then medium-low for 8–9 min, then 1 min on high for the socarrat. [Valldigna: use more broth (≈ 1.6 L) and simmer about 20 min in the iron calder.]
4. **Resting Phase:** Rest 4 min. Serve with allioli.
