# Sup No Mai / ซุปหน่อไม้

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan; a fixture of every *ran som tam*.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: shredded bamboo shoot cooked in green *yanang*-leaf juice, then tossed like a salad with toasted rice, chilli, lime, fish sauce, shallot and herbs; a moist, olive-green, sour-spicy "salad" (not a soup); eaten with sticky rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot over charcoal for the bamboo and yanang juice, a stone mortar for the toasted rice; sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Silpa-Wattanatham asks why this hit of Isan som tam shops is called *sup* (soup) when it is nothing like a Western soup: it is a pounded-and-tossed bamboo-shoot salad, and the traditional versions add no fish, crab, shrimp or shellfish ([Silpa-mag – ทำไมเรียก “ซุบหน่อไม้”](https://www.silpa-mag.com/from-the-fingertip/article_11073)). A Wongnai recipe tosses 200 g of bamboo shoot boiled in *yanang* juice with shallot, sawtooth coriander, 1–2 spoons of toasted rice, ground chilli, fish sauce and lime ([Wongnai – sup no mai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/1b08b593f8334b18a8655e2075bb8a1c)); *yanang* leaves are squeezed for their green juice ([HDmall – yanang](https://hdmall.co.th/plants-herbs/plant/yanang/how-to-use/)).

**Culinary science:** boiling bamboo shoots removes bitterness and cyanogenic compounds; the chlorophyll-rich yanang juice colours and slightly thickens the dressing, and toasted rice adds crunch and body.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Home Isan style:** bamboo + yanang + khao kua + herbs ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/1b08b593f8334b18a8655e2075bb8a1c)).
*   **Sup makhuea:** the same *sup* method with roasted eggplant ([Pholfoodmafia – sup makhuea](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=20837)).
*   **With sesame:** a modern variation ⚠️ (not in the traditional sources).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Isan sup no mai with yanang.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Bamboo shoot, shredded, boiled | 400 g |
| Yanang leaf juice | 300 ml |
| Khao kua (toasted ground rice) | 3 tbsp |
| Ground dried chilli / fish sauce / lime | 1–2 tsp / 2 tbsp / 2 tbsp |
| Shallots, sawtooth coriander, spring onion, mint | 1 handful |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Khao kua : bamboo | 1–2 tbsp : 200 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Bamboo:** simmer the shredded bamboo in the yanang juice 10 min; drain, keeping a little liquid.
3. **Toss:** mix with chilli, fish sauce, lime, shallot and herbs.
4. **Finish:** add the khao kua; serve warm or at room temperature with sticky rice.
