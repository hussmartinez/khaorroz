# Arroz a la Sevillana / Arroz con Cosas

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Andalucía](provincial_andalucia.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** "arroz a la sevillana" is a long-standing name on Spanish menus, but I found **no documented canonical recipe or history**. The only sources I found were a personal account of eating it at Expo '92 (with chorizo, shellfish and fish) and Andalusian *arroz con cosas* / *arroz campero* recipes. What follows describes the mixed Andalusian rice that the name usually refers to. Treat it as reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Sevilla province, the marismas del Guadalquivir (Isla Mayor, La Puebla del Río), and the Sevilla city ventas.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). Andalusian mixed rices are usually wetter than a Valencian paella.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide *paellera* or *perol* over gas or wood, at ventas and Sunday family meals.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Sevilla province is **Spain's leading rice producer**. The **marismas del Guadalquivir** around Isla Mayor were drained and put under rice in the 20th century, notably by Valencian growers who settled there from the 1920s–1930s. The region's mixed rice, known colloquially as **"arroz con cosas"** ("rice with things"), freely combines chicken or pork with shellfish, peas, piquillo or roasted red pepper and sometimes chorizo. It is the opposite of Valencian orthodoxy. The Diputación de Sevilla runs an annual homemade rice recipe contest that promotes this tradition. "A la sevillana" on mid-20th-century menus usually signalled this generous, colourful mixed rice, garnished with strips of red pepper and peas.

**Culinary science:** mixing meat stock and shellfish produces a broth rich in both gelatin and glutamates and inosinates, a natural umami synergy. Because the rice is meloso, it can be stirred lightly, so the many ingredients can be added at different times.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — reconstructed, verify locally)
*   **Sevilla city ventas:** Chicken + pork + shrimp/*cigalas* + peas + piquillo. Meloso.
*   **Isla Mayor / marismas:** **Duck** or **cangrejo de río** (crayfish), the local products; see [arroz caldoso con pato](recipe_arroz_caldoso_con_pato_marismas.md).
*   **Sierra Norte de Sevilla:** **Arroz campero** with pork, rabbit and garden vegetables, without seafood.
*   **Expo-'92-era restaurant style:** With **chorizo**, fish and shellfish. A tourist-menu mixed rice.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Sevilla venta "arroz con cosas"**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, chopped | 500 g |
| Pork loin/ribs | 250 g |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Onion | 1 |
| Green + red pepper | 1 each |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| White wine (or fino) | 100 ml |
| Chicken or fish stock | 1.6 L |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:** Brown the meats (**12 min**). Add the onion and peppers (10 min), the garlic and pimentón, then the tomato (5 min). Add the wine and reduce.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 350 g |
| Stock | 1.4–1.5 L (≈ 4:1) |
| Shrimp/cigalas | 8–12 |
| Mussels | 300 g |
| Peas | 100 g |
| Roasted red pepper strips | 1 pepper |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toasting:** Add the rice to the fondo for 1 min.
2. **Liquid:** Pour in the boiling stock and saffron.
3. **Fire:** Strong for 8 min → medium for 8 min. Add the mussels and peas at min 10 and the shrimp at min 13.
4. **Finish:** Lay the red-pepper strips on top. Rest **3 min**.
