# Nam Phrik Num / น้ำพริกหนุ่ม

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** no source documenting the dish's origin was found; one encyclopaedia mirror calls it Lao, most sources call it northern Thai (Lanna).

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna, above all Chiang Mai (Kad Luang/Warorot market); also Laos.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a coarse, smoky green-chilli relish of roasted young chillies, shallot and garlic pounded with salt and fish sauce; eaten with sticky rice, steamed vegetables and crisp pork rind (*khaep mu*).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Embers or a charcoal grill for roasting, a stone mortar (*khrok*), sticky rice from the *huad* kept in a woven *kong khao* / *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Phrik num* means "young man" chilli, the long, mild-to-hot green chilli of the north. Reference works describe nam phrik num as a northern Thai dip of roasted green chillies, shallots, garlic and coriander, sometimes with fish sauce and lime, served with sticky rice, vegetables and pork cracklings ([Wikipedia – Nam phrik num](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_phrik_num); [Wikipedia – Nam phrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_phrik)). Chiang Mai's northern-food shops sell it as a set with [nam phrik ong](recipe_nam_phrik_ong.md), boiled vegetables and khaep mu, and the Kad Luang stalls pair it with [sai ua](recipe_sai_ua.md) ([Wongnai – Chiang Mai northern food](https://www.wongnai.com/listings/northern-food-cm)). Lanna food more broadly is built on wild and forest produce ([TruePlookpanya](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/82522/-blog-blo-foocui-foo-)).

**Culinary science:** roasting chillies whole over embers softens the flesh, chars the skin for smokiness and mellows capsaicin's sharpness; pounding (not blending) keeps a coarse texture that clings to sticky rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Chiang Mai market style:** coarse, with fish sauce, sold by weight ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/listings/northern-food-cm)).
*   **With roasted eggplant:** some recipes pound in long green eggplant ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_phrik_num)).
*   **Lao version:** ⚠️ described as Lao in one mirror; not further documented.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Chiang Mai market nam phrik num.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Phrik num (long green chillies) | 250 g |
| Shallots | 6 |
| Garlic | 1 head |
| Salt / fish sauce | ½ tsp / 1 tbsp |
| Coriander | 1 small handful |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Steamed vegetables and khaep mu | 1 plate each |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Roast:** grill the chillies, shallots and garlic over embers until blistered and soft; peel.
3. **Pound:** pound garlic and shallot with salt, add the chillies and pound coarsely; season with fish sauce and coriander.
4. **Serve:** with sticky rice, steamed vegetables and crisp pork rind.
