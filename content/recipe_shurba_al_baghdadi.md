# Shurba (Medieval Rice Soup with Lamb and Chickpeas) / شوربا

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The recipe text is attested in al-Baghdadi's *Kitab al-Tabikh* (Baghdad, 1226), as quoted from A. J. Arberry's translation in Cariadoc's medieval Islamic recipe collection, together with a modern worked version. It is an **Abbasid Iraqi** text, not a Syrian one. It is included here because the *shurba* family of meat-and-rice soups was shared across the medieval Arab Near East, and in 1756 Alexander Russell recorded that Aleppine meals began with "a sort of broth, or soup". The quantities below are reduced from that worked version.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Abbasid Baghdad (source). The *shurba* tradition was common to the medieval and Ottoman Near East, including Aleppo and Damascus.
*   **Historical Source:** *Kitab al-Tabikh*, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Baghdadi, 1226, Baghdad (tr. A. J. Arberry, 1939)
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice soup. Loose, soupy rice in a lamb broth fragrant with cinnamon, coriander and dill, with whole chickpeas and optional little meatballs.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep pot (*qidr*) over a wood or charcoal fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Al-Baghdadi's recipe, in Arberry's translation: cut fat meat into middling pieces, melt fresh tail fat and discard the sediment, and brown the meat in it. Cover with lukewarm water and add salt, "a handful of peeled chickpeas", cinnamon bark and sprigs of dry dill. When the meat is cooked, add ground coriander, ginger and pepper, and more water. Boil hard, remove the dill, then add rice "washed several times", cooking until done but not so long "that the rice becomes hard set". Optionally add "some cabobs of minced meat".

*Shurba* (also *shorba*, Turkish *çorba*) became the standard opening course of Ottoman meals. Russell's *Natural History of Aleppo* (1756) describes Aleppine dinners that open with broth or soup and end with pilaw. Today's Levantine *shorbat ruz* and the Ramadan *shorba* descend from this family.

**Culinary logic:** the rice goes in last, into fast-boiling broth, so it thickens the soup without turning to glue. That's the reason for the medieval warning against leaving it until "hard set".

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Baghdad, 1226 (source):** Tail fat, chickpeas, cinnamon, dill, coriander, ginger, pepper, optional meatballs.
*   **Ottoman Aleppo (Russell, 1756):** Soup is the first course and pilaw the last. Russell gives no recipe.
*   **Modern Levant:** Rice soup with lamb or chicken, often with vermicelli, served at the Ramadan *iftar*.
*   **Spanish echo:** The soupy rices of Spain, such as [arròs amb fesols i naps](recipe_arroz_con_fesols_i_naps.md), share the logic of meat broth and late-added rice. This is a parallel, not proven descent.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern adaptation; reduced from the worked version in Cariadoc's collection.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Boneless lamb, middling pieces | 700 g |
| Rendered lamb fat (or ghee) | 2 tbsp |
| Cooked chickpeas | 250 g |
| Cinnamon sticks | 2 |
| Dried dill, tied in muslin | 2 tsp |
| Ground coriander | 1 tsp |
| Ground ginger | ¼ tsp |
| Black pepper | ½ tsp |
| Water | 1 L + 2 L |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice, washed several times | 250 g |
| Broth at rice-in | ≈ 2.5 L (a soup) |
| Optional meatballs: minced lamb + cinnamon, ginger, coriander | 250 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Brown:** Render the fat and brown the lamb (and the meatballs, if using) for **5 min**.
2. **Simmer:** Cover with 1 L of water and add the salt, chickpeas, cinnamon and dill. Simmer until the meat is tender (**45–60 min**).
3. **Season:** Add the coriander, ginger, pepper and the remaining 2 L of water. Bring to a full boil and remove the dill.
4. **Rice:** Add the rice, return to the boil, then simmer covered for about **20 min**, stirring now and then. Stop before the rice sets.
