# Arroz con Hígado de Toro / Arròs amb Fetge de Bou

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented only in the *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria*'s 2022 survey of **forgotten dishes** of the Valencian Community (photo credited to writer and documentarian Eduardo Boix López). The source **gives no town or comarca**. The geographic attribution and the variations below are my inference: offal of fighting bulls was traditionally distributed after *bous al carrer* and bullfight festivities. Treat them as hypotheses to check locally.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Not specified by the source. Probably bull-festival towns of the Valencian Community (⚠️ inferred).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). The source states the rice "has to end up with a creamy consistency".
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A paella (the source cooks it in a paella).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
This is an offal rice: **liver, sweetbread (*molleja*), heart and *entrañas*** of a bull, stretched with **chickpeas** and a large amount of **escarole**. The RENC places it among the Valencian Community's "forgotten dishes or dishes of occasional consumption". It belongs to the *aprovechamiento* (nothing-wasted) culture that also produced [arròs al forn](recipe_arroz_al_horno.md) and [arroz con pata de ternera](recipe_arroz_con_pata_de_ternera.md).

**Culinary science:** liver toughens and turns grainy when overcooked, because its proteins set at relatively low temperatures. The source fries the offal briefly with **guindilla** (chilli) at the start, then lets it finish gently in the broth. Escarole contributes bitterness to cut through the iron-rich offal, and it wilts down drastically, so it is added "in large quantity".

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **The RENC version:** Liver + sweetbread + heart + entrañas, guindilla, escarole, chickpeas, pimentón, saffron. Broth ≈ **2× the rice**. Meloso.
*   **Other towns:** ⚠️ No documented variants found. Plausible substitutions in modern kitchens include beef or pork liver, but these are not attested.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **RENC 2022** (quantities as published).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Bull liver | 100 g |
| Sweetbread (*molleja*) | 100 g |
| Heart | 100 g |
| Entrañas | 70 g |
| Olive oil | 60 ml |
| Guindillas (dried chillies) | 1–2 |
| Garlic | 1–2 cloves |
| Tomato, grated | 100 g |
| Pimentón | 1 tbsp |
| Escarole, chopped | 1–2 heads |
| Cooked chickpeas | 400 g |
| Saffron (ground) | pinch |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice | 400 g |
| Water | ≈ 2× the rice volume (≈ 0.8–1 L) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Fry:** Heat the oil in the paella. Add the offal in medium pieces with the guindilla. Fry, the liver last, then add a little salt.
2. **Sofrito:** Add the garlic, then the tomato and pimentón at once.
3. **Escarole:** Add a lot of it; it will reduce sharply.
4. **Broth:** Add the water (≈ 2× the rice), the ground saffron and the chickpeas. Boil **15 min**.
5. **Rice:** Distribute it evenly. Boil hard for the first minutes, then lower the heat until done, aiming for a **meloso** finish.
6. **Finish:** Serve immediately.
