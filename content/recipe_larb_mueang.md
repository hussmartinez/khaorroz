# Larb Mueang / ลาบเมือง (Larb Khua / ลาบคั่ว)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the contrast with Isan larb is well documented, but the exact composition of the *phrik larb* spice mix (including *makhwaen* pepper) was not found in a single quantified source; the paste below is a reconstruction from the cited descriptions.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna: Chiang Mai, Lamphun, Lampang (famed for larb noodle shops), Nan (larb mu khua), Phrae.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a deep-red, dry, warmly spiced minced-meat dish, often "stir-dried" (*khua*) in the pan with herbs; no lime. Eaten with sticky rice, bitter herbs and crisp pork rind.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Cleaver and wooden block (the rhythmic chopping is part of the ritual), wok (*kratha*) over charcoal for *larb khua*, and sticky rice from the *huad* kept in a woven *kong khao* / *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
"Larb mueang" means the larb of the *khon mueang*, the northern Thai people. Thai food media stress that it is "the same name but a different world" from Isan larb: northern larb uses a dry chilli-and-spice paste (*phrik larb*) and is often cooked (*khua*), while Isan larb relies on lime, fish sauce and toasted rice ([Sanook](https://sanook.com/news/9908290)). Nan is known for *larb mu khua*, pork larb stir-dried with herbs, and Lampang for noodles dressed with *nam phrik larb* ([Sanook](https://sanook.com/news/9908290)). Raw versions with blood and offal exist (*larb dip*), as do buffalo larbs with bitter bile ([Thai Food Master – Northern larbs](https://thaifoodmaster.com/thai_food/thai_food_recipes-th/laab_recipes-th?lang=th); [Thai Food Master – Lanna catfish larb](https://thaifoodmaster.com/origin/northern_thai)).

Larb is the festive dish of the north: men chop the meat together for weddings, house-warmings and New Year, and it is eaten with [sticky rice](recipe_khao_niao_nueng.md).

**Culinary science:** toasting dry spices (long pepper, makhwaen, cumin-like seeds) before pounding releases their oils; cooking the dressed mince briefly in its own fat (*khua*) blooms the paste and dries the dish so it can be pinched up with sticky rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Chiang Mai:** pork larb khua with crisp fried shallot, garlic and chilli; side plate of bitter and astringent herbs.
*   **Nan:** *larb mu khua*, cooked longer with herbs ([Sanook](https://sanook.com/news/9908290)).
*   **Lampang:** larb as a noodle seasoning, *kuai tiao larb* ([Sanook](https://sanook.com/news/9908290)).
*   **Buffalo and catfish larbs:** Lanna-wide variants ([Thai Food Master](https://thaifoodmaster.com/origin/northern_thai)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Chiang Mai cooked pork larb khua (reconstructed spice paste).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Minced pork (with some fat) | 400 g |
| Dried chillies, toasted | 8–10 |
| Makhwaen (Zanthoxylum) seeds, toasted | 1 tsp |
| Long pepper (*di plee*), toasted | 1 |
| Garlic and shallots, roasted | 4 cloves / 3 |
| Shrimp paste or pla ra | 1 tsp |
| Fried garlic and shallot, spring onion, coriander, sawtooth coriander | to finish |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Phrik larb paste : meat | ≈ 2 tbsp : 400 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Paste:** pound the toasted chillies and spices to a powder, then pound in the roasted garlic, shallot and shrimp paste.
3. **Khua:** fry the paste briefly in a dry wok with a little pork fat, add the pork and stir-dry over medium heat until cooked and almost dry (5–6 min).
4. **Finish:** toss with herbs, fried garlic and shallot; serve with sticky rice, bitter herbs and crisp pork rind.
