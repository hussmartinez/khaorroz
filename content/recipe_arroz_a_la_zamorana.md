# Arroz a la Zamorana / Arroz Zamorano

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Castilla y León](provincial_castilla_y_leon.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Province of Zamora: Aliste (Alcañices), Tierra de Campos (Villalpando paprika), and Zamora city.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). Cooked with about twice the broth of the rice; the grain keeps some bite. The official Junta version is finished under a grill with crisped bacon.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide clay *cazuela* or paella over a wood or gas fire. The official version is finished under a very hot grill.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Arroz a la zamorana is Castilla y León's great rice and a child of the **matanza** (pig slaughter). Rural families in the Aliste comarca used every part of the pig. The noble cuts (ham, belly, chorizo, ribs) went in alongside cheaper ones (**ear, snout, tail, trotter**), which made the rice more filling. According to EsDiario, the earliest written reference is an **1884 *Almanaque culinario*** describing a rice served in **Alcañices**, and the dish appears in ***El Practicón* (1890)**. Directo al Paladar groups it with the *chanfaina salmantina* as one of the region's rices with offal or *casquería*; see [chanfaina](recipe_chanfaina_salmantina.md).

**Culinary science:** ear, snout and trotter are rich in collagen. Simmering them first produces a gelatinous broth, which gives the rice its creamy body without stirring. Lard (*manteca*) rather than olive oil is the traditional fat. **Sweet paprika from Villalpando** is added to the hot fat for seconds only, so its carotenoids colour the fat without burning. In the Junta's version, the grill crisps the bacon covering into *torreznos* over the rice, which acts as a lid and seasoning together.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Official Junta / Zamora Style (Turismo Castilla y León):** Ear + snout simmered with turnip, onion, garlic, parsley, oregano and thyme in lard. Rice sautéed with *chichas* (seasoned minced pork) and ham, Villalpando paprika, then covered with **thin bacon slices crisped under the grill**.
*   **Aliste / "recipe of the grandfather" Style (EsDiario):** A broth of belly, ear, tail, trotter, ham tip and ribs with bay leaf. Tomato sofrito, sliced chorizo. Cooked in the pan for **18–20 min**, then rested 3–4 min. Optional green or red pepper and rosemary.
*   **Restaurant Style (Directo al Paladar):** No offal. Belly, chorizo *picadillo*, ham cubes, fresh sausages and chorizo with red pepper, thyme, rosemary and oregano. Twice the broth of the rice, in the cazuela.
*   **La Rúa, Zamora (restaurant tradition):** Served with **chorizo, ribs and ear**.

| Variable | Junta / Zamora | Aliste (EsDiario) | Restaurant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offal | Ear + snout | Ear, tail, trotter | None |
| Fat | Lard | Olive oil | Lard + oil |
| Finish | Bacon under the grill | Rest 3–4 min | Served in the cazuela |
| Herbs | Parsley, oregano, thyme | Bay, rosemary | Thyme, rosemary, oregano, bay |

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Junta de Castilla y León official recipe** (500 g of rice, so generous for 4), with the EsDiario broth method.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pig's ear + pig's snout, cleaned, in small pieces | 1 + 1 |
| Onion | 1 |
| Turnip | 1 |
| Zamora garlic | 3 cloves |
| Parsley, oregano, thyme | 1 sprig / pinch / pinch |
| Lard (or oil) | 6 tbsp (≈ 80 g) |
| Water | ≈ 2 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 400–500 g |
| Pork *chichas* (seasoned minced pork) | 200 g |
| Ham, diced | 100 g |
| Sweet paprika (Villalpando) | 1 tsp |
| Broth at rice-in | ≈ 2 × rice volume (≈ 1–1.1 L) |
| Thin bacon slices to cover | ≈ 150 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Offal broth:** Melt half the lard in a pot and sauté the turnip, onion, garlic and herbs. Add the ear and snout, cover generously with water, and simmer until tender (**≈ 1.5 h**).
2. **Toasting:** In a paella pan with the rest of the lard, sauté the rice with the chichas and ham (3 min). Add the paprika (5 s).
3. **Liquid Addition:** Add the contents of the pot, including the meat and broth. Top up with hot water if needed.
4. **Fire:** Strong for 8 min, then medium for 8–10 min. It should stay moist.
5. **Grill finish:** When the rice is nearly done, take it off the heat and cover it with the bacon slices. Put it under a **very hot grill** until the bacon turns into crisp *torreznos* (3–5 min).
6. **Resting:** Rest 3 min and serve from the pan.
