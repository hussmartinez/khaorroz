# Kibbeh Arnabieh / كبة أرنبية

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish itself, its tahini-and-bitter-orange sauce and its pairing with rice are well attested in Lebanese food media. The origin of the name *arnabiyeh* ("hare-like") is not explained in any source found, and its town attribution (Beirut vs Tripoli) differs between sources. Quantities are a reconstruction from published home recipes.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Beirut (especially West Beirut Sunni households) and Tripoli; a Ramadan and winter dish of the bitter-orange season.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: small kibbeh balls simmered in a pale, tangy-nutty sauce of tahini and citrus juices (bitter Seville orange, mandarin, lemon), ladled over fluffy white or vermicelli rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide heavy pot (*tanjara*) on the stove; the rice in a separate lidded pot.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Kibbeh arnabieh* is one of Beirut's signature home dishes: kibbeh (bulgur-and-meat shells stuffed with minced meat, onion and pine nuts) poached in a sauce of tahini thinned with the juice of up to seven kinds of citrus. Seville oranges (*bousfeir*) are preferred, with mandarin, sweet orange and lemon to balance them; the sauce is enriched with meat broth, puréed onion and, in some homes, pomegranate molasses, and finished with toasted pine nuts ([Food Heritage Foundation – Lebanese kebbeh in all its shapes and tastes](https://food-heritage.org/lebanese-kebbeh-in-all-its-shapes-and-tastes/); [Taste & Flavors – Kibbeh arnabie](https://www.pressreader.com/lebanon/taste-flavors/20150601/282905204164857)). A French-language survey of kibbeh calls it a point of pride in Beirut and Tripoli ([Generation Voyage](https://generationvoyage.fr/kibbeh/)).

It is above all a **winter and Ramadan** dish, cooked when bitter oranges ripen. Al Jazeera's report on young mothers reviving their grandmothers' Ramadan dishes lists Beirut's *arnabiyeh* among them, "served with fluffy white rice" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.net/women/2019/5/31/أمهات-صغيرات-يستعدن-أكلات-الجدات)); recipe sites likewise serve it with vermicelli rice ([Whisk – كبة أرنبيّة](https://stc-my.whisk.com/recipes/1077770e078312e4e8090ae2061ab9439af)). The rice is not a garnish: the sauce is meant to be spooned over it. It belongs to the same Lebanese "kibbeh in sauce" family as [kibbeh labanieh](recipe_kibbeh_labanieh.md), where yogurt replaces the tahini-citrus sauce.

**Culinary science:** tahini seizes when acid is added and loosens again with liquid; cooks whisk the citrus and broth in gradually and keep the sauce below a hard boil so the sesame emulsion does not split.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Beirut (West Beirut households):** bitter orange dominant, puréed onion in the sauce, toasted pine nuts on top; served with plain white *mfalfal* rice ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.net/women/2019/5/31/أمهات-صغيرات-يستعدن-أكلات-الجدات)).
*   **Tripoli (⚠️):** cited as a centre of the dish; some cooks add pomegranate molasses and use more mandarin; town-specific recipes were not found.
*   **Home shortcut:** lemon juice replaces bitter orange outside the season ([Taste of Beirut feed – Kibbeh with citrus sauce](https://www.tasteofbeirut.com/kibbeh-saucers/feed/)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Beirut style, with vermicelli rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Small stuffed kibbeh balls (bulgur-meat shell, meat-onion-pine nut filling) | 24 (≈ 800 g) |
| Tahini | 200 g |
| Bitter (Seville) orange juice | 250 ml |
| Mandarin or sweet orange juice | 150 ml |
| Lemon juice | 50 ml |
| Meat broth | 400 ml |
| Onions, grated or puréed | 2 |
| Olive oil | 30 ml |
| Pine nuts, toasted | 40 g |
| Salt | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium- or long-grain rice | 300 g |
| Vermicelli (*sh'ariyyeh*) | 40 g |
| Water for the rice | 500 ml |
| Butter or ghee for the rice | 25 g |
| Sauce : rice | about 1 L of sauce for 300 g rice |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** toast the vermicelli golden in the butter, add the rinsed rice, stir 1 minute, add 500 ml boiling salted water, cover and cook on the lowest heat 18 minutes; rest 10 minutes.
2. **Onion base:** cook the puréed onion gently in the olive oil until soft and pale (10 minutes); add the broth.
3. **Sauce:** in a bowl, whisk the tahini with the citrus juices little by little until smooth and pourable; stir into the pot and bring just to a simmer, stirring.
4. **Kibbeh:** slide in the kibbeh balls (fried or raw) and simmer gently 15–20 minutes until cooked through and the sauce coats a spoon. Season.
5. **Serve:** rice in deep plates, kibbeh and sauce ladled over, pine nuts on top.
