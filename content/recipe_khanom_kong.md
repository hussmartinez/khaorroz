# Khanom Kong (Khanom Khai Pla) / ขนมกง (ขนมไข่ปลา)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the method is documented by a producers' group in Nong Kae (Uthai Thani) via GotoKnow, and the meaning in the southern Tenth-Month festival by PSU and Matichon. Quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand (Uthai Thani and the central plains) for weddings and Songkran; the south, as one of the five Sat Duean Sip offering sweets.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice-flour festival sweet: a wheel-shaped ring of sweetened mung-bean paste, dipped in a batter of toasted rice and glutinous-rice flours and deep-fried until crisp outside and soft within.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok of hot oil (*kratha*) over a charcoal or wood fire; a mortar for the mung-bean paste.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khanom kong is an old sweet made for the Thai New Year (*trut Songkran*) and for weddings, where it is one of the *khan mak* sweets carried in the betrothal procession. It is shaped into a ring like a cartwheel (*kong kwian*), a symbol of a stable life. The Nong Kae producers' group in Uthai Thani soaks split mung beans 30 min and steams them 10–15 min for the filling. The coating batter uses rice flour and glutinous rice flour that are **toasted first**, then mixed with water ([GotoKnow – khanom kong, Nong Kae](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/312942)). In the southern **Sat Duean Sip** (Tenth-Month) festival, khanom kong, also called *khanom khai pla*, is one of the five sweets offered to the ancestors and stands for **jewellery** ([PSU RC-WellCare](https://rc-wellcare.psu.ac.th/images/2025/File/news-2025-09.pdf); [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/region/news_2950763)).

**Culinary science:** toasting the rice flours denatures their proteins and dries the starch, so the batter fries crisper and absorbs less oil.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Nong Kae, Uthai Thani:** wheel-shaped, for Songkran and weddings ([GotoKnow](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/312942)).
*   **Southern Sat Duean Sip:** called *khanom khai pla*; offered with khanom la, phong, ba and dee sam ([PSU](https://rc-wellcare.psu.ac.th/images/2025/File/news-2025-09.pdf); [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/344435)). See [khanom la](recipe_khanom_la.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Split mung beans, soaked 30 min | 200 g |
| Palm sugar | 120 g |
| Grated coconut | 60 g |
| Salt | pinch |
| Rice flour (toasted) | 80 g |
| Glutinous rice flour (toasted) | 40 g |
| Water (batter) | ≈ 150 ml |
| Oil for frying | 1 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Filling per ring | 30 g |
| Rings | about 12 |
| Rice flours in the batter | 120 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Filling:** Steam the mung beans 10–15 min, pound smooth, then cook with the palm sugar, coconut and salt until a stiff paste forms.
2. **Shape:** Roll into thin ropes and form small cartwheel rings.
3. **Batter:** Toast the rice flours in a dry wok until fragrant, cool, then whisk with water to a coating batter.
4. **Fry:** Dip the rings and fry in hot oil until golden and crisp. Drain and cool.
