# Khantok / ขันโตก

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the "khantok dinner" as a hosted tourist event is a modern institution; the dish set below is the commonly documented one, not a fixed historical menu.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna, above all Chiang Mai.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a round, footed tray (*khantok*) of several northern dishes — larb, *kaeng hang le*, *nam phrik ong*, *nam phrik num*, *sai ua*, crisp pork rind, boiled vegetables — shared around sticky rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A low round lacquered or teak tray on a pedestal; sticky rice steamed in a *huad* and served in woven *kong khao* / *kratip* baskets.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The khantok is the low round tray at which northern families and guests eat; on it pork, chicken, beef and fish dishes are the core and *sai ua* almost always appears ([Michelin Guide – Khantok dining in the north](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/on-the-culinary-trail-khan-tok-dining-in-the-north)). Silpa-Wattanatham traces the "khantok dinner" as a hospitality ritual for guests and describes trays by size: *khantok noi*, *khantok ham* and *khantok luang* ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=152854)). The northern sticky-rice basket, *kong khao*, has a base, body and lid of woven bamboo ([TruePlookpanya](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/60010)).

**Culinary science:** sticky rice, pinched into balls, acts as the spoon; dry relishes (*nam phrik*) and rich curries balance one another, and bitter or raw vegetables cleanse the palate between bites.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Family khantok:** everyday shared meal, smaller *khantok noi* ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=152854)).
*   **Khantok dinner (Chiang Mai):** hosted feast with dance ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=152854)).
*   **Isan equivalent:** the *pha khao* tray around a *kratip* of sticky rice. ⚠️ Not documented in detail here.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: a home khantok built from recipes in this atlas.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| [Larb mueang](recipe_larb_mueang.md) | 1 portion (400 g meat) |
| [Kaeng hang le](recipe_kaeng_hang_le.md) | ½ portion |
| [Nam phrik ong](recipe_nam_phrik_ong.md) and [nam phrik num](recipe_nam_phrik_num.md) | 1 bowl each |
| [Sai ua](recipe_sai_ua.md) | 300 g |
| Crisp pork rind, boiled and raw vegetables | 1 plate each |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 400 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 400 g raw (≈ 100 g per person) |
| Dishes on the tray | 5–7 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Plan:** cook the relishes and curry first (they keep), the larb and sausage last.
2. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
3. **Arrange:** set small bowls on the khantok with the vegetables and pork rind in the centre.
4. **Serve:** each diner takes sticky rice from the basket and eats by hand.
