# Arroz Marinero Andaluz / Arroz Caldoso Marinero (Málaga y Cádiz)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Andalucía](provincial_andalucia.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Málaga's gastronomy guide documents a "soupy seafood rice" as the city's rice, but I found no canonical town-specific recipe. The town variations below are reconstructed from common coastal practice. Verify them locally.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Andalusian Mediterranean and Atlantic coast: Málaga (El Palo, Pedregalejo), the Costa del Sol, the Bahía de Cádiz and Sanlúcar.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep *perol*, *cazuela* or *caldero* over gas, or over a fire on the beach.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Andalucía, rice by the sea is mostly eaten **soupy**: a fishermen's pot of rice, fish and shellfish built on a broth. In Málaga this "arroz caldoso marinero" grew out of the *jábega* boats and the beach *merenderos* of El Palo and Pedregalejo, the same world as the *espetos*. The Málaga Gastronomy Guide describes it as the soupy version of Málaga rice, shaped by a seafaring city with a varied catch. Further west, in Cádiz and Sanlúcar, the same logic produces rices with *galeras*, *chirlas* (small clams), *langostinos* and cuttlefish, often with a dash of manzanilla or fino in the base.

**Culinary science:** with a broth ratio of about 5–6:1, the grains cook submerged, so they swell evenly without forming a crust. Released starch thickens the liquid only slightly. Shellfish such as clams and mussels open in the last minutes and contribute their briny liquor directly.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — reconstructed, verify locally)
*   **Málaga (El Palo / Pedregalejo):** Rape (monkfish), *almejas*, *mejillones*, squid. Onion + green pepper + tomato *sofrito*. Often a little *guindilla*.
*   **Cádiz (Bahía):** *Chocos* (cuttlefish), *galeras*, langostinos. A splash of **fino**. Plenty of parsley.
*   **Sanlúcar de Barrameda:** **Langostino de Sanlúcar** + *manzanilla* in the base. Very loose broth.
*   **Costa del Sol interior villages:** "Arroz campero"-style mixes of seafood and pork. Not strictly marinero.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Málaga caldoso**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Fish fumet (rockfish, heads) | 2 L |
| Olive oil | 70 ml |
| Onion, finely chopped | 1 (150 g) |
| Green pepper | 1 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| Guindilla (optional) | 1 |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:** Sweat the onion and pepper for **10 min**. Add the garlic and pimentón (5 s), then the tomato (6 min).

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short/round rice | 320 g |
| Fumet | 1.8–2 L (≈ 6:1) |
| Monkfish, cubed | 300 g |
| Squid, rings | 200 g |
| Clams + mussels | 500 g |
| Peeled prawns | 12 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Purge the clams in salted water for 1 h. Scrub the mussels.
2. **Sear:** Sear the squid in the pot for 3 min, then build the sofrito on top.
3. **Toasting:** Add the rice and stir for 1 min.
4. **Liquid:** Add the boiling fumet and saffron. Simmer **12 min**.
5. **Seafood:** Add the monkfish, clams and mussels and cook **4 min**. Add the prawns for the last **2 min**.
6. **Finish:** Serve **immediately**, without resting, with chopped parsley.
