# Colas de Cordero con Arroz / Guiso de Colas de Viernes Santo

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Aragón, Navarra & La Rioja](provincial_aragon_navarra_rioja.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** this is a **forgotten dish** documented in the *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria* supplement on Aragón's forgotten dishes. There, the lamb-tail stew is made with potatoes and "rice can also be added" as a variant. The rice-forward version and the quantities below are my blueprint. Town variations beyond the documented Good Friday custom are not sourced.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Sheep-farming Aragón: the Pyrenean valleys, Bajo Aragón, the Monegros steppe, and Zaragoza (where the chef Teodoro Bardají served it).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** An iron or clay pot over a wood fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
On **Good Friday**, shepherds *escodaban*, docking the tails of the ewes kept for breeding. The tails became a stew. According to the RENC account, it was said that those who docked the tails had licence to eat them **even on a day of fasting** (*vigilia*), and that it was "the only meat eaten from a living animal". The celebrated Aragonese cook **Teodoro Bardají**, at the Hotel Oriente in Zaragoza, reportedly served it to a famous vedette from the nearby Teatro Principal, who returned for it whenever she was in town. The stew is spiced with **cinnamon**, an old Aragonese and Andalusi-inflected seasoning, and rice can be added.

**Culinary science:** lamb tails are mostly fat, skin and collagen around small vertebrae. **Singeing** (*socarrar*) the wool, then frying the tails until golden, renders fat and creates Maillard flavour. The collagen gives a gelatinous broth that suits rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Documented base (RENC):** Potatoes cooked with salt, **cinnamon**, parsley, garlic and bay. Tails singed, fried and added. Rice is optional.
*   **Zaragoza (Bardají, Hotel Oriente):** A refined restaurant version of the shepherd stew.
*   *Further town variants are not documented; verify with Aragonese pastoral communities.*

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **RENC base with rice added**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb tails, singed, cleaned | 1.2 kg |
| Olive oil (for frying) | 100 ml |
| Potatoes, in chunks | 500 g |
| Garlic | 4 cloves |
| Bay leaf | 2 |
| Ground cinnamon | 1 g (pinch) |
| Parsley | 1 small bunch |
| Water | 2.5 L |
| Salt | to taste |

**Extraction:** Fry the tails until golden (**12 min**). Separately, simmer the potatoes in the water with the garlic, bay, cinnamon, parsley and salt. Add the fried tails and simmer **60–75 min** until tender.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 250 g |
| Broth in the pot | ≈ 1.8 L |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Add the rice to the simmering stew for the last **18 min**.
2. **Finish:** Rest 2 min. Serve with a pinch of cinnamon.
