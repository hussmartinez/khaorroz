# Yakhnet al-Kamaʾ (Desert Truffle Stew with Rice) / يخنة الكمأة

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented by Aramco World (John Monroe, "Meats and Sweets of Syria", 1975) as a Damascene restaurant dish of the season, "eaten with boiled rice". No quantities are given there; the master recipe below is reconstructed from that description. Household variants in the Syrian steppe (Badia) are not documented online.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Damascus, using desert truffles (*kamaʾ*) from the Syrian steppe near Palmyra (Tadmur); a late-winter and spring dish.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: peeled desert truffles and cubes of fried meat simmered with onion and lemon juice into a light, savoury stew, spooned over plain boiled rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot on the stove; a separate pot for boiled rice.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
After good winter rains, the Syrian desert produces **desert truffles** (*kamaʾ*, *Terfezia* and *Tirmania*), gathered by Bedouin and sold in city markets as a seasonal luxury. In 1975 Aramco World described how in Damascus they were "soaked thoroughly to remove sand, then peeled", added to **fried meat cubes with onions** and simmered with **lemon juice**, "before being eaten with boiled rice". The writer advised ordering the dish a day ahead at the Orient Club in Damascus ([Aramco World – Meats and Sweets of Syria, 1975](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/197506/meats.and.sweets.of.syria.htm)).

The dish is a rare survival of the urban taste for steppe produce, and it belongs to the family of Syrian *yakhneh* eaten over rice. The truffle season is short and the harvest depends on rain, so it has always been an occasional dish.

**Culinary science:** desert truffles are milder and more earthy than European black truffles. Their delicate aroma survives gentle simmering but not a hard boil. Lemon brightens the stew and offsets the richness of the meat; plain rice, unspiced, lets the truffle flavour lead.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascus, restaurant (documented):** Meat cubes and onion fried, truffles added, simmered with lemon juice; boiled rice.
*   **Steppe and Bedouin practice:** ⚠️ not documented online. Truffles are widely reported to be roasted in embers or cooked with rice and meat across the Syrian and Iraqi desert, but no source was found.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Damascene truffle stew** after Aramco World, quantities reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Desert truffles (*kamaʾ*) | 500 g |
| Lamb (shoulder), small cubes | 400 g |
| Onion, chopped | 1 large |
| Ghee or olive oil | 3 tbsp |
| Lemon juice | 3 tbsp |
| Water or light lamb stock | 400 ml |
| Salt, black pepper | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice, washed | 320 g |
| Water for the rice | ≈ 480 ml |
| Butter or ghee for the rice | 1 tbsp |
| Salt | ½ tsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Clean the truffles:** Soak in several changes of water, brushing off all sand; peel and cut into walnut-sized pieces.
2. **Fry the meat:** Brown the lamb cubes in ghee, then add the onion and cook until soft.
3. **Simmer:** Add the water or stock, season and simmer covered about 40 min until the meat is nearly tender.
4. **Add truffles and lemon:** Add the truffle pieces and lemon juice; simmer gently 15 min more without boiling hard.
5. **Rice:** Boil the rice with water, butter and salt, then cover and cook on very low heat 15–18 min; rest 5 min.
6. **Serve:** Spoon the stew over the plain rice.
