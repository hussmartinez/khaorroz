# Kaeng Phet Pet Yang / แกงเผ็ดเป็ดย่าง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The Chinese-Thai origin rests mainly on David Thompson's account; no academic history was found.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok (Chinatown/Yaowarat influence) and Central Thai celebratory tables.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: red curry made with Chinese-style roast duck, cherry tomatoes and fruit (pineapple, lychee or grapes), a rich festive curry over jasmine rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok or pot for the curry; the roast duck is bought from a Chinatown roaster.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
David Thompson writes that as more Chinese settled in Bangkok, Thai cooks borrowed their red-roasting techniques and used the roast duck sold in Chinatown shophouses in their own curries ([*Classic Thai Cuisine* via ckbk](https://app.ckbk.com/recipe/clas55631c02s001ss002r001/roast-duck-curry)). The result pairs a Thai red curry with a Cantonese/Teochew roast bird. Sources describe it as a dish for special occasions such as weddings and formal ceremonies rather than everyday shop food ([Thaiways](https://mail.thaiwaysmagazine.com/thailand/thai-food/kaeng-phet-pet-yang.html); [Window on Phuket](https://www.windowonphuket.com/dining/1630/gaeng-phed-ped-yang)).

It is a cousin of the plain [kaeng phet](recipe_kaeng_phet.md) and is always eaten with rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **With pineapple:** the most common fruit, adding acidity.
*   **With lychee or rambutan:** a sweeter, more luxurious version for banquets.
*   **With grapes:** a modern variant found in restaurant recipes (⚠️).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Chinese roast duck, boned and sliced | ½ duck (≈ 600 g) |
| Red curry paste | 50 g |
| Coconut milk | 600 ml |
| Pineapple chunks or peeled lychees | 150 g |
| Cherry tomatoes | 8 |
| Thai eggplant | 100 g |
| Fish sauce, palm sugar | 2 tbsp, 1 tsp |
| Makrut lime leaves, Thai basil, red chili | 4, 1 handful, 2 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) | 400 g, rinsed (+ 520 ml water, absorption method) |
| Curry per plate | ≈ 220 ml with 3–4 duck slices |
| Rice per plate | ≈ 180 g cooked |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Fry the coconut cream until it splits; fry the paste 4 min.
2. Add the rest of the coconut milk and bring to a simmer.
3. Add eggplant, then the duck, fruit and tomatoes; simmer only 3–4 min so the duck stays tender.
4. Season with fish sauce and sugar; add lime leaves, basil and chili.
5. Cook the rice: rinse 400 g jasmine rice until the water runs nearly clear, add 520 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 min, then rest 10 min covered (or use an electric rice cooker / *mo hung khao*).
6. Serve with rice.
