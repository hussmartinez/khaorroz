# Arroz con Conejo / Arròs amb Conill

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md) · [Región de Murcia](provincial_murcia.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** this is a **distinct dish** from [arroz con conejo y caracoles](recipe_arroz_conejo_y_caracoles.md): local cooks treat it as its own everyday rice, not a reduced version of the snail rice. However, published recipes rarely name it on its own, and most print sources fold it into the snail version. The structural differences in §2 reflect cooking practice in the Alicante interior, the Vega Baja and Murcia. The town variations are partly reconstructed. Verify them with local cooks.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Alicante interior (Vinalopó, Alcoià), La Vega Baja del Segura, the Región de Murcia, and inland Valencia.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). The everyday paella form. A *caldós* version with chicken also exists in Valencia (see §3).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A thin steel paella over wood (sarmiento, olive or pine) or gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The rabbit was the countryside's most accessible meat: bred in every farmyard (*conejo de corral*) or hunted in the *monte*. Snails, by contrast, are **seasonal and laborious**. They must be gathered after rain, purged for days and blanched, so they belong to the festive or special-occasion rice. **Arroz con conejo** is therefore the everyday and weekday rice. Because it lacks the snails' herbal, earthy broth (the snails have usually fed on thyme), it builds its flavour in other ways:

*   **Longer, harder browning of the rabbit**, including its **liver**, which is often fried and pounded into a final *picada*.
*   **More pepper:** strips of **red pepper** fried and laid on top, and/or **ñora** pounded with garlic. Murcian and Vega Baja cooks often add **guindilla** for a *picante* version.
*   **A herb added directly:** a sprig of **thyme or rosemary** in the broth replaces the herbal note that thyme-fed snails would bring.

A Generalitat Valenciana tourism page documents the Valencian sister dish, **arròs caldós amb pollastre i conill** (chicken and rabbit with bajoqueta, garrofó, pebre roig and rosemary, built on a sofregit with **no stock**). In Murcia the same "rabbit + peppers" pairing is called the most widespread form of rabbit stews, with snails added only in certain areas.

**Culinary science:** without snail gelatin and minerals, the broth is lighter. That is why rabbit-only rices rely on **Maillard browning** (20–25 min) and on **ñora or pimentón carotenoids** dissolved in the oil for depth. The pounded liver picada, a Valencian and Murcian old-school trick, thickens the broth slightly and adds an iron-rich savoury note in the final minutes.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Alicante interior (Vinalopó, Castalla, Pinoso) — a la leña:** Rabbit, tomato, red pepper strips, saffron, and a thyme or rosemary sprig. Thin layer, socarrat, wood fire. When the snails come in, it becomes [conejo y caracoles](recipe_arroz_conejo_y_caracoles.md).
*   **Vega Baja / Murcia — conejo con pimientos:** Calasparra rice, generous red pepper, ñora picada. The *picante* variant adds guindilla and a mortar paste of ñora, garlic and chilli stirred in at the end, sometimes finished in the oven.
*   **Valencia (Torrent, L'Horta Sud) — arròs caldós amb pollastre i conill:** Chicken + rabbit + bajoqueta + garrofó, rosemary, **soupy**, no stock, just a sofregit and water.
*   **Castellón and Catalan uplands:** Rabbit + wild mushrooms; see [arroz de montaña](recipe_arroz_de_montana_castellon.md).

| Variable | Arroz con conejo | Arroz con conejo y caracoles |
|---|---|---|
| Occasion | Everyday / weekday | Festive, seasonal (after rains) |
| Prep time | ≈ 1 h | Snails need **3–5 days** of purging |
| Flavour driver | Long browning, peppers, liver picada | Snail broth + thyme |
| Herb | Added sprig | Often carried by the snails |

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Alicante interior / Vega Baja seco**, with the optional liver picada.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rabbit, jointed small, **liver reserved** | 1 (≈ 1.1–1.2 kg) |
| Olive oil | 100 ml |
| Red pepper, wide strips | 1 |
| Garlic | 4 cloves |
| Ñora, seeded (or 4 g pimentón dulce) | 1 |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Thyme or rosemary | 1 sprig |
| Saffron | 0.3 g |
| Water | 1.3 L |
| Guindilla (picante version) | 1 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice (Senia/Bahía, or Calasparra in Murcia/Vega Baja) | 350 g |
| Broth at rice-in | ≈ 1.05 L (≈ 3 : 1, thin layer) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Pepper and ñora:** Fry the pepper strips and set them aside. Fry the ñora for 10 s and set it aside.
2. **Browning:** Brown the rabbit slowly with salt for **20–25 min**. Fry the liver for 2 min and remove it.
3. **Picada:** Pound the ñora, 2 garlic cloves, the liver (and the guindilla, if using) with a splash of water.
4. **Sofrito:** Add 2 sliced garlic cloves, then the tomato, and cook until jammy (6 min).
5. **Broth:** Add the water, saffron and herb sprig. Simmer **20 min** and remove the herb.
6. **Rice:** Scatter and level. High heat for 8 min, then stir in the **picada** and lay the pepper strips on top. Medium-low for 8 min, then a brief high flame for socarrat.
7. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Rest 4 min.
