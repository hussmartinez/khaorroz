# Khao Pong (Sun-Dried Sticky-Rice Crackers) / ข้าวโป่ง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented in a Thai academic study on promoting local know-how in khao pong production, based on village housewives' and elders' groups. Only its abstract and partial text were readable, so quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan village households (and parts of the north), made after the harvest.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** A sun-dried rice cracker. Steamed sticky rice is pounded with sugar, pressed into thin oiled sheets and sun-dried; heated over embers, each sheet puffs into a light, crackly, faintly sweet disc.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A *huad* steamer, a wooden mortar, banana leaves or mats for pressing, sun-drying racks, and a charcoal grill for puffing.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khao pong* is a household snack. Sticky rice is steamed, pounded, mixed with sugar and other ingredients, pressed into thin sheets, oiled so they don't stick and dried in the sun. It is reheated just before eating ([TCI – local wisdom in khao pong production](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/var/article/view/259197)). It belongs to the same family of "rice turned into keeping-food" as Lampang's [khao taen](recipe_khao_taen.md) and Laplae's rice papers, and it differs from [khao jee](recipe_khao_jee.md), which is grilled fresh from leftover rice.

**Culinary science:** pounding gelatinised rice traps air and moisture in a starch network. Drying fixes the sheet; on reheating, the remaining water flashes to steam and puffs the glassy starch into a crisp, porous wafer.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Village production groups:** Sweetened sheets, sometimes with sesame (⚠️ the extras are not specified in the readable text) ([TCI](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/var/article/view/259197)).
*   **Grilled vs fried:** Grilling over embers is traditional; ⚠️ frying is a modern shortcut.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: village sweet khao pong (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice, soaked overnight | 500 g |
| Sugar (or palm sugar) | 60 g |
| Salt | ½ tsp |
| Vegetable oil (for the sheets) | 2 tbsp |
| Black sesame (optional) | 2 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sheets | ≈ 12, 15 cm across, 3 mm thick |
| Sun-drying | 1–2 days |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Steam:** Steam the rice until tender (see [khao niao nueng](recipe_khao_niao_nueng.md)).
2. **Pound:** While hot, pound with the sugar, salt and sesame to a smooth, sticky dough.
3. **Press:** On oiled banana leaf, press small balls into thin rounds.
4. **Dry:** Sun-dry on racks, turning once, until hard and translucent (**1–2 days**).
5. **Puff and serve:** Toast the sheets over low embers, turning quickly, until puffed and crisp. Eat straight away.
