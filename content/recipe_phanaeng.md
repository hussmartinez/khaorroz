# Phanaeng / พะแนง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The evolution from grilled to stewed form and its dating to the Rama VII era rest on Thai popular-press accounts.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand (Bangkok).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a thick, dry-ish red coconut curry clinging to slices of beef, pork or chicken, scented with makrut lime leaf, eaten with jasmine rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok (*kratha*); historically a grill (see §2).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The oldest known written reference is in Mom Somchin Rachanupraphan's cookbook of 1890 (B.E. 2433, reign of Rama V), spelt *phanaeng* (ผะแนง) and describing chicken basted with curry paste and coconut milk and then grilled, not a curry. Later cooks, finding the grilling laborious, stir-fried large pieces of duck or chicken with paste and coconut cream; the stewed curry known today is said to have developed by the reign of Rama VII ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/life/2657966); [Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=307815)). The paste is close to red curry paste, and some modern recipes add ground peanuts, which older recipes often omit.

It is a classic *kap khao*, rich enough that a small amount seasons a whole plate of rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Phanaeng nuea (beef):** the most common restaurant version.
*   **Phanaeng mu / kai:** pork or chicken, quicker to cook.
*   **With peanuts:** modern style; older recipes omit them.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Phanaeng or red curry paste | 50 g |
| Coconut cream | 400 ml |
| Beef sirloin, thinly sliced | 400 g |
| Fish sauce, palm sugar | 2 tbsp, 1 tbsp |
| Makrut lime leaves, finely shredded | 6 |
| Red chili, thinly sliced | 1 |
| Ground roasted peanuts (optional) | 2 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) | 400 g, rinsed (+ 520 ml water, absorption method) |
| Curry per plate | ≈ 150 g meat with sauce |
| Rice per plate | ≈ 180 g cooked |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Reduce half the coconut cream in the wok until it splits.
2. Fry the paste until fragrant; add peanuts if using.
3. Add the meat and cook through; add the remaining cream and reduce to a thick sauce.
4. Season with fish sauce and palm sugar; top with shredded lime leaves and chili.
5. Cook the rice: rinse 400 g jasmine rice until the water runs nearly clear, add 520 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 min, then rest 10 min covered (or use an electric rice cooker / *mo hung khao*).
6. Serve with rice.
