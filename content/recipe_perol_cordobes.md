# Perol Cordobés / Arroz de Perol (Día de San Rafael)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Andalucía](provincial_andalucia.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the perol is well documented as a **ritual** (San Rafael, 24 October; "cucharada y paso atrás"). Published sources give ingredients but **no canonical quantities or timings**, and they don't settle the texture. The quantities below are a reconstruction consistent with the documented ingredients and the spoon-eaten serving custom.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Córdoba city and its Sierra: Parque Periurbano de Los Villares, El Arenal, and the patios and countryside around the capital.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). A rice *guiso* eaten standing, by the spoonful, straight from the pot.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** The ***perol***, a wide, tall, two-handled pot that gives the dish its name, over an open **wood fire** outdoors.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Córdoba "perol" means three things at once: the pot, the rice dish cooked in it, and the **outdoor gathering** around it. Its great day is **24 October, San Rafael**. The archangel is Córdoba's *Custodio*, and his feast defines local identity more than the official patrons. Families and groups of friends head for the Sierra, parks and patios, light a wood fire and cook rice in the open air. People eat it standing in a circle around the pot, each with bread and a spoon, following the rule of ***"cucharada y paso atrás"***: take a spoonful, then step back so the next person can reach the pot. Dessert is *pastel cordobés* with sweet wine. Since the pandemic, individual portions have become more common. The same perol is also used for **migas**. The Cátedra de Gastronomía de Andalucía (Universidad de Córdoba) has studied the perol as food heritage, with an eye on UNESCO intangible heritage recognition.

In the past the protein was whatever was "caught that day" in the monte: **rabbit or small game birds**. Today the usual meats are **magro and pork ribs, chicken and rabbit**. A glass of **Montilla-Moriles fino** goes in before the water, which is Córdoba's own wine signature.

**Culinary science:** a tall pot, rather than a flat paella, keeps a deeper layer of rice submerged. The rice therefore releases starch into a loose, spoonable sauce instead of drying to a socarrat. The fino's acetaldehyde and nutty flor notes deglaze the sofrito and season the broth.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Córdoba city, classic:** Magro + pork ribs + chicken (± rabbit), with a sofrito of tomato, onion, garlic and pepper, and **fino de Montilla**. Saffron.
*   **Sierra / game version:** **Rabbit** instead of chicken, with **wild asparagus and wild mushrooms** (*setas*). ⚠️ Attested in secondary sources.
*   **Seasonal veg version:** **Artichokes** or **setas** in the sofrito (both documented as optional).
*   **Modern drift:** Some groups now cook it in a **paella pan**, which pushes it towards a drier [arroz campero](recipe_arroz_campero_andaluz.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Córdoba classic** (reconstructed quantities).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil (Córdoba DO oils) | 100 ml |
| Pork magro, cubed + pork ribs | 300 g + 300 g |
| Chicken (or rabbit), in pieces | 500 g |
| Onion, chopped | 1 |
| Green/red pepper | 1 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Artichokes or setas (optional) | 4 / 200 g |
| Fino Montilla-Moriles | 1 glass (100 ml) |
| Saffron | 0.3 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 400 g |
| Water or light stock | ≈ 1.6–1.8 L (≈ 4 : 1, meloso) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Fire:** Build a wood fire under the perol.
2. **Meats:** Brown the pork and chicken in the oil (15 min).
3. **Sofrito:** Add the onion, pepper and garlic (8 min), then the tomato (5 min), and the artichokes or setas if using.
4. **Wine:** Pour in the fino and let the alcohol cook off (2 min).
5. **Liquid:** Add the water and saffron and simmer **20 min**.
6. **Rice:** Add the rice and cook **18 min** at a lively simmer, stirring occasionally. Pull embers away from the pot for the last 5 min.
7. **Rest & eat:** Rest 3 min, then gather round with spoons and bread: *cucharada y paso atrás*.
