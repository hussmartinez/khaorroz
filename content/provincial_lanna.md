# Northern Thailand (Lanna) / ภาคเหนือ (ล้านนา)

> ⬅ [README](README.md)

## 1. Overview
Lanna, the old northern kingdom centred on Chiang Mai, is sticky-rice country. Its everyday starch is **glutinous rice** (*khao niao*, in the north usually *khao nueng*, "steamed rice"). Grains are soaked overnight, steamed in a conical woven-bamboo steamer (*huad*) set over a pot (*mo nueng*), then kept warm in a lidded basket (*kratip* or *kong khao*). People roll the rice into a ball with their fingers and use it to scoop chilli dips (*nam phrik*), grills and curries ([Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/travel/national/photos-of-thailand-northern-thai-food-khao-soi%20); [Table Journey](https://tablejourney.com/thailand/dish/sticky-rice/)). A 2018 review of Lanna food culture calls sticky rice the main energy dish of the region, eaten with chilli paste and local vegetables ([Food Culture in Lanna Way of Life, TCI](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/sdust/article/view/183834)).

The north shares a sticky-rice culture with Laos and Isan. It is also shaped by the Tai Yai (Shan), the only group in the region that habitually ate non-glutinous rice, which is why a handful of Lanna dishes use ordinary rice ([Wikipedia – Khao kan chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_kan_chin)). The new-rice season in the cool months brings warm sticky-rice snacks such as *khao puk nga*. Bamboo-roasted *khao lam* was traditionally offered to monks at the full moon of the fourth lunar month ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2654618)).

## 2. Cultural Sub-Regions
| Sub-region | Rice personality | Key towns |
|---|---|---|
| **Chiang Mai basin** | Sticky rice with nam phrik and grills; khao kan chin at markets | Chiang Mai, Lamphun, San Kamphaeng |
| **Chiang Rai & the Mekong** | Tai Lue and Lao-influenced sticky-rice tables | Chiang Rai, Chiang Saen, Chiang Khong |
| **Lampang** | *Khao taen* rice crackers (GI-claimed) | Lampang |
| **Nan & Phrae** | Tai Lue cooking, new-rice snacks | Nan, Phrae |
| **Phayao** | Cool-season *khao puk nga* in markets | Phayao |
| **Mae Hong Son (Tai Yai)** | Non-glutinous rice dishes: *khao som*, *khao kan chin* | Mae Hong Son, Pai, Mae Sariang |
| **Uttaradit (Laplae)** | Rice-flour sheets: *khao phan phak*, *khao khaep* | Laplae |
