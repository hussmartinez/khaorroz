## 1. Overview
Murcia shares the Segura basin with Alicante's Vega Baja, and with it the **ñora**, the **serranas** snails and the irrigated huerta. Its distinctive asset is **D.O. Arroz de Calasparra**. Rice has been grown on the terraces of the Segura and Mundo rivers since at least the 14th century, and in **1986** Calasparra became the first rice in the world to receive a Denominación de Origen (and the first non-wine D.O. in Spain). Only **bomba** and **balilla × sollana** qualify. Murcian rice cooking has three settings: the **Mar Menor coast** (the caldero), the **huerta** (pork and vegetable rices) and the **mountain interior** (rabbit and snails over a wood fire, plus Lenten and forgotten dishes such as the *empedrao*).

## 2. Cultural Sub-Regions
| Sub-region | Rice personality | Key towns |
|---|---|---|
| **Campo de Cartagena / Mar Menor** | Caldero (ñora, mújol), seafood rices | Cartagena, San Pedro del Pinatar, Cabo de Palos, San Javier |
| **Huerta de Murcia / Vega del Segura** | Magra + costillejas + vegetables, lemon on the side | Murcia, Alcantarilla, Molina, Beniel |
| **Noroeste** | Calasparra rice, rabbit + serranas, arroz de ayuno | Calasparra, Caravaca, Moratalla, Cehegín |
| **Altiplano** | Vine-shoot fire, highland empedrao | Jumilla, Yecla |

**Arroz y costra:** Murcian households near the Vega Baja border also cook the egg-crusted rice. Its documented home is Alicante (Elche and Orihuela), and I found no separate Murcian source, so see [recipe_arroz_con_costra.md](recipe_arroz_con_costra.md).
