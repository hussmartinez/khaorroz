# Tam Khanun / ตำขนุน (Tam Ba Nun / ตำบ่าหนุน)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** no source gave a quantified recipe; quantities and method below are reconstructed from the descriptions cited.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna (Chiang Mai, Lamphun, Phrae, Nan).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: young green jackfruit boiled and pounded to a rough paste with minced pork and a northern chilli paste; hearty, spicy, eaten with sticky rice and crisp pork rind.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large pot to boil the jackfruit, a big wooden mortar, a wok to cook the pork; sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Tam khanun is a spicy northern "yam" of young green jackfruit, boiled whole, then pounded with a mortar and pestle together with minced pork; it is usually eaten with sticky rice and pork rind ([Wikipedia – Tam khanun (image page)](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tam_khanun.jpg); [Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/5498065/)). Its sibling is *kaeng ba nun* (young jackfruit curry), called *kaeng ba nun pak pi* in northern dialect ([Thai PBS – Krua Naree](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/NaReeKraJang/watch/50425)). Jackfruit (*khanun*) is an auspicious tree whose name puns on "support".

**Culinary science:** unripe jackfruit is starchy and neutral; boiling softens its fibres so pounding turns them into a meaty, absorbent mass that takes up the fat and chilli.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Tam khanun with pork:** the standard ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/5498065/)).
*   **Kaeng khanun (curry):** with pork ribs and eggplant ([Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/NaReeKraJang/watch/50425); [HDmall – young jackfruit curry](https://hdmall.co.th/nutrition-hub/items/curry-young-jackfruit-1161)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: reconstructed Lanna tam khanun with pork.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Young green jackfruit, peeled and chunked | 800 g |
| Minced pork | 200 g |
| Northern chilli paste (dried chilli, shallot, garlic, shrimp paste) | 3 tbsp |
| Tomatoes | 2 |
| Fried garlic, coriander, spring onion | to finish |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Jackfruit : pork | 4 : 1 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Boil:** boil the jackfruit until very tender (40–60 min); drain.
3. **Cook:** fry the chilli paste, add pork and tomatoes and cook until dry.
4. **Pound & serve:** pound the jackfruit coarsely, mix in the pork, finish with fried garlic and herbs; serve with sticky rice and pork rind.
