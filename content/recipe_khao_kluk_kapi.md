# Khao Kluk Kapi / ข้าวคลุกกะปิ

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** well documented as a dish and in its garnishes. No source found dates its origin. Town variants are partly reconstructed from shop menus and food writing.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok and the Central Plains (shrimp-paste coast of Samut Sakhon, Samut Songkhram, Rayong).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Tossed rice: **cooked jasmine rice fried or tossed with shrimp paste (*kapi*)** until every grain is coated and savoury. Served in a ring of garnishes: sweet pork (*mu wan*), shredded omelette, dried shrimp, green mango, shallot, chili, long beans and lime, mixed at the table.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Wok (*kratha*) over gas or charcoal; mortar (*khrok*) for the kapi; a large plate for the ring of garnishes.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao kluk kapi ("rice mixed with shrimp paste") is a classic Central Thai one-plate meal. Its balance of salty, sweet, sour and hot comes from the garnishes rather than the rice. Thairath and LINE Today describe sweet pork, Chinese sausage, egg threads, sour mango and fresh vegetables as the standard companions ([LINE Today](https://today.line.me/th/v3/article/YaKLaEa); [Thairath – mu wan](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2153456)). The writer Krit Lueamlamai traces its "dressed-up" form from a simple rice tossed with fresh kapi, shallot, chili and lime ([Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/prachachuen/news_688344)).

**Culinary science:** *kapi* is fermented krill or shrimp paste, packed with glutamates and amino acids. Briefly frying it in oil removes its raw ammonia edge and toasts it. Cold, slightly dry rice takes up the paste evenly. Green mango's acid and the palm-sugar sweetness of the pork round out the salty base.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok shop plate**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Day-old jasmine rice | 800 g |
| Shrimp paste (*kapi*) | 3 tbsp |
| Garlic, chopped | 4 cloves |
| Oil | 3 tbsp |
| **Mu wan:** pork belly, sliced | 300 g |
| Mu wan: palm sugar, dark soy, fish sauce | 80 g, 1 tbsp, 1 tbsp |
| Eggs (thin omelette, shredded) | 3 |
| Dried shrimp (fried), green mango, shallots, long beans, chilies, lime | 40 g, 1, 4, 100 g, 4, 2 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Kapi : rice | 1 tbsp : ≈ 270 g |
| Garnishes | about one-third of the plate |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Mu wan:** simmer the pork in a little water with the palm sugar, dark soy and fish sauce until sticky and glazed (25 min).
2. **Omelette:** make thin omelettes, roll them and shred finely.
3. **Kapi rice:** fry the garlic in oil, add the kapi (dissolved in 2 tbsp water) and fry 1 min. Add the rice and toss until evenly coated.
4. **Serve:** mound the rice in the centre with sweet pork, egg, dried shrimp, shredded green mango, sliced shallot, long beans, chili and lime around it. Each diner mixes their own.
