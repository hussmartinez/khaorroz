# Arroz a la Cazuela / Arròs a la Cassola

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Catalunya](provincial_catalunya.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Barcelona and the Catalan coast (Maresme, Garraf), the Empordà (see [arròs a la cassola de Pals](recipe_arros_a_la_cassola_de_pals.md)), and the Camp de Tarragona.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). The chef Maria Nicolau calls it "melós i suau" (creamy and smooth), and the opposite of a dry paella.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep glazed **earthenware cassola** on a wood or coal stove, today on gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The Gran Enciclopèdia Catalana defines arròs a la cassola as rice cooked in a cassola **"with fish, meat, poultry, vegetables, etc."**, a dish that "admits many combinations". In Barcelona it was the Sunday and feast-day rice. VilaWeb notes that before *canelons* took over, many households ate arròs a la cassola on **Sant Esteve** (26 December). The TV3 *Cuines* reference recipe combines **sofregit, meat and seafood**: rabbit, pork, squid, mussels and langoustines, plus peas. This makes it the classic Catalan **mar i muntanya** ("sea and mountain") rice. Some writers, including Nicolau, argue for keeping the name "arròs a la cassola" rather than calling it a "paella".

**Culinary science:** the Catalan **sofregit** is the engine of this dish. Onion is cooked slowly for a long time until it is dark and jammy, then a little tomato is added; Nicolau warns against overdoing the tomato. A **picada** (pounded garlic, parsley, fried bread or almonds, sometimes saffron) is stirred in near the end to thicken and season. The deep clay cassola gives a thicker rice layer with gentle evaporation, which produces a creamy texture rather than a socarrat.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Barcelona (TV3 reference):** Rabbit + pork + squid + mussels + langoustines + peas. Onion sofregit. Picada.
*   **"Parellada" (Barcelona restaurant tradition):** ⚠️ The parellada is traditionally described as a rice with all shells and bones removed, the Catalan counterpart of the [senyoret](recipe_arroz_del_senyoret.md). I found no primary source for it in this research, so verify.
*   **Empordà / Pals:** Made with **arròs de Pals**, whose grains stay loose. See [recipe_arros_a_la_cassola_de_pals.md](recipe_arros_a_la_cassola_de_pals.md).
*   **Palamós (cuina de barca):** A fishermen's version cooked with the day's catch. The Museu de la Pesca cookery school demonstrates it.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Barcelona mar i muntanya** (structure of the TV3 reference recipe).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Rabbit, small pieces | 400 g |
| Pork ribs or loin | 250 g |
| Squid, rings | 250 g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 2 (300 g) |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 100 g |
| Fish and meat stock | 1.4 L |
| Mussels | 300 g |
| Langoustines | 8 |
| Peas | 120 g |
| **Picada:** garlic 2 cloves, parsley, 10 toasted almonds or 1 slice fried bread, saffron 0.2 g | — |

**Extraction:** Brown the rabbit and pork (**12 min**) and set aside. Sear the squid (**3 min**). Add the onion and cook slowly until dark (**20–25 min**), then the tomato (**5 min**). Return the meats. Open the mussels separately in a lidded pan and keep their liquid for the stock.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice (bomba or Pals/Delta) | 320 g |
| Stock | ≈ 1.2–1.3 L (≈ 4:1, meloso) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Add the rice to the sofregit and stir for **1 min**.
2. Add the boiling stock and the peas.
3. Simmer **10 min**, then stir in the **picada**. Lay the langoustines on top.
4. Cook another **6–7 min**, shaking the cassola instead of stirring. Add the mussels at the end.
5. Rest **2 min**, covered. The rice should be loose and creamy.
