# Kaeng Tai Pla / แกงไตปลา

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish itself is very well attested. Its *origin* is not documented, and the claim of a Phatthalung origin "since Rama II" rests on Wikipedia mirrors. The quantities are a reconstruction from published home recipes.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The whole Thai south: Nakhon Si Thammarat, Phatthalung, Songkhla, Trang, Satun, Surat Thani. It is a fixture of the southern curry-rice shops (*khao kaeng pak tai*).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a fiery, thin, turmeric-tinged curry built on fermented fish innards (*tai pla*), with grilled mackerel, pumpkin, bamboo shoot, pea aubergines and young jackfruit. It is spooned over plain jasmine rice and eaten with a basket of raw vegetables (*phak no*) to cool the heat.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay pot (*mo din*) or aluminium pot on a charcoal stove; the curry paste is pounded in a stone mortar (*khrok*).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Tai pla* is the salted, fermented stomach and entrails of fish (often mackerel or tuna), cured for between ten days and a month until it becomes a pungent, deeply savoury sauce ([Wikipedia – Kaeng tai pla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_tai_pla)). Southern cooks pound a paste of dried and fresh chilies, turmeric, lemongrass, galangal, garlic, shallot, kaffir-lime peel and shrimp paste, and simmer it with the tai pla. The resulting curry is so central to southern identity that when the website TasteAtlas ranked it among the "worst dishes in the world" in 2024, it caused a public outcry. Thai media reported that even the Prime Minister defended it as the most delicious dish for southerners ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2775836); [Matichon – Satun curry-rice vendors](https://www.matichon.co.th/region/news_4508374)).

It is never eaten alone. Kaeng tai pla is a **kap khao**, something eaten *with rice*. It is served over steamed rice, with fresh herbs, cucumber, long beans and sato beans on the side. Family recipes from Nakhon Si Thammarat are described as passed down "from grandparents" and are prized for being "hot but not salty" ([MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/smes/detail/9660000051208)). The main warning in every recipe is salt, because the tai pla is already very salty ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2775836)).

**Culinary science:** fermentation breaks fish proteins down into free amino acids (glutamate) and peptides, which give a meaty depth and the dish's distinctive smell. Turmeric adds colour and an earthy bitterness, and starchy vegetables such as pumpkin and jackfruit seeds slightly thicken the thin broth.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Nakhon Si Thammarat:** Dry-style versions (*tai pla haeng*) reduced almost to a paste, as well as the soupy form. Grilled mackerel is flaked in.
*   **Phatthalung / Songkhla:** Soupy, with bamboo shoot, pumpkin and pea aubergine. Some cooks add prawns ⚠️.
*   **Satun:** A best-seller in curry-rice shops. Vendors stress that it is "not as bad as people think" ([Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/region/news_4508374)).
*   **With coconut milk:** Some households add a little coconut milk to soften the heat ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_tai_pla)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: soupy southern home style, reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Tai pla (fermented fish innards) | 120 g |
| Water | 1 L |
| Southern curry paste: dried chilies 15 g, bird's-eye chilies 10, turmeric 20 g, lemongrass 2 stalks, galangal 10 g, garlic 6 cloves, shallots 4, kaffir-lime peel 1 tsp, shrimp paste 1 tsp | ≈ 120 g |
| Grilled mackerel (pla thu), flaked | 2 fish |
| Pumpkin, in chunks | 200 g |
| Bamboo shoot, sliced | 150 g |
| Pea aubergines | 100 g |
| Kaffir-lime leaves | 6 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (served with it) | 400 g raw, steamed |
| Curry : rice | about 1 ladle of curry per plate of rice |
| Fresh vegetables (cucumber, long beans, herbs) | 1 basket |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Rinse 400 g of jasmine rice twice and steam or cook it with 500 ml of water (≈ 18 min); rest 10 min.
2. **Tai pla stock:** Boil the tai pla in the water for 10 min, then strain out the bones and grit.
3. **Paste:** Pound the curry-paste ingredients to a smooth paste in the mortar.
4. **Curry:** Add the paste to the strained stock, then the pumpkin and bamboo shoot. Simmer 10 min.
5. **Finish:** Add the flaked mackerel, pea aubergines and torn kaffir-lime leaves; simmer 3 min. Taste before adding any salt.
6. **Serve:** Ladle over or beside the rice, with the vegetable basket.
