# Lamprea a la Bordelesa con Arroz / Lamprea á Bordelesa con Arroz (Arbo)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Norte](provincial_norte.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** included as a rice tradition of the **Miño**. In Arbo the lamprey stewed in its own blood is **always served with white rice and fried bread**, and across the river in Portugal it becomes ***arroz de lampreia***, with the rice cooked in the sauce. Sources describe the pairing but give **no measured Galician rice recipe**, so the rice part is reconstructed. Lamprey fishing is seasonal and regulated.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Galicia, on the lower Miño: **Arbo** (Pontevedra), plus As Neves and Salvaterra. The Portuguese bank (Minho) has its own tradition.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). Plain white rice served with the lamprey stew (the Portuguese *arroz de lampreia* is wetter).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay *cazuela* for the stew and a pot for the rice.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The **sea lamprey** (*Petromyzon marinus*) is a jawless fish of prehistoric lineage. It swims up the Miño to spawn in winter and spring ("hasta que cante el cuco", until the cuckoo sings). **Arbo** has fished it for centuries from stone weirs called *pesqueiras*. It celebrates the **Festa da Lamprea**, a Fiesta of National Tourist Interest, with dried, stuffed, empanada and *bordelesa* preparations. The *bordelesa* stew is cooked **in its own blood** with the region's **red wine** and onion, and served in clay with **fried bread** and **white rice**. On the Portuguese side, rice is cooked directly in the sauce as *arroz de lampreia*.

**Culinary science:** the blood is collected when the fish is cleaned and stirred into the sauce near the end. Like a *civet*, it thickens and darkens the wine sauce. Plain rice is the counterweight to this very rich, iron-heavy sauce.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Arbo (Galicia):** Lamprey *á bordelesa* with white rice and fried bread on the side. Also dried lamprey (*lamprea seca*), stuffed, and empanada.
*   **Minho (Portugal), cross-border:** *Arroz de lampreia*, rice cooked in the blood-and-wine sauce.
*   ⚠️ Other Galician Miño towns are not documented separately.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Arbo style**. The lamprey method follows the sources; the quantities are reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamprey, cleaned (blood reserved) | 1 large (≈ 1–1.2 kg) |
| Red wine (Galician, e.g. Condado/Ribeiro tinto) | 500 ml |
| Onion | 2 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Bay leaf, salt, pepper | to taste |
| Bread slices, fried | 8 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| White rice | 300 g |
| Water | 600 ml (2 : 1) |
| Garlic / oil (rice) | 1 clove / 20 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Clean:** Scald and scrape the lamprey, remove the cartilage cord, and cut it into pieces. **Collect the blood** in the cazuela with a splash of wine.
2. **Marinate:** Leave the pieces in the red wine with the blood for several hours.
3. **Sofrito:** Soften the onion and garlic in oil, add the lamprey and the marinade, and simmer gently until tender (≈ 45 min to 1 h). Stir in the blood near the end.
4. **Rice:** Fry the garlic in oil, add the rice and water, and cook ≈ 17 min until dry.
5. **Serve:** Lamprey and sauce in the clay cazuela, with fried bread and a mound of white rice. [Portuguese style: cook the rice directly in the sauce.]
