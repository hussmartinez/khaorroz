# Kaeng Matsaman / แกงมัสมั่น

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Origins are contested: one tradition credits the Persian merchant Sheikh Ahmad Qomi at the 17th-century Ayutthaya court (largely family and scholarly tradition), others see a southern Malay/Indian or Siamese-Muslim origin. The Rama II poem citation is well attested.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand (Ayutthaya and Bangkok court tradition) and Thai-Muslim communities; also claimed by the south.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a mild, rich coconut curry with dry spices (cardamom, cinnamon, clove, nutmeg), potatoes, peanuts and beef or chicken, a little sweet and sour from palm sugar and tamarind, spooned over jasmine rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot for the long braise; a wok and stone mortar (*khrok*) for the paste of roasted chilies and spices.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The name comes from *mosalman*, an archaic Persian-derived word for 'Muslim' ([Wikipedia – Massaman curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massaman_curry)). Thai scholarship (Santi Sawetwimon) associates the dish with the Ayutthaya court through Sheikh Ahmad Qomi, a Persian merchant who settled in Ayutthaya in the early 17th century and from whom the Bunnag family descends ([Wikipedia – Sheikh Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Ahmad_(nobleman_of_Siam))). Others argue for a southern or Siamese-Muslim origin; the evidence for the Persian story is family tradition rather than contemporary records ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/deconstructing-massaman-curry-thailand-mellow-classic)).

What is certain is its royal praise: massaman is celebrated in *Kap He Chom Khrueang Khao Wan*, the poem of food and desserts attributed to Prince Itsarasunthon, the future King Rama II (c. 1800), a text studied as a source of standardised court recipes ([Dusit Thani College Journal, 2024](https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/journaldtc/article/download/276077/177807/1092579); [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/weekly/?p=155712)). It also appears in *Mae Khrua Hua Pa*, often called the first Thai cookbook ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Khrua_Hua_Pa)). See also [khao mok kai](recipe_khao_mok_kai.md) and the Persian-style court rice [khao hung prung yang thet](recipe_khao_hung_prung_yang_thet.md).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Beef (*nuea*):** the classic Central Thai version, braised until tender.
*   **Chicken (*kai*):** lighter and quicker; the version canned and exported.
*   **Thai-Muslim households:** halal beef or goat, more whole spices.
*   **Southern versions:** spicier and less sweet (⚠️ general description).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Massaman paste (dried chilies, shallot, garlic, lemongrass, galangal + roasted coriander, cumin, cardamom, clove, cinnamon, nutmeg) | 70 g |
| Beef chuck, in 4 cm cubes | 600 g |
| Coconut milk | 800 ml |
| Potatoes, in chunks | 300 g |
| Shallots or small onions | 8 |
| Roasted peanuts | 50 g |
| Tamarind water, fish sauce, palm sugar | 2 tbsp each, approx. |
| Cardamom pods, cinnamon stick, bay leaves | 4, 1, 2 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) | 400 g, rinsed (+ 520 ml water, absorption method) |
| Curry per plate | ≈ 250 ml with meat and potato |
| Rice per plate | ≈ 180 g cooked |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Simmer the beef in the thin coconut milk with cinnamon, cardamom and bay for 1–1½ h until tender.
2. Fry the coconut cream until it splits and fry the paste until dark and fragrant.
3. Add the fried paste to the pot with potatoes, shallots and peanuts; simmer 20 min.
4. Balance with tamarind (sour), palm sugar (sweet) and fish sauce (salty).
5. Cook the rice: rinse 400 g jasmine rice until the water runs nearly clear, add 520 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 min, then rest 10 min covered (or use an electric rice cooker / *mo hung khao*).
6. Serve with rice; the curry improves the next day.
