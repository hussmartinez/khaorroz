# Khao Nasi Prajit (Khao Pradap, Siamese-Javanese Seasoned Rice) / ข้าวนาซิประจิต (ข้าวประดับ)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The dish is catalogued by the Thaifoodmaster Siamese recipe archive as a c. 1901 "Siamese seasoned mix rice, Javanese style", also called *khao pradap* ("adorned rice"). It is seasoned with chilli relish and roasted coconut and served with an assortment of meats and vegetables, and the archive's version follows a recipe credited to a court lady of the Fifth Reign. The archive's full recipe is behind a paywall, so only that description is attested. The quantities and the exact garnish set below are a reconstruction based on related, better-documented court "mixed rices" (*khao khluk*), which Thai sources describe as an Indonesian idea adapted in the Siamese palace.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The palace kitchens of Bangkok in the reign of King Chulalongkorn (Rama V, 1868–1910).
*   **Historical Source:** Siamese court recipe c. 1901 (Fifth Reign, Bangkok), catalogued in the Thaifoodmaster Siamese Recipe Archive as *khao nasi prajit / khao pradap*
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Mixed seasoned rice. Cooked rice tossed (*khluk*) with a chilli relish and roasted grated coconut until every grain is coated and slightly moist, then "adorned" (*pradap*) with small piles of meats, egg and vegetables.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok (*krathaa*) over charcoal for warming the rice through; a mortar for the relish; a large footed tray or platter for the adorned presentation.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Nasi* is Malay and Javanese for cooked rice, and the dish's double name, *nasi prajit* and *khao pradap*, records its journey. King Chulalongkorn travelled to Java in 1871, 1896 and 1901 ⚠️ (dates from general knowledge, not from the sources below), and the palace kitchens of his reign absorbed Javanese and Malay ideas. Thaifoodmaster describes the dish as "a Siamese adaptation of Javanese-style seasoned rice dishes", seasoned with chilli relish and roasted coconut and "served with various meats and vegetables as a side dish" ([Thaifoodmaster – c1901 khaao naasi prajit](https://thaifoodmaster.com/courses/nasi_prajit_siamese_javanese_seasoned_rice?lang=th)).

Thai food writers place it in the family of court ***khao khluk*** ("tossed rices"). MGR Online notes that *khao khluk* "is a food culture from Indonesia" which, on reaching Siam long ago, was adapted by frying rice that had been tossed with *nam phrik*. It adds that the *khao khluk* recipe credited to Chao Chom Sadap, head of the royal kitchen in the Fifth Reign, is the same as the palace's *khao nam phrik long ruea* ([MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/celebonline/detail/9550000013132)). *Silpa Wattanatham* likewise records *khao khluk* as a snack King Vajiravudh (Rama VI) enjoyed, and King Chulalongkorn's travel diary longing for a *khao khluk kapi* "made by his grandmother" ([Silpa-mag – khao khluk, Rama VI](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=163606); [Silpa-mag – Rama V's "diary of hunger"](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=10687)).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   ***Khao nasi prajit / khao pradap* (c. 1901, palace):** Rice seasoned with chilli relish and roasted coconut, with meats and vegetables around it.
*   ***Khao khluk nam phrik long ruea* (Fifth Reign, Chao Chom Sadap):** Rice tossed and fried with the "boat" chilli relish (*nam phrik long ruea*, with sweet pork and salted egg).
*   **[*Khao khluk kapi*](recipe_khao_kluk_kapi.md) (household, still common):** Rice tossed with fried shrimp paste and served with sweet pork, shredded omelette, green mango, shallot, chilli and dried shrimp. It is the living descendant ⚠️ (its direct line from the 1901 dish is inferred).
*   **Java/Malaya (source tradition):** Seasoned and garnished *nasi* platters such as *nasi campur* ⚠️ (the specific Javanese model is not named in the sources).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern reconstruction: rice tossed with a pounded chilli-shallot relish and roasted coconut, adorned with sweet pork, egg, cucumber and herbs.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried long red chillies, soaked and seeded | 6 |
| Shallots | 80 g |
| Garlic | 30 g |
| Shrimp paste (*kapi*), toasted | 15 g |
| Palm sugar | 20 g |
| Fish sauce | 20 ml |
| Freshly grated coconut, dry-roasted golden | 120 g |
| Neutral oil or lard | 40 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Cooked jasmine rice, a day old | 800 g (from 400 g raw) |
| Sweet braised pork (*mu wan*), sliced | 200 g |
| Thin omelette, shredded | 2 eggs |
| Dried shrimp, fried | 30 g |
| Cucumber, sliced, and fresh herbs (coriander, Thai basil) | 200 g |
| Lime wedges | 2 limes |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Roast the coconut:** Toast the grated coconut in a dry wok over low heat, stirring constantly, until evenly golden (8–10 min). Set aside half for garnish.
2. **Pound the relish:** Pound the chillies, shallots, garlic and toasted *kapi* to a paste, then season it with the palm sugar and fish sauce.
3. **Fry the relish:** Heat the oil in the wok and fry the paste over medium heat for 3–4 minutes, until it darkens and smells sweet.
4. **Toss the rice (*khluk*):** Add the rice and half the coconut and toss over medium heat until every grain is coloured and hot (4–5 min). Taste and adjust with fish sauce or sugar.
5. **Adorn (*pradap*):** Mound the rice in the centre of a tray and arrange small piles of sweet pork, omelette, dried shrimp, cucumber and herbs around it. Scatter the remaining coconut on top and serve with lime.
