# Jok / โจ๊ก

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok (Sam Yan, Bang Rak / Charoen Krung, Talat Noi) and every Thai town's morning market.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice porridge: **broken jasmine rice simmered until it dissolves into a smooth, thick, silky congee**, topped with pork balls, an egg, ginger, spring onion and fried garlic.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large pot stirred for hours over charcoal or gas. Some shops toast the rice first for a smoky *chao tah* style.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The word **jok** is not Thai: it comes from the Cantonese pronunciation of congee ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=257280)). The dish arrived with Chinese migrants and was adapted to Thai taste ([Thailand Blog](https://www.thailandblog.nl/en/eten-drinken/geheimen-van-de-thaise-keuken-jok-hartige-rijstepap/)). It is Bangkok's breakfast. **Joke Samyan** is famous for smoky, charcoal-cooked congee ([Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/thai-congee-rice-porridge-at-joke-samyan)). **Jok Prince** on Charoen Krung has served pork congee for nearly a century ([En Primeur Club](https://www.enprimeurclub.com/restaurants/jok-prince-bangkok-restaurant); [Johor Kaki](https://johorkaki.blogspot.com/2023/03/jok-prince-porridge-famous-chao-tah.html)).

**Culinary science:** broken rice has more exposed starch. Long simmering with stirring ruptures the starch granules until amylopectin thickens the water into a smooth gel. Toasting the rice first adds roasted, smoky notes. The raw egg is cooked by the heat of the congee.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Sam Yan (Joke Samyan):** smoky, charcoal-cooked, with pork balls and egg ([Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/thai-congee-rice-porridge-at-joke-samyan)).
*   **Charoen Krung / Bang Rak (Jok Prince):** pork only, toasted *chao tah* style ([Johor Kaki](https://johorkaki.blogspot.com/2023/03/jok-prince-porridge-famous-chao-tah.html)).
*   **Talat Noi / Chinatown:** classic Teochew-Cantonese morning stalls ([The World of Street Food](https://www.theworldofstreetfood.com/2020/08/jok-or-thai-rice-porridge-in-bangkok.html)).
*   **Toppings across Thailand:** pork balls, liver, century egg, a raw or soft egg, and *patongko* (fried dough).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok pork-ball jok with egg**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Broken jasmine rice (or jasmine rice, pulsed) | 250 g |
| Pork stock | 2.5 L |
| Minced pork, seasoned with soy, white pepper and garlic | 300 g |
| Eggs (raw or soft) | 4 |
| Ginger, julienned; spring onion; fried garlic | 30 g; 3; 3 tbsp |
| Light soy, white pepper, salt | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice : stock | 1 : 10 by weight |
| Cooking time | 1.5–2 h |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toast (optional, *chao tah*):** dry-toast the broken rice in a pan until lightly coloured.
2. **Simmer:** add the rice to the boiling stock, then simmer 1.5–2 h, stirring often, until it breaks down to a silky porridge. Add water if it gets too thick.
3. **Pork balls:** drop small balls of seasoned pork into the congee for the last 8 min.
4. **Egg:** crack an egg into each bowl and ladle the boiling congee over it so it half-cooks.
5. **Finish** with ginger, spring onion, fried garlic, a dash of soy and white pepper.
