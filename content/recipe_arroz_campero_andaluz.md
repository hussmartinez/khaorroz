# Arroz Campero Andaluz / Arroz con Cosas (de Campo)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Andalucía](provincial_andalucia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Inland Andalucía: the countryside of Sevilla, Córdoba and Cádiz, the Montilla-Moriles and Jerez wine country, and the cortijos (farm estates).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). Cooked in a paella until almost no liquid remains, then rested under a cloth.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A paella or wide pan over wood or gas, cooked outdoors at the cortijo.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Campero* means "of the countryside". This is Andalucía's everyday open-air rice, cooked at cortijos, *romerías* and Sunday outings. Directo al Paladar calls it "el más típico de Andalucía" and notes that it is the very definition of **"arroz con cosas"** ("rice with things"): a rice that takes whatever the larder and the season offer. The base is **pork magro and lots of vegetables**, but chicken or rabbit are equally accepted. Two Andalusian signatures set it apart from Valencian rices: a splash of **fino** (Montilla-Moriles or Jerez) in the sofrito, and serving it with **a slice of lemon** and sprigs of thyme or rosemary.

**Culinary science:** fino adds acidity and oxidative nutty aromas that brighten a pork-based fondo without stock gelatin. Adding the peas mid-cook keeps them green. The final 7–8 minutes under a cloth let the remaining surface moisture redistribute, giving loose, dry grains.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Generic cortijo version (documented):** Magro, green and red pepper, artichoke hearts, peas, tomato, fino, saffron or colourant.
*   **Córdoba:** The festive, meloso version eaten from a tall pot on San Rafael is the [perol cordobés](recipe_perol_cordobes.md).
*   **Sevilla marshes:** With duck, as the [arroz caldoso con pato de las marismas](recipe_arroz_caldoso_con_pato_marismas.md).
*   **Jaén:** Ham, chorizo and green olives, as the [arroz cortijero](recipe_arroz_cortijero_jaen.md).
*   ⚠️ Further town-level variants are not documented in the sources found.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Directo al Paladar's traditional Andalusian campero** (4 persons).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 45 ml (3 tbsp) |
| Pork magro, bite-size cubes | 400 g |
| Green Italian pepper | 1 |
| Red pepper, chopped | 1 piece |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |
| Tomato, chopped | 1 (in the method, not the source's list) |
| Artichoke hearts | 8 |
| Fino Montilla-Moriles or Jerez | 1 small glass |
| Saffron or colourant | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 360 g |
| Chicken or meat stock | ≈ 3× the rice volume (≈ 1.1 L) |
| Peas | 50 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Sofrito:** Fry the peppers and garlic in the oil. Before they brown, add the seasoned pork and colour it.
2. **Vegetables & wine:** Add the artichokes and tomato, then the fino, and let the alcohol evaporate.
3. **Rice:** Add the rice, stir for 2 min, then add the colourant and stock and bring to the boil.
4. **Fire:** High heat for **8 min**. Add the peas, then the lowest heat for **8 min**, until almost no liquid remains.
5. **Rest:** Off the heat, cover with a cloth and rest **7–8 min**. Serve with lemon slices and thyme or rosemary sprigs.
