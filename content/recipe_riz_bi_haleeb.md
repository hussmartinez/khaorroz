# Riz bi Haleeb (Rice Pudding) / رز بحليب

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Aleppo and Damascus; the same family of milk puddings is made across Lebanon, Palestine and Jordan.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy): a spoonable pudding, set by chilling. This is a dessert.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy-bottomed pot on low heat (stirred), then individual bowls chilled.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Riz bi haleeb* ("rice with milk") is the Levant's everyday dessert, and the direct cousin of the Spanish and Valencian [arroz con leche / arròs amb llet](recipe_arroz_con_leche.md). Both descend from the medieval Arab-Mediterranean love of rice cooked in milk with sugar. Where Spain flavours it with **cinnamon and lemon peel**, al-Sham perfumes it with **orange-blossom or rose water** and often **mastic** (*mistka*), the resin of the Chios mastic tree. Its aroma is resinous, and it gives the pudding a slight stretch. Crushed pistachios on top are standard.

Aleppo adds a celebrated layered version, ***roz bi-haleeb mbattan*** ("lined" rice-milk pudding). A creamy rice-and-milk base is chilled and then topped with a set **orange-juice layer**. It is published in Marlene Matar's *The Aleppo Cookbook*, a collection of recipes from Aleppian families, restaurants and chefs.

**Culinary science:** short-grain rice (Egyptian or calrose; arborio works) releases amylopectin and thickens the milk as it cooks. Most cooks stop when the pudding has the consistency of béchamel, because it **sets considerably when chilled**. Mastic must be pounded with a pinch of sugar before it goes in, or it clumps. The flower waters go in **off the heat**, because their aroma is volatile. Cornflour in the Aleppo *mbattan* gives the clean set needed to carry the second layer.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascus / Syrian standard:** Egyptian short-grain rice, full-fat milk, sugar, **mastic + orange-blossom water**; pistachios.
*   **Aleppo — mbattan:** Rice-milk base thickened with cornflour, then a **layer of orange juice and zest** set with cornflour; almonds or pistachios.
*   **Lebanon:** Often **rose water** and/or orange-blossom water; mastic optional; cooked on the stovetop to béchamel thickness.
*   **Palestine (*aruz bil-halib*):** Similar flavourings; frequently topped with **cinnamon** as well as nuts.
*   **Without mastic or flower water:** Some cooks use vanilla, converging with Western rice pudding.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Syrian mastic and orange-blossom**, scaled from a published Syrian recipe (2 L milk : 170 g rice : 170 g sugar : 1 g mastic).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Full-fat milk | 1 L |
| Sugar | 85 g |
| Mastic tears, pounded with 1 tsp sugar | 0.5 g |
| Orange-blossom water (or rose water) | 1 tbsp |
| Pinch of salt | — |
| Crushed pistachios, to finish | 30 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice (Egyptian/calrose), rinsed | 85 g |
| Water to pre-cook rice | 250 ml |
| Milk : rice | ≈ 12 : 1 by weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Pre-cook:** Simmer the rice in the water, covered, until it is absorbed (**12–15 min**).
2. **Milk:** Add the milk and bring to a gentle boil. Lower the heat and simmer, stirring often (especially across the bottom), for **30–40 min** until it is thick like béchamel.
3. **Sweeten:** Add the sugar and the pounded mastic and stir 5 min more, until dissolved.
4. **Perfume:** Off the heat, stir in the orange-blossom water.
5. **Set:** Pour into bowls, cool, then chill **3–4 h**.
6. **Serve:** Top with pistachios. [Aleppo *mbattan*: once set, pour over a cooled orange layer (600 ml juice + zest + 100 g sugar + 30 g cornflour, boiled until thick) and chill again.]
