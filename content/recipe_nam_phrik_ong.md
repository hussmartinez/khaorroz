# Nam Phrik Ong / น้ำพริกอ่อง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna (Chiang Mai, Lamphun, Lampang, Chiang Rai).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: an orange-red, thick, sweet-sour relish of minced pork and tomato cooked with a dried-chilli paste and fermented soybean (*thua nao*); spooned onto sticky rice with raw or steamed vegetables and pork rind.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Stone mortar for the paste and a wok (*kratha*) over charcoal; sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip* / *kong khao*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Nam phrik ong is a northern Thai dish of ground pork and tomato, eaten with sticky rice, pork and fresh vegetables such as cucumber and cabbage, sometimes with pork rinds ([Wikipedia – Nam phrik ong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_phrik_ong)). Its paste is dried chilli, salt, garlic, shallot and shrimp paste, with *thua nao* (fermented soybean disks) giving the northern character; recipes note that if thua nao is unavailable a little shrimp paste can stand in ([Food Network Solution – นํ้าพริกอ่อง](https://www.foodnetworksolution.com/cookbook/item/0002/น้ำพริกอ่อง); [Bangkok Biznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/951905)). Spice levels vary widely between households ([Wikibooks – Nam Phrik Ong](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook:Nam_Phrik_Ong_(Thai_Pork_and_Tomato_Dip))).

**Culinary science:** frying the paste first blooms the chilli and fermented-soy aromas in fat; cooking down the tomatoes evaporates their water and concentrates acidity and sugar into a thick, spoonable sauce.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Chiang Mai:** sweet-sour, with thua nao ([Food Network Solution](https://www.foodnetworksolution.com/cookbook/item/0002/น้ำพริกอ่อง)).
*   **Beef version (nam phrik ong nuea):** documented as a variant ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=22584)).
*   **Thua nao khaep relish:** a related Lanna relish of fermented soybean ([Thai PBS – Krua Naree](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/NaReeKraJang/watch/56781)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Chiang Mai pork nam phrik ong.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried chillies, soaked | 8 |
| Shallots / garlic | 5 / 6 cloves |
| Thua nao (fermented soybean disk), toasted | 1 disk |
| Shrimp paste / salt | 1 tsp / ½ tsp |
| Minced pork | 250 g |
| Small tomatoes, chopped | 300 g |
| Oil | 2 tbsp |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Paste : pork : tomato | ≈ 3 tbsp : 250 g : 300 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Paste:** pound chillies with salt, then shallot, garlic, thua nao and shrimp paste.
3. **Cook:** fry the paste in oil until fragrant, add pork and stir until cooked, add tomatoes and simmer until thick (10–15 min).
4. **Serve:** with sticky rice, cucumber, cabbage, long beans and pork rind.
