# Kaeng Hang Le / แกงฮังเล

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a Burmese (or Shan) origin is widely accepted but framed differently by sources; regional (Lampang vs Chiang Mai) differences were not documented in detail.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna: Lampang (where Burmese and Shan communities settled), Chiang Mai, Phrae.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a rich, dark-red, oily pork-belly curry with no coconut milk, sweet-sour with tamarind and palm sugar, fragrant with dry spices, ginger and pickled garlic; unusually for the north it is eaten with plain non-glutinous rice as often as with sticky rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot or clay pot (*mo din*) over a slow charcoal fire; rice cooked in a pot or steamed in a *huad*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Lampang in its founding era had Shan, Burmese and other communities living together, and *kaeng hang le* is cited as a dish northerners adopted from that mix ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/706391/)). Reference works call it a rich northern Thai pork curry related to Burmese pork curries, possibly arriving via the Shan; Chiang Mai restaurants often call it "Burmese curry", and older accounts derive the name from Burmese *hin lay* ([Wikipedia – Kaeng hang le](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_hang_le)). It differs from typical Lanna curries by using dried spices and by often being eaten with plain rice instead of sticky rice ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_hang_le)). Academic work treats it as a festive dish of northern rituals ([TCI journal article – "Kaeng hang le", culture and beliefs](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/var/article/view/213511)); Austin Bush documents its preparation ([Austin Bush – How to make kaeng hang lay](https://www.austinbushphotography.com/blog/blog/how-to-make-kaeng-hang-lay.html)).

**Culinary science:** long, slow braising of pork belly renders fat, which then carries the fat-soluble aromas of the dry spices; tamarind and palm sugar build the characteristic sweet-sour gloss as the liquid reduces.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Lampang:** linked to the city's Burmese and Shan heritage ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/706391/)).
*   **Chiang Mai:** sold as "Burmese curry" in markets and restaurants ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_hang_le)).
*   **Hang le powder versions:** modern recipes use ready-made curry powder ([Wongnai – hang lay curry](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/hang-lay-curry)). ⚠️

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Chiang Mai/Lampang pork-belly kaeng hang le, served with plain rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pork belly (and some shoulder), cubed | 800 g |
| Curry paste: dried chillies, shallots, garlic, lemongrass, galangal, shrimp paste | ≈ 5 tbsp |
| Hang le / masala powder | 1 tbsp |
| Turmeric | 1 tsp |
| Ginger, julienned | 40 g |
| Pickled garlic | 1 head |
| Tamarind pulp / palm sugar / fish sauce | 3 tbsp / 2 tbsp / 2 tbsp |
| Jasmine rice (or sticky rice) | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice | 300 g raw, cooked in 450 ml water (or steamed sticky rice) |
| Water for braising | ≈ 700 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Marinate:** rub the pork with the paste, hang le powder and turmeric; marinate 1 h.
2. **Braise:** fry the pork until the paste smells cooked, add water and simmer gently 1½–2 h until tender.
3. **Season:** add ginger, pickled garlic, tamarind, palm sugar and fish sauce; reduce until oily and glossy.
4. **Rice:** rinse the jasmine rice and cook it covered in 450 ml water for 15 min, then rest 10 min (or steam sticky rice in a *huad*); serve the curry with the rice.
