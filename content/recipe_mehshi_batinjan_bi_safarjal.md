# Mehshi Batinjan bi-Safarjal (Aleppo Jewish Stuffed Eggplant with Quince) / محشي باذنجان بالسفرجل

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** recipe from Poopa Dweck's *Aromas of Aleppo: The Legendary Cuisine of Syrian Jews*, as adapted in the Canadian Jewish News (2009). It documents the Aleppo-Jewish community and its diaspora (Brooklyn); Muslim and Christian Aleppine kitchens make other stuffed eggplants (see [mahshi halabi](recipe_mahshi_halabi.md)). Hebrew name: מחשי בטנג'ן.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Aleppo's Jewish community and its diaspora (Brooklyn, Deal NJ, Mexico City, Panama, Israel).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vegetables (*mehshi*). Tiny eggplants filled with *hashu* (raw rice, ground beef, allspice and cinnamon), layered with quince and braised in a sweet-sour tamarind sauce until the rice is soft and the sauce glossy.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide ovenproof pot, weighted with a heatproof plate, started on the stove and finished in a low oven.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Poopa Dweck calls Aleppo "the queen of mehshis", and in the Aleppo-Jewish kitchen the stuffing is **hashu**: soaked short-grain rice kneaded raw with ground meat, allspice and cinnamon. The sauce marks the community's signature: **tamarind concentrate** (*ou*), used where neighbouring Arab kitchens reach for pomegranate molasses. **Rice** is central to the Aleppian Passover table, because Sephardi and Mizrahi Jews eat rice at Passover while most Eastern European Jews do not. A Passover meal "will typically include several mehshis" stuffed with hashu ([CJN – Some traditional Syrian recipes, 2009](https://thecjn.ca/arts-culture/traditional-syrian-recipes)). The fruity-sour sauces of Aleppo's Jews are often linked to Persian influence on the community ([Tablet – Yebra](https://100jewishfoods.tabletmag.com/yebra)); see also [yebra](recipe_yebra.md).

Dweck's parents left Aleppo in 1947, after the anti-Jewish violence that followed the UN partition vote, and the community's recipes were preserved in its diaspora cookbooks.

**Culinary science:** the rice goes into the eggplant **raw** (only soaked), so it absorbs the tamarind sauce as it braises. Starting on the stove under a weight makes the eggplants release water before the sauce is added; finishing in a low oven reduces the sauce to a glaze without scorching.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Aleppo-Jewish, Passover (Dweck):** Tiny eggplants, hashu, quince, tamarind-lemon-sugar sauce; oven-braised.
*   **Other Aleppo-Jewish mehshis with hashu:** stuffed zucchini, onions ([mehshi basal](recipe_mehshi_basal.md)), and carrots (*mehshi jazar*, sometimes with prune butter instead of tamarind).
*   **Leftover hashu:** rolled into 2-inch balls and cooked in the same sauce.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Aleppo-Jewish Passover mehshi** (Dweck via CJN, halved).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Very small eggplants, cored | 12 |
| Quinces, peeled, cored, in 6 pieces each | 1½ |
| Tamarind concentrate (*ou*) | 2 tbsp |
| Lemon juice | 2 tbsp |
| Sugar | 1 tbsp |
| Salt | ½ tsp |
| Water | 120 ml + to ¾ cover |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain white rice, soaked 30 min and drained (hashu) | 65 g (⅓ cup) per 450 g meat; use 130 g |
| Ground beef | 900 g |
| Allspice / cinnamon | 2 tsp / 2 tsp |
| Vegetable oil | 4 tbsp |
| Salt / white pepper | 2 tsp / ½ tsp |
| Pine nuts and chopped onion (optional) | 1 cup / 1 small |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Hashu:** Mix the soaked rice, beef, spices, oil, salt, pepper (and optional onion and pine nuts).
2. **Stuff:** Fill the cored eggplants loosely with hashu (the rice expands).
3. **Layer:** In an ovenproof pot, alternate quince pieces and stuffed eggplants.
4. **Sauce:** Mix the tamarind, lemon, sugar, salt and 120 ml water; pour over, cover with a heatproof plate and cook on medium-low 10 min.
5. **Simmer:** Off the heat, add water to cover three-quarters of the eggplants; bring to a boil, then simmer covered 30 min until a quarter of the liquid remains.
6. **Oven:** Braise at 150 °C (300 °F) for 45 min covered, then 30 min uncovered. Serve warm.
