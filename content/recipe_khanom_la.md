# Khanom La / ขนมลา

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish, its tools and its festival role are well sourced (Wikipedia, Thai Government PR Department, Thai PBS, Thai academic studies of Pak Phanang producers). No detailed producer recipe with quantities was accessible, so the batter proportions are reconstructed. The meanings of two of the five festival sweets differ between sources.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Nakhon Si Thammarat, especially the villages of **Pak Phanang** and **Hua Sai**, and the Thai-Buddhist south in general.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** A rice-flour lace: a thin batter of rice flour, palm sugar and egg yolk is drizzled in fine threads onto a hot, oiled pan to form a gauzy golden sheet, then folded. There are two styles: soft and pliable (***la chet***) or sun-dried and crisp (***la krop***).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A shallow iron pan over a low charcoal fire, and a **coconut shell pierced with tiny holes** (*kala*), now often a tin can with holes, through which the batter is sprinkled.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khanom la is the most famous of the **five Sat Duean Sip sweets**. Sat Duean Sip is the southern festival of the tenth lunar month, when ancestors (*pret*) are believed to return from the underworld for half a month. Their descendants receive them on the first waning day and send them back on the fifteenth with offerings at the temple, the *hmrap* (Thai PBS; PRD). Each sweet stands for something the dead will need:
- **khanom la:** clothing. Its fine threads also allow *pret*, whose mouths are "the size of a needle's eye", to eat.
- **khanom phong** (puffed sticky rice): a raft or vehicle.
- **khanom kong / khai pla:** jewellery.
- **khanom dee sam** (*khanom rhu*): money, from its coin-like hole. ⚠️ Some sources give it a toy (*saba*) meaning instead.
- **khanom ba:** saba seeds for games, or money. ⚠️ Sources differ.

The name may come from ***kala***, the coconut shell drilled with holes to make the lace (Wikipedia). Once made only for the festival, khanom la is now a year-round product of Nakhon community enterprises.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Pak Phanang (Nakhon Si Thammarat):** The historic producing villages, known for *la chet* folded into semicircles.
*   **La krop:** The soft sheet is sprinkled with extra sugar and sun-dried until crisp. It's sold rolled or in fans.
*   **Rolled method:** A thicker batter with more oil is wound round a stick, dried and slipped off.
*   **Hua Sai (Ban Tha Som):** Community-enterprise production for gift packaging.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **la chet**, with the batter reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Fine rice flour | 250 g |
| Palm sugar (*nam tan to'nd*), melted with a little water | 150 g |
| Duck-egg yolks | 2 |
| Water | about 200 ml |
| Oil, for greasing the pan | 80 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Batter consistency | thin enough to stream in threads (like single cream) |
| Sheets per batch | about 20 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Batter:** Knead the rice flour with the palm-sugar syrup, then whisk in the egg yolks and water to a smooth, thin batter. Rest **2–3 hours**, then strain.
2. **Pan:** Heat a shallow pan over low charcoal or gas and wipe it with an oiled cloth.
3. **Lace:** Pour batter into the pierced coconut shell or can and swirl it over the pan in fast circles so fine threads criss-cross into a lace sheet (**about 30 seconds**).
4. **Fold:** Lift the sheet with a thin stick while it's still pliable and fold it into a semicircle or fan.
5. **Crisp (optional):** For *la krop*, sprinkle with sugar and dry the sheets in the sun until brittle.
