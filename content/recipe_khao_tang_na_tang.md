# Khao Tang Na Tang / ข้าวตังหน้าตั้ง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** described by Thai Airways and others as a once-exclusive royal recipe. No dated court source was found. The dish is also claimed by Cambodia as *nataing*, and that origin is contested; it is presented here as a shared tradition. The "run full tilt" etymology is a folk story. Quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok and the court kitchens of the Central Plains; now an everyday snack across Thailand.
*   **Historical Source:** Rattanakosin court snack tradition (oral; cited in David Thompson's *Thai Food*); no single dated text found.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Court snack: **crisp rice crackers** (*khao tang*, rice dried and fried until puffed and shattering) with a rich, sweet-salty dip of coconut cream, minced pork, prawns and peanuts.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A brass pan or wok (*krata*) for the dip, a wide pan whose scorched base yields the crust, and a charcoal grill or deep oil for crisping.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khao tang* is the **rice crust scraped from the bottom of the pot**. In thrifty households and palace kitchens alike it was dried in the sun, then grilled or fried until crisp. Paired with a coconut-cream dip of pork and prawns (*na tang*), it became a refined snack that, according to Thai Airways' guide to rice snacks, began as an exclusive royal recipe before spreading to the whole country ([Thai Airways – Thai rice snacks](https://sawasdee.thaiairways.com/thai-rice-snacks/)). Cambodia knows a near-identical dish, **nataing**, and both countries claim it as royal heritage ([Wikipedia – Nataing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nataing); [Grantourismo](https://grantourismotravels.com/natang-recipe-pork-coconut-peanut-dip/)).

**Culinary science:** dried cooked rice holds trapped moisture in gelatinised starch. In hot oil that water flashes to steam and puffs the grains into a porous, glassy crisp. The dip is cooked down until the coconut cream "cracks" and releases its oil, which makes it glossy and keeps it stable at room temperature.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Court style (Bangkok):** pork and prawn with coconut cream, shallot, coriander root, peanuts, palm sugar and fish sauce; crackers from scorched pot crust.
*   **Modern street and packaged versions:** commercially made round crackers, a sweeter dip, sometimes chicken.
*   **Cambodian *nataing* (cross-border cousin):** similar pork-coconut-peanut dip, often redder with dried chili ([Grantourismo](https://grantourismotravels.com/natang-recipe-pork-coconut-peanut-dip/)).
*   **Vegetarian/jackfruit version:** modern adaptations replace the pork ([Wholesomm](https://www.wholesomm.com/vegetarian-jackfruit-na-tang/)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **court-style pork and prawn dip** with home-made crackers.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Coconut cream | 300 ml |
| Minced pork | 200 g |
| Prawns, peeled and minced | 100 g |
| Shallots, sliced | 3 |
| Garlic + coriander root + white peppercorns, pounded | 1 tbsp paste |
| Roasted peanuts, crushed | 60 g |
| Palm sugar | 2 tbsp |
| Fish sauce | 2 tbsp |
| Coriander leaf and red chili to garnish | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Cooked jasmine rice for crackers | 400 g (or 200 g bought rice crackers) |
| Oil for frying | 750 ml |
| Dip per person | ≈ 150 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Crackers:** press cooked rice into a thin layer on a tray (or use the scorched crust of the pot). Cut it into rounds and sun-dry or oven-dry it at 90 °C for 3–4 h until hard.
2. **Fry:** deep-fry the dried rounds at 180 °C for a few seconds until puffed and pale gold. Drain.
3. **Crack the cream:** simmer half the coconut cream until the oil separates. Fry the garlic paste and shallots in it until fragrant.
4. **Cook the dip:** add the pork and prawns and stir until cooked. Add the rest of the cream, palm sugar, fish sauce and peanuts, and simmer to a thick, glossy dip (8–10 min).
5. **Serve:** put the dip in a bowl in the centre of a platter of crackers, garnished with coriander and chili.
