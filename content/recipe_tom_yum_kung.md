# Tom Yum Kung / ต้มยำกุ้ง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Early-history details vary between official summaries.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand (riverside communities of the Chao Phraya); national dish.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a hot and sour prawn soup with lemongrass, galangal, makrut lime leaf, lime juice and chilies, served in a pot at the centre of a rice meal.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot or a charcoal-heated hot-pot (*mo fai*); clay pot (*mo din*) for serving.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
On 3 December 2024 UNESCO inscribed Tom Yum Kung on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, Thailand's fifth element after Khon, Nuad Thai, Nora and Songkran; the nomination stressed the riverside farming communities of central Thailand and their knowledge of herbs ([Thai government](https://thailand.go.th/issue-focus-detail/tom-yum-kung-inscribed-as-unescos-intangible-cultural-heritage-of-humanity?hl=en); [Khaosod English](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/life/food/2024/12/05/tom-yum-kung-thailands-iconic-soup-achieves-unesco-heritage-status/)). Reports trace earlier fish-based versions to the late 19th century, the prawn version appearing in later royal cookbooks ([The Investor](https://theinvestor.vn/thai-soup-tom-yum-kung-becomes-new-unesco-intangible-cultural-heritage-d13716.html)).

In a Thai meal the soup is not a starter: it is set on the table with the other dishes and spooned over or beside rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Nam sai (clear):** the older, clear broth.
*   **Nam khon (creamy):** with evaporated milk or coconut milk and *nam phrik phao*, the modern Bangkok style.
*   **Tom yum pla / kai:** with fish or chicken instead of prawns.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| River prawns (with heads) | 500 g |
| Water or prawn-head stock | 1 L |
| Lemongrass, galangal, makrut lime leaves | 3 stalks, 6 slices, 6 |
| Straw mushrooms | 150 g |
| Bird's-eye chilies, bruised | 6–10 |
| Fish sauce, lime juice | 3 tbsp, 4 tbsp |
| Nam phrik phao (optional, for nam khon) | 1 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) | 400 g, rinsed (+ 520 ml water, absorption method) |
| Soup per person | ≈ 300 ml |
| Rice per person | ≈ 180 g cooked |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Make a quick stock from the prawn heads; strain.
2. Boil the stock with lemongrass, galangal and lime leaves for 3 min.
3. Add mushrooms, then prawns; cook 1–2 min.
4. Off the heat, season with fish sauce, lime juice and chilies (lime is added off the heat to keep it fresh).
5. Cook the rice: rinse 400 g jasmine rice until the water runs nearly clear, add 520 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 min, then rest 10 min covered (or use an electric rice cooker / *mo hung khao*).
6. Serve in a pot at the centre of the table with rice.
