# Khao Plee (Fried Rice Baked in a Banana Blossom) / ข้าวปลี

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The dish comes from *Tamrap Sai Yaowapa* (ตำรับสายเยาวภา, 1935), the court cookbook printed for the cremation of Princess Yaovabha Bongsanid. The dish and its ingredients (jasmine fried rice with tamarind relish, semi-salted fish, amaranth, eggplant and carrot, stuffed into a char-grilled banana blossom and baked, with milk and spinach unusually present) are attested through the Thaifoodmaster archive's description of the recipe. The archive's full quantities and steps are paywalled, so the amounts and the role given to milk below are a reconstruction.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The princely households of Bangkok (the court circle of King Chulalongkorn's daughters), 1900s–1930s.
*   **Historical Source:** *Tamrap Sai Yaowapa* (ตำรับสายเยาวภา), compiled in memory of Princess Yaovabha Bongsanid, edited by Phraya Sihasak Sanitwong, B.E. 2478 (1935), Bangkok
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Court fried rice served in a vegetable vessel. Sour, salty and sweet jasmine fried rice packed into the hollowed heart of a smoky, charred banana blossom, then baked, so that the rice takes on the blossom's smokiness.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok over charcoal for the rice; the banana blossom grilled directly on embers; a charcoal or early Western-style oven for baking.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Tamrap Sai Yaowapa* belongs to a distinctively Thai genre: the cremation-volume cookbook, handed out as a keepsake at a royal or noble funeral. *Silpa Wattanatham* describes it as a book of court food compiled for the royal cremation of Princess Yaovabha Bongsanid, a daughter of King Chulalongkorn, in 1935, edited by Phraya Sihasak Sanitwong. It gathers savoury and sweet recipes of her household, relatives and attendants, and stands among the earliest printed collections of palace cooking ([Silpa-mag – the origins of *Sai Yaowapa*](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=42563)). Thailand's first cookbook, *Mae Khrua Hua Pa*, had appeared in 1908 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Khrua_Hua_Pa)).

*Khao plee* (*plee* = banana blossom) shows the Fifth-Reign court's taste for novelty. The Thaifoodmaster archive describes it as "a dish of stuffed banana blossom with sour, salty, sweet, and fragrant jasmine fried rice", fried with tamarind relish, semi-salted fish, amaranth, eggplant and carrot. It notes that milk and spinach are "not usually associated with Siamese cuisine", while the blossom is char-grilled and the whole is baked ([Thaifoodmaster – c1935 khaao bplee fried rice](https://thaifoodmaster.com/courses/c1935-khaao-bplee-fried-rice?lang=th)). Carrots, spinach and dairy were Western ingredients that palace households were adopting in those decades.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Princess Yaovabha's household (1935):** Banana blossom stuffed with tamarind-relish fried rice, salted fish and vegetables, then baked.
*   **Other court fried rices of the same era:** *Khao phat sam si* (three-colour fried rice, also *Sai Yaowapa* 1935) and the 1940s palace fried rices; see [khao phat chao wang](recipe_khao_phat_chao_wang.md).
*   **Modern descendants:** Restaurant "royal Thai" menus revive vessel-served fried rices (in pineapple, in young coconut) ⚠️ (no direct line from *khao plee* is documented).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern reconstruction for 4 banana-blossom "cups" (or 2 large blossoms halved).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Tamarind pulp, dissolved in 60 ml warm water and strained | 40 g |
| Palm sugar | 25 g |
| Fish sauce | 25 ml |
| Shallots, sliced | 60 g |
| Garlic, chopped | 20 g |
| Dried chillies, soaked and pounded | 3 |
| Semi-salted fish (*pla khem*), soaked, flaked | 120 g |
| Neutral oil | 50 ml |
| Milk (as in the archive's description; added at the end) | 50 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Day-old cooked jasmine rice | 700 g |
| Banana blossoms | 2 large |
| Amaranth or spinach leaves, chopped | 100 g |
| Thai round eggplants, diced | 120 g |
| Carrot, small dice | 80 g |
| Lime | 1 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Char the blossoms:** Strip the tough outer bracts and grill the blossoms over embers or a gas flame, turning, until blackened outside and tender inside (10–12 min). Cool, peel off the burnt layers, halve lengthwise, and hollow each half into a cup, rubbing it with lime.
2. **Make the relish:** Fry the shallots, garlic and pounded chilli in the oil until golden. Add the tamarind water, palm sugar and fish sauce and reduce to a thick, glossy relish (3–4 min).
3. **Fry the fish and vegetables:** Add the flaked salted fish, carrot and eggplant and stir-fry for 3 minutes. Add the amaranth or spinach until just wilted.
4. **Fry the rice:** Add the rice and toss over high heat until hot and evenly coated. Splash in the milk and toss until absorbed, so the rice turns glossy rather than wet.
5. **Stuff and bake:** Pack the rice into the blossom cups and bake at 200 °C for 10–12 minutes, until the tops crisp slightly and smell smoky. Serve in the blossoms.
