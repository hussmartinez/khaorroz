# Kousa bi-Laban (Hebron Zucchini in Yogurt) / الكوسا باللبن الخليلية

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** al-Khalil (Hebron) and its villages; also Jerusalem and Lebanon, where it is widely cooked.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vegetables (*mahashi*) in yogurt. Small zucchini stuffed with rice and meat, poached, then simmered in a hot, silky yogurt (or *jameed*) sauce finished with fried garlic, mint and pine nuts.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep pot on the stove. The yogurt is stirred in one direction so it doesn't split.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Hebron is Palestine's dairy and *jameed* country. The UAE newspaper *Al-Ittihad* presents *kousa bi-laban* as "a dish from the heritage of the Palestinian city of Hebron". It explains that cooking rice and meat with yogurt is a Hebron custom: the city is famous for *laban maʿqud* (cooked, thickened yogurt), Hebron mansaf, and a jameed from its villages considered "the best of all". Hebron versions may use **jameed** (dried yogurt), as reference sources note for the related zucchini dish *sheikh al-mahshi*. The yogurt is stabilised with starch or flour and stirred constantly, the same technique as the yogurt sauces of [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md) and [shakriyeh](recipe_shakriyeh.md).

The dish is the yogurt twin of the tomato-cooked [mahshi](recipe_mahshi.md). Lebanese cooks make an almost identical *kousa bi-laban*, so this file links both regions.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Hebron (al-Khalil):** Fresh yogurt or buttermilk (*laban makhid*), or village **jameed**; dried and fresh mint; pine nuts; often veal shank in the broth.
*   **Jerusalem (⚠️):** Lighter yogurt sauce, more mint and garlic.
*   **Lebanon:** Same structure, cow's-milk yogurt with egg or starch, dried mint and garlic.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Hebron (after the Al-Ittihad recipe by chef Radwan Abu Zaki, halved).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Small zucchini (kousa) | 1 kg |
| Coarse minced meat | 200 g |
| Egyptian or short-grain rice | 65 g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 40 g |
| Garlic, finely chopped | 20 g (divided) |
| Dried + fresh mint | 12 g |
| Pine nuts | 10 g (divided) |
| Cinnamon, allspice, white pepper, salt | 1 g / 1 g / pinch / to taste |
| Full-fat yogurt (or reconstituted jameed) | 750 g |
| Cornflour or flour | 15 g |
| Oil | 10 g |
| Stock or water | ≈ 500 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Stuffing per zucchini | ¾ full (rice swells) |
| Yogurt : stock | ≈ 3 : 2 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Core:** Core the zucchini and soak them in salted water for 20 minutes.
2. **Filling:** Sauté the onion and half the garlic, stir in the rice briefly, then mix with the meat, spices, salt and dried mint. Fold in half the pine nuts, toasted.
3. **Stuff and poach:** Stuff the zucchini, leaving 1 cm of space. Simmer in the stock until the zucchini and rice are cooked (≈ 30 min).
4. **Yogurt:** Whisk the yogurt with the starch and salt. Heat gently, stirring in one direction, for about 10 minutes until it boils.
5. **Combine:** Add the zucchini and some of their stock to the yogurt with the dried mint and simmer 5 minutes.
6. **Finish:** Fry the remaining garlic and pine nuts, add fresh mint, and pour over. Serve with vermicelli rice.
