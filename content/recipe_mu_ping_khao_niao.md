# Mu Ping Khao Niao / หมูปิ้งข้าวเหนียว

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok and the whole of Thailand, at morning street stalls.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sticky-rice pairing: a small bag of steamed sticky rice eaten with sweet, garlicky grilled pork skewers — Thailand's grab-and-go breakfast.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A long charcoal grill (*tao than*) for the skewers; sticky rice steamed in a *huad* and kept warm in a *kratip* basket or banana leaf.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Thai cooks describe *mu ping* with sticky rice as a near-national breakfast because it is easy to eat on the move and filling ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=22031); [Pholfoodmafia – cocoa mu ping](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=38188)). Stalls open early alongside grilled chicken and jok ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/photos/333f8e254f754438b97341bdff42d861)), and vendors keep reinventing it — one built a brand wrapping it in small banana-leaf parcels ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=1568)). ⚠️ No source dates its origin.

**Culinary science:** a marinade of palm sugar, garlic, coriander root, soy and coconut milk caramelises on charcoal; brushing with coconut milk while grilling keeps the pork juicy.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Bangkok stall standard:** pork shoulder slices with fatty bits, brushed with coconut milk ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=22031)).
*   **Novelty marinades:** e.g. cocoa mu ping ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=38188)).
*   **Banana-leaf packs:** sticky rice and pork wrapped together ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=1568)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok morning stall**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pork shoulder/collar, thinly sliced | 600 g |
| Garlic, coriander root, white pepper (pounded) | 6 cloves, 3, 1 tsp |
| Palm sugar / oyster sauce / soy / fish sauce | 3 tbsp / 2 tbsp / 1 tbsp / 1 tbsp |
| Coconut milk (marinade + basting) | 120 ml |
| Bamboo skewers | 16–20 |
| Glutinous rice (*khao niao*) | 400 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 400 g raw, soaked overnight |
| Skewers per person | 4–5 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Soak:** soak the glutinous rice 6–12 h; drain.
2. **Marinate:** mix the pounded paste, sugar, sauces and half the coconut milk; marinate the pork 2 h or overnight; thread onto skewers.
3. **Steam the rice:** steam in a *huad* over boiling water 25–30 min, flipping once; keep warm in a *kratip*.
4. **Grill:** grill over medium charcoal, turning and brushing with coconut milk, until caramelised (8–10 min).
5. **Serve:** a fist of sticky rice in a bag or banana leaf with the skewers and a chili-tamarind dip.
