# Musluʿa Nabulsiyya (Nablus Lentils and Rice) / المسلوعة النابلسية

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a **little-documented Nablus dish**. *Asharq Al-Awsat*, reporting on Mirna Bamieh's research into forgotten Palestinian dishes, defines *al-musluʿa* as "a winter dish of lentils and rice" from Nablus. An Egyptian press list of city dishes adds that it is lentils and rice topped with a fried *sabb* (a fried onion-and-oil topping); its Arabic spelling there is uncertain (one source also gives *al-madluqa*). The distinction from the mujaddara family (see §2), the texture and the quantities are reconstructed and should be verified with Nablus cooks.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Nablus.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice and legumes, a winter dish. Brown lentils and rice cooked together until soft and creamy, finished with a sizzling fried topping.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot on the stove; a small frying pan for the topping.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Lentils-and-rice dishes are everywhere in Bilad al-Sham. [Mujaddara](recipe_mujaddara.md) is the best known, with separate grains under crisp onions. Its puréed, porridge-like Lebanese cousin and Nablus *musluʿa* are the softer, spoonable forms. The name comes from the root *s-l-ʿ* and suggests something cooked down until it "gives way" ⚠️ (etymology unconfirmed). Bamieh lists *musluʿa* among the dishes she is documenting before they "fall out of memory", next to the Nablus stuffed leaves [lakhneh](recipe_lakhneh.md). As a meatless dish it suits both the Christian Lenten calendar and poor winter households. It is the Nablus counterpart of the Vega Baja's meatless [arroz de los tres puñaos](recipe_arroz_de_los_tres_punaos.md).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Nablus (documented):** Lentils and rice, a winter dish, with a fried topping.
*   **Mujaddara / mudardara (Palestine, Lebanon, Syria):** Separate grains and crisp onions. See [mujaddara](recipe_mujaddara.md).
*   **Nazareth:** Local lists associate a soft, mashed lentil-and-rice pot with Nazareth under the name *mujaddara* ⚠️.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: reconstructed Nablus musluʿa.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Brown lentils, rinsed | 250 g |
| Short-grain rice, rinsed | 150 g |
| Water | 1.6 L |
| Ground cumin | 1½ tsp |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |
| **Topping (sabb):** olive oil, onions sliced, garlic | 80 ml / 2 / 2 cloves |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lentils : rice | ≈ 5 : 3 by weight |
| Liquid | ≈ 4 × the dry weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Lentils:** Simmer the lentils in the water for 20 minutes until starting to soften.
2. **Rice:** Add the rice, cumin and salt and cook on low, stirring often, for 25–30 minutes until both collapse into a thick, creamy pot. Add hot water if needed.
3. **Mash:** Beat with a wooden spoon (or briefly with a hand blender) for a smoother texture.
4. **Topping:** Fry the onions in the oil until deep golden, adding the garlic for the last minute.
5. **Serve:** Spoon into bowls, pour the hot oil and onions over, and serve with radishes, olives and pickles.
