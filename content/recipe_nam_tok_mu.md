# Nam Tok Mu / น้ำตกหมู

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the origin of the name ("waterfall") is popularly explained by meat juices dripping onto the coals, but that explanation comes from a forum, not an authoritative source.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan and Laos; now standard in Isan restaurants nationwide.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: sliced grilled pork (often neck) dressed like larb with lime, fish sauce, chilli, shallot, mint and toasted rice; juicy, charred slices rather than mince. Eaten with sticky rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Charcoal grill (*tao than*), stone mortar for the toasted rice, sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Nam tok is larb's grilled cousin: cooks note that the two are "very similar", differing in the cut — minced meat makes larb, slices make nam tok ([Pholfoodmafia – grilled pork-neck nam tok](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=26087)). English reference works list *nam tok mu* as an Isan dish ([Wikipedia – Nam tok mu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nam_tok_mu)). The name is usually explained by the juices that drip from the grilling meat onto the coals, but this is folk explanation ⚠️ ([Ruean Thai forum](https://www.tapatalk.com/groups/rueanthai2/-t4146435.html)). Toasted rice is essential, about 1½ tbsp for 300 g of meat; toasted sesame is a modern substitute ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=26087); [Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/a65a54ddef524dcbb926f51cd5d29127)).

**Culinary science:** grilling a fatty cut such as neck renders fat and chars the surface; slicing it thinly and dressing it while warm lets the acidic dressing penetrate the meat.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Kho mu yang (grilled neck):** the classic cut ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=26087)).
*   **Fried jowl version:** a modern variant ([Pholfoodmafia – fried jowl nam tok](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=39935)).
*   **Beef nam tok (*nam tok nuea*):** common in Isan and Laos.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: grilled pork-neck nam tok.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pork neck | 500 g |
| Fish sauce | 2½ tbsp |
| Lime juice | 3 tbsp |
| Ground dried chilli | 1–2 tsp |
| Khao kua (toasted ground rice) | 2 tbsp |
| Shallots, sliced / mint / spring onion / sawtooth coriander | 3 / 1 handful |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Khao kua : meat | 1½ tbsp : 300 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Grill:** salt the pork neck lightly and grill over medium charcoal until charred outside and just cooked (12–15 min); rest 5 min and slice thinly.
3. **Dress:** toss the warm slices with fish sauce, lime and chilli, then the shallots and herbs.
4. **Finish:** add the khao kua last; serve with sticky rice and raw vegetables.
