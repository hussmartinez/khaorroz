# Khao Lam / ข้าวหลาม

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Across Thailand. It is a temple and festival food in Lanna and Isan, and a famous roadside speciality at Nong Mon (Chonburi) in the east, with bamboo from Kanchanaburi and Tak.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sticky rice roasted in bamboo. Glutinous rice, coconut milk, salt and sugar are packed into a bamboo tube and roasted in the embers until set; it is eaten by peeling away the bamboo to reveal a sweet-salty rice log wrapped in a fine papery membrane.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Fresh bamboo tubes (thin-walled species with a soft inner membrane), plugged with coconut husk or banana leaf, stood or laid on a long charcoal or wood fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Cooking in a bamboo tube (*lam*) is thought by food archaeologists to be one of the oldest cooking methods in mainland Southeast Asia. The earliest versions probably used only water, and coconut milk and sugar came later and turned the dish into a sweet ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2654618)). In the **north**, *khao lam* was offered to monks at the full moon of the fourth lunar month. Elsewhere it became a travellers' gift: **Nong Mon** villagers developed it as extra income from their shrine fair, and the town became synonymous with it ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2654618)). Kanchanaburi bamboo is prized because its walls are thin, its cavity wide and its membrane peels easily ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/1089953/); [Thai PBS – Kid Rangers](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/KidRangers/watch/137500)).

**Culinary science:** the sealed tube works as a pressure-free steamer. The coconut milk is absorbed as the rice gelatinises, and the bamboo's inner membrane detaches and clings to the rice, which makes it easy to peel and adds a faint grassy aroma. Even turning on the fire prevents scorching on one side.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Nong Mon (Chonburi):** A rich mix (about 10 cups of rice to 4 cups of coconut milk plus salt and sugar), with fillings such as black beans, custard, taro, young coconut and banana ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2654618)).
*   **Lanna (north):** Merit-making khao lam for the fourth-month full moon, often with black beans.
*   **Isan:** Roadside khao lam made with local sticky rice and black or red beans. ⚠️ General practice; no Isan-specific source found.
*   **Modern:** Pandan, durian and salted-egg-mushroom fillings, and gas grills replacing wood ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/1089953/)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: classic black-bean khao lam (after Thairath's pandan recipe, scaled to about 6 tubes).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice, soaked 3–5 h | 750 g |
| Coconut milk | 700 ml |
| Sugar | 200 g |
| Salt | 1 tbsp |
| Cooked black beans (soaked 2 h, boiled) | 1 cup |
| Pandan leaves (optional) | 6 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Bamboo tubes | 6 tubes, 30 cm (12 in) |
| Rest before roasting | ≈ 5 h |
| Roasting | 30–60 min, turned often |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Prepare the tubes:** Wash the bamboo and dry it upside down.
2. **Fill:** Mix the drained rice with the black beans and pack into the tubes about three-quarters full.
3. **Coconut milk:** Dissolve the sugar and salt in the coconut milk, then pour slowly into each tube until the rice is just covered, avoiding air pockets. Push in a pandan leaf and plug with coconut husk.
4. **Rest:** Let the tubes stand for **≈ 5 h** so the rice absorbs the liquid.
5. **Roast:** Lean or lay the tubes on a bed of medium charcoal and turn them often for **30–60 min**, until the bamboo is scorched yellow-brown and the rice is set. Cool, then split the bamboo and peel it off to serve.
