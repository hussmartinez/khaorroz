# Khao Puk Nga / ข้าวปุกงา

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna: Phayao, Chiang Mai, Nan and the hill markets. Also written *khao nuk nga* / *khao kluk nga*, and nicknamed *mochi doi* ("mountain mochi").
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Pounded sticky rice with toasted perilla seed. New-crop sticky rice is pounded while hot with roasted *nga khi mon* into a stretchy, nutty, mochi-like mass.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A *huad* steamer for the rice, a dry pan for the seeds, and a large wooden mortar and pestle (*khrok*/*sak*).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khao puk nga* is a cool-season snack. It is made when the new glutinous rice comes in, and it is eaten warm "to keep the body warm". Naewna reports it sold at the Phayao municipal market at weekends ([Naewna](https://www.naewna.com/likesara/843419)). Its other ingredient is *nga khi mon*, perilla (*Perilla frutescens*), which northerners roast and pound with fragrant new rice. What are called its "seeds" are botanically tiny fruits ([HDmall – perilla seed](https://hdmall.co.th/blog/health/perilla-seed/)).

**Culinary science:** pounding hot, freshly steamed amylopectin-rich rice aligns and stretches the starch into an elastic dough, the same principle as Japanese mochi. Roasting perilla releases its aromatic oils, and salt in the seed mix balances the rice's mild sweetness.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Phayao:** Sold warm at the Saturday–Sunday municipal market ([Naewna](https://www.naewna.com/likesara/843419)).
*   **Salty vs sweet:** Some cooks pound in salt only. Others dust the finished pieces with sugar or serve them with palm sugar.
*   **Grilled:** ⚠️ Leftover pieces are sometimes grilled until crisp outside (household practice; no source found).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Lanna salted khao puk nga (proportions after Phol Food Mafia's 150 g base, scaled up).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice (*khiao ngu*), soaked overnight | 400 g |
| Perilla seed (*nga khi mon*) | 60 g |
| Salt | 1 tsp |
| Sugar (optional, to serve) | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice : perilla | ≈ 7 : 1 |
| Pounding | 5–10 min while hot |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Steam:** Steam the soaked rice in a *huad* until fully tender (**25–30 min**; see [khao niao nueng](recipe_khao_niao_nueng.md)).
2. **Roast the seeds:** Dry-toast the perilla over low heat until it pops and smells nutty, then pound coarsely with the salt.
3. **Pound:** While the rice is still very hot, pound it in a mortar, folding in the perilla mixture until the mass is smooth and stretchy.
4. **Shape:** With oiled or wet hands, pull off pieces and flatten them into small cakes.
5. **Serve:** Eat warm, plain or dusted with sugar.
