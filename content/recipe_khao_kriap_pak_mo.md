# Khao Kriap Pak Mo / ข้าวเกรียบปากหม้อ

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Its origin is linked to Vietnamese steamed rice rolls (*pak mo yuan*), mainly from vendors' accounts; no academic history was found.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand and Bangkok markets; also the Mekong towns of Isan (Mukdahan).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice-flour dumplings: a thin rice-flour batter steamed on a cloth stretched over a pot's mouth, folded around a sweet-savoury filling of pork, shallot, peanut and salted radish, finished with fried garlic oil.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot of boiling water with a cloth stretched over its mouth (*pak mo*, "pot mouth") under a domed lid — the same rig as for [khao phan phak](recipe_khao_phan_phak.md).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The name means "crackers from the pot's mouth": the batter is spread on a cloth over boiling water and lifted off with a stick. Thai sources link it to Vietnamese rice-sheet cookery — the variant *pak mo yuan* ("Vietnamese pak mo") is equally popular ([My-Best](https://th.my-best.com/51744)) and in Mukdahan on the Mekong such dishes are common ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/420034/)). Batters are mostly rice flour with some tapioca, sometimes coloured with butterfly pea, pandan or beetroot ([Wongnai recipe](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/steamed-rice-dumpling); [Le Cordon Bleu Dusit](https://www.cordonbleu.edu/thailand/lcbd-kow-greip-pak-mor-workshop/th)).

**Culinary science:** a rice-flour batter with a little tapioca gelatinises in seconds of steam into a sheet that is translucent and elastic enough to fold.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Central (*khao kriap pak mo*):** pork-peanut-radish filling, garlic oil ([GotoKnow](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/266199)).
*   **Pak mo yuan (Mekong / Vietnamese-Thai):** softer rolls with varied fillings ([My-Best](https://th.my-best.com/51744); [Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/420034/)).
*   **Coloured herbal batters:** butterfly pea, pandan, beetroot ([Le Cordon Bleu Dusit](https://www.cordonbleu.edu/thailand/lcbd-kow-greip-pak-mor-workshop/th)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Central pork-peanut filling**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice flour | 120 g |
| Tapioca flour | 2 tbsp |
| Water | ≈ 400 ml |
| Minced pork | 200 g |
| Shallots, chopped | 4 |
| Roasted peanuts, chopped | 50 g |
| Salted radish (*chai po*), chopped | 30 g |
| Palm sugar / fish sauce / white pepper | 2 tbsp / 1 tbsp / ½ tsp |
| Garlic oil + fried garlic | 4 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice-flour batter | ≈ 120 g rice flour per 4 people (≈ 24 dumplings) |
| Filling per dumpling | ≈ 1 heaped tsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Batter:** whisk the flours with water to a thin cream; rest 30 min.
2. **Filling:** fry shallots, add pork, radish, peanuts, sugar, fish sauce and pepper; cook until sticky and dry.
3. **Steam:** stretch a cloth tightly over a pot of boiling water; spread a thin circle of batter, cover 30–40 s until translucent.
4. **Fold:** spoon filling on, fold the sheet with a flat stick, lift onto an oiled plate.
5. **Serve:** brush with garlic oil, scatter fried garlic; eat with chilies and lettuce.
