# Mansaf Halabi (Aleppo Rice and Freekeh Feast) / المنسف الحلبي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented by an Arabic Post recipe feature on Aleppine cooking. No historical or municipal source dates the dish, and differences between Aleppo households are not recorded beyond the optional spices noted in that source. Not to be confused with the Jordanian [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md), which is defined by its jameed sauce.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Aleppo (Halab) and its countryside.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Feast platter of two grains under meat. A pressed bed of smoky green **freekeh**, then a layer of fluffy short-grain **rice**, crowned with boiled lamb and toasted nuts; both grains are cooked in the same lamb broth, so they stay separate but share one flavour.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep pot (*tanjara*) for the lamb and broth, two smaller lidded pots for the grains, and a large round serving tray (*sedr*); stovetop.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Aleppo calls itself "the mother of mahashi and kibbeh" (*حلب أمّ المحاشي والكبب*), and its feast rice reflects the city's other great grain: **freekeh**, young green wheat roasted in the field. Arabic Post's feature on the dish calls the mansaf of lamb, rice and freekeh "one of the most famous dishes of Aleppine cuisine" and recalls that al-Maʿarri's *Risalat al-Ghufran* imagines summoning Aleppo's cooks to a feast in paradise ([Arabic Post – المنسف الحلبي](https://arabicpost.net/%d9%84%d8%a7%d9%8a%d9%81-%d8%b3%d8%aa%d8%a7%d9%8a%d9%84/2019/04/10/%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%85%d9%86%d8%b3%d9%81-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%ad%d9%84%d8%a8%d9%8a/)). Unlike the Bedouin-Jordanian [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md), there is no yogurt sauce: the dish is about the meeting of the two grains and the spiced broth.

The double-grain tray echoes an old Levantine pattern in which wheat (bulgur, freekeh) was the everyday grain and rice the festive one. Placing the rice *on top* of the freekeh shows off the prestige grain while the cheaper, more filling wheat sits underneath. Russell's 18th-century Aleppo already ended feasts with "a large pilaw" ([Ottoman pilav](recipe_pilav_ottoman_aleppo.md)).

**Culinary science:** freekeh absorbs about 1½ times its volume of liquid and keeps a chewy bite; short-grain rice needs a little more and softens faster. Cooking them separately in the *same* broth lets each reach its own texture, and pressing each layer onto the tray makes the platter slice cleanly when served.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Aleppo household version (documented):** Lamb boiled with onion, cinnamon, bay leaf and a pinch of cumin; some cooks add **cloves** or **wild thyme** (*zaʿtar barri*) to the broth. Rice gets a cinnamon stick and a cardamom pod; freekeh is fried in ghee first. Almonds, pine nuts, walnuts and cashews on top.
*   **Lighter version:** the source suggests reducing ghee or swapping in lighter fats.
*   **Other Syrian cities:** ⚠️ not documented. Damascus favours rice-only feast trays ([riz a djej](recipe_riz_a_djej.md)-style); freekeh-with-meat platters are associated with Aleppo and Idlib.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Aleppo household mansaf** (Arabic Post proportions, halved and scaled to 4).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb (shoulder/neck on the bone), in medium pieces | 1 kg |
| Onion, quartered | 1 |
| Bay leaves | 2 |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Cumin, ground | pinch |
| Cloves or wild thyme (optional) | 2 cloves / 1 sprig |
| Salt and black pepper | to taste |
| Water | 2.5 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain (Egyptian) rice, washed and soaked 20 min | 400 g |
| Lamb broth for the rice | ≈ 600 ml |
| Freekeh, picked over and rinsed | 2 cups (≈ 320 g) |
| Lamb broth for the freekeh | 3 cups (≈ 1½ cups per cup) |
| Ghee (samneh) | 3 tbsp (1 for rice, 2 for freekeh) |
| Cinnamon sticks + cardamom pods | 2 + 2 |
| Almonds, pine nuts, walnuts, cashews (toasted) | 30 g each |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Broth:** Cover the lamb with water and bring to a boil; skim. Add the onion, bay, cinnamon, cumin (and cloves or thyme), salt and pepper; simmer until tender, about 1½ hours. Lift out the meat and strain the broth.
2. **Freekeh:** Melt 2 tbsp ghee, fry the freekeh 2–3 min, stirring. Add salt, pepper, a cinnamon stick and a cardamom pod and 3 cups of broth. Boil 5 min, then cover and cook on low until tender (about 25–30 min).
3. **Rice:** Melt 1 tbsp ghee in about 600 ml broth with a cinnamon stick and cardamom pod; salt carefully (the broth is seasoned). Add the drained rice, boil 3–5 min, then cover and cook on very low heat 20–25 min.
4. **Brown the meat (optional):** Sear the boiled lamb briefly in a little ghee for colour.
5. **Assemble:** Spread the freekeh on a large tray and press it flat with a spoon; spread the rice over it and press again. Lay the lamb on top and scatter the toasted nuts.
6. **Serve:** Hot, with a bowl of the remaining broth and plain yogurt or a cucumber-yogurt salad.
