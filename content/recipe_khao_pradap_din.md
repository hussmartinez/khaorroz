# Khao Pradap Din / ข้าวประดับดิน (Haw Khao Noi / ห่อข้าวน้อย)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the rite is described by Thai educational and community sources (TruePlookpanya, GotoKnow), not academic ethnography; details vary by village. This is an offering, not a dish to be eaten; the "recipe" reconstructs a typical household set of parcels.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan (Northeast Thailand) and Lao-speaking communities. It is one of the twelve monthly merit-making customs (***heet sip song***) and the north-eastern counterpart of the south's Sat Duean Sip.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Small offering parcels: steamed sticky rice with a pinch of each savoury and sweet dish, fruit, betel and tobacco, wrapped in banana leaf "a little of everything".
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A **huad** (woven bamboo cone) over a **mo nueng** (pot) for the sticky rice, a **kratip** (lidded basket) to hold it, and banana-leaf wrappings pinned with bamboo slivers.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Bun Khao Pradap Din* ("merit of rice decorating the earth") falls on the **14th waning day of the ninth lunar month**, about a month into Buddhist Lent. Before dawn, villagers quietly lay their **haw khao noi** (little rice parcels) at the foot of big trees and around the temple's stupa and ordination hall, without the gongs and drums of other festivals. The parcels are offered to ancestors and to hungry ghosts (*pret*) who have no living kin to feed them. Later that morning, food is offered to the monks and merit is transferred to the dead by pouring water (*kruat nam*). Popular accounts trace the rite to a Dhammapada story about the deceased relatives of King Bimbisara.

Rice here is both food and currency of merit: the same sticky rice that is eaten daily from the kratip is shared with the invisible dead.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Rural Isan villages:** Parcels placed under trees and along temple walls before dawn, in silence.
*   **Towns:** Parcels are also hung on the temple fence or set on stands. ⚠️ Local forms are documented only anecdotally.
*   **Southern counterpart:** The *hmrap* trays of [Sat Duean Sip](recipe_khanom_la.md) in Nakhon Si Thammarat.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **a household set of about 20 haw khao noi** (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice, soaked overnight | 400 g |
| Small portions of household dishes (grilled fish, *jaew*, curry) | 2 tbsp each |
| Sweets (e.g. [khao tom mat](recipe_khao_tom_mat.md) slices, bananas) | 20 small pieces |
| Betel leaf, areca nut, a little tobacco or cigarettes | 20 sets |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Banana leaves, cut into squares and wiped | 20 + 20 |
| Bamboo slivers (*mai klat*) | 20 |
| Sticky rice per parcel | about 1 small ball |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Steam:** Drain the soaked rice and steam it in the huad over boiling water for **25–30 minutes**, turning once. Keep it in a kratip.
2. **Compose:** On each leaf square put a ball of sticky rice, a pinch of each savoury dish, a sweet, fruit and betel set.
3. **Wrap:** Fold the parcels and pin them with bamboo slivers; some households make a double parcel (food plus betel).
4. **Lay out:** Before dawn on the 14th waning day of the 9th month, place them quietly under trees and around the temple buildings.
5. **Merit:** In the morning, offer food to the monks and pour water to transfer merit to the dead.
