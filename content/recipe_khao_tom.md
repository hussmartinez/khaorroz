# Khao Tom / ข้าวต้ม

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok (Yaowarat, Talat Noi and night markets) and Central Thai towns with Teochew communities.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice soup: whole grains in a light broth or plain water. Either **khao tom kruang** (rice soup with pork, prawn or fish, garlic oil and celery) or **khao tom kui / khao tom plao** (plain watery rice gruel eaten with a spread of salty-sweet side dishes).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A tall pot (*mo*) of simmering stock or rice gruel over gas or charcoal, and small dishes for the sides.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Teochew Chinese in Thailand call rice gruel *muay* and have long eaten both *muay* and *jok* for breakfast ([Huachiew Chalermprakiet University](https://has.hcu.ac.th/xmlui/handle/123456789/3543?show=full)). The original Teochew *muay* is thick, with much rice and little water, while the version sold across Thailand is thin and clear ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=92983)). **Khao tom kui**, once seen as "poor people's food", became a beloved late-night meal in Thai cities, with shops lined with plates of salted egg, pickled greens, stir-fried morning glory and minced pork with olives ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_108493)). Thai-style *khao tom kruang* with pork, prawn or fish in broth is the everyday breakfast and convalescent food.

**Culinary science:** in *khao tom* the grains stay whole and the liquid stays thin and only lightly starchy. Jok, by contrast, is broken rice simmered until it disintegrates. Garlic fried in oil until golden (*krathiam jiao*) and a scatter of Chinese celery give a clean broth its aroma.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Khao tom kui / plao (Teochew, Bangkok night shops):** plain rice gruel with many sides ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_108493)).
*   **Khao tom mu / kung / pla (Thai breakfast):** rice in pork stock with minced pork balls, prawns or fish, an egg cracked in, garlic oil, celery and white pepper.
*   **Thick Teochew muay (home):** closer to the original Teochew texture ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=92983)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **khao tom mu (pork rice soup)**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pork bones (for stock) | 800 g |
| Water | 2.5 L |
| Minced pork, seasoned with white pepper and soy | 300 g |
| Garlic, chopped, fried golden in oil (*krathiam jiao*) | 6 cloves |
| Fish sauce, light soy, white pepper | 2 tbsp, 1 tbsp, 1 tsp |
| Chinese celery, spring onion, ginger shreds | 1 handful each |
| Eggs (optional) | 4 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Cooked jasmine rice | 500 g |
| Stock | ≈ 1.8 L |
| Rice : stock | ≈ 1 : 3.5 by weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Stock:** blanch the bones, then simmer them in the water for 1.5 h, skimming. Strain.
2. **Pork balls:** bring the stock to a simmer and drop in small balls of seasoned pork. Cook 5 min.
3. **Rice:** add the cooked rice and simmer 5 min, just until the grains swell. Do not stir them to a mush.
4. **Season** with fish sauce, soy and pepper. Crack an egg into each bowl if using, and ladle the hot soup over.
5. **Finish** with garlic oil, Chinese celery, spring onion and ginger.
