# Khao Phan Phak (Laplae Steamed Rice Sheets with Vegetables) / ข้าวพันผัก

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the origin and method are documented in Thai press and Thai PBS, but no source gives quantities, so the batter proportions are reconstructed. Despite the name, there is **no whole rice grain** in it: the sheets are made from rice flour and water.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Laplae district, Uttaradit (lower north), now also sold in Phitsanulok.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Steamed rice-flour sheets. A thin rice-flour batter is spread on a cloth stretched over a steaming pot, cooked in seconds, then rolled around stir-fried vegetables, glass noodles or meat. The result is soft, translucent and slightly elastic.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay pot of boiling water with a cloth stretched taut over its mouth (*pak mo*), a domed lid, and a bamboo stick to lift the sheet, over a wood fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khao phan phak* ("rice wrapped around vegetables") is the local food of **Laplae**. Villagers there made **khao khaep**, sun-dried rice-flour sheets that kept well and could be carried to the orchards and fields. The fresh steamed sheet filled with vegetables developed from that tradition ([Thai Post](https://www.thaipost.net/main/detail/57089); [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/life/2866199)). Dried *khao khaep* is still used to roll seasoned noodles into *mi phan* ([MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/travel/detail/9580000111801)). The method resembles central Thailand's *khao kriap pak mo*. Modern shops add egg, meat and other noodles ([Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/KonSuRoak/watch/150079)).

**Culinary science:** a very thin layer of rice-flour batter gelatinises almost instantly in steam at 100 °C and sets into a cohesive film. Using rice flour rather than wheat makes the sheet gluten-free and slightly translucent.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Laplae classic:** Sheets filled with stir-fried green vegetables, glass noodles, carrot and cabbage, with a garlic oil and chilli-vinegar dip ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/life/2866199)).
*   **Modern shops (Phitsanulok, Uttaradit):** Added egg, pork or other noodles ([Thai Post](https://www.thaipost.net/main/detail/57089)).
*   **Khao khaep / mi phan:** Sun-dried sheets rolled around seasoned noodles ([MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/travel/detail/9580000111801)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Laplae vegetable khao phan phak (reconstructed batter).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice flour | 200 g |
| Tapioca starch | 2 tbsp |
| Water | 500 ml |
| Salt | ½ tsp |
| Mixed vegetables (cabbage, carrot, morning glory), shredded | 400 g |
| Glass noodles, soaked | 50 g |
| Garlic, minced + oil | 4 cloves + 3 tbsp |
| Soy sauce / fish sauce | 1 tbsp / 1 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Batter per sheet | ≈ 3 tbsp |
| Sheets | ≈ 16 |
| Steaming per sheet | 30–60 s |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Batter:** Whisk the rice flour, tapioca, salt and water smooth, and rest it **30 min**.
2. **Filling:** Fry the garlic in the oil, then stir-fry the vegetables and noodles briefly with the soy and fish sauce. Keep crisp.
3. **Set up the cloth:** Stretch a cloth tightly over the mouth of a pot of boiling water and brush it lightly with oil.
4. **Steam the sheets:** Spread a ladle of batter in a thin circle, cover with the lid for **30–60 s** until translucent, and spoon filling onto the sheet.
5. **Roll and serve:** Lift with a bamboo stick, fold over the filling, and transfer to a plate. Serve with garlic oil and chilli vinegar.
