# Khao Phat Tom Yum / ข้าวผัดต้มยำ

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Documented mainly by recipe sites and food blogs; no history of its origin was found.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok and tourist Thailand, at seafood and food-court stalls.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Fried rice: rice fried with tom yum aromatics — lemongrass, galangal, makrut lime leaf, chili paste — usually with prawns or mixed seafood; hot, sour and fragrant.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok (*kratha*) over high heat.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
A modern cross of the famous soup and fried rice: recipe writers describe protein, rice and vegetables fried with lemongrass, galangal and makrut lime leaves and a tom yum paste of chili and tamarind, sweet-salty-sour-hot ([Hungry in Thailand](https://hungryinthailand.com/tom-yum-fried-rice/); [Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/tom-yum-fried-rice/print/7983)). In Bangkok it is sold as *khao phat tom yum thale* at food courts ([The World of Street Food](https://www.theworldofstreetfood.com/2023/07/thai-seafood-tom-yum-fried-rice-in.html)).

**Culinary science:** *nam phrik phao* (roasted chili paste) brings sugar and oil that caramelise on the rice; lime goes in at the end so its acidity stays bright.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Seafood (*thale*):** prawns and squid ([The World of Street Food](https://www.theworldofstreetfood.com/2023/07/thai-seafood-tom-yum-fried-rice-in.html)).
*   **Prawn (*kung*):** the commonest restaurant version ([Hungry in Thailand](https://hungryinthailand.com/tom-yum-fried-rice/)).
*   **Creamy (*nam khon*) style:** with evaporated milk, mirroring creamy tom yum ([Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/tom-yum-fried-rice/print/7983)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Prawn**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Cooked jasmine rice, day-old | 800 g |
| Prawns | 300 g |
| Lemongrass, galangal, makrut lime leaves (minced) | 2 stalks, 3 slices, 5 |
| Roasted chili paste (*nam phrik phao*) | 2 tbsp |
| Fish sauce / lime juice | 1½ tbsp / 2 tbsp |
| Mushrooms, onion, tomato | 100 g, ½, 1 |
| Oil | 3 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 400 g raw, cooked a day ahead |
| Prawns per plate | ≈ 75 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** cook 400 g jasmine rice with 520 ml water the day before; chill.
2. **Aromatics:** fry lemongrass, galangal and lime leaf in oil until fragrant; add chili paste.
3. **Prawns and vegetables:** add prawns, onion, mushroom and tomato; cook 1–2 min.
4. **Rice:** add rice and fish sauce, toss on high heat.
5. **Finish:** off the heat stir in lime juice; serve with coriander and a lime wedge.
