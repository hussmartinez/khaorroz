# Nasi Dagae / นาซิดาแฆ

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is well attested in Pattani (Bangkok Post, Atlas Obscura, Thai academic food-science work), but no Pattani cookbook recipe with quantities was accessible. The blueprint reconstructs the Malay *nasi dagang* method as described for the Pattani version. Treat the quantities as a starting point.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Malay-Muslim deep south: Pattani, Yala and Narathiwat. The same dish is Terengganu's and Kelantan's *nasi dagang* across the border.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice steamed with coconut milk, fenugreek and shallots. Fragrant, slightly sticky grains (a mix of jasmine and glutinous rice is common) served with a sour-spicy **tuna curry** and a braised egg.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A two-stage steamer: the rice is steamed, then mixed with seasoned coconut milk and steamed again. The curry is cooked in a pot over charcoal or gas, and the dish is sold wrapped in banana leaf or paper at breakfast stalls.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Nasi dagang* means "traders' rice" in Malay. It is rooted in the fishing and trading towns of the Malay east coast, which included the old **Sultanate of Pattani**. In Thailand it survives only in the Malay-speaking deep south, where Thai sources spell it *nasi dagae* (นาซิดาแฆ). The Bangkok Post notes that it was once reserved for important occasions and is now everyday food. Famous Pattani stalls sell out before 9 a.m.

The rice is enriched with **coconut milk**, **fenugreek seeds**, shallot and ginger, and the classic partner is **gulai ikan tongkol**, a tuna (or mackerel) curry soured with tamarind or *asam keping*. A **telur pindang**-style braised egg comes alongside. It belongs to the same family as the Spanish and Levantine "rice plus stew" plates, such as [riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md) under stews, but its aromatics are entirely Malay.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Pattani:** Tuna curry; braised egg; often a mix of white and glutinous rice.
*   **Yala and Narathiwat:** ⚠️ The same dish is sold in the markets; local differences (chilli level, fish species) aren't documented in accessible sources.
*   **Terengganu / Kelantan (Malaysia, cross-border reference):** Terengganu uses partly glutinous "nasi dagang" rice; Kelantan sometimes uses red rice. Both have tuna curry and sambal.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Pattani nasi dagae** (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Tuna (or mackerel) steaks | 600 g |
| Curry paste: dried chillies, shallots, garlic, ginger, turmeric, lemongrass | 120 g |
| Thin coconut milk | 500 ml |
| Tamarind water (or 2 slices asam keping) | 60 ml |
| Fenugreek seeds (for the curry) | 1 tsp |
| Palm sugar, salt | 1 tbsp / to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice + glutinous rice, soaked 3 h | 300 g + 100 g |
| Coconut milk (for the rice) | 300 ml |
| Fenugreek seeds (for the rice) | 1 tsp |
| Shallots, thinly sliced / ginger, julienned | 4 / 15 g |
| Eggs, hard-boiled then simmered in the curry | 4 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **First steaming:** Drain the soaked rice and steam it for **20 minutes**.
2. **Season:** Warm the coconut milk with salt, fenugreek, shallots and ginger, then fold it into the rice.
3. **Second steaming:** Steam for **15–20 minutes** more, until the grains are glossy and tender.
4. **Curry:** Fry the paste and fenugreek in oil until fragrant, add the coconut milk, tamarind and palm sugar, then the fish and the peeled eggs. Simmer **15 minutes**.
5. **Serve:** Put a mound of rice on banana leaf with a piece of tuna, curry sauce and half an egg.
