# Kai Yang / ไก่ย่าง (Kai Yang Khao Suan Kwang / ไก่ย่างเขาสวนกวาง)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the origin story of Khao Suan Kwang's grilled chicken comes from local press and restaurant accounts, which differ on dates.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan, famously Khao Suan Kwang district (Khon Kaen); Laos.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: marinated, flattened free-range chicken grilled over charcoal until the skin is crisp and the meat juicy, dipped in *jaew*; sold with sticky rice wrapped in banana leaf.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Split-bamboo clamps over a long charcoal trough; sticky rice from the *huad*, sold in banana leaf parcels or a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Kai yang (also *kai ping*) is described as a grilled chicken of the Lao people of Laos and Isan, typically dipped in *jaew bong* and eaten with sticky rice; Wikimedia captions show the canonical trio of som tam, kai yang and khao niao ([Wikipedia mirror – Kai yang](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Kai_yang); [Wikimedia – Somtam kaiyang khaoniaow](https://download.osmand.net/wiki/File:Somtam_kaiyang_khaoniaow.jpg)). The most famous version is Khao Suan Kwang's: villagers of Ban Chom Phon Phatthana and Ban Thang Phat raised native chickens and sold them grilled, with sticky rice wrapped in banana leaf, at Khao Suan Kwang railway station, moving to the roadside when travel switched to cars ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=53876)). One shop, Wanna, opened in 1973 and is listed by the Michelin Guide ([Michelin – Kai Yang Wanna](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/khon-kaen-region/khon-kaen/restaurant/kai-yang-wanna)).

**Culinary science:** small, lean native chickens grill quickly; flattening them evens the thickness so the skin crisps before the breast dries, and a marinade with sugar promotes browning.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Khao Suan Kwang (Khon Kaen):** native chicken, crisp skin, sticky rice in banana leaf ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=53876); [MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/smes/detail/9660000037497)).
*   **Wichian Buri (Phetchabun):** a well-known roadside style. ⚠️ No detailed source found.
*   **Southern Muslim kai yang:** closer to Malaysian grilled chicken, with turmeric and coconut ([Thailandblog](https://www.thailandblog.nl/en/eten-drinken/geheimen-van-de-thaise-keuken-kai-yang-of-gai-yang-gegrilde-kip-uit-isaan/)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Isan roadside kai yang with jaew.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Small free-range chicken, butterflied | 1 (≈ 1.2 kg) |
| Garlic, coriander root, white pepper (pounded) | 6 cloves / 3 roots / 1 tsp |
| Fish sauce / light soy | 2 tbsp / 1 tbsp |
| Palm sugar | 1 tbsp |
| [Jaew bong](recipe_jaew_bong.md) or tamarind jaew (to dip) | ½ cup |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw (in 4 banana-leaf parcels) |
| Marinade time | 3 h – overnight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Marinate:** rub the chicken with the pounded paste, sauces and sugar; marinate at least 3 h.
2. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket. Wrap portions in banana leaf to keep warm.
3. **Grill:** clamp the chicken in split bamboo and grill over low-medium charcoal, turning often, 35–45 min, until the skin is crisp and the juices run clear.
4. **Serve:** chop into pieces; serve with jaew, sticky rice and som tam.
