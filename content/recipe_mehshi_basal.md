# Mehshi Basal / محشي بصل

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** stuffed onion layers are made across the Levant, Egypt and Turkey. The **tamarind-sauced Aleppo version** is documented through Poopa Dweck's *Aromas of Aleppo* (Syrian-Jewish) and Israeli and Jewish recipe media; the general Aleppine (non-Jewish) version is attested in Syrian and Palestinian home cooking. Exact town distinctions are partly reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Aleppo (Muslim, Christian and Jewish households), the Syrian-Jewish diaspora (Brooklyn, Israel, Mexico), and Palestine.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vegetables (*mahashi*): onion layers rolled around spiced **meat and rice**, braised until soft and **caramelised**, in a sticky sweet-sour tamarind (or pomegranate) glaze.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot or shallow baking dish; stovetop braise, often finished in the oven to colour the tops.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Onions are hollowed by boiling, then separated into layers that curl naturally around a filling. In Aleppo this becomes one of the stars of the *mahashi* table, cooked in the city's beloved **sweet-sour** register: tamarind (*ou'*), lemon and a little sugar, or pomegranate molasses. Poopa Dweck's *Aromas of Aleppo* records a version of ground beef, rice and spices with a tamarind–lemon sauce; she suggests pomegranate syrup as a substitute. Israeli food writers describe *basal mechshe* made with long onions, round rice and *baharat*, and Syrian-Jewish families bake them with brown sugar and *ou'* until sticky.

**Culinary science:** boiling softens the onion's pectin so each layer can be rolled without tearing; the long braise then converts its sugars (fructans and sucrose) into deep caramel notes. The acid of tamarind balances that sweetness and keeps the rice inside from turning mushy.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Aleppo Jewish (Halabi):** Beef and rice filling, *ou'* tamarind, lemon, sugar; sticky glaze (*Aromas of Aleppo*).
*   **Brooklyn / Syrian-Jewish US:** Brown sugar and tamarind; often served for Sukkot, together with other *mehshi* ⚠️.
*   **Israel:** *Basal mechshe* with long onions, round rice and *baharat*; Syrian and Iraqi families each claim their style.
*   **Aleppo (general):** Pomegranate molasses rather than tamarind; Aleppo pepper ⚠️.
*   **Palestine:** Onion rolls with rice, meat and sumac, sometimes in a mixed *mahashi* pot (see [mahshi](recipe_mahshi.md)).
*   **Egypt (contrast):** *Mahshi basal* with herbed rice, no meat, tomato broth.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Aleppine tamarind-glazed**, after published Syrian-Jewish recipes.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Large onions (long, if available) | 6 |
| Ground beef | 400 g |
| Round (short-grain) rice, rinsed | 130 g |
| Allspice, cinnamon, salt, pepper | 1 tsp / ½ tsp / 1½ tsp / ½ tsp |
| Tamarind paste (*ou'*) | 3 tbsp |
| Lemon juice | 60 ml |
| Brown sugar | 2 tbsp |
| Vegetable oil | 3 tbsp |
| Water | 250 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Meat : rice (filling) | ≈ 3 : 1 |
| Filling per onion layer | ≈ 1 heaped tbsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Onions:** Trim the ends, cut a slit from top to centre on one side, and simmer whole in boiling water 8–10 minutes until the layers loosen; cool and separate them.
2. **Filling:** Mix the beef, raw rice, spices and a splash of water.
3. **Stuff:** Place a spoonful of filling in each onion layer and roll it closed; chop the small inner layers.
4. **Pack:** Oil the pot, scatter the chopped onion, and pack the rolls seam-side down.
5. **Sauce:** Whisk the tamarind, lemon, sugar and water; pour over.
6. **Braise:** Cover and cook on low for 45–60 minutes until the rice is tender; uncover (or move to a 200 °C oven) for the last 15–20 minutes to caramelise.
7. **Serve:** Warm, with the glaze spooned over.
