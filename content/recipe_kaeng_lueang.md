# Kaeng Lueang (Kaeng Som Pak Tai) / แกงเหลือง (แกงส้มปักษ์ใต้)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The whole Thai south, where it is simply called *kaeng som* ("sour curry"). Central Thais and Bangkok call it *kaeng lueang* ("yellow curry") to distinguish it from the central red kaeng som.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a thin, sour, very hot turmeric-yellow fish curry with no coconut milk, with green papaya, bamboo shoot or morning glory. It is ladled over steamed rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot over charcoal; the paste is pounded in a stone mortar (*khrok*).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Southerners make this curry more often than any other spicy curry ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=97632)). A modern southern paste is mostly dried chilies with turmeric, garlic and shallot, in published proportions of 45 % dried bird's-eye chili, 20 % turmeric, 15 % garlic and 15 % shallot ([JobBKK – "Lung Phon" southern pastes](https://jobbkk.com/variety/detail/4477)). Silpa Watthanatham magazine traced an older palace-cookbook version that used fresh chilies, lemongrass, onion, garlic and fresh turmeric, with the sourness coming from fairly mature *madan* leaves (*Garcinia schomburgkiana*), because young leaves are astringent rather than sour ([Silpa-mag – the origin of kaeng lueang](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=19464)).

The sourness comes from lime juice, *som khaek* (dried garcinia) or *madan*. Vegetables vary with the season: bamboo shoot, *dok khae* (sesbania flowers), morning glory, green papaya or pickled mustard greens ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=97632)). Cooks follow a strict rule: once the fish is in, **do not stir**, or the fish breaks up and turns the broth fishy.

**Culinary science:** with no coconut fat to carry the flavours, everything depends on the paste. Turmeric's curcumin gives the colour, and acid (lime, garcinia) brightens the fish and firms its proteins.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Southern home style:** Dried-chili paste, sea fish (mackerel, barracuda, *pla insi*), sour from lime or *som khaek*.
*   **Old palace style (central record of a southern dish):** Fresh chilies, lemongrass and *madan* leaves ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=19464)).
*   **Vegetables by season:** bamboo shoot (most common), green papaya, *dok khae*, morning glory, pickled mustard ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/sentangsedtee/?p=97632)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: southern home style.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried bird's-eye chilies, soaked | 20 g |
| Fresh turmeric | 10 g |
| Garlic | 6 g |
| Shallots | 6 g |
| Shrimp paste (kapi) | 1 tsp |
| Water | 800 ml |
| Firm sea fish (e.g. mackerel or barracuda), in steaks | 500 g |
| Bamboo shoot or green papaya | 250 g |
| Lime juice | 4–5 limes |
| Fish sauce | 1–2 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (served with it) | 400 g raw, steamed |
| Curry per plate | 1 ladle with 1–2 pieces of fish |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Steam or cook 400 g of jasmine rice with 500 ml of water; rest.
2. **Paste:** Pound the chilies, turmeric, garlic, shallots and shrimp paste to a smooth paste.
3. **Broth:** Bring a little of the water to a boil first, so the paste stays concentrated, then add the paste and the rest of the water.
4. **Vegetables:** Add the bamboo shoot or papaya and simmer 8 min.
5. **Fish:** Slide in the fish and simmer 5 min **without stirring**.
6. **Season:** Off the heat, add lime juice and fish sauce until it tastes sour first, then salty and hot. Serve over the rice.
