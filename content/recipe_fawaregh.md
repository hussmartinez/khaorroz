# Fawaregh / فوارغ

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a traditional offal dish well documented in recipes (Taste of Beirut, Food Heritage Foundation, mountain-village sites), but national attribution is contested: Wikipedia files stuffed intestines under Syrian cuisine, Lebanese sources claim it as Lebanese, and an Amman restaurant serves it as a Jordanian specialty. It is pan-Levantine; town details are thin.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lebanon (Beirut and mountain villages such as Hadath el-Jebbeh in the north), and across the Levant: Damascus and Aleppo, Amman (where *fawaregh* and *krush* are sold at offal restaurants).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed offal. Sheep's intestines filled with rice, minced meat and spices (sometimes chickpeas or pine nuts), tied into sausages and simmered until the rice is plump and soft inside the casing. Served sliced, with the broth or with yogurt.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large pot of simmering spiced broth (bay leaf, cinnamon) on the stove; a funnel or a piece of reed to fill the casings.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Fawaregh* means "empties": the hollow intestines that are washed, turned and refilled. Like [ghammeh](recipe_ghammeh.md), it is a dish of the slaughter day, when nothing of the lamb was wasted. Today it survives in a few homes and at specialist restaurants. Taste of Beirut describes cleaning the casings "thoroughly with lemon, coarse salt and vinegar" before stuffing them with minced meat, rice and spices. Lebanon's Food Heritage Foundation lists meat, rice, onions, chickpeas and spices. Egyptian cooks use the much larger beef intestine, which is easier to stuff.

**Culinary science:** the casing is filled only loosely, because the rice expands to about twice its volume as it absorbs the broth; packed too tight, the sausages burst. Pricking each one a few times lets steam escape. Long, gentle simmering tenderises the casing and lets the rice cook evenly through the meat fat.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Beirut (Taste of Beirut):** Rice, minced meat, onion, allspice, cinnamon and black pepper; casings cleaned with lemon, salt and vinegar.
*   **Food Heritage Foundation (Lebanon):** Adds **chickpeas** to the rice and meat stuffing.
*   **Hadath el-Jebbeh (North Lebanon mountains):** A village site publishes the dish as a local tradition ("stuffed tripes") ⚠️ (details not accessible).
*   **Goat version (The Recipe Hunters):** Butter, sweet onions, Egyptian rice, minced goat and seven-spice, simmered in a bay-leaf broth.
*   **Amman:** Sold as *fawaregh* alongside *krush* (stuffed tripe) at specialist offal restaurants.
*   **Egypt (*mumbar*):** Larger ox intestines, for comparison only.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Beirut home version with chickpeas.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sheep intestines (casings), cleaned and turned | ≈ 3 m |
| Lemon, coarse salt, vinegar (for cleaning) | as needed |
| Short-grain rice, rinsed | 250 g |
| Minced lamb or beef (with some fat) | 300 g |
| Cooked chickpeas | 100 g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 1 |
| Allspice, cinnamon, black pepper | 1 tsp, ½ tsp, ½ tsp |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |
| Bay leaves, cinnamon stick (broth) | 2, 1 |
| Water | ≈ 2.5 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Fill the casings | loosely, about half full |
| Rice : meat | ≈ 0.8 : 1 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Clean:** Rinse the casings repeatedly inside and out, rubbing them with lemon, coarse salt and vinegar, then rinse again.
2. **Stuffing:** Mix the raw rice, meat, chickpeas, onion, spices and salt.
3. **Fill:** Tie one end of each casing, fill it loosely through a funnel, and tie it off in sausages of about 15 cm. Prick each a few times.
4. **Simmer:** Lower the sausages into simmering water with the bay leaves and cinnamon, and cook gently **≈ 1 h**, until the rice is cooked through.
5. **Serve:** Slice and serve hot with a little broth, or with yogurt and a salad.
