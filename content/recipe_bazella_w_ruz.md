# Bazella w Ruz / بازيلا ورز

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the Homs-born author Anas Atassi documents this as a Syrian family *yakhni* served with vermicelli rice. A Syrian-Jewish (Aleppo) version, "green peas and rice with coriander and meat", is listed in Poopa Dweck's *Aromas of Aleppo*, but the recipe text was not accessible; that variant is described from the title only.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Syria (Homs, Damascus, Aleppo), also Lebanon and Palestine.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice with stew (*ruz w yakhneh*). Tender beef or lamb and green peas in a tomato sauce lifted with pomegranate molasses, spooned over fluffy vermicelli rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy covered pot on the stovetop.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Bazella* (peas, from Italian *piselli* via Levantine trade) cooked with meat is one of the everyday *yakhni*: the family stews that, in Syria, are always eaten with rice. Anas Atassi, born in Homs, writes that this stew "is served with rice in the Syrian style" and that it belongs to close family meals rather than formal dinners: being served it means you are considered family. His version browns the beef, sweats onion and carrot with an ***a'atryaat*** bundle (cinnamon, cloves, cardamom and bay), braises in tomato juice and finishes with peas and **pomegranate molasses**.

In Aleppo's Jewish community the same pairing appears as a rice dish of green peas, coriander and meat (Poopa Dweck, *Aromas of Aleppo*), a reminder that the *yakhneh-and-rice* system crossed communities in the city.

**Culinary science:** browning the meat hard before braising builds the sauce's depth; frozen or very young peas go in only for the last minutes so they keep colour and sweetness. Pomegranate molasses adds acid and fruit sugars that balance the tomato.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Homs (Atassi family):** Beef, onion, carrot, *a'atryaat* whole spices, tomato juice, paprika, pomegranate molasses; vermicelli rice.
*   **Damascus ⚠️:** Lamb more common; finished with lemon instead of molasses.
*   **Aleppo, Jewish community ⚠️:** Peas, rice, coriander and meat (*Aromas of Aleppo*, p. 118 per reader notes).
*   **Lebanon / Palestine:** Similar; Palestinian cooks may cook the rice *with* peas and minced meat as one pot, topped with pine nuts.
*   **Lebanese style (*bazella w riz*, everyday Beirut and Mount Lebanon lunch):** Peas and **carrots** with lamb shoulder or leg, beef shank (bone in), chuck or oxtail, browned in olive oil, then simmered with tomato paste, garlic, bay and seven-spice. Served over vermicelli rice.
*   **Lebanese variants:** A quick version with minced beef and frozen peas and carrots; some households add diced potato; a vegan version with vegetable stock and an extra onion.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Homs**, after Anas Atassi, scaled to 4.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Stewing beef, 4 cm cubes | 400 g |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | 3 tbsp |
| Onions, chopped | 2 |
| Carrots, in pieces | 2 |
| A'atryaat: cinnamon stick, cloves, cardamom pods, bay leaf | 2 / 2 / 3 / 1 |
| Tomato paste | 2 tbsp |
| Paprika | 1 tbsp |
| Tomato juice | 650 ml |
| Green peas (frozen) | 400 g |
| Pomegranate molasses | 2 tbsp |
| Salt, pepper | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Water added to the stew | 400 ml |
| Vermicelli rice: rice + vermicelli | 300 g + 50 g |
| Rice water | ≈ 1.5 : 1 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Brown:** Heat the oil and brown the beef well on all sides; set aside.
2. **Sweat:** In the same pot cook the onion, carrot and *a'atryaat* for 5 minutes.
3. **Paste:** Return the beef with the tomato paste and paprika; cook 1 minute.
4. **Braise:** Add the tomato juice, water, salt and pepper; bring to the boil, cover and simmer about 1 hour, until tender.
5. **Peas:** Remove the whole spices; add the peas and molasses and simmer until the peas are just cooked.
6. **Rice:** Toast the vermicelli in butter, add the rice and boiling salted water, cover and cook 15 minutes.
7. **Serve:** Immediately, the stew over or beside the vermicelli rice.
