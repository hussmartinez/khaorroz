# Riz bi Sh'ariyyeh / رز بشعيرية

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Lebanon, and everyday home cooking across Syria, Palestine and Jordan.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Everyday white rice with vermicelli, the base rice served under stews. Fluffy, separate grains flecked with golden toasted *sh'ariyyeh*.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy-bottomed pot with a tight lid, on the stove (today often a rice cooker).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Riz bi sh'ariyyeh* ("rice with vermicelli") is the **everyday rice of the Levant**: the plain rice that goes under the stews (*yakhnehs*) of beans, okra, peas, green beans and spinach that make up the weekday table. Its one technique is the defining one. Short lengths of **wheat (semolina) vermicelli** are fried in butter or oil until golden brown before the rice is added, which gives the dish a nutty, toasted taste and its speckled look. Lebanese cookbook authors (Abla Amad, Samira Kazan and others) treat it as the base recipe from which much of the rest of the table follows.

Its Spanish cousin is the toasted-grain logic of the [arrossejat](recipe_arrossejat.md), where rice is browned in oil before the broth, and of the [fideuà](recipe_fideua.md), where noodles are toasted before cooking.

**Culinary science:** browning the vermicelli (pasta made from durum semolina) triggers Maillard reactions between its starch, protein and the butter's milk solids. It burns very fast, so constant stirring matters. Rinsing the rice until the water runs clear removes surface starch, so the grains stay separate. Cooking covered on very low heat at about 1:1.5–2 water, then resting, finishes the grain in steam.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Butter vs oil vs ghee:** Ghee or butter for a richer, "Sunday" version; olive oil for an everyday one.
*   **Ratio:** Typically 1 part vermicelli to 4 parts rice by volume. Some cooks use more (up to 1:2).
*   **Rice type:** Long-grain or basmati in the cities, Egyptian medium-grain in many homes. Use **wheat** vermicelli, never rice vermicelli.
*   **Garnish:** Plain, or with toasted pine nuts on festive days.
*   **Regional variants:** ⚠️ Not documented by town. This is a household base rather than a regional dish.
*   **Diaspora: *arroz árabe* in Latin America (⚠️ origin not traced to a single community; see also [arroz árabe chileno](recipe_arroz_arabe_chileno.md)):** Rice fried with fine noodles (*cabello de ángel*) is popular in Colombia, the Dominican Republic, Peru and Chile under the name *arroz árabe* or *arroz con fideos*. In Peru and Chile it is a **Christmas Eve** side dish served with turkey or pork, often with raisins and nuts. It is usually linked to the Levantine immigrants (Lebanese, Syrian and Palestinian, all called *turcos* in the old records) who arrived from the 1870s on. In Mexico, recipes for *arroz árabe con fideos* circulate alongside the Lebanese community's wider cuisine. ## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Lebanese everyday**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Butter + olive oil (or ghee) | 15 g + 1 tbsp |
| Wheat vermicelli, broken into 2 cm pieces | 50 g (≈ ½ cup) |
| Salt | 1 tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Long-grain or basmati rice, rinsed until clear | 300 g (≈ 1½ cups) |
| Boiling water | ≈ 600 ml (1 : 2 by volume; 1 : 1.5 for basmati) |
| Vermicelli : rice | ≈ 1 : 4 by volume |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toast:** Melt the butter with the oil over medium heat. Add the vermicelli and stir **constantly** until evenly golden-brown (**2–3 min**). Watch it, because it burns in seconds.
2. **Rice:** Add the drained rice and salt and stir **1 min** to coat.
3. **Liquid:** Pour in the boiling water and stir once.
4. **Fire Manipulation:** Bring to the boil, then cover and cook on the **lowest heat for 15–18 min**.
5. **Rest:** Off the heat, keep covered for **10 min**, then fluff with a fork. Serve under a stew such as [fasoulia](recipe_fasoulia_w_riz.md), okra or peas.
