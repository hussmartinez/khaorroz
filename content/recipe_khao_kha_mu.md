# Khao Kha Mu / ข้าวขาหมู

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok and the Teochew communities around the Gulf of Thailand; now nationwide (Chiang Mai's Chang Phueak gate stall is famous).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** One-plate rice: **jasmine rice under slices of five-spice braised pork leg**, with a dark, glossy braising liquid spooned over, half a braised egg, pickled mustard greens and blanched Chinese kale.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large aluminium or clay braising pot (*mo phalo*) kept at a slow simmer on charcoal or gas all day.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao kha mu ("pork-leg rice") was created by **Thai Chinese of Teochew origin**. It descends from the Teochew braising tradition, *lou*, that came with migrants from southern China and settled around Bangkok and the Gulf coast ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_kha_mu); [The World of Street Food](https://www.theworldofstreetfood.com/2023/07/khao-kha-moo-or-braised-pork-leg-at.html)). It is now one of Bangkok's most popular market lunches, sold from stalls where whole legs sit glistening in a vat of braise ([BK Magazine](https://bk.asia-city.com/node/61620)).

**Culinary science:** pork leg is rich in collagen. Hours in a sweet-salty braise of soy, rock sugar and five-spice (star anise, cinnamon, clove, Sichuan pepper, fennel) at a bare simmer turn the collagen to gelatin, which makes the skin silky and gives the braising liquid enough body to coat the rice. Palm or rock sugar caramelises and deepens the colour.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Bangkok Teochew-style:** five-spice-forward and dark, with pickled mustard greens (*phak kat dong*), kale and a vinegar-chili-garlic dip.
*   **Chiang Mai (Chang Phueak gate, "Cowboy Hat Lady"):** famous northern stall; sweeter braise ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_kha_mu)). ⚠️ Differences are anecdotal.
*   **Old-style (*kha mu boran*):** some vendors keep an older, lighter recipe ([Thailex](https://thailex.info/THAILEX/THAILEXENG/LEXICON/khao%20kha%20moo%20bohraan.htm)).
*   **Mixed plates:** with *mu krop* (crisp pork) or *kha mu yat sai* (stuffed trotter).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok Teochew-style**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pork leg (front hock), skin on | 1.5 kg |
| Water | 2.5 L |
| Light soy / dark sweet soy | 60 ml / 45 ml |
| Rock or palm sugar | 80 g |
| Five-spice: star anise, cinnamon stick, cloves | 4 / 1 / 5 |
| Garlic, coriander root, white pepper, pounded | 1 head, 4 roots, 1 tsp |
| Eggs, hard-boiled and peeled | 4 |
| Pickled mustard greens, Chinese kale | 150 g / 200 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 400 g (+ 520 ml water) |
| Braised leg per plate | ≈ 200 g |
| Braising liquid per plate | 3–4 tbsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Blanch:** boil the leg for 5 min, then rinse to clear scum. Scorch any hairs off the skin.
2. **Caramel:** melt the sugar in a little oil until amber. Fry the garlic paste, add the soy sauces, spices and water, and bring to a boil.
3. **Braise:** add the leg and eggs. Simmer gently, covered, for 2.5–3 h, turning occasionally, until the skin is yielding and the liquid glossy.
4. **Rice and greens:** cook the rice. Blanch the kale.
5. **Serve:** slice the leg and lay it on rice with half an egg, mustard greens and kale. Spoon braise over and serve the chili-garlic vinegar alongside.
