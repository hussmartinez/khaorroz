# Medieval Mujaddara (Rice, Lentils and Meat) / مجدّرة

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Secondary sources (Wikipedia, citing food-history literature) agree that the **first recorded mujaddara recipe** is in al-Baghdadi's *Kitab al-Tabikh* (Baghdad, 1226), and that it contained **rice, lentils and meat**, served at celebrations, while the meatless form was poor people's food. I have not seen the recipe text. The spicing and method below are a period-plausible reconstruction, modelled on al-Baghdadi's documented [shurba](recipe_shurba_al_baghdadi.md) technique. A popular-history site also describes a 13th-century *Syrian* recipe under this name built on meat, chickpeas and rice; that version is unverified and not used here.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Abbasid Baghdad (source). It became the Levant's everyday and fasting-day dish across Syria, Lebanon and Palestine.
*   **Historical Source:** *Kitab al-Tabikh*, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Baghdadi, 1226, Baghdad
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice and legumes with meat. Rice and brown lentils cooked together in a fatty meat broth until the grains are loose; the "pockmarked" (*mujaddar*) look gives the dish its name.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep copper pot over a wood fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Mujaddara* means "pockmarked" in Arabic, because the dark lentils speckle the pale rice. In its earliest written form the dish was a meat dish fit for a celebration. Over the following centuries it shed the meat and became the iconic dish of thrift and of Christian fasting days in Syria, Lebanon and Palestine, now crowned with crisp fried onions instead of lamb. In 1756 Alexander Russell observed that Aleppo's Christians "eat more *burgle*, and less rice" than their Muslim neighbours, and lived on "pulse, dressed with oil" on fast days. That is the social world in which a meatless rice-or-bulgur-and-lentil pot thrived.

See the living dish: **[Mujaddara / Mudardara](recipe_mujaddara.md)**. Its Spanish cousin in spirit is the Vega Baja's Lenten [arroz de los tres puñaos](recipe_arroz_de_los_tres_punaos.md).

**Culinary logic:** lentils take longer than rice, so they go in first; the rice then cooks in the lentil-and-meat broth and absorbs its flavour.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Baghdad, 1226:** Rice, lentils and meat, a festive dish (per secondary sources).
*   **Ottoman Aleppo (Russell, 1756):** Fasting-day pulses and bulgur; rice was the more prestigious grain.
*   **Modern Levant:** Meatless *mujaddara* (puréed) and *mudardara* (whole grains) with fried onions; bulgur versions in the countryside. See [mujaddara](recipe_mujaddara.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern adaptation; reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb shoulder, small cubes | 400 g |
| Rendered lamb fat or ghee | 3 tbsp |
| Brown lentils, rinsed | 200 g |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Ground coriander | 1 tsp |
| Ground cumin | 1 tsp |
| Water | 1.5 L |
| Salt | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice, washed | 200 g |
| Lentils : rice | 1 : 1 |
| Broth at rice-in | ≈ 600 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meat:** Brown the lamb in the fat. Add the water and cinnamon and simmer **40 min**.
2. **Lentils:** Add the lentils, coriander, cumin and salt. Simmer **15–20 min**, until the lentils are just tender.
3. **Rice:** Adjust the broth to about 600 ml, bring to a boil, add the rice and cook covered on low heat for **18 min**.
4. **Rest and serve:** Rest **10 min** and fluff gently. Serve with yogurt.
