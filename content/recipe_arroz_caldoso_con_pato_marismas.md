# Arroz Caldoso con Pato de las Marismas / Arroz de Pato Cortijero

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Andalucía](provincial_andalucia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The marismas del Guadalquivir (Sevilla): Isla Mayor, La Puebla del Río, Villafranco, and the rice cortijos of the Bajo Guadalquivir.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay *cazuela* or iron pot over a wood fire in the cortijo kitchen.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The rice paddies of the Guadalquivir marshes are Spain's largest rice-growing area, and like L'Albufera they are full of waterfowl. Directo al Paladar documents an **"arroz caldoso con pato" as a traditional cortijo recipe of the Sevillian marismas**: the rice workers' and farmhands' (*cortijeros*) stew, made with duck from the marsh. Like its Valencian cousin [arròs amb ànec](recipe_arroz_con_pato.md), it reflects the link between wetland, rice and wildfowl. The Andalusian version, however, is **soupy** and built on an onion-and-pepper sofrito, not the Valencian dry pan.

**Culinary science:** duck fat renders into the sofrito and coats the grains. A long braise breaks down the bird's collagen, and the resulting broth carries a lot of body into the caldoso.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Isla Mayor:** Duck, sometimes with **cangrejo rojo** (red swamp crayfish, now a major local product).
*   **La Puebla del Río:** Duck + green pepper + **fino** or manzanilla in the braise.
*   **Cortijo (traditional):** Duck + potato + whatever garden vegetables are at hand. Stretched with more broth for a larger crew.
*   *Variations are only lightly sourced; verify locally.*

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **cortijo caldoso**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Duck, jointed | 1 (≈ 1.6 kg) |
| Olive oil | 40 ml |
| Onion | 1 large |
| Green pepper | 2 |
| Garlic | 4 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 4 g |
| Bay leaf | 2 |
| White wine or fino | 150 ml |
| Water | 2.5 L |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:** Render the duck skin-side down (**15 min**). Pour off the excess fat. Add the onion and peppers (12 min), the garlic, pimentón and tomato (5 min), then the wine. Add the water and bay leaf. Simmer covered for **75 min**. You should have ≈ 2 L of broth.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice (marisma variety) | 320 g |
| Duck broth | ≈ 1.9 L (≈ 6:1) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Bring the broth to a boil, add the rice and saffron.
2. **Fire:** Steady simmer for **17 min**.
3. **Finish:** Serve at once, with a piece of duck in each plate.
