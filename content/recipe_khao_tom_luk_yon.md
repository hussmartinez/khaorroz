# Khao Tom Luk Yon / ข้าวต้มลูกโยน (Khanom Tom / ขนมต้ม)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Nationwide for **Ok Phansa** (the end of Buddhist Lent). In the south it is called **khanom tom** and is tied to the **Chak Phra** boat-pulling festival in Surat Thani, Phatthalung, Nakhon Si Thammarat and Songkhla.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Leaf-wrapped sweet sticky rice for merit-making. Glutinous rice is half-cooked in salted, sweetened coconut milk, wrapped in a pyramid (south: a water-chestnut-shaped triangle) with a long "tail", and boiled until dense, chewy and rich.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok (*kratha*) to stir-fry the rice in coconut milk, wrapping leaves (young coconut, pandan, nipa or, in the south, **kapho palm** leaves) and a large boiling pot over a wood or gas fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The name means "thrown boiled rice". On the day after Ok Phansa, Thai Buddhists hold **Tak Bat Thewo**, an alms-giving that recalls the Buddha's descent from the Tavatimsa heaven (*Thewo Rohana*). Tradition says the crowds were too great to reach him, so people **threw** their rice parcels into his bowl. The long tail of the wrapping makes it easy to throw. Today temples re-enact the descent and the faithful place luk yon in the monks' bowls (Thai PBS; Thai Government PR Department).

In the south the same parcel, **khanom tom** wrapped in **kapho palm** (*bai kapho*), belongs to **Chak Phra / Lak Phra**: on the day after Ok Phansa, a Buddha image is pulled through town on a boat or float, and villagers throw khanom tom onto it or hang them on the boat's prow as merit. Southern versions use white or black glutinous rice fried with coconut cream.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Central Thailand (Tak Bat Thewo):** Pyramid parcels in young coconut or pandan leaf, topped with boiled **black beans**, with a long tail. Also called *khao tom hang* ("tailed rice").
*   **Surat Thani and the Chak Phra south:** **Khanom tom** in **kapho-palm** leaves folded into a triangle like a water chestnut (*krachap*). White or black sticky rice; thrown onto or hung from the Buddha boats.
*   **Nipa-leaf versions:** Coastal areas wrap in nipa palm (*bai chak*).
*   **Cousin:** the banana-filled [khao tom mat](recipe_khao_tom_mat.md), tied in pairs in banana leaf and given at the same festival.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **central luk yon with black beans**, with the southern kapho-leaf fold noted.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Coconut milk | 400 ml |
| Sugar | 60 g |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |
| Black beans, soaked overnight and boiled until just tender | 60 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice, soaked 2–3 h | 400 g |
| Young coconut / pandan / kapho leaves (wiped) | about 20 strips |
| Makes | about 16 parcels |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Soak:** Soak the glutinous rice for **2–3 hours**, then drain.
2. **Fry:** Heat the coconut milk with the sugar and salt in a wok, add the rice and stir over low heat until the liquid is absorbed and the rice is about half-cooked (**10–15 minutes**). Cool slightly.
3. **Wrap:** Spoon rice into a leaf cone, add a few black beans and fold it into a pyramid, leaving a long tail. For the southern style, fold a kapho leaf into a triangle. Tie firmly.
4. **Boil:** Cover with boiling water and simmer for **1 hour**, until the rice is fully cooked and dense.
5. **Serve:** Drain and cool. Offer at the temple, or eat with sugar or condensed milk.
