# Mehshi Selek bi-Zeit / محشي سلق بالزيت

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lebanon, especially Christian households of Mount Lebanon and Beirut during **Lent**. Wherever chard is grown, it is a spring and autumn olive-oil dish.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed leaves cooked in olive oil (*mahashi bi-zeit*). Chard rolls filled with rice, chickpeas, tomato and herbs, slowly cooked in lemon and olive oil until the rice is soft and slightly tangy. Eaten at room temperature.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide, heavy pot on a low flame, the rolls packed tightly and weighed down with a plate.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Mehshi selek* is the meatless cousin of [warak enab](recipe_warak_enab.md) and [malfouf mehshi](recipe_malfouf_mehshi.md). It belongs to the **bi-zeit** ("in oil") family of Lebanese dishes: cooked with olive oil instead of meat or butter, eaten cold, and essential on Christian fasting days, when meat and dairy are excluded. L'Orient-Le Jour published a Lenten version by Liza Soughayar Asseily: rolls lightly browned and then "confit" in olive oil, filled with white rice, chickpeas, parsley and mint. Anissa Helou calls *mehshi silq bil-zeyt* "one of my favourite Lebanese dishes". Lebanese cooks use chard and vine leaves interchangeably for the same filling.

**Culinary science:** chard leaves are thinner and more tender than cabbage and need only a quick dip in boiling water to become pliable. With no meat in the filling, the rice is enriched by olive oil and the juice of chopped tomato, and given body by chickpeas. Cooking slowly in oil and lemon creates a light emulsion that coats each roll, which is why the dish tastes best once it has cooled.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **L'Orient-Le Jour Lenten confit (Beirut):** The rolls are browned first, then cooked slowly in plenty of olive oil, with chickpeas in the filling; the leaves are prepared the day before.
*   **Anissa Helou's *mehshi silq bil-zeyt*:** A classic olive-oil version from her book *Levant*.
*   **With meat:** Outside Lent some households stuff chard with minced lamb and rice and cook it in tomato sauce, like a summer version of malfouf.
*   ⚠️ No published source distinguishes particular towns.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Lenten olive-oil version with chickpeas.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Swiss chard leaves, large (stems removed) | 30–35 leaves (≈ 800 g) |
| Short-grain rice, rinsed | 200 g |
| Cooked chickpeas, lightly crushed | 120 g |
| Ripe tomatoes, finely diced | 2 (≈ 250 g) |
| Flat-leaf parsley, chopped | 50 g |
| Fresh mint, chopped | 15 g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 1 small |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | 150 ml |
| Lemon juice | 120 ml |
| Salt, black pepper, sumac (optional) | to taste |
| Water | ≈ 600 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Filling per leaf | 1 tbsp |
| Rice : chickpeas | ≈ 5 : 3 |
| Liquid (oil + lemon + water) | to just cover |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Leaves (can be done the day before):** Dip the chard leaves in boiling water for a few seconds until pliable, then drain. Cut large leaves in half along the rib.
2. **Filling:** Mix the rice, chickpeas, tomato, parsley, mint and onion with half the oil, salt and pepper.
3. **Roll:** Roll each piece of leaf tightly around a tablespoon of filling, tucking in the sides.
4. **Brown (optional, L'Orient-Le Jour method):** Lightly brown the rolls in a little oil in the pot, then pack them in tight layers.
5. **Cook:** Pour over the remaining oil, the lemon juice and enough water to cover. Weigh down with a plate and simmer very gently, covered, for **50–60 min**, until the rice is tender.
6. **Rest:** Let the rolls cool in the pot so the oil and lemon set around them. Serve at room temperature.
