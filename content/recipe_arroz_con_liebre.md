# Arroz con Liebre / Arroz Meloso de Liebre (La Mancha y Extremadura)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Castilla-La Mancha & Extremadura](provincial_castilla_la_mancha_extremadura.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Mancha (Ciudad Real, Albacete, Cuenca, Toledo plains) and Extremadura (Vegas del Guadiana, Tierra de Barros), where it is listed among traditional Extremaduran meat dishes.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). One recipe insists it must be "very meloso, not dry, and not caldoso either".
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay *cazuela* over a holm-oak or vine-shoot fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The hare (*liebre*) is the prized small game of the open cereal plains of La Mancha and the Extremaduran dehesa. Manchegan cooking turns it into *gazpacho manchego* (game with flatbread) and *morteruelo* (a game pâté thickened with bread), and into **rice**. One published recipe calls arroz con liebre "a typically Castilian preparation that recalls the aroma of the countryside". Extremadura is Spain's **second rice-producing region** (Vegas del Guadiana), which gives the hare rice a strong local footing there.

**Culinary science:** hare is a dark, iron-rich, strong-flavoured meat. Cooks often **marinate** it in wine with bay and garlic to temper it, and cook it slowly so it becomes tender. A pressure cooker cuts the braise to about 40 min. The braising liquid, strained, becomes the rice broth.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Castilian/Manchegan basic (OKDiario):** Hare, onion, **4 bay leaves**, **8 garlic cloves**, water. Pressure-cooked, broth strained. Meloso.
*   **La Mancha (Ciudad Real / Albacete):** Marinated in **red wine** overnight. Often with *níscalos* in autumn. Clay cazuela over wood.
*   **Extremadura (Vegas del Guadiana):** Local paddy rice + hare + **pimentón de la Vera**.
*   *Town-level variations are only partly sourced; verify locally.*

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **La Mancha marinated, meloso**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Hare, jointed | 1 (≈ 1.8 kg) |
| Red wine (marinade) | 500 ml |
| Bay leaves | 4 |
| Garlic | 8 cloves |
| Onion | 1 |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Pimentón | 3 g |
| Water | 2.5 L |
| Thyme | 1 sprig |

**Extraction:** Marinate the hare in the wine with the bay and garlic for **12 h**. Drain and dry it. Brown it in the oil (12 min). Add the onion (10 min), tomato and pimentón (5 min), then the marinade liquid (reduce by half) and the water. Braise **90 min** (or 40 min under pressure). Strain and keep ≈ 1.6 L of broth. Bone the meat if you like.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 360 g |
| Hare broth | ≈ 1.5 L (≈ 4:1) |
| Hare meat | ≈ 900 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Add the rice to the cazuela with the hare. Pour in the boiling broth.
2. **Fire:** Strong for 8 min → medium for 9 min, stirring gently.
3. **Finish:** Rest 3 min. It should be loose, not dry and not soupy.
