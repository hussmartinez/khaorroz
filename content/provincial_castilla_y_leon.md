# Castilla y León

> ⬅ [README](README.md)

## 1. Overview
Castilla y León grows no rice, yet rice is woven into its **matanza** (pig-slaughter) culture. The most famous example is the **arroz a la zamorana**, documented since an 1884 almanac describing a rice served in Alcañices. Rice also appears in the region's offal stews (Salamanca's **chanfaina**), in the river crayfish tradition of **La Bañeza**, in Lenten legume pots, and above all inside its charcuterie: the rice **morcillas** of Burgos and of Villada (Tierra de Campos), where rice cooked with onion is mixed with blood, lard and spices. The *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria*'s 2022 survey of forgotten dishes of Castilla y León records several of these village rices.

## 2. Cultural Sub-Regions
| Sub-region | Rice personality | Key towns |
|---|---|---|
| **Zamora (Aliste, Tierra de Campos)** | Arroz a la zamorana: matanza pork, ear, snout, Villalpando paprika | Zamora, Alcañices, Villalpando |
| **Salamanca (Campo Charro)** | Chanfaina: lamb offal rice with cumin and egg | Salamanca |
| **León (La Bañeza, Órbigo)** | River crayfish rice, chickpea-and-rice potaje | La Bañeza |
| **Burgos & Palencia** | Rice in charcuterie: morcilla de Burgos, morcilla de Villada | Burgos, Villada |
| **El Bierzo** | Restaurant rices with *botillo* (⚠️ not yet documented) | Ponferrada |
