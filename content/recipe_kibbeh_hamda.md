# Kibbeh Hamda (Hamud) / كبة حامضة

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented as a **Halabi (Aleppo) Jewish** recipe by the Jewish Food Society and Jewish media, with family recipes from Brooklyn and Mexico. Similar sour kibbeh soups exist among Iraqi, Egyptian and Lebanese Jewish families; attribution here is to the Aleppo community as the sources state. Whether it is served over rice varies by family: several accounts serve it alongside rice, the Passover account does not.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Aleppo Jewish community historically; Syrian-Jewish diaspora in Brooklyn, Deal (NJ), Mexico City and Israel.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice in two forms: small **dumplings with a shell of ground rice and meat**, stuffed with spiced lamb, simmered in a **sour lemon–mint broth** with vegetables; spooned over white rice in many households.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large soup pot on the stovetop.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Hamud* means "sour" in Arabic, and in Syrian-Jewish homes the word names this tangy broth, short for *kibbeh hamda*. Its distinctive feature is the **kibbeh shell made from ground rice** instead of bulgur: finely ground long-grain rice kneaded with lamb and beef, wrapped around a filling of lamb, parsley, allspice and cinnamon. The dumplings poach in stock with celery, garlic, carrots, potatoes, squash, dried mint and a large amount of lemon or lime juice.

The Jewish Food Society publishes Jennifer Abadi's family recipe, learned from her grandmother Fritzie, whose family came from Aleppo to Brooklyn in 1921–1923; there it opened the **Passover Seder**. Other writers describe it as the taste of **Friday-night Shabbat**. In Mexico City's Syrian-Jewish community, a **hamud batata** (potato hamud) Friday dish is remembered as a family emblem.

**Culinary science:** ground rice, unlike bulgur, has little gluten-like structure, so the meat in the shell is what binds it; chilling the dough and wetting the hands make it workable. Adding the lemon juice late keeps its brightness and avoids toughening the meat.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Aleppo / Brooklyn (Abadi family):** Ground-rice shell, lamb filling; celery, scallions, garlic, carrots, potatoes, squash; dried mint; lemon or lime; Passover starter.
*   **Shabbat versions:** Lemon–mint broth with celery and garlic; served with white rice ⚠️.
*   **Mexico City (Syrian-Jewish, diaspora variant):** *Hamud batata*, with potatoes prominent; Friday-night meal.
*   **Iraqi & Egyptian Jewish cousins (contrast):** Similar sour soups (Iraqi *kubba hamuth*), with different vegetables (turnip, beet); outside Bilad al-Sham.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Aleppine Passover hamud**, after the Abadi family recipe, scaled to 4.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| **Filling:** ground lamb | 150 g |
| Allspice, cinnamon, salt; chopped parsley | ¼ tsp each, ½ tsp salt; 3 tbsp |
| **Shell:** finely ground long-grain rice | 70 g |
| Ground lamb + ground beef (shell) | 150 g + 150 g |
| **Broth:** chicken or meat stock | 2 L |
| Celery, sliced | 250 g |
| Scallions, garlic | 1 bunch / 4 cloves |
| Carrots, potatoes, yellow squash (diced) | 1 / 1 / 1 small |
| Dried mint | 2 tsp |
| Lemon or lime juice | 80 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dumplings | ≈ 16–20, walnut-size |
| White rice to serve (optional) | 250 g rice : 375 ml water |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Filling:** Mix the filling lamb with spices, salt, parsley and 2 tbsp cold water.
2. **Shell:** Knead the ground rice with the shell meats and salt into a smooth dough; chill 20 minutes.
3. **Shape:** With wet hands, flatten walnut-size pieces of shell, add filling, close into balls.
4. **Broth:** Bring the stock to a boil with the celery, scallions, garlic, carrots and potatoes; simmer 10 minutes.
5. **Poach:** Lower the dumplings in and simmer 20–25 minutes; add the squash for the last 10.
6. **Sour:** Stir in the dried mint and lemon juice; taste for salt; simmer 2 minutes.
7. **Serve:** In bowls, or ladled over white rice.
