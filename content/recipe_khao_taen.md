# Khao Taen (Lampang Rice Crackers) / ข้าวแต๋น

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** origin and ingredients are documented in Thai sources (Silpa Wattanatham, Dusit University journal, Daily News), but no published quantified recipe was found. Quantities are reconstructed. The Lampang GI status is claimed by producers and has not been verified here with the Department of Intellectual Property.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna, especially **Lampang**. Also made in Khorat and Udon Thani (Isan) as a souvenir snack.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Fried sticky-rice cracker. Discs of dried glutinous rice puff in hot oil and are drizzled with caramelised cane or palm sugar. They are light, shattering and sweet-salty.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Bamboo ring moulds on banana leaf for shaping, sun-drying racks, and a deep wok of oil over charcoal or gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khao taen* began in the north as a snack and spread to other regions under different names ([Silpa Wattanatham](https://www.silpa-mag.com/tag/%e0%b8%82%e0%b9%89%e0%b8%b2%e0%b8%a7%e0%b9%81%e0%b8%95%e0%b9%8b%e0%b8%99)). Its colour tells you its base: white from plain rice, **red from rice mixed with cane juice or watermelon juice**, and black from purple sticky rice (*khao kam*). The red watermelon version is the most popular ([Dusit University journal](https://ejournals.dusit.ac.th/openpdf/openpdf.php?id=113); [Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/5278892/)). Makers insist on mid-season glutinous rice, which puffs better and stays crisp longer ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/5278892/)). Udon Thani's "rainbow" watermelon khao taen developed from the central Thai *khanom nang let* ([PRD](https://prd.go.th/main/article/8d32de13-8f21-4eac-9ada-9e0aead32e6f)).

**Culinary science:** steamed rice dried to low moisture becomes a glassy starch disc. In 180 °C oil the residual water vaporises explosively and puffs it into a light, porous crisp, the same physics as [khao pong](recipe_khao_pong.md). The sugar drizzle then sets into a brittle glaze.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Lampang (red, watermelon):** Watermelon juice in the rice, with a cane-sugar drizzle ([Dusit](https://ejournals.dusit.ac.th/openpdf/openpdf.php?id=113)).
*   **Black (*khao taen khao kam*):** Made from purple sticky rice ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/5278892/)).
*   **Khorat:** Watermelon khao taen made with mid-season rice ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/5278892/)).
*   **Udon Thani "rainbow":** Multicoloured, a development of *khanom nang let* ([PRD](https://prd.go.th/main/article/8d32de13-8f21-4eac-9ada-9e0aead32e6f)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Lampang red khao taen (reconstructed quantities).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice, soaked overnight | 500 g |
| Watermelon juice (strained) | 150 ml |
| Salt | 1 tsp |
| Cane sugar or palm sugar (drizzle) | 150 g |
| Vegetable oil (frying) | 1 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Discs | ≈ 24, 7 cm, 5 mm thick |
| Drying | 1–2 days in sun |
| Frying | 180 °C, a few seconds each |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Steam:** Steam the soaked rice until just cooked (see [khao niao nueng](recipe_khao_niao_nueng.md)).
2. **Flavour:** While hot, mix in the watermelon juice and salt until evenly pink-red.
3. **Mould:** Press into bamboo ring moulds on banana leaf to form thin discs.
4. **Dry:** Sun-dry until hard and fully dry (**1–2 days**), turning once.
5. **Fry and glaze:** Fry a few at a time in hot oil until they puff (seconds), then drain. Melt the sugar with a splash of water to a thread syrup and drizzle it over in lines. Cool until crisp.
