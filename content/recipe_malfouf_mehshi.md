# Malfouf Mehshi / ملفوف محشي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Lebanon, especially in winter, when cabbage is in season. It is a home dish rather than a restaurant one, cooked in Beirut, Mount Lebanon and the Bekaa alike.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed leaves (*mahashi*). Thin cabbage rolls packed with rice and minced meat, cooked layered with whole garlic cloves in a lemony broth until the rice is soft and the leaves are silky. Served with some of the garlic-lemon liquid.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot (*tanjara*), with the rolls packed tightly in layers, weighed down with an upturned plate, and simmered on the stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Cabbage rolls are the winter partner of [warak enab](recipe_warak_enab.md). When the vine leaves are long gone, the same rice-and-meat stuffing (*hashweh*) is rolled into blanched cabbage leaves. The Lebanese signature is **garlic**: whole cloves are tucked between the layers, and the cooking liquid is sharpened with lemon juice and **dried mint**. L'Orient-Le Jour, the Beirut daily, publishes it as a family classic (*mehshé malfouf*), and Joumana Accad's *Taste of Beirut* calls it "mehche malfoof".

**Culinary science:** cabbage leaves contain sulphur compounds that turn harsh with long boiling. Blanching them briefly first and finishing them in an acidic broth softens the cabbage taste and keeps the leaves tender. The raw rice in the filling swells with broth and meat juices; the rolls are packed tightly and weighted with a plate so they don't unroll as the rice expands.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Classic home version (Beirut / Mount Lebanon):** Minced beef or lamb with short-grain rice, seven-spice, and garlic between the layers. Lemon-garlic broth with dried mint.
*   **Lenten / meatless version (*bi-zeit*):** Rice with chopped tomato, onion, parsley and plenty of lemon and olive oil; one published variant replaces the meat with lentils. This is the Christian fasting-day form, parallel to [mehshi selek](recipe_mehshi_selek.md).
*   **Serving variants:** Some households serve it with a garlic-yogurt sauce on the side, others just with Lebanese bread.
*   ⚠️ Specific town-level differences (e.g. Zahle vs Tripoli) were not found in published sources.

### Palestinian style (malfuf mahshi): Nablus, Jerusalem, Hebron
*(Merged from the Palestine research.)*

*   **Gaza (The Gaza Kitchen):** Garlic-forward broth; the sweet Gaza cabbage; leftover stuffing rolled in romaine lettuce.
*   **West Bank family style (Palestine in a Dish):** Lamb or beef, 1 tbsp **cumin**, allspice, oil and butter; unpeeled garlic layered in; flipped onto a platter like maqluba; served with lemon, yogurt or cucumber-yogurt salad.
*   **Lenten / vegetarian (Christian households of Bethlehem and Beit Jala) (⚠️):** Rice, tomato, parsley and chickpeas without meat. A common Levantine practice, not specifically documented here.
*   **Diaspora (Chile):** *Rellenos* (stuffed vegetables) are ubiquitous on Santiago's *comida árabe* menus.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: classic Lebanese home version with meat.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Green cabbage, large (leaves separated, ribs shaved) | 1 head (≈ 1.5 kg) |
| Short-grain rice (Egyptian or Calrose), rinsed | 250 g |
| Minced beef or lamb (not too lean) | 350 g |
| Lebanese seven-spice (or allspice) | 1 tsp |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |
| Olive oil or melted butter | 3 tbsp |
| Garlic cloves, peeled | 1 head |
| Lemon juice | 120 ml |
| Dried mint | 1 tbsp |
| Water or light stock | ≈ 900 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Filling per roll (finger-thick, ≈ 8 cm) | 1 heaped tbsp |
| Rice : meat (by weight) | ≈ 0.7 : 1 |
| Liquid | to just cover the rolls |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Blanch:** Cut the core out of the cabbage and blanch it in boiling salted water, peeling off the leaves as they soften. Cut out the thick central rib and halve large leaves.
2. **Filling:** Mix the raw rice, meat, spice, salt and oil.
3. **Roll:** Put a line of filling along the base of each leaf piece and roll it tightly into a thin cigar.
4. **Layer:** Line the pot with the trimmed ribs and torn leaves. Pack the rolls in tight rows, scattering garlic cloves and a pinch of dried mint between each layer.
5. **Cook:** Weigh the rolls down with an upturned plate. Add water to cover with a pinch of salt and bring to a boil, then cover and simmer gently **45–60 min**, until the rice is fully cooked.
6. **Finish:** Pour in the lemon juice mixed with the remaining mint and simmer **10 min**. Rest 10 min before turning out or serving from the pot with the garlic.
