# A History of Rice in Spain / Historia del arroz en España

> ⬅ [README](README.md)

A sourced narrative of how rice reached the Iberian Peninsula, how it reshaped the wetlands of the Mediterranean coast, why it was repeatedly banned, and how a field-worker's pan of rice became one of the world's best-known dishes. Points where sources disagree or are thin are marked **⚠️**.

## 1. The Andalusi Introduction

Asian rice (*Oryza sativa*) reached the eastern Mediterranean long before Islam. Recent scholarship places it there by about the 2nd century AD, and challenges the old assumption that rice in western Eurasia is simply an "Arab legacy" ([Dialnet – study on the introduction of Asian rice in al-Andalus](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/5163799.pdf)). What al-Andalus did was turn it into a **crop**. Andalusi agronomists describe it. Ibn Bassal, for example, records sowing in March–April and reaping in September. In the early centuries rice seems to have been a **luxury food** of privileged groups, and the volume grown was probably small, since documentation from the period is scarce (same source; ⚠️ the scale of Andalusi rice farming remains debated).

The vocabulary tells the story. Castilian *arroz* and Valencian/Catalan *arròs* come from Andalusi Arabic *ar-ruzz*. The word **albufera** comes from *al-buḥayra*, "the lagoon" or "little sea" ([Wiktionary – albuhera](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/albuhera); [iAgua – ¿Qué es una albufera?](https://www.iagua.es/respuestas/que-es-albufera)). *Acequia*/*séquia* (irrigation channel) is usually traced to Arabic *as-sāqiya* (⚠️ the etymologies of *arroz* and *acequia* are standard dictionary derivations, but the web sources in this search were popular rather than philological).

## 2. Séquies, Acequias and the Tribunal de les Aigües

Romans had already dug some channels in the Valencian *horta*, but it was the Andalusi period that turned the plain into an intensively irrigated garden ([Biblioteca Virtual Sénior UJI – agricultura valenciana](https://bibliotecavirtualsenior.uji.es/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/TRABAJO-AGRICULTURA-DEFINITIVO.pdf)). Eight main *séquies* drawn from the Túria still water the Vega de València. Their users settle disputes before the **Tribunal de les Aigües** (Water Tribunal). The *síndics* of the eight channels meet every **Thursday** at the Apostles' Door of Valencia Cathedral. Rulings are given orally, on the spot, and cannot be appealed ([Cátedra L'Horta de València – Universitat de València](https://www.uv.es/catedra-horta-valencia/es/catalogo-patrimonio/tribunal-aguas/tribunal-aguas.html); [MAPA – Ministerio de Agricultura](https://www.mapa.gob.es/en/prensa/ultimas-noticias/detalle_noticias/luis-planas-recibe-la-blusa-de-s-ndico-de-honor-del-tribunal-de-las-aguas-de-la-vega-de-valencia-/bcdd5486-2fe2-4055-baa9-cb5098bdd7bc)).

On **30 September 2009** UNESCO inscribed the Tribunal, together with Murcia's *Consejo de Hombres Buenos*, on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. ⚠️ The traditional founding date of **960** (under Caliph ʿAbd al-Raḥmān III / al-Ḥakam II) and the claim that it is "Europe's oldest court" are repeated widely, but they rest on tradition rather than archival documents ([Museu d'Història de València – teaching dossier](https://mhv.valencia.es/sites/default/files/mhv/pdfs/tribunal_recursos_alumno_1507.pdf)).

## 3. L'Albufera: from Salt Lagoon to Rice Bowl

The Albufera of Valencia was originally a **brackish** lagoon, open to the sea through a natural *gola* (outlet). It stayed salty into the 18th century. The decisive change came when the **Acequia Real del Júcar** was extended in the 18th century, promoted by the Duke of Híjar to irrigate the lordship of Sollana. The resulting freshwater inflow allowed rice growers to advance across the marsh ([Ajuntament de València – Devesa-Albufera](https://www.valencia.es/web/albufera/cas/conocela/ecosistemas-ambientes); [Gran Enciclopèdia Catalana – l'Albufera](https://www.enciclopedia.cat/gran-enciclopedia-catalana/lalbufera)). By the late 18th century rice farmers were expanding fast. After the liberal reforms they took control of the lake's management, which they had long contested with the fishermen. In the first third of the 19th century the rice-landowner-dominated **Junta de Desagüe** took charge of drainage ([Publicacions UV – Regadiu i canvi ambiental](https://puv.uv.es/media/relatedcontent/Regadiu_i_canvi_ambiental_2.pdf)). Most of today's paddies between Alfafar and Sueca are land reclaimed from the lagoon during the 19th and 20th centuries.

Ownership followed. A law of **23 June 1911** transferred the lake and its Devesa from the State to the **Ajuntament de València**, with the transfer completed in 1927. In **1986** (Decreto 89/1986, 8 July) the Generalitat declared the lake, its wetland and the Devesa del Saler the **Parc Natural de l'Albufera**. It was the first natural area protected by the Generalitat ([Wikipedia – Albufera Natural Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albufera_Natural_Park); [DOGV 1986](https://dogv.gva.es/datos/1986/07/23/pdf/1986_901159.pdf); ⚠️ sources give both 8 July and 23 July 1986, the latter probably the publication date).

## 4. Rice and Malaria: Bans, *Acotamientos* and *Cotos Arroceros*

Standing water bred *Anopheles* mosquitoes, and malaria (*paludisme*, *tercianes*) followed the paddies. From the 13th century to the 19th, the history of Valencian rice is also a history of **prohibitions**:

* **Jaume I (after 1238):** tourism material says the conqueror banned rice around the city of Valencia on health grounds ([Comunitat Valenciana – Ruta 10 Albufera arroz (PDF)](https://multimedia.comunitatvalenciana.com/A6686EE3740C44FF99DB90ED65A39022/doc/76144C4966A24C3AA1565966960054BA/RUTA_10_ALBUFERA_ARROZ_VALENCIA_COCEMFE_CV_TAMBIEN_PARA_TI_2023.pdf)). ⚠️ This is a promotional source. I found **no** source for a ban by **Jaume II**, which is sometimes cited.
* **1342:** a ban issued by the *Jurats* of Valencia was confirmed in the Corts by **Pere IV** (Pedro el Ceremonioso).
* **1403:** **Martí l'Humà** extended the ban to the whole Kingdom of Valencia, and **Alfons el Magnànim** ratified it in 1467 ([Fundación Juan March – historical study of Valencian rice](https://cdndigital.march.es/fedora/objects/linz:R-35172/datastreams/OBJ/content)).

The bans were repeatedly evaded, renewed and reinterpreted. The compromise that emerged was the **acotamiento**: rice was allowed only inside officially demarcated *cotos* (zones) kept at a distance from large towns. In the 18th century authorities tried to push paddies away from populated centres. At Orihuela, by contrast, local rice policy ignored earlier bans and the health warnings of neighbouring towns ([Hispania (CSIC) – Peset on 18th-century Valencia](https://hispania.revistas.csic.es/index.php/hispania/article/download/329/327/328); [CORE – Orihuela rice study](https://core.ac.uk/download/498697134.pdf)). The modern legal frame came with a **Royal Order of 1860** and a **regulation of 1881**, under which cultivation outside a coto was prohibited ([Fundación Juan March](https://cdndigital.march.es/fedora/objects/linz:R-35172/datastreams/OBJ/content)). A CSIC study traces the link between rice, mosquitoes and malaria in the province of Valencia from the 18th to the 20th century ([Digital.CSIC – Cultivo del arroz y paludismo](https://digital.csic.es/bitstream/10261/288418/1/Cultivo_arroz_paludismo.pdf)). Malaria was not eliminated from Spain until the mid-20th century (⚠️ Spain was officially certified malaria-free in 1964; that date comes from general knowledge and was not verified in this search).

## 5. The Valencian Rice Economy and Its Diaspora

Rice became the economic engine of **La Ribera Baixa**. **Sueca** is described as the leading rice-producing centre of the Catalan-speaking lands, with 30–40 million kg a year ([Gran Enciclopèdia Catalana – Sueca](https://www.enciclopedia.cat/gran-enciclopedia-catalana/sueca)). Cullera, Sollana, Silla and Algemesí ring the Albufera with paddies, and Sueca's **Festa de l'Arròs** and its paella competition celebrate that identity. Today the sector faces price crises that threaten a centuries-old crop ([València Plaza – Arroz amargo](https://valenciaplaza.com/arroz-amargo-la-crisis-del-precio-pone-al-cultivo-centenario-en-una-situacion-limite)).

Valencian know-how then spread to other wetlands:

* **Delta de l'Ebre (Catalonia):** in the mid-19th century the delta was still a marginal, thinly populated space of hunting, fishing and gathering. Rice is what drove its agrarian colonisation. Irrigation canals on the right bank were planned in **1851**. The arrival of the Zaragoza–Barcelona railway killed the companion navigation canal, and irrigation became the priority ([Documents d'Anàlisi Geogràfica (UAB)](https://dag.revista.uab.cat/article/view/v5-canoves-sauri); [iAgua – Delta del Ebro](https://www.iagua.es/blogs/emma-mora/cooperacion-en-concepto-de-agua-en-el-delta-del-ebro)). The area still covered about 17,600 ha in 1960 before a decline in the 1960s ([MAPA – Distribución y Consumo 2006](https://www.mapa.gob.es/ministerio/pags/Biblioteca/Revistas/pdf_DT/DT_2006_12_28_31.pdf)).
* **Marismas del Guadalquivir (Andalusia):** the marshes near Seville were drained by colonisation companies (e.g. the *Poblado de Alfonso XIII*) ([UPO – Poblado de Alfonso XIII](https://www.upo.es/patio-colorado/2022/05/25/conoce-el-poblado-de-alfonso-xiii/)). Rice took off with **Valencian settlers**: González Arteaga's census data show **no Valencians in 1925 and 178 by 1955**, mostly arriving between **1937 and 1955** ([Revista de Estudios Andaluces – Valencianos y arroz en las Marismas](https://revistascientificas.us.es/index.php/REA/article/download/5546/4887/17522)). The town, first named *Villafranco del Guadalquivir*, was renamed **Isla Mayor** by popular vote in 2000 ([Wikipedia – Isla Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Isla_Mayor)). ⚠️ The common claim that Valencians colonised Isla Mayor "in the 1920s" is **not** supported by this data.
* **Calasparra (Murcia):** mountain rice grown on terraces irrigated by the Segura and Mundo rivers, documented since the **14th century** ([MAPA – Pliego Calasparra](https://mapa.gob.es/ca/alimentacion/temas/calidad-diferenciada/pliegocalasparra_tcm34-543260.pdf)).

## 6. How "Paella" Got Its Name

In Valencian, *paella* first meant the **pan**: the wide, shallow, two-handled vessel. The dish took its name from it, just as *cassola* or *olla* name their dishes. The word comes from Latin **patella** ("small pan, shallow dish", a diminutive of *patina*), through Old Catalan and Old French **paele** ([Etymonline – paella](https://www.etymonline.com/es/word/paella); [Etymonline – patella](https://www.etymonline.com/es/word/patella)). The popular folk etymology **"para ella"** ("for her", a man cooking for his beloved) has no documentary basis, and neither does the claimed Arabic *baqiyya* ("leftovers"). Both are myths ([Economist & Jurist – ¿Por qué la paella se llama paella?](https://www.economistjurist.es/zestilo-de-vida/por-que-la-paella-se-llama-paella/)). ⚠️ No source I found explicitly "refutes" *para ella*. It is rejected because it lacks any documentary support and ignores the much older Latin-Romance word for the pan.

### Origin legends: what is true and what isn't

**Legend 1: "Paella" comes from Arabic *baqiyya* ("leftovers").** The story goes that the servants of the Moorish kings of Valencia took home the leftover rice, chicken, fish and vegetables from their masters' banquets, cooked them together, and called the result *baqiyya* ("what remains", بقية). The story is especially widespread in Arabic-speaking countries ([Hispanopedia – Paella](https://es.hispanopedia.com/wiki/Paella); [Al Jazeera blogs – بقية في إسبانيا](https://www.ajnet.me/blogs/2019/12/30/بقية-في-إسبانيا)).

**Verdict: not supported. The Latin etymology is the established one.**
*   **The word is a Romance word for a pan.** It descends from Latin *patella* ("small pan, shallow dish"), which also gave French *poêle* ("frying pan"). Many Romance languages have a close relative of the word for a pan, all with no Arabic involved ([Wiktionary – paella](https://www.wiktionary.com/wiki/paella); [Etymonline – paella](https://www.etymonline.com/word/paella)).
*   **The sound change fits Romance, not Arabic.** Joan Coromines, the great Catalan etymologist, argues the Catalan word came via Old French *paele*, because a direct Latin descent would have given *padella* (as reported by [Wikipedia – Paella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paella)). Either way, the path runs through Latin, not Arabic. ⚠️ I couldn't check Coromines's original entry directly.
*   **The dates don't fit.** The word *paella* for the *dish* appears only in the 19th century (1840s–1850s; see below), centuries after Muslim Valencia fell to Jaume I in 1238. The *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria* calls the *baqiyya* story "una historia preciosa pero realmente no está contrastada" (a lovely story that has never been verified), "difícil de sostener puesto que los primeros escritos que se conocen aparecen siglos después" (hard to sustain, since the first known writings appear centuries later) ([RENC 2022, parte 1](https://renc.es/imagenes/auxiliar/files/2022_RENC_VOL_28_SUPL1_PLATOS_OLVIDADOS_COMUNITAT_VALENCIANA_parte_1__1_.pdf)).
*   **The kernel of truth.** The *ingredients and infrastructure* behind paella are Andalusi: rice (*arròs* < Arabic *ar-ruzz*), saffron (*safrà* < *za'farān*), and the irrigation channels (*séquies*) that made the Albufera paddies possible (see §1–3). Paella owes a real debt to al-Andalus, but its *name* isn't part of that debt.

**Legend 2: paella began as fishermen's leftovers.** This conflates two different traditions.
*   **True for the seafood rices.** The rices that really did come from fishermen making use of unsaleable catch are the [arroz a banda](recipe_arroz_a_banda.md) and the [caldero](recipe_caldero_santa_pola_tabarca.md). There, the bony *morralla* the boats couldn't sell was boiled into a broth, the fish was eaten first and the rice was cooked in what remained. "Leftovers + rice + fishermen" is accurate for *those* dishes.
*   **Not true for the original paella.** The earliest paella is a **land** dish of the Albufera rice fields and the *horta*. It was cooked by farmhands and labourers at midday over wood, with rabbit, chicken, duck, snails, *bajoqueta* and *garrofó* (RENC 2022, above). Seafood paellas (*paella de marisco*, *mixta*) came later, with the 20th-century coastal merenderos and tourism.

**Legend 3: "para ella" ("for her").** A man cooking for his beloved. This is charming, but it has no documentary basis and ignores the Latin word for the pan (see above).

When did the dish name appear? Printed recipes for **"arroz a la valenciana"** appear in Levantine press around **1788–1789**. The word *paella* for the dish starts to appear between **1840 and 1860** ([ESdiario – La paella entre el mito y la realidad](https://www.esdiario.com/comunidad-valenciana/250810/164798/paella-mito-realidad_amp.html)). Food historians cite **1840** as the first newspaper use of *paella* for the recipe rather than the pan ([Wikipedia – Paella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paella)), and **1857** for the first printed recipe under that name, in a Madrid book ("Sartén a la valenciana (Paella)") ([Última Hora – Valencianos y vascos se enredan en un debate](https://www.ultimahora.es/xaloc/gastronomia/2023/05/10/1933987/valencianos-vascos-enredan-debate-sobre-origen-paella.html)). ⚠️ The newspaper often named for 1840 (the *Diario Mercantil de Valencia*) could not be confirmed.

The dish itself was born in the Albufera marshes as a midday meal for farmhands, cooked over wood with rabbit, chicken, duck, snails, beans and whatever the *horta* supplied. The *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria* supplement on forgotten Valencian dishes summarises the two competing etymologies and places the origin firmly in the Albufera ([RENC 2022 – Platos olvidados de la Comunitat Valenciana, parte 1](https://renc.es/imagenes/auxiliar/files/2022_RENC_VOL_28_SUPL1_PLATOS_OLVIDADOS_COMUNITAT_VALENCIANA_parte_1__1_.pdf)).

## 7. From the *Barraca* to the World (19th–20th Centuries)

According to the same RENC study, the paella "jumped from the *barracas* and *alquerías* to the eating-houses and *merenderos*" of the beaches: the **Malva-rosa** in Valencia, **El Grau de Castelló**, the **Albufereta** of Alicante. At the Malva-rosa, summer *merenderos* set up on the sand cooked paellas over open fires. Some evolved into permanent restaurants (e.g. **Casa Isabel**, and **Casa Carmela**, reputedly founded in 1922) ([Visit València – Casa Isabel](https://www.visitvalencia.com/que-hacer-valencia/gastronomia/restaurantes-valencia/casa-isabel); [Mueble de España – Casa Carmela](https://www.muebledeespana.com/projects/restaurant-casa-carmela); [València Plaza – Las paellas que se fueron](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/las-paellas-que-se-fueron)). The novelist **Vicente Blasco Ibáñez** built his chalet on the beach in 1902, and his Albufera novels (*Cañas y barro*) put the rice world into Spanish literature ([Comunitat Valenciana – Blasco Ibáñez y la Malvarrosa](https://www.comunitatvalenciana.com/es/mediterraneo-en-accion/blasco-ibanez-malvarrosa)). ⚠️ Claims of famous visitors (Hemingway at Casa Carmela) rest on restaurant promotion.

The mass-tourism boom turned paella into a **national** cliché. Arrivals rose from 2.5 million in 1955 to 14.2 million in 1965, under the "**Spain is different**" campaign associated with Manuel Fraga's Ministry of Information and Tourism ([Rebelión – Ficciones culturales durante la modernización franquista](https://rebelion.org/ficciones-culturales-durante-la-modernizacion-franquista/); [History Today – Spain Was Different](https://www.historytoday.com/node/57441)). Paella (often "with peas"), sangría and flamenco became the tourist triad, a legacy later criticised by exhibitions on Francoist tourism imagery ([Público – Postales de sol y playa](https://www.publico.es/culturas/postales-sol-playa-blanquear-dictadura-spain-is-different.html)). Seafood and "mixed" paellas, unknown in the inland tradition, multiplied in coastal restaurants and Spanish restaurants abroad. ⚠️ I found no scholarly source directly documenting paella's role in official tourism posters. The link rests on the general tourism literature.

## 9. Modern Debates

* **What goes in a paella valenciana?** The **Wikipaella** project gathered answers from more than 170 restaurants and many home cooks across Valencian towns and concluded there is a shared core but no single recipe. An academic survey of 400 amateur cooks in 266 towns produced a similar ten-ingredient list: rice, chicken, rabbit, *garrofó*, *bajoqueta*, tomato, oil, saffron, salt, water ([MDZ – receta de la clásica paella valenciana](https://www.mdzol.com/food-lovers/2020/12/24/y-si-comemos-paella-receta-de-la-clasica-paella-valenciana-128372.html/amp); [Guía Michelin](https://guide.michelin.com/es/es/articulo/features/uno-de-nuestros-platos-mas-internacionales-la-paella)). See [recipe_paella_valenciana.md](recipe_paella_valenciana.md).
* **Bien de Interés Cultural Inmaterial (2021):** the Generalitat opened the file on 23 April 2021 (BOE 21 May 2021). **Decreto 176/2021, of 29 October** declared *"La paella valenciana, l'art d'unir i compartir"* an Intangible Asset of Cultural Interest, in force from **10 November 2021**, and recorded in the BOE on 15 March 2022 ([BOE-A-2022-4133](https://boe.es/boe/dias/2022/03/15/pdfs/BOE-A-2022-4133.pdf); [València Plaza](https://valenciaplaza.com/paella-valenciana-declarada-bien-interes-cultural-inmaterial); [elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/paella-arte-unir-compartir-oficialmente-bic-valenciano_1_8472168.html)). Notably, the decree **does not fix a single recipe**. It protects the practice, the name and its regional variants ([Directo al Paladar](https://www.directoalpaladar.com/actualidad-1/paella-bien-interes-cultural-inmaterial-norma-no-se-atreve-a-definir-receta-concreta)).
* **World Paella Day (20 September):** a Valencia tourism-backed celebration with an international chefs' competition, the *World Paella Day Cup* ([Spain.info Social Newsroom](https://socialnewsroom.spain.info/celebrate-the-secrets-of-authentic-valencian-paella-for-world-paella-day-2020/); [The Taste](https://www.thetaste.ie/world-paella-day-a-tribute-to-valencias-most-universal-dish-will-be-held-on-20-september-with-a-series-of-activities-around-the-globe)). ⚠️ The reason for the date (one outlet links it to the rice harvest) is unverified ([Euro Weekly News](https://euroweeklynews.com/2020/09/20/will-you-try-your-hand-at-the-valencian-dish-on-world-paella-day/)).
* **#PaellaGate (October 2016):** Jamie Oliver tweeted a paella with **chicken and chorizo** and set off a Spanish social-media storm ("we don't negotiate with terrorists"). José Andrés defended it as "a Spanish *arroz*", and Oliver stood by his version ([The Local](https://www.thelocal.es/20161005/jamie-oliver-messed-with-paella-and-spain-didnt-like-it/); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/life-and-style/food-and-drink/jamie-oliver-causes-outrage-in-spain-with-paella-recipe-1.2817616); [The Olive Press](https://theolivepress.es/spain-news/2016/10/05/jamie-olivers-spanish-paella-likened-to-botched-fresco-painting-in-twitter-backlash/)). The episode crystallised the distinction many Valencians draw between **paella (valenciana)** and **arroz**, a distinction that runs through this whole atlas.
