# Fasoulia w Riz / فاصوليا ورز

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a ubiquitous home dish documented in many family recipes, but **no published source gives its history or town variants**. The bean and meat choices in §3 are the variations that recur across Lebanese recipe writers.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Lebanon (urban and village home cooking). The same *yakhneh* (stew) and rice pairing is cooked in Syria and Palestine.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stew over rice (*yakhneh w ruz*). A tomato and white-bean stew ladled over vermicelli rice at the table.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot for the stew and a second pot for the rice, on the stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Fasoulia* means "beans" in Arabic, and it is also the name of one of Lebanon's most popular stews: **beans in a tomato sauce with beef or lamb, garlic and coriander**, served over [riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md) (vermicelli rice). Hence the full name *fasoulia w riz*, "beans with rice". It is part of the large family of Levantine *yakhneh* (stews of a vegetable or legume with a little meat and tomato) that are always eaten **with rice**. Others are *bamieh* (okra), *bazella* (peas), *loubieh* (green beans) and *sabanekh* (spinach). Weekday lunch is often one of these, plus rice and a salad.

The Spanish structural parallel is the bean-and-pork winter pot rices such as [arròs amb fesols i naps](recipe_arroz_con_fesols_i_naps.md) and [arroz y habichuelas](recipe_arroz_y_habichuelas_murciano.md). The Lebanese table, however, keeps the stew and the rice **separate until the plate**.

**Culinary science:** browning small cubes of lamb or beef before the tomato builds the stew's base. Simmered beans release starch, which thickens the sauce. If it's too thin, cooks mash a few beans or reduce it uncovered. A final *taklieh* of garlic fried with fresh coriander, stirred in at the end, adds the bright Lebanese finish.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **White beans (cannellini, *fasoulia baida*):** The most common everyday version.
*   **Large lima or butter beans (*fasoulia aarida*):** With slow-cooked lamb, a richer winter stew.
*   **Red kidney beans:** With small meat cubes, onion, tomato paste and garlic. Some call it "Lebanese chili".
*   **Vegetarian / Lenten:** Without meat, with olive oil (*bi zeit*).
*   **Rice:** Always vermicelli rice; long-grain is preferred over basmati in many households.
*   **Town variants:** ⚠️ Not documented.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **white-bean fasoulia with lamb**, over vermicelli rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried white beans, soaked overnight (or 2 × 400 g tins, drained) | 250 g dried |
| Lamb or beef shoulder, 2 cm cubes | 400 g |
| Olive oil | 3 tbsp |
| Onion, chopped | 1 |
| Tomato paste + crushed tomatoes | 2 tbsp + 400 g |
| Water or stock | ≈ 800 ml |
| Lebanese 7-spice, salt, pepper | 1 tsp / to taste |
| **Taklieh:** garlic 4 cloves + fresh coriander 1 bunch fried in 2 tbsp oil | — |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| [Riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md) | 300 g rice + 50 g vermicelli, 600 ml water |
| Stew : rice per plate | ≈ 2 ladles over 1 cup of rice |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Beans:** Simmer the soaked beans in fresh water for **45–60 min** until just tender (skip for tinned).
2. **Meat:** Brown the meat in the oil (**8 min**). Add the onion (5 min), then the tomato paste (1 min) and spices.
3. **Stew:** Add the crushed tomatoes, the water and the beans. Simmer covered for **45–60 min**, until the meat is tender and the sauce coats a spoon. Mash a few beans if it's too thin.
4. **Taklieh:** Fry the garlic and coriander in the oil for **1 min** and stir into the stew. Check the seasoning.
5. **Rice:** Meanwhile, make [riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md).
6. **Serve:** Rice on the plate and stew ladled over it, with pickles, radishes and fresh onion.
