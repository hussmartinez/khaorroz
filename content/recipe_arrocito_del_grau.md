# Arrocito del Grau de Castelló / Arrosset del Grau

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província de Castelló](provincial_castellon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the "arrocito" of El Grau de Castelló is cited by the regional tourism board and local guides as a signature dish, but **I found no published canonical recipe**. The description below reflects what is commonly served in El Grau's restaurants: a marinera rice with a moist finish and a pronounced shellfish broth. Verify it locally, for example during Castelló's *Mes de l'Arròs*.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Plana Alta, specifically **El Grau de Castelló** (the port district of Castelló de la Plana).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). Local custom serves it looser than a paella and tighter than a caldoso.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A steel paella or medium-depth pan over gas, in port taverns.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
El Grau is Castelló's fishermen's quarter, built around the port and its fish auction (*llotja*). Its tavern tradition produced the **"arrocito"**, the affectionate diminutive used locally: a small-portion marinera rice made with whatever the boats bring in (sepia, galeres, prawns, *rape*, *pulpo*), finished meloso. It sits beside Castelló's other grand dishes, the **olla de la Plana** (a bean-and-vegetable stew), *fideuà*, and the **Magdalena** festival paellas. Castelló de la Plana dedicates an annual **Mes de l'Arròs** to the city's rices.

**Culinary science:** a meloso marinera relies on gelatin from shellfish and rockfish fumet and on gentle agitation to release amylopectin from the grain surface. A medium-depth pan, deeper than a paella, gives a thicker rice layer that stays moist.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **El Grau de Castelló:** Sepia + gamba + **galeres** in season. Meloso.
*   **Burriana / Vila-real coast (La Plana Baixa):** Similar marinera rice with **ñora**-free sofrito and more saffron. Often with **artichoke** in winter.
*   **Benicàssim / Orpesa:** Restaurant marinera rices, frequently caldoso with **bogavante** (lobster) for tourists. A modern rather than traditional form.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **El Grau meloso marinero** (house-style reconstruction).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rockfish + galera/prawn-shell fumet | 1.6 L |
| Sepia, diced | 300 g |
| Gambas (heads used in fumet) | 12 |
| Galeres (optional, winter) | 6 |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:** Sear the sepia until caramelised (8 min). Add the garlic, pimentón (5 s) and tomato (5 min).

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 350 g |
| Fumet | 1.4–1.5 L (≈ 4:1, meloso) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toasting:** Rice in the sofrito, 1 min.
2. **Liquid:** Boiling fumet and saffron.
3. **Fire:** Strong for 8 min → medium for 8 min, stirring gently 2–3 times. Lay the peeled prawns and the galeres on top at min 13.
4. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Rest 1–2 min and serve loose.
