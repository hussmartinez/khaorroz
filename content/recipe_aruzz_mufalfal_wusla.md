# Aruzz Mufalfal of the Wusla (Medieval Syrian Pilaf) / أرز مفلفل

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Food-history references (*Oxford Companion to Food*, cited by Wikipedia's "Pilaf" article) credit the 13th-century Syrian *Kitab al-Wusla* with the first recipe for a dish resembling modern pilaf, on a base of **rice, meat, chickpeas and garlic**. I have not seen the recipe text itself. The method and quantities below reconstruct a period-plausible version from that description and from the *mufalfal* ("loose-grained") technique described in medieval Arab rice cookery. Treat it as an informed adaptation, not a transcription.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Ayyubid Syria (Aleppo, Damascus). The technique later spread through the Mamluk and Ottoman Levant.
*   **Historical Source:** *Kitab al-Wusla ila al-Habib*, anonymous (attributed to Ibn al-'Adim of Aleppo), mid-13th century, Ayyubid Syria; with al-Baghdadi, *Kitab al-Tabikh*, 1226, Baghdad
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Savoury pilaf, *mufalfal* ("peppered", meaning loose and separate). Each grain stays whole, glossed with fat, studded with lamb and chickpeas.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy copper pot over charcoal, the lid sealed with a cloth for the final steaming.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Medieval Arab cooks used rice in three ways: porridges (*aruzziyyat*), puddings with milk (*aruzz bi-l-laban*), and the *mufalfal* pilaf, in which washed rice is added to a meat stew and cooked until the liquid is absorbed and the grains stay separate. The name refers to the loose grains, not to pepper. Two 13th-century books, the Syrian *Wusla* and al-Baghdadi's *Kitab al-Tabikh*, contain the earliest known pilaf-type recipes, three centuries before the word "pilaf" appears in a Persian cookbook of 1521. The *Wusla*'s rice chapter lists both a plain rice pilaf and a saffron-yellow pilaf.

In today's Levant, *ruz mfalfal* is still the term for well-made loose rice (see [ruz bi-djaj with hashweh](recipe_ruz_bi_djaj_hashweh.md) and [riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md)), a direct linguistic survival of the medieval name.

**Culinary logic:** washing removes surface starch. Adding the rice to a boiling, fatty broth, then finishing it covered on very low heat, lets the grains absorb the liquid without bursting.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Wusla (Syria, 13th c.):** Rice, meat, chickpeas, garlic (per the secondary description); a yellow saffron pilaf is listed separately.
*   **Al-Baghdadi (Baghdad, 1226):** Comparable rice-in-meat-stew dishes, e.g. the [shurba](recipe_shurba_al_baghdadi.md) rice soup.
*   **Ottoman *sade pilav* (from the 16th c.):** Butter replaces tail fat, and the meat is served separately; see [Ottoman pilav](recipe_pilav_ottoman_aleppo.md).
*   **Modern Levant:** *Ruz mfalfal* with vermicelli, or under *hashweh* (minced meat and nuts).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern adaptation; reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb shoulder, 3 cm cubes | 600 g |
| Ghee or rendered lamb fat | 50 g |
| Garlic, crushed | 4 cloves |
| Cooked chickpeas | 200 g |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Ground coriander | 1 tsp |
| Water | 1.2 L |
| Salt | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium-grain rice, washed & soaked 30 min | 350 g |
| Broth at rice-in | ≈ 600 ml (≈ 1.7 : 1) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meat:** Brown the lamb in the fat. Add the garlic, coriander, cinnamon and water. Simmer covered **60 min**, until tender.
2. **Chickpeas:** Add the chickpeas and salt. Ladle off any broth above ≈ 600 ml and keep it for later.
3. **Rice:** Bring to a hard boil, add the drained rice, stir once and boil uncovered until craters form (≈ 5 min).
4. **Steam:** Cover with a cloth under the lid and cook on the lowest heat for **15 min**. Rest **10 min**, then fluff gently so the grains stay *mufalfal*.
