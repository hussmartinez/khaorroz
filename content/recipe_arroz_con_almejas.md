# Arroz con Almejas / Arroz con Ameixas

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Norte (Galicia, Asturias, Cantabria, País Vasco)](provincial_norte.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** clam rice is common across the Cantabrian and Galician coast, but I found **no canonical "traditional" Galician recipe**. The sources agree on Galician clams, white wine or fish stock, and garlic and parsley, and differ on proportions. The town variations below are reconstructed from the closely related *almejas a la marinera* tradition.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Rías Baixas (Carril/Vilagarcía, Cambados, O Grove), the Rías Altas, and the Cantabrian and Asturian coast.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay *tarteira* or deep pot over gas or a wood range.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Galicia's *rías* are among Europe's richest shellfish grounds. Carril (Vilagarcía) is famous for its clams, and the *mariscadoras* who gather them on foot are a cultural institution. **Almejas a la marinera** is the Rías Baixas classic, and the same garlic-wine-parsley sauce, stretched with fish stock, becomes **arroz con almejas**, a soupy spoon rice. An Asturian source notes that its marinera differs from the Galician one by omitting tomato and pimentón. In other words, the Galician version usually includes them.

**Culinary science:** clams release a briny, glutamate- and succinate-rich liquor as they open. Adding them at the end keeps them tender and puts that liquor straight into the broth. A small amount of flour in the garlic oil (marinera style) lightly binds the caldo.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — reconstructed)
*   **Rías Baixas (Carril / Cambados):** *Ameixa fina* or *babosa*. Albariño wine. **Pimentón** + a little tomato. Caldoso.
*   **Asturias:** Marinera base **without tomato or pimentón**. Garlic, parsley, sidra or white wine.
*   **Cantabria (Santoña / Laredo):** Clams + sometimes **anchovies**. Cooked in fish stock.
*   **País Vasco:** *Arroz con almejas* in **salsa verde** (parsley, garlic, flour, txakoli).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Rías Baixas caldoso**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Live clams (Galician), purged 1 h in salted water | 800 g |
| Olive oil | 60 ml |
| Onion | 1 small |
| Garlic | 4 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 100 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| Flour | 1 tsp |
| Albariño or dry white wine | 150 ml |
| Fish stock | 1.6 L |
| Parsley | 2 tbsp |

**Extraction:** Sweat the onion (8 min), then the garlic (1 min), flour (30 s) and pimentón (5 s). Add the tomato (4 min), then the wine (reduce 2 min).

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round/bomba rice | 300 g |
| Fish stock | ≈ 1.5–1.6 L (≈ 5:1) |
| Clams | 800 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Add the rice to the sofrito and stir for 1 min. Add the boiling stock.
2. **Fire:** Simmer **14 min**.
3. **Clams:** Add the clams, cover, and cook **3–4 min** until they open. Discard any that stay closed.
4. **Finish:** Add the parsley and serve immediately.
