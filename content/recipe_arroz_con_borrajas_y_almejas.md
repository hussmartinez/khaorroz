# Arroz con Borrajas y Almejas / Borrajas con Arroz y Almejas

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Aragón, Navarra & La Rioja](provincial_aragon_navarra_rioja.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Zaragoza huerta (Ebro, Jalón and Huerva valleys) and the Ribera de Navarra.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy). Some modern restaurants serve it meloso.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay *cazuela* over gas or a stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
**Borraja** (borage, *Borago officinalis*) is Aragón's emblematic vegetable. It is grown commercially almost nowhere else, and Aragón's agricultural research centre (CITA) points to a book of **113 borage recipes** (J. M. Urtasun, 2004) that includes "the famous borrajas with rice and clams". The combination of borage, rice and clams is a **Christmas Eve** dish in many Aragonese homes. Clams are a striking ingredient in an inland region; they arrived by rail from the north coast and became a festive luxury.

**Culinary science:** borage stems are fibrous and covered in fine hairs, so they must be scraped and boiled. Their **cooking water** holds a delicate, cucumber-oyster flavour and becomes the rice stock. A light *roux* of garlic, flour and white wine (the "*a la marinera*" technique used for the clams) binds the caldo.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Classic Zaragoza home style (Consumer recipe):** Borage boiled, broth kept. Onion pochada, rice + borage, moistened with **borage broth**, clams in marinera sauce added at the end. Caldoso.
*   **Stems-only version (Público):** Only scraped **stems**, with garlic, a little flour and white wine. Rice al dente.
*   **Restaurant Gayarre (modern):** The rice is coloured green with a **liquefied borage-leaf** purée. A meloso evolution.
*   **Navarra (Ribera):** Borage with potato rather than rice is more common; the rice version appears on festive tables.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Zaragoza Christmas Eve** (Consumer structure).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Borage (stems + some leaves), cleaned, scraped, in 4 cm pieces | 1 kg |
| Water (for borage) | 2 L |
| Clams (almejas), purged | 500 g |
| Olive oil | 60 ml |
| Onion, finely chopped | 1 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Flour | 1 tbsp |
| White wine | 100 ml |
| Parsley | 1 tbsp |

**Extraction:** Boil the borage in salted water for **15–20 min** until almost tender. Drain it and **keep the broth** (≈ 1.5 L). For the clams: fry the garlic, add the flour (1 min), the wine and a ladle of broth, then the clams, covered, until they open (3 min). Set aside with their sauce.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 280 g |
| Borage broth | ≈ 1.5 L (≈ 6 cups) |
| Borage (cooked) | ≈ 700 g |
| Clams | 500 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Sofrito:** Sweat the onion in the oil for **10 min**.
2. **Rice:** Add the rice and borage and stir for 1 min.
3. **Liquid:** Add the hot borage broth and simmer **16 min**.
4. **Finish:** Add the clams with their sauce and the parsley. Simmer 1 min and serve.
