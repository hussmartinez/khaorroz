# Basmashkat (Damascene Meat Rolls Stuffed with Rice) / بسماشكات

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** described by Arabic search summaries of Damascene food writing and by a recipe site that ties it to the Ottoman palace kitchens and Damascene weddings. The "tie/sew" etymology from Ottoman Turkish is reported, not verified, and no primary historical source was found. Quantities follow the recipe site.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Damascus.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Feast dish of rice inside meat: thin, pounded slices of veal or lamb rolled around a saffron rice and minced-lamb filling, sewn shut, seared and simmered in a light butter-flour broth until tender. The rice inside is soft and fragrant; the rolls are sliced and served with their sauce.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot (*tanjara*) on the stove; kitchen thread and a needle for sewing the rolls.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Basmashkat* is one of the "lost" show-pieces of the old Damascene table. Damascene food writing links it to the grand **Ottoman kitchens**, where display was part of food culture, and explains the name as an old Ottoman Turkish word meaning "to tie" or "to sew the edge", after the thread used to close each roll. It was not an everyday dish: it was cooked for **weddings and family celebrations** as a sign of the host's generosity and skill. Thin slices of meat are pounded soft, filled with minced meat, rice, nuts and saffron, rolled, sewn and boiled in rich broth ([Clove Kitchen – Basmashkat](https://clove.kitchen/recipes/-basmashkat-veal-rolls--RCBc9rE5pHI)). Wikipedia's Syrian cuisine pages list it as stuffed meat rolls with rice ([Wikipedia – Syrian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_cuisine)) ⚠️ (listing only).

It is the Damascene cousin of the stuffed whole lamb of Ottoman Aleppo ([kharuf mahshi](recipe_kharuf_mahshi_halabi.md)) and of the rice-stuffed vegetables (*mahashi*): the same *hashweh* of rice and meat, wrapped in meat instead of a vegetable.

**Culinary science:** the rice is **half-cooked** before stuffing, so it finishes inside the roll by absorbing the meat's juices without bursting it. A light dusting of flour and a brief sear seal the surface; the roux-thickened broth then braises the rolls gently for 45 min until the pounded meat is tender.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascene wedding version:** Lamb or veal slices, rice-meat-saffron filling with pine nuts, sewn with thread and boiled in broth.
*   **Modern restaurant version:** Veal scaloppine, toothpicks instead of thread, butter-flour sauce; served with rice, fries or mashed potato.
*   ⚠️ No other city's version is documented. The Damascene dish *al-basha wa ʿasakru* ([see](recipe_al_basha_w_asakru.md)) is unrelated despite its similar Ottoman story.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Damascene festive rolls** (recipe-site proportions, scaled to 4).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Veal or lamb leg, thin slices pounded flat | 700 g (≈ 12 slices) |
| Butter (filling) | 1½ tbsp |
| Onion, finely chopped | 1 |
| Minced lamb | 300 g |
| Allspice | ¾ tsp |
| Black pepper | ½ tsp |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |
| Saffron threads, soaked in 2 tbsp warm water | a pinch |
| Turmeric | ½ tsp |
| Pine nuts, toasted | 3 tbsp |
| Flour (for dusting and roux) | 4 tbsp |
| Butter (searing and sauce) | 3 tbsp |
| Beef or lamb broth, boiling | ≈ 1 L |
| Bay leaf | 1 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium-grain rice, washed and drained (filling) | 250 g |
| Broth for par-cooking the filling rice | ≈ 300 ml |
| Plain rice to serve (optional) | 200 g |
| Filling per slice | ≈ 2 tbsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Filling:** Soften the onion in butter; brown the minced lamb with allspice, pepper and salt.
2. **Par-cook the rice:** Stir in the rice, turmeric and saffron water; add ≈ 300 ml boiling broth, cover and cook on very low heat about 15 min until partly cooked. Cool on a tray and mix in the pine nuts.
3. **Roll and sew:** Put a spoonful of filling on each slice, roll tightly and sew shut with thread (or pin with toothpicks); dust lightly with flour.
4. **Sear:** Brown the rolls in 2 tbsp butter; remove.
5. **Sauce:** Melt 1 tbsp butter, stir in 1 tbsp flour, whisk in the boiling broth until smooth; add bay and salt.
6. **Braise:** Return the rolls and simmer gently about 45 min until tender. Remove the thread, slice and serve with the sauce (and plain rice if wished).
