# Arroz Negro Catalán / Arròs Negre de l'Empordà

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Catalunya](provincial_catalunya.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Empordà coast (L'Escala, Roses, Palamós) and the rest of the Catalan coast. TasteAtlas notes its popularity in Tarragona.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). Some Empordà cassola versions are cooked slightly melós.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A paella or earthenware cassola over gas or wood.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Barcelona Turisme lists **"Arròs negre de l'Empordà"** among its Catalan classics. The Catalan version has the same base as the [Valencian arròs negre](recipe_arroz_negro.md): cuttlefish or squid, fish stock and ink, served with allioli. The main difference is the **sofregit**. A *La Columna* (Girona) article recalls that the **original dark colour came from a very dark onion sofregit**, and that ink only became the usual colouring later. Barcelona Review similarly describes the flavour as coming from cuttlefish, stock, **onion** and ink, with no herbs. Valencian versions, by contrast, use no onion.

**Culinary science:** onion cooked slowly for 30–40 minutes caramelises (Maillard reactions plus caramelisation of its sugars) into an almost black paste. That paste gives colour and a bitter-sweet depth, which ink later intensified. Some cooks mix ink into the allioli (*allioli negat*); that detail comes from a less reliable source.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Empordà (L'Escala / Roses):** Very dark onion sofregit + cuttlefish + ink. Allioli made in a mortar. Sometimes **clams** (goodtaste.tv recipe).
*   **Barcelona:** Squid more often than cuttlefish. Shorter sofregit, ink-led colour.
*   **Tarragona coast:** ⚠️ reconstructed. Lighter sofregit, closer to the Ebre and Castellón style.
*   **How it differs from the Valencian negre:** onion sofregit (Catalunya) versus garlic, tomato and pebre roig (Valencia).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Empordà** (dark onion sofregit).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 90 ml |
| Cuttlefish (sípia), diced, ink sacs reserved | 500 g |
| Onion, very finely chopped | 2 large (400 g) |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 100 g |
| Cuttlefish or squid ink | 8–10 g |
| Fish fumet | 1.3 L |
| Clams (optional) | 250 g |

**Extraction:** Sear the cuttlefish until it is dry and golden (**8 min**). Add the onion and cook slowly, stirring often, until **very dark** (**30–35 min**; add a splash of water if it catches). Add the garlic and tomato (**5 min**). Dissolve the ink in hot fumet.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice (bomba / Pals / Delta) | 380 g |
| Fumet + ink | ≈ 1.15–1.2 L |
| Allioli | 150 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Add the rice to the sofregit and toast **1 min**.
2. Pour in the boiling fumet with the ink. Correct the salt.
3. Cook high for 8 min, then medium-low for 9 min. Add the clams at minute 14 if using.
4. Rest 3 min. Serve with allioli.
