# Arroz con Cangrejos (La Bañeza) / Arroz con Cangrejos de Río Bañezano

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Castilla y León](provincial_castilla_y_leon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented in the *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria*'s 2022 survey of **forgotten dishes of Castilla y León** (source: Carmen González, La Bañeza, León). The source gives ingredients and method but **no quantities**, which are reconstructed below. Native white-clawed crayfish are now protected; modern versions use the introduced American red crayfish under regional fishing rules.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Bañeza (León), where the Duerna, Tuerto and Órbigo rivers meet.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). The rice is stewed in a *tartera* with the blended crayfish sauce.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A *tartera* (shallow cooking pot) on the stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
La Bañeza's crayfish tradition is "more than two centuries" old, according to the RENC source. The meeting of three rivers meant that from **early June** crayfish could be fished, and the catch became the excuse for family gatherings and *tertulias*. The same streams also yielded trout, frogs' legs, barbel, boga and tench (the last three now practically extinct, says the source). The crayfish were cooked three ways: as a pan-fried aperitif, as *salsa de cangrejos con tomate* with a second course, and as a main course of **arroz con cangrejos**. The rice uses the same sauce as the tomato dish, except that the sauce is **blended** first.

**Culinary science:** live crayfish must be **gutted** (the intestinal vein is pulled out through the tail fan) or they turn bitter. That is the same "capar" technique as in Cantabria's [arroz del Pedrón de Parayo](recipe_arroz_del_pedron_de_parayo.md). The shells release astaxanthin-bound fats into the oil and sofrito, which explains the red colour. Blending the sauce carries the shell flavour into the rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **La Bañeza (RENC):** Garlic and guindilla, crayfish sautéed, white wine. A long, slow sofrito of red and green pepper, onion and tomato with paprika. The sauce is **blended**, then the rice is sautéed in it and cooked with warm water. The crayfish go back in **5 min before the end**, either whole on top or only the tails.
*   **Rest of Castilla y León:** The Junta documents a **guiso de cangrejos** (crayfish stew with Rueda white wine and brandy) as a regional dish, but not as a rice. Palencia has *cangrejos con tomate*. ⚠️ No other town rices were found.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **La Bañeza (RENC 2022)**, with reconstructed quantities.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Live crayfish, gutted | 800 g (≈ 40) |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Guindilla | 1 |
| Dry white wine | 150 ml |
| Onion, red pepper, green pepper (finely chopped) | 1 / 1 / 1 |
| Ripe tomato (crushed) | 300 g |
| Sweet paprika | 1 tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 320 g |
| Warm water | ≈ 0.9–1 L (≈ 3 : 1) |
| Salt | to taste |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Crayfish:** Fry the garlic and guindilla. Sauté the crayfish for a few minutes until red. Add the wine and reduce. Set the crayfish aside.
2. **Sofrito:** In the same oil, cook all the vegetables **very slowly** until soft (20 min). Add the paprika and tomato and cook 10 more min. **Blend** to a smooth sauce.
3. **Rice:** In the tartera, sauté the rice in the sauce (2 min). Add warm water.
4. **Cook:** Simmer **≈ 18 min**. With **5 min to go**, return the crayfish, whole on top or tails only.
5. **Finish:** Rest 2 min and serve.
