# Arroz con Magra, Costillejas y Verduras / Arroz Huertano Murciano

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Región de Murcia](provincial_murcia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Huerta de Murcia (Murcia city's pedanías, Alcantarilla, Beniel, Santomera) and the Vega del Segura.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). Served in the pan with lemon.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A steel paella over wood or gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The Huerta de Murcia is the irrigated plain of the Segura, watered by Andalusi channels (*acequias* and *azarbes*). Its everyday Sunday rice combines **pork** (*magra*, lean pork, and/or *costillejas*, short pork ribs) with whatever the huerta offers: red pepper, *bajocas* (green beans), peas, artichoke, cauliflower or broccoli, broad beans. It is seasoned with saffron and often **thyme** or **rosemary**. Spain.info lists "arroz con magra y costillejas" among the region's essential rices, and Directo al Paladar documents a family recipe for "arroz con costillejas y verduras". The rice is central to the **Bando de la Huerta**, the huerta festival held during Murcia's spring fiestas.

**Culinary science:** the ribs mainly add **flavour**: bone, collagen and fat that browns into the fondo. That is why many cooks add *magra* as well, to put more meat in the dish. Spring vegetables go in by toughness: artichokes and green beans first, peas and broad beans later. Lemon at the table brightens a broth that has no seafood acidity.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
> ⚠️ Pedanía-level variations are thinly documented online. The patterns below come from the sources cited plus general huerta practice. Verify them with local cooks.

*   **Huerta de Murcia (classic):** Costillejas + magra + red pepper + bajocas + peas + artichoke or cauliflower. Saffron, thyme, rosemary.
*   **Vegetable-only (Lent and everyday):** The same base without meat. Murcian sources describe this lighter version as "just as good".
*   **Vega Media (Molina, Alguazas):** ⚠️ reconstructed. Heavier on **artichoke** and broad beans in spring.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Huerta de Murcia, spring**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 90 ml |
| Pork ribs (costillejas), short | 400 g |
| Lean pork (magra), cubed | 200 g |
| Red pepper, strips | 1 |
| Bajocas (green beans) | 150 g |
| Artichokes, eighths, lemon-rubbed | 4 |
| Peas | 100 g |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Pimentón dulce (optional) | 2 g |
| Saffron | 0.3 g |
| Thyme / rosemary | 1 sprig |
| Water or light chicken stock | 1.5 L |

**Extraction:** Fry the pepper and set it aside. Brown the ribs and magra (**15 min**). Add the artichokes and bajocas (**5 min**), then the garlic, pimentón and tomato (**5 min**). Add the liquid and saffron and simmer **20 min**, reducing to about 1.15 L. Add the peas and pepper strips for the last 5 min.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Calasparra or bomba rice | 360 g |
| Stock | ≈ 1.1–1.15 L |
| Lemon | 1 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Prep:** Clean the artichokes into lemon water.
2. **Fondo:** As above.
3. **Rice:** Scatter it in and add the herb sprig. Don't stir after this point.
4. **Fire:** High for 8 min → medium-low for 9 min → brief high.
5. **Finish:** Remove the herbs and rest 4 min. Serve with lemon.
