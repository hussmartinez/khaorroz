# Riz bi Foul (Rice with Fava Beans) / رز بفول

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a common spring home dish of Syria and Lebanon, documented mainly by Arabic recipe sites (several versions, with and without meat). There's no dedicated historical source, and town-level variations are not documented; the variants below are household styles found in published recipes.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Damascus and Syrian home cooking; also Lebanon.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice with legumes. A fluffy spring pilaf of rice, fresh fava beans and dill, with lamb.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot with a tight lid; stovetop.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In spring, when **fresh green fava beans** (*foul akhdar*) arrive in the markets, Levantine kitchens cook them into rice. The everyday version is meatless: onions fried in oil or ghee, the shelled (or young, whole-pod) beans, cumin and rice. The festive version starts with **lamb on the bone** and is crowned with **dill** and toasted nuts. In Syria it is eaten with plain yogurt or a cucumber-yogurt salad, a pairing that runs through the whole al-Sham rice repertoire.

**Culinary science:** young fava skins are tender and can stay on. Older beans are best peeled after blanching, because the skin turns leathery and bitter. Dill and cumin carry the green, slightly bitter edge of the beans. Rice and beans cook in the same broth, so the beans go in first, cooked about halfway, before the rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Everyday Syrian (meatless):** Onions, fava, cumin, rice; served with yogurt.
*   **With lamb on the bone:** The meat is fried in ghee, then simmered with the beans and black pepper; rice is added when the broth boils; finished with dill and toasted nuts.
*   **Lebanese style:** Basmati rice, fresh dill, stock and black pepper; served hot.
*   ⚠️ No documented town-specific versions (e.g. Damascus vs. Aleppo).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **festive version with lamb and dill** (the meatless version omits step 1).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb on the bone (neck/shoulder pieces) | 600 g |
| Ghee | 40 g |
| Onion, chopped | 1 large |
| Fresh fava beans, shelled (or frozen) | 500 g |
| Ground cumin | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper, allspice | ½ tsp each |
| Fresh dill, chopped | 1 small bunch (≈ 20 g) |
| Toasted almonds / pine nuts | 40 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice (Egyptian short-grain or basmati), soaked 20 min | 300 g |
| Liquid (meat broth) at rice-in | ≈ 450–550 ml (1.5–1.75 × rice volume) |
| Fava : rice | ≈ 5 : 3 by weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meat:** Brown the lamb in ghee, cover with water, add pepper and allspice, and simmer **60 min**. Keep the broth.
2. **Base:** In a heavy pot, fry the onion in ghee until golden. Add the fava beans and cumin and stir for 3 min.
3. **Broth:** Add the measured broth and the meat. Bring to a boil.
4. **Rice:** Add the drained rice and salt. Cover tightly and cook on low **18–20 min**.
5. **Rest:** Off the heat for 10 min. Fold in the dill.
6. **Serve:** Top with the nuts. Serve with plain yogurt or cucumber-yogurt salad.
