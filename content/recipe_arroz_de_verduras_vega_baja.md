# Arroz de Verduras de la Vega Baja / Arroz de Verano (con Habas y Alcachofas)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** La Vega Baja del Segura: Orihuela, Almoradí, Callosa de Segura, Cox, Dolores, Rojales, Catral.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). Traditionally eaten straight from the pan.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A steel paella over wood (orange or olive) or gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
La Vega Baja is the irrigated floodplain of the River Segura. It is one of Europe's oldest continuously farmed huertas, irrigated by Andalusi *acequias* and *azarbes*, and it is famous for the **Alcachofa de la Vega Baja**. This vegetable rice is the comarca's everyday rice. Local recipe collections (CONVEGA) call one version **"arroz de verano"**, cooked at family gatherings with whatever the huerta gives: artichokes, peas, broad beans, cauliflower, red pepper, potato. In spring the defining combination is **habas + alcachofas**. In **Lent** the comarca makes an **arroz de vigilia** with artichokes and **boquerones** (fresh anchovies), or with cod.

**Culinary science:** artichokes and broad beans oxidise (polyphenol oxidase), which blackens the rice. Vega Baja cooks counter this with **lemon** on the cut artichokes and sometimes a squeeze in the broth. The vegetable "broth" has no meat gelatin, so flavour depends on a **long, dark sofrito** of tomato, pepper and ñora, plus a light vegetable stock.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **The Almoradí Style:** **Artichoke-dominant**. Almoradí holds artichoke fairs and is the artichoke capital. Artichokes are cut in eighths and fried first until their edges caramelise. Red pepper strips. **Lemon** is served.
*   **The Orihuela Style:** **Habas + alcachofas + guisantes** in spring. Ñora in the sofrito. Sometimes **embutido oriolano** on non-fast days.
*   **The Callosa / Cox Style:** "Arroz de verano" with **cauliflower** and **potato**, and sometimes **coles** (cabbage). Cooked on a light caldo.
*   **The Cuaresma (Lent) Style, whole comarca:** **Alcachofas + boquerones** (or bacalao). Boquerones are laid on top at minute 12.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Orihuela–Almoradí spring** (habas + alcachofas + ñora).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 90 ml |
| Artichokes (Vega Baja), cleaned, in eighths, rubbed with lemon | 6 (≈ 500 g cleaned) |
| Fresh broad beans (*habas*), shelled | 250 g |
| Red pepper, in strips | 1 |
| Ñora, seeded | 1 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 2 g |
| Peas (optional) | 100 g |
| Light vegetable stock (onion, carrot, celery, leek, 40 min) | 1.3 L |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |
| Lemon | 1 |

**Extraction:** Fry the ñora for 10 s, remove it and pound it with the garlic. Fry the artichokes until golden-edged (**8 min**), then the pepper strips and habas (4 min). Add the ñora-garlic paste, pimentón (5 s) and tomato (5 min). Add the stock and saffron and simmer for **10 min**.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice (Bomba de Calasparra is popular here) | 350 g |
| Stock | ≈ 1.1–1.2 L |
| Vegetables | ≈ 1 kg cleaned |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Clean the artichokes into lemon water. Shell the habas.
2. **Fondo:** As above.
3. **Rice:** Scatter and level. A few drops of lemon in the broth keep the rice light-coloured.
4. **Fire:** High for 8 min → medium-low for 9 min → brief high for socarrat.
5. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Rest 4 min. Lemon wedges on the side. [**Lent:** at min 12 lay 300 g of cleaned, opened boquerones skin-up on the surface.]
