# Sai Krok Isan / ไส้กรอกอีสาน

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Isan; sold in markets throughout the region and Thailand.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice-fermented sausage: pork, garlic and cooked sticky rice stuffed into casings and left to sour for a few days, then grilled; tangy, garlicky, with soft rice grains inside; eaten with fresh ginger, chilli, cucumber and cabbage, and sticky rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Tied links fermented at room temperature, then grilled over charcoal; sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Sai krok Isan is a fermented sausage of the northeastern provinces made with pork and rice; the rice is what drives the fermentation, giving the signature tang ([Wikipedia mirror – Sai krok Isan](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Sai_krok_Isan); [Fish Sauce in My Bag – Thai sausages](https://fishsauceinmybag.substack.com/p/deep-dive-thai-sausages-pt-2)). It is grilled and eaten with young ginger, cucumber, lettuce and chillies, and recipes vary by village and family ([The World of Street Food](https://theworldofstreetfood.com/2024/05/sai-krok-issan-or-fermented.html)). Daily News lists pork and belly fat with garlic, black pepper, cooked rice and a little sticky rice among the ingredients ([Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/5319762/)). Health authorities recommend buying from hygienic producers ([MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/qol/detail/9560000033040)).

**Culinary science:** cooked rice provides sugars for lactic-acid bacteria; the falling pH preserves the meat and gives sourness, while salt and garlic suppress spoilage organisms.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Round "ball" links (*sai krok kon*):** the market standard ([The World of Street Food](https://theworldofstreetfood.com/2024/05/sai-krok-issan-or-fermented.html)).
*   **Glass-noodle versions:** some families replace part of the rice with noodles. ⚠️
*   **Village/family recipes:** vary in garlic and souring time ([The World of Street Food](https://theworldofstreetfood.com/2024/05/sai-krok-issan-or-fermented.html)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: home-made sai krok Isan (rice-soured).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Minced pork with belly fat | 500 g |
| Cooked sticky rice (or jasmine rice) | 150 g |
| Garlic, minced | 1 head |
| Salt / black pepper | 12 g / 1 tsp |
| Sausage casing | 1.5 m |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice in the sausage : pork | ≈ 1 : 3.3 |
| Sticky rice to serve | 300 g raw |
| Fermentation | 2–3 days at room temperature |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Mix:** mix pork, cooked rice, garlic, salt and pepper; stuff and tie into balls.
2. **Ferment:** hang 2–3 days in a cool, airy place until pleasantly sour.
3. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
4. **Grill & serve:** grill over low charcoal until browned; serve with sticky rice, young ginger, chillies, cucumber and cabbage.
