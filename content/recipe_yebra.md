# Yebra / يبرق (Aleppo Jewish stuffed vine leaves)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** yebra is documented by Jewish food institutions and Syrian-Jewish (Halabi) family cooks in Brooklyn, Mexico and Israel. The dish developed in **Aleppo's Jewish community** before its 20th-century emigration. Family variations (apricot, prune, tamarind) are well attested; precise historical dating is not. The general Arabic word *yabraq* is also used in Aleppo for meat-and-rice vine leaves (see [warak enab](recipe_warak_enab.md)).

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Aleppo (Halab) Jewish community historically; today the Syrian-Jewish diaspora: Brooklyn (New York), Deal (New Jersey), Mexico City (Maguén David, Aleppine), Panama, Argentina and Israel.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vine leaves (*mahashi*) with a **meat-and-rice** filling, braised in a **sweet-and-sour sauce** (*ou'*/tamarind, or dried apricots or prunes); the rolls come out glossy, dark and tender, and are served warm.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide pot lined with dried apricots or leaves, the rolls packed tightly and weighted; slow stovetop braise (also oven-finished in many diaspora kitchens).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Yebra* is the Aleppine Jewish name for stuffed grape leaves. Its signature is the **fruity-sour sauce**: *ou'* (Syrian-Jewish tamarind concentrate) with lemon and sugar, often with **dried apricots** layered in the pot, or **prunes** in some families. Writers on Jewish food note that this fruit-and-sour profile distinguishes Halabi Jewish cooking, possibly reflecting Persian influences among Aleppo's Jews. The filling is ground beef or lamb with short-grain rice and allspice.

Yebra is a Shabbat and holiday dish. Because many Sephardi and Mizrahi communities eat rice on Passover, it also appears at Seder tables; Mexican-Jewish community spokespeople list *mejshi* in tamarind sauce among the most sought-after Passover dishes. A Tel Aviv family with roots in **Qamishli** (north-east Syria) fills the leaves with rice sweetened with dried fruit, a meatless cousin.

**Culinary science:** tamarind (tartaric acid) and lemon (citric acid) keep the rice grains firm during the long braise; dried apricots release pectin and sugar that thicken and glaze the sauce. Packing tightly and weighting stops the rolls from bursting as the rice swells.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Aleppo (historic Halabi Jewish):** Meat-and-rice filling, *ou'* tamarind sauce, lemon, sugar; apricots on top.
*   **Brooklyn / Deal (US Syrian community):** Apricot-and-tamarind versions dominate; some families (e.g. the Dabah family) use **prunes** instead of tamarind for the *ou'*.
*   **Mexico City (Maguén David, diaspora variant):** *Mejshi* (stuffed vegetables and leaves) in tamarind sauce are Passover favourites; local adaptations noted in community sources ⚠️.
*   **Israel (Qamishli-rooted family):** Meatless filling of rice sweetened with dried fruit.
*   **Damascus Jewish community ⚠️:** Not separately documented in the sources found.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Aleppine apricot-and-tamarind yebra**, after published Syrian-Jewish recipes.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Grape leaves (jarred, rinsed) | 50 leaves |
| Ground beef or lamb | 500 g |
| Short-grain rice, rinsed | 150 g |
| Allspice | 1 tsp |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |
| Dried apricots | 200 g |
| Tamarind paste (*ou'*) | 80 ml (⅓ cup) |
| Lemon juice | 2–3 lemons |
| Sugar | 2 tbsp |
| Water | ≈ 500 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Meat : rice (filling) | ≈ 3 : 1 by weight |
| Sauce: tamarind : lemon : water | 1 : 1 : 6 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Filling:** Mix the meat, raw rice, allspice and salt with a splash of water.
2. **Roll:** Place a small amount of filling on each leaf (vein side up), fold in the sides and roll tightly.
3. **Pack:** Line the pot with torn leaves and half the apricots; pack the rolls tightly seam-side down; top with the remaining apricots.
4. **Sauce:** Whisk the tamarind paste, lemon juice, sugar and water; pour over to just cover; weight with a plate.
5. **Braise:** Bring to a simmer, then cook covered on low for about 1–1½ hours, until the rice is tender and the sauce is glossy (add water if needed).
6. **Rest and serve:** Rest 15 minutes; serve warm with the apricots and sauce spooned over.
