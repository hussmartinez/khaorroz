# Arroz con Mariscos y Pescados de Candamo / Arroz de Grullos

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Norte](provincial_norte.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the recipe comes from Vivir Asturias, which associates it popularly with **Grullos (concejo de Candamo)**. The source gives ingredient proportions (½ kg shellfish to 1 kg fish, or the reverse) but not the rice or liquid quantities, which are reconstructed below. No historical origin is given.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Asturias, specifically Grullos in the concejo of Candamo (Nalón valley), with seafood from the central Asturian coast.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). Boiled about 15 min, then **finished in the oven until golden**.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** An oven-proof *cazuela*, hob plus oven.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
An Asturian seafood rice built on the Cantabrian catch: **andaricas** (velvet crabs, *nécoras*), **cigalas**, clams and prawns, with **pixín** (Asturian for monkfish), squid and **congrio** (conger). Unusually for an inland concejo, it is tied to **Grullos**, the agricultural village famous for its **strawberries**; the Festival y Feria de la Fresa de Candamo is held on the first Sunday of June. The method sets it apart from Mediterranean rices: shellfish are **boiled first**, the clams' own liquor becomes the cooking broth, and the dish goes into the oven to brown on top.

**Culinary science:** the clam liquor is salty and rich in glutamates, which makes it a ready-made stock. Andaricas are halved so their roe and juices flavour the rice. Oven-finishing gives top heat for a golden surface, similar to the Valencian [arròs al forn](recipe_arroz_al_horno.md) logic applied to seafood.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Grullos / Candamo (Vivir Asturias):** Cigalas, andaricas, clams, prawns + monkfish, squid, conger, peas, a spoon of tomato sauce. Oven finish.
*   **Asturian coast:** ⚠️ Other caldoso shellfish rices with andaricas are cooked along the coast, but no town recipe was found. See also [arroz con almejas](recipe_arroz_con_almejas.md) and [bogavante del Cantábrico](recipe_arroz_con_bogavante_norte.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Grullos / Candamo**, with reconstructed rice and liquid quantities.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Andaricas (velvet crabs) | 4 |
| Cigalas | 4 |
| Prawns | 8 |
| Clams | 300 g |
| Monkfish (pixín), conger, squid | 1 kg total |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |
| Onion | ½ |
| Parsley | 1 tbsp |
| Tomato sauce | 1 tbsp |
| Peas | 100 g |
| Olive oil | 60 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 350 g |
| Clam liquor + shellfish cooking water | ≈ 1 L (≈ 3 : 1) |
| Oven | 200 °C |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Shellfish:** Boil the andaricas, cigalas and prawns (andaricas ≈ 15 min). Peel the cigalas and prawns and halve the andaricas. Keep the water.
2. **Clams:** Open them in a little water and keep their liquor.
3. **Fish:** In the cazuela, sauté the squid, monkfish and conger with the chopped garlic, parsley and onion.
4. **Rice:** Add the tomato sauce, andaricas, prawns, cigalas, peas, clams and rice. Sauté well.
5. **Broth:** Pour in the reserved liquors, season, and boil **≈ 15 min**.
6. **Oven:** Drizzle with oil and bake until the top is golden (≈ 5–8 min at 200 °C).
