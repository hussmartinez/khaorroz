# Arroz Árabe (Chilean-Palestinian Rice with Noodles) / رز بالشعيرية في تشيلي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a **diaspora dish**. Ethnographic research on Chile's Palestinian community and the analysis of Chilean-Palestinian cookbooks document *arroz árabe* (rice with noodles) as a canonical dish of that cuisine, in all three cookbooks studied. No source gives a single fixed recipe, so the blueprint below is reconstructed from the common Levantine method and the Chilean descriptions.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Palestinian diaspora in Chile (Santiago and the Patronato neighbourhood, Quillota, La Calera, Viña del Mar), with Beit Jala, Bethlehem and Beit Sahour as the main towns of origin. The same "arroz árabe" spread to Peru, Colombia, the Dominican Republic and Central America through Levantine migration.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Diaspora everyday rice. Fluffy, separate grains flecked with golden fried noodles (*fideos cabello de ángel*), sometimes with almonds or raisins on festive days.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy saucepan with a tight lid on a gas hob.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Chile is home to the largest Palestinian community outside the Arab world, estimated at several hundred thousand people of Palestinian descent. Most descend from Christian families of Beit Jala, Bethlehem and Beit Sahour who emigrated from the late 19th century onward. In the sociologist Nicholas Bascuñan-Wiley's fieldwork, a Beit Jala-born cook in Quillota explains that she cooks "certain basic foods differently than her Chilean friends — making rice with noodles instead of plain". The recipes were never written down; she learned them by watching her mother and grandmother.

By the 2020s the dish had entered print. A study of three recent Chilean-Palestinian cookbooks finds that **all three include *arroz árabe (rice with noodles)***, alongside hojas de parra (stuffed vine leaves), hummus, kibbe and rellenos. The cookbooks are *Cocina de la diáspora* (Dalal Halabi, 2021), *Cocinando con Thermohabibi* (Elizabeth Aduay, 2022) and *Nafas* (Mujeres Palestinas por Gaza, 2024). The older reference book *Lo mejor de la cocina árabe* (Ketty Berr & Norma Yunis, 1983) served Chilean households for decades. In Chile "comida árabe" and "comida palestina" are often used interchangeably.

The dish is the diaspora form of the Levantine everyday rice with toasted vermicelli. See [riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md) for the homeland version and [fideuà](recipe_fideua.md) for a Spanish noodle cousin. Elsewhere in Latin America *arroz árabe* became a **Christmas** side dish, especially in Peru and Colombia.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Beit Jala families in Chile (Quillota, La Calera):** The everyday version, rice with fried noodles, served under stews and with *rellenos* (stuffed vegetables). Learned by watching, never measured.
*   **Santiago festive / cookbook version (⚠️ reconstructed):** Butter or oil, more noodles, sometimes almonds or raisins on top, served with chicken or lamb.
*   **Wider Latin American "arroz árabe" (Peru, Colombia, Dominican Republic):** A Christmas side dish with more noodles and often raisins and almonds. It was spread by Levantine immigration and is no longer specifically Palestinian.
*   **Homeland reference (Palestine, Lebanon, Syria):** *ruz bi sh'ariyyeh*, Egyptian or short-grain rice with vermicelli. Chilean cooks use local long-grain rice and "cabello de ángel" noodles.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Chilean-Palestinian everyday arroz árabe (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Butter or olive oil | 30 g / 2 tbsp |
| Fine noodles (cabello de ángel / vermicelli), broken | 60 g |
| Long-grain rice, rinsed | 300 g |
| Hot water or light chicken stock | 600 ml |
| Salt | 1 tsp |
| Toasted almonds or raisins (festive, optional) | 30 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice : liquid | 1 : 2 by volume |
| Noodles : rice | ≈ 1 : 5 by weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toast the noodles:** Melt the butter and fry the broken noodles, stirring constantly, until evenly golden (2–3 min).
2. **Coat the rice:** Add the drained rice and stir for 1 minute until every grain is glossy.
3. **Liquid:** Add the hot water or stock and the salt, and bring to a boil.
4. **Steam:** Cover tightly and cook on the lowest heat for 15–18 minutes, until all the liquid is absorbed.
5. **Rest and fluff:** Rest off the heat, covered, for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork. Top with almonds or raisins for festive tables.
