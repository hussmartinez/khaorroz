# Mok No Mai / หมกหน่อไม้

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** an Isan *mok* of fish with pla ra could not be documented with a cited recipe; the best-sourced Isan-style *mok* found is the bamboo-shoot version below, explicitly served with hot sticky rice.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan and the north (mok, *aep*, *ho nueng* are the banana-leaf steamed and grilled parcels of the region).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: shredded bamboo shoot with pork belly, herbs and seasoning wrapped in banana leaf and steamed until set and fragrant; moist, herbal; eaten with hot sticky rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Banana leaf parcels steamed over a pot (or grilled on embers); sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Mok* means to wrap and steam (or grill) in banana leaf, a basic technique of Isan and Lao cooking alongside grilling and pounding ([Silpa-mag – Isan foodways](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=40863)). Wongnai's *mok no mai* steams bamboo shoot with pork belly in banana leaf for 15–20 minutes, served with hot sticky rice; the leaf changing colour shows it is nearly done ([Wongnai – steamed bamboo shoot with pork belly](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/steamed-bamboo-shoot-with-pork-belly)). It belongs with the central *ho mok*, which is thickened with coconut and egg ([Pholfoodmafia – snakehead ho mok](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=22291)).

**Culinary science:** the banana leaf traps steam and lends a green, tea-like aroma; pork-belly fat bastes the lean bamboo as it cooks.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Mok no mai (Isan):** bamboo + pork belly ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/steamed-bamboo-shoot-with-pork-belly)).
*   **Mok pla (fish):** ⚠️ common in Isan, not documented here.
*   **Central ho mok:** with coconut cream and curry paste ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=22291)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Isan mok no mai with pork belly.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Bamboo shoot, shredded and boiled | 400 g |
| Pork belly, thinly sliced | 200 g |
| Lemongrass, shallot, chilli (pounded) | 2 stalks / 3 / 4 |
| Pla ra or fish sauce | 2 tbsp |
| Lemon basil / dill | 1 handful |
| Banana leaves | 4 large |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Parcels | 4 (≈ 150 g filling each) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Mix:** combine bamboo, pork, pounded aromatics, seasoning and herbs.
3. **Wrap & steam:** fold into banana leaf parcels and steam 15–20 min, until the leaf darkens.
4. **Serve:** open at the table with hot sticky rice.
