# Arroz a la Cubana (Canarias) / Arroz con Huevo y Plátano

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Canarias](provincial_canarias.md) · [Madrid](provincial_madrid.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** arroz a la cubana is eaten all over Spain, especially in Madrid *casas de comidas* and in Canarias. Directo al Paladar describes it as typical of Canarias, served with fried egg, banana, chips and tomato sauce. Its "Cuban" origin is often attributed to returning emigrants (*indianos*), but that attribution is not documented in the sources I found. Treat the regional variations as reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Canary Islands (household staple), Madrid and Castile (*menú del día* classic).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). White rice moulded and plated.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A covered pot plus a frying pan, on a gas stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
A plate of **white rice**, **tomato sauce**, **fried egg** and **fried plantain or banana** (in Canarias, the local *plátano de Canarias*), sometimes with *papas fritas* or a sausage. The name points to the Caribbean world of the 19th and early 20th centuries, when many Canarians and Asturians emigrated to Cuba, and the plate is still close to Cuban home cooking. In Canarias the local banana makes it feel native. In Madrid it became a cheap, filling *menú del día* standard.

**Culinary science:** contrast is everything: the starchy rice, the acidity of the tomato sauce, the fat of the runny yolk and the caramelised sugar of the fried banana. The rice is cooked by absorption with a garlic clove and bay leaf and pressed into a mould.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — reconstructed)
*   **Canarias:** **Plátano de Canarias** fried in oil. Sometimes *papas fritas*. Homemade tomato sauce.
*   **Madrid:** Banana optional. Often with a **sausage** (*salchicha*) or *picadillo*.
*   **Cuban/Caribbean root:** Fried ripe plantain, sometimes with *picadillo*.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Canarian**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil (sauce) | 40 ml |
| Onion, finely chopped (sauce) | 1 |
| Garlic, sliced (sauce) | 2 cloves |
| Crushed tomato (sauce; makes ≈ 500 ml) | 600 g |
| Sugar (sauce) | pinch |
| Garlic clove + bay leaf (for rice) | 1 + 1 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Long-grain rice | 320 g |
| Water | 640 ml (2:1) |
| Eggs | 4 |
| Plátanos de Canarias | 4 |
| Olive oil for frying | 200 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Sauce:** Sweat the onion (10 min), add the garlic, tomato and sugar, and simmer **20 min**. Blend.
2. **Rice:** Fry the garlic clove in a little oil, add the rice (1 min), then the boiling water, bay and salt. Cover and cook on minimum for **15 min**. Rest 5 min.
3. **Fry:** Fry the bananas, halved lengthwise, until caramelised. Fry the eggs, basting with oil, so the yolks stay runny.
4. **Assembly:** Mould the rice, then plate it with sauce, egg and banana.
