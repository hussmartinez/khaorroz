# Palestine / فلسطين

> ⬅ [README](README.md)

## 1. Overview
Rice is not grown in Palestine. It has always arrived by trade, historically from Egypt, and later as Egyptian short/medium grain and imported long-grain. Even so, it became the grain of celebration. A Palestinian cookbook quoted in reference sources calls rice "unquestionably the basic ingredient in ceremonial dishes": mansaf and qidreh are expected at weddings, funerals, baptisms and circumcisions. Everyday rural cooking was built on **wheat**. The *fellahin* (village farmers) grew, threshed and processed it themselves, eating **bulgur** and **freekeh** (green wheat, roasted in the field). Dishes such as mujaddara and even maqluba were often made with bulgur or freekeh before rice took over in the 20th century. Displacement after 1948 accelerated the shift, as families cut off from their fields came to depend on purchased staples.

Three broad culinary registers shape Palestinian rice:
1.  **Madani (urban) cooking** of Jerusalem (al-Quds), Nablus, Yafa and Haifa: refined spiced rices, rice stuffings (*hashweh*) with pine nuts and almonds, stuffed vegetables (*mahshi*).
2.  **Fellahi (village) cooking** of the hill country (Ramallah, Jabal Nablus, Jenin, al-Khalil): grain-and-pulse dishes, yogurt and *jameed*, wood-fired ovens (*taboun*).
3.  **Coastal and southern cooking**, Yafa and above all **Gaza**: fish rices (*sayadieh*), dill, green and red chillies, lemon. It is "more herby, spicy, peppery, lemony" than the neighbours' food, in the words of *The Gaza Kitchen*.

Rice dishes shared with the rest of Bilad al-Sham are documented on the region pages that lead on them: **mansaf** ([Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)), **sayadieh** and **mujaddara** ([Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)), **ouzi** and rice puddings ([Syria](provincial_syria.md)).

## 2. Cultural Sub-Regions
| Sub-region | Rice personality | Key towns |
|---|---|---|
| **al-Quds / Jerusalem** | Urban maqluba (sometimes with vermicelli & chickpeas), hashweh, the Saladin maqluba legend | Jerusalem, Bethlehem |
| **Jabal Nablus** | Maqluba with cauliflower and, in season, *'akkoub* (gundelia); refined madani stuffings | Nablus, Tulkarm, Jenin |
| **al-Khalil / Hebron hills** | **Qidreh** (rice and lamb baked in copper pots in bakery ovens), rice with yogurt, *kousa bi-laban*, Hebron mansaf | Hebron, Halhul, Bani Na'im |
| **Ramallah & fellahi villages** | Wheat-first cooking: freekeh, bulgur, musakhan; rice for feasts | Ramallah, al-Bireh, villages of Bani Zeid |
| **Yafa & the coast / Gaza** | Fish rices (*sayadieh*), spicy Gaza maqluba, Gaza qidreh in clay urns with chickpeas & garlic | Yafa (Jaffa), Gaza City, Khan Yunis, Rafah |
| **al-Jalil / Galilee** | Village wheat traditions (green-wheat roasting), stuffed vegetables, Christian festive rices | Nazareth, Akka, Galilee villages |
