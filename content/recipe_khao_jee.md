# Khao Jee / ข้าวจี่

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Isan and Laos. A cool-season street and temple food.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Grilled sticky-rice cakes. Patties of salted sticky rice are toasted over embers, brushed with beaten egg and grilled again, leaving a crisp, smoky shell over a chewy centre.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A small clay charcoal stove (*tao than*) with a wire grill, or the rice threaded on bamboo skewers over the embers.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao jee is the Lao and Isan way of using up yesterday's sticky rice. Silpa Wattanatham notes that it comes from leftover rice, especially rice kept overnight, which is salted, shaped, egg-coated and grilled ([Silpa Wattanatham](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_153901)). Wikipedia describes it as an old Laotian method of grilling glutinous rice ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_jee)), and the plain, egg-free form, *khao ping*, is still eaten in Laos with just salt or a smear of *padaek* fermented fish sauce ([Phakhao Lao – Grilled sticky rice](https://www.phakhaolao.la/en/stories/grilled-sticky-rice-khaw-ping)).

The dish also has a ritual life. In the third lunar month of the *Heet Sip-Song* calendar, Isan villages hold **Boon Khao Jee**, offering grilled rice cakes to monks at dawn, often around Makha Bucha. The custom is tied to the legend of the servant Punnatasi, whose humble rice cake the Buddha accepted ([GotoKnow – Heet Sip-Song](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/51539); [Tim's Thailand – Boon Khao Jee](https://www.timsthailand.com/boon-khao-jee/)).

**Culinary science:** grilling day-old rice works because the starch has partly retrograded and firmed up, so the patties hold their shape. Salt draws a little moisture to the surface, and the egg wash browns quickly through the Maillard reaction to give the lacquered crust.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Isan street style:** Salted patties, egg-washed twice, eaten for breakfast with grilled pork or on their own.
*   **Sweet / stuffed:** Some cooks add sugar to the egg, or hide a piece of **cane sugar** inside the patty ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_jee)).
*   **Lao *khao ping*:** No egg; salted rice grilled plain or brushed with *padaek* ([Phakhao Lao](https://www.phakhaolao.la/en/stories/grilled-sticky-rice-khaw-ping)).
*   **Merit-making (Boon Khao Jee):** Large batches are grilled at dawn by households and offered to monks ([Tim's Thailand](https://www.timsthailand.com/boon-khao-jee/)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Isan egg-glazed khao jee.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Cooked sticky rice, ideally day-old (see [khao niao nueng](recipe_khao_niao_nueng.md)) | 600 g |
| Salt | 1 tsp |
| Eggs, beaten | 2 |
| Fish sauce (for the egg) | 1 tsp |
| Sugar (optional, sweet version) | 1 tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Patty size | ≈ 75 g each (8 patties) |
| Grilling | ≈ 10 min plain + 5 min after each egg coat |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Season:** Knead the salt into the sticky rice with wet hands.
2. **Shape:** Press the rice into flat oval patties, or pack them onto bamboo skewers.
3. **First grill:** Toast over medium charcoal, turning, until dry and lightly golden (**≈ 10 min**).
4. **Glaze:** Beat the eggs with fish sauce (and sugar, if using). Dip or brush the patties all over.
5. **Second grill:** Return to the grill until the egg sets and browns (**≈ 3 min**). Repeat the coat and grill once more for a thicker crust. Serve hot.
