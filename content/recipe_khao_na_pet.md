# Khao Na Pet / ข้าวหน้าเป็ด

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok's Chinatown and Chinese-Thai shophouses; Teochew and Cantonese communities.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** One-plate rice: sliced roast duck over jasmine rice under a dark, slightly sweet soy gravy, with pickled ginger, cucumber and a bowl of duck broth.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Roasted in a hanging oven; the gravy simmered in a pot; served on a plate with a cleaver-cut duck.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Roast-duck rice is a Chinese diaspora dish found across Southeast Asia, braised or roasted and eaten with plain rice; Teochew cooks are credited with the braised style ([Wikipedia – Duck rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck_rice)). In Bangkok the Teochew are the largest Chinese group ([Khaosod](https://www.khaosod.co.th/technologychaoban/?p=152901)). Long-running shops show its two schools: Jib Kee in Nang Loeng roasts Cantonese-style ducks daily ([The Standard](https://thestandard.co/jib-kee/)), while Prachak adapted the gravy with Thai aromatics such as ginger, galangal, lemongrass and makrut lime leaf ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/articles/prachak-roasted-duck-ros-dee)). ⚠️ No source dates its arrival in Thailand.

**Culinary science:** maltose-glazed skin dried before roasting crisps and lacquers; the gravy is thickened with starch so it coats rice without soaking it.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Cantonese roast (Jib Kee, Nang Loeng):** crisp lacquered skin, light gravy ([The Standard](https://thestandard.co/jib-kee/)).
*   **Thai-accented gravy (Prachak):** gravy infused with ginger, galangal, lemongrass and makrut lime ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/articles/prachak-roasted-duck-ros-dee)).
*   **Teochew braised (*pet phalo*):** five-spice braised duck instead of roast, as in Teochew duck rice ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck_rice)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok roast duck with soy gravy**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Roast duck (bought or home-roasted) | ½ bird (≈ 1 kg) |
| Duck or chicken stock | 500 ml |
| Light / dark sweet soy | 2 tbsp / 1 tbsp |
| Sugar, five-spice | 2 tsp, pinch |
| Tapioca starch slurry | 1 tbsp in 3 tbsp water |
| Pickled ginger, cucumber, coriander | to serve |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 400 g raw |
| Duck per plate | ≈ 150 g sliced |
| Gravy per plate | ≈ 60 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Cook 400 g jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) with 520 ml water; rest 10 min covered.
2. **Gravy:** simmer stock with soys, sugar and five-spice 5 min; thicken with the slurry until it coats a spoon.
3. **Carve:** chop the duck through the bone into bite-size slices.
4. **Assemble:** mound rice, lay the duck over, spoon gravy across.
5. **Serve:** with pickled ginger, cucumber, coriander and a small bowl of hot stock.
