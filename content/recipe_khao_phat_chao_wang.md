# Khao Phat Chao Wang (Palace Fried Rice of the Cremation Cookbooks) / ข้าวผัดชาววัง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Three court fried rices from Bangkok cremation-volume cookbooks are attested through the Thaifoodmaster Siamese recipe archive's catalogue descriptions. **Palace-style fried rice** (*khao phat chao wang*, Lady Gleep Mahithaawn, 1949) is "cooked in the rendered fat of salt-stewed pork with shrimp and egg". **Three-colour fried rice** (*khao phat sam si*, *Sai Yaowapa*, 1935) is pork, shrimp and chicken rices "seasoned with fish sauce, tomato sauce and curry powder, plated in sections". **"Delicious rice"** (*khao na aroi*, Jeeb Bunnag's 1945 cookbook) is a royal fried rice of shrimp, pork and chicken with elaborate garnishes, including pickled vegetables and salted egg. The full recipes are paywalled. The master recipe below rebuilds the 1949 *chao wang* version from that one-line description; all quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok palace and noble households, 1930s–1940s.
*   **Historical Source:** Cremation-volume cookbooks: *Tamrap Sai Yaowapa*, B.E. 2478 (1935); Jeeb Bunnag's cookbook, 1945; Lady Gleep Mahithaawn's cookbook, 1949; Bangkok (as catalogued by Thaifoodmaster)
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Court wok-fried rice. Separate, glossy jasmine grains fried in rendered pork fat, threaded with shrimp and egg, and served with an orderly set of garnishes.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A cast-iron wok (*krathaa*) over a charcoal stove (*tao an lo*); a ladle-spatula (*tahlio*).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Wok frying came to the Siamese kitchen with Chinese settlers. A modern Thai academic review lists "the wok and spatula for stir-frying and frying" among the Chinese contributions, alongside pork husbandry in the Ayutthaya period ([Dusit Thani College Journal 18(2), 2024](https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/journaldtc/article/download/276077/177807/1092579)). By the early 20th century, fried rice had been taken up and refined by the palace. The printed record of this "court fried rice" lies in the cremation-volume cookbooks that noble families distributed at funerals, such as *Tamrap Sai Yaowapa*, compiled for Princess Yaovabha Bongsanid in 1935 ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=42563)).

The Thaifoodmaster archive catalogues three such recipes ([Thaifoodmaster – stir-fry archive](https://thaifoodmaster.com/thai_food/thai_food_recipes/stir_fry)):
*   *Khao phat sam si* (1935): "pork, shrimp, and chicken rices, seasoned with fish sauce, tomato sauce and curry powder, plated in sections".
*   *Khao na aroi* (1945): "a royal Thai fried rice combining shrimp, pork, and chicken, with elaborate garnishes including pickled vegetables and salted egg".
*   *Khao phat chao wang* (1949): rice "cooked in the rendered fat of salt-stewed pork with shrimp and egg".

Tomato sauce and curry powder in a 1935 palace recipe show how quickly Western and Anglo-Indian condiments entered Bangkok's elite kitchens.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   ***Khao phat sam si* (*Sai Yaowapa*, 1935):** Three separately seasoned fried rices (pork, shrimp, chicken) with fish sauce, tomato sauce and curry powder, plated as three coloured sections.
*   ***Khao na aroi* (Jeeb Bunnag, 1945):** Mixed shrimp, pork and chicken fried rice served with pickled vegetables and salted egg.
*   ***Khao phat chao wang* (Lady Gleep Mahithaawn, 1949):** Rice fried in the fat rendered from salt-stewed pork (*mu khem*), with shrimp and egg.
*   **Modern street descendant:** [*Khao phat*](recipe_khao_phat.md) *kung/mu* with lime, cucumber and *phrik nam pla*. It is the everyday Thai fried rice, plainer than the court versions.
*   **In a banana blossom:** See [khao plee](recipe_khao_bplee.md) (1935).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern reconstruction of the 1949* chao wang *version: salt-stewed pork first, its fat used for frying.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pork belly, in one piece | 400 g |
| Salt (for the stew) | 15 g |
| Water | 1 L |
| Garlic, chopped | 30 g |
| Shallots, sliced | 40 g |
| Fish sauce | 25 ml |
| Sugar | 10 g |
| White pepper | 2 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Day-old cooked jasmine rice | 800 g |
| Rendered fat from the stewed pork | 60 ml |
| Shrimp, peeled | 250 g |
| Eggs | 4 |
| Spring onion, coriander, cucumber, lime (garnish) | 150 g total |
| Salted duck egg, halved (as in the 1945 garnish) | 2 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Salt-stew the pork (*mu khem*):** Simmer the pork belly in the salted water for 60–75 minutes until tender. Cool, chill, and lift off the set fat. Render that fat with the skin trimmings in a dry wok until you have about 60 ml of clear lard. Slice the meat thinly.
2. **Aromatics:** Heat the lard in the wok and fry the garlic and shallots until pale gold.
3. **Shrimp and egg:** Add the shrimp and toss for 1 minute. Push them aside, crack in the eggs, scramble them softly, and fold them through.
4. **Fry the rice:** Add the rice and the sliced salted pork, and toss over the highest heat for 3–4 minutes so the grains separate and catch slightly. Season with the fish sauce, sugar and white pepper.
5. **Serve the court way:** Mound the rice on plates with spring onion, coriander, cucumber slices, lime wedges and halved salted egg arranged neatly around it.
