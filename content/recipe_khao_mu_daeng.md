# Khao Mu Daeng / ข้าวหมูแดง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is a well-attested Bangkok Chinese-Thai classic, but no source found documents its origin precisely. Its kinship with Cantonese *char siu* is general knowledge, not taken from a cited source. The quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok's Chinese quarters (Talat Noi, Samphanthawong, Wang Burapha), and Chinese-Thai shops nationwide.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** One-plate rice: **jasmine rice with slices of red roast pork**, often with crisp pork, Chinese sausage and egg, all under a **thick, sweet, glossy red gravy** (*nam rat*).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A charcoal roasting oven or grill for the pork (*tao op*), and a pot for the gravy thickened with starch.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao mu daeng ("red-pork rice") is a pillar of Bangkok's Chinese-Thai shophouse food. Celebrated shops trace long lineages. **Khao Mu Daeng Si Morakot** in Talat Noi is billed as an original. **Nai Hui** at Wang Burapha has served for more than 70 years and is known for a gravy built on many spices. **Sonthot** is known for Cantonese-style pork scented with charcoal smoke ([Wongnai – Si Morakot](https://www.wongnai.com/chains/simorakot); [Time Out Bangkok](https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/th/restaurants/ข้าวหมูแดงจานเด็ดที่ต้องลองในกรุงเทพฯ); [The Standard – Bangkok 100 local tastes](https://thestandard.co/life/bangkok-100-local-tastes/)).

**Culinary science:** the pork is marinated in a red, sugar-rich glaze and roasted hot, so the surface sugars caramelise and the edges char. The gravy is meat juices, soy and sugar thickened with starch, which makes it cling to rice and pork alike. Sesame seeds and a vinegar-chili side cut the sweetness.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Talat Noi / Samphanthawong (Si Morakot):** the full plate with red pork, crisp pork, Chinese sausage and egg.
*   **Wang Burapha (Nai Hui):** gravy built on several spices.
*   **Cantonese style (Sonthot, Chokchai Ruam Mit):** char-siu-like, scented with charcoal smoke.
*   **Chatuchak (Nai Jiu):** a family recipe for about 29 years, pork roasted fresh for a sweeter flavour ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/newspaper/1509016)). ⚠️ Shop-level differences come from reviews.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok shophouse plate**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Pork shoulder or loin, in long strips | 800 g |
| Marinade: sugar, light soy, oyster sauce | 4 tbsp, 2 tbsp, 2 tbsp |
| Marinade: five-spice, garlic, red colouring (or red fermented bean curd) | ½ tsp, 3 cloves, a little |
| Gravy: strained marinade + pork juices + water | 400 ml |
| Gravy: sugar, dark soy, cornstarch slurry | 2 tbsp, 1 tsp, 2 tbsp |
| Toasted sesame seeds | 1 tbsp |
| Chinese sausage, boiled eggs, cucumber | 2 / 2 / 1 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 400 g (+ 520 ml water) |
| Red pork per plate | ≈ 150 g |
| Gravy per plate | ≈ 80 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Marinate** the pork overnight.
2. **Roast:** at 220 °C (or over charcoal) for 30–35 min, basting with marinade, until lacquered with charred edges. Rest and slice.
3. **Gravy:** simmer the strained marinade, pan juices and water with sugar and dark soy. Thicken with the slurry until glossy and pourable, then add sesame.
4. **Sides:** steam the sausage and halve the eggs.
5. **Serve:** put rice on the plate, lay the pork, sausage and egg on top, ladle gravy over and add cucumber. Serve chili vinegar and a light broth alongside.
