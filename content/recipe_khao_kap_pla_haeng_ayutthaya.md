# Khao kap Pla Haeng (Ayutthaya Rice and Dry Fish) / ข้าวกับปลาแห้ง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** This is not a named recipe but the everyday Siamese meal as described by the French envoy Simon de La Loubère, who was in Ayutthaya in 1687–88 and published *Du Royaume de Siam* in 1691 (English translation 1693). What is attested: rice boiled in clear water as the staple; "a little dry or salt Fish"; plain sauces of "a little Water with some Spices, Garlic, Chibols, or some sweet Herb"; and a prized "liquid Sauce, like Mustard", made from fermented shrimp (*kapi*). The quantities, the choice of fish, and the way the relish is pounded are a modern reconstruction built from the dishes those words most likely describe: *pla haeng/pla khem* and *nam phrik kapi*.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Ayutthaya and the lower Chao Phraya plain (Central Thailand), the capital of Siam in the 17th century.
*   **Historical Source:** *Du Royaume de Siam* / *A New Historical Relation of the Kingdom of Siam*, Simon de La Loubère, 1691 (English 1693), Paris/London; describing Ayutthaya, 1687–88
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Plain boiled rice eaten with a small portion of dried or salted fish and a pungent shrimp-paste relish. The grains are soft and intact, pressed into a mouthful with the fingers.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** An earthenware rice pot (*mo din*) on a wood or charcoal hearth (*tao*); a stone mortar (*khrok hin*) for the relish; dried fish grilled over embers.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
La Loubère's chapter "Concerning the Table of the Siameses" opens bluntly: "their common Food is Rice and Fish." A labourer, he wrote, "makes a very good Meal with a pound of Rice a day, which amounts not to more than a Farthing", eaten "with a little dry or salt Fish, which costs not more", and the Siamese "love Fish ill season'd and dry better than fresh". Their sauces "are plain, a little Water with some Spices, Garlic, Chibols, or some sweet Herb", but they "do very much esteem a liquid Sauce, like Mustard, which is only Cray-Fish corrupted": the shrimp paste the Siamese call *kapi* ([La Loubère, Part II, Chap. IV, via Vajirayana](https://vajirayana.org/v2/asia/a-new-historical-relation-of-the-kingdom-of-siam/part-ii-of-the-manners-of-the-siameses-in-general/chap-iv)). In "Of the Grain of Siam" he calls rice "the principal Harvest of the Siameses, and their best Nourishment". They "measure the water, fire and time necessary to the Rice, without bursting the Grain", "generally they boil the Rice in clear water", and they "squeeze it a little between the ends of their Fingers to reduce it into a Paste" ([Part I, Chap. VII](https://vajirayana.org/v2/asia/a-new-historical-relation-of-the-kingdom-of-siam/part-i-of-the-country-of-siam/chap-vii-of-the-grain-of-siam)).

A French culinary account of the embassy adds that the crew "express[ed] regret when we went back to eating dry bread after having eaten cooked rice for more than 3 months", and that the embassy's colleague Céberet du Boullay received many jars of *kapi* ([Le Cordon Bleu – Sawasdee France](https://cordonbleu.edu/news/sawasdee-france/en)). A modern Thai academic review makes the same point from the Thai side: authentic old Siamese food was simple, "mostly rice and fish", grilled, boiled or sun-dried for keeping ([Dusit Thani College Journal 18:2, 2024](https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/journaldtc/article/download/276077/177807/1092579)).

**The thread to today:** *nam phrik kapi* with fried or grilled fish and fresh vegetables (the *nam phrik pla thu* set) is still the backbone of the central Thai table, more than three centuries later.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **La Loubère's labourer's meal (1680s):** About a pound of boiled rice a day with "a little dry or salt Fish" and a plain sauce of water, spices, garlic, chibols (spring onions) and a "sweet herb" he compares to balm (probably holy basil or lemon basil ⚠️).
*   **With the "mustard" sauce:** The fermented-shrimp relish (*kapi*) diluted to a liquid and eaten with rice. This is the ancestor of *nam phrik kapi* ⚠️ (the 1680s seasoning cannot have included chillies in today's quantity: New World chillies were then still spreading in Southeast Asia).
*   **"Boiled with milk" (fasting-day rice):** La Loubère notes that "sometimes they boil it with milk, as we do on fasting days". This is a rarer preparation and may refer to coconut milk ⚠️ (he does not say which).
*   **Modern descendant (Central Plains):** *Khao suai* with *pla thu thot* (fried short mackerel), *nam phrik kapi* and blanched vegetables.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern adaptation: boiled rice, ember-grilled dried fish and a pounded shrimp-paste relish, kept close to the spare 17th-century description (lime, garlic, shallot, a little chilli).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Thai shrimp paste (*kapi*), wrapped in banana leaf and toasted | 30 g |
| Garlic cloves | 4 |
| Shallots (the "chibols") | 3 |
| Bird's-eye chillies (optional, modern) | 2–4 |
| Palm sugar | 10 g |
| Lime juice | 30 ml |
| Water | 30–45 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Long-grain Thai white rice | 400 g |
| Water for boiling (drained method) | 2.5 L |
| Dried or salted fish (e.g. *pla salid*, salted mackerel) | 300 g |
| Fresh herbs (holy basil, lemon basil) and raw or blanched vegetables | 300 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toast the paste:** Wrap the *kapi* in banana leaf or foil and toast it over embers or in a dry pan for 3–4 minutes, until fragrant.
2. **Pound the relish:** In a mortar, pound the garlic, shallots and chillies (if using) to a rough paste. Add the toasted *kapi* and palm sugar, then loosen with lime juice and water until it pours "like mustard".
3. **Boil the rice:** Rinse the rice twice. Boil it in plenty of water for 10–12 minutes, until just tender, then drain off the water (keep it: *nam khao* was drunk with a pinch of salt). Return the pot to very low heat, covered, for 8–10 minutes (see [khao hung chet nam](recipe_khao_hung_chet_nam.md)).
4. **Grill the fish:** Soak salted fish for 20 minutes if very salty, pat it dry, and grill it over embers or fry it in a little oil until crisp at the edges.
5. **Serve:** Put out the rice, fish, relish and herbs separately. Eat by pressing a little rice into a ball with the fingers, dipping it into the relish, and taking a piece of fish.
