# Mnazalet Batinjan / منزلة الباذنجان

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Syria (where Arabic sources place its origin, also called *mnazaleh bi-aswad*, "black mnazaleh"), Palestine, Lebanon and Jordan; the Druze mountain communities have a meatless version.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: fried or roasted eggplant rounds layered or simmered with minced meat and tomato into a soft casserole, served beside rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wide shallow pot or an oven tray (*saniyeh*); a lidded pot for the rice.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Mnazaleh* ("brought down", i.e. stewed) is the Levantine eggplant-and-meat casserole, cousin of the Egyptian *musaqa'a*. Arabic sources call it Syrian in origin, "usually made with minced meat" and suited to "the Ramadan table with a plate of rice according to the traditions of its origin". One version even builds it in layers with rice: "eggplant, rice and meat, with rice again on top".

English-language Levantine cooks give two schools. A Palestinian-style version stuffs or tops the eggplant with meat, cooks it in tomatoes and serves it with rice, garnished with parsley and fried pine nuts. A Lebanese cookbook version bakes it in the oven, to be eaten with flatbread "or even with rice". Its siblings are [mnazalet zahra](recipe_mnazalet_zahra.md) (cauliflower) and the meatless Druze version below.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Syria (*mnazaleh bi-aswad*):** Eggplant rounds roasted or fried, simmered with minced meat in tomato; rice on the side.
*   **Palestine:** Eggplant stuffed or topped with meat, cooked in tomatoes, served with rice; parsley and pine nuts.
*   **Lebanon:** Oven-baked casserole; bread or rice.
*   **Druze (Mount Lebanon / Jabal al-Arab, ⚠️ single source):** Meatless, eggplant and chickpeas.
*   **Broth version:** Some recipes cook it in meat broth instead of tomato.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Syrian-Palestinian mnazalet batinjan with white rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Eggplant, peeled in stripes, 1.5 cm rounds | 1 kg |
| Minced lamb or beef | 400 g |
| Onion, chopped | 1 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Tomatoes, sliced + tomato paste | 3 + 2 tbsp |
| Allspice, cinnamon, salt, pepper | 1 tsp each |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Water | 250 ml |
| Pine nuts + parsley (garnish) | 30 g + handful |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice (Egyptian or medium-grain) | 300 g |
| Water for the rice | 450 ml |
| Butter or ghee | 30 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Eggplant:** Salt the rounds, brush with oil and roast at 180 °C for 25 min (or fry).
2. **Meat:** Fry the onion, garlic and mince with the spices until browned.
3. **Layer:** In a pan or tray, layer eggplant, meat and tomato slices; pour over the tomato paste loosened in the water.
4. **Cook:** Cover and simmer **30 min** on the hob (or bake 35 min at 180 °C).
5. **Rice:** Rinse the rice, fry it briefly in butter, add boiling salted water, cook covered 15 min, rest 10 min.
6. **Serve:** Casserole beside the rice, scattered with fried pine nuts and parsley.
