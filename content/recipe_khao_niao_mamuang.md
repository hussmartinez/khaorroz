# Khao Niao Mamuang / ข้าวเหนียวมะม่วง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand and Bangkok, and today the whole country, mainly in the hot-season mango months (March–May).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** A sticky-rice sweet. Steamed glutinous rice is soaked in warm, salted, sweetened coconut milk (***khao niao moon***) until glossy and plump, served beside ripe mango, with thick salted coconut cream poured over and crisp yellow mung beans scattered on top.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A **huad** (bamboo cone) or metal steamer over a pot for the rice, a saucepan for the coconut milk, and a banana-leaf-lined plate.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Coconut-soaked sticky rice (*khao niao moon*) is the base of a whole family of Thai sweets. Mango is only its best-known partner, alongside [sangkhaya](recipe_khao_niao_sangkhaya.md) custard, shrimp floss (*khao niao na kung*) and durian. Its history is less documented than its fame. A Thai PBS account places the pairing in the early Rattanakosin period. It notes that a royal poem of about 1800 mentions mango but not sticky rice, and that there is a record of the dish under **King Rama V** (1868–1910). Wikipedia cites *Matichon* for the Rama II-era name **"khao niao sai si sok"**. ⚠️ The poem and dating are reported second-hand.

In 2022 the rapper Milli ate it on stage at Coachella and Thai sales soared. The Department of Cultural Promotion then announced plans to propose it for intangible-heritage listing. ⚠️ No UNESCO inscription is confirmed in reliable sources. The preferred mangoes are **nam dok mai** and the smaller, sweeter **ok rong**.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Bangkok old-town shops (e.g. Charoen Krung, Sor Boonprakob):** Long-established specialists in *khao niao moon* sold by weight.
*   **Coloured rice:** Butterfly-pea blue or pandan green *khao niao moon* is a modern market style.
*   **Black sticky rice (*khao niao dam*):** Used instead of white in some shops.
*   **Regional mango choice:** *nam dok mai* (central) vs *ok rong* (prized for sweetness).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Bangkok khao niao mamuang**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Coconut milk (for soaking) | 300 ml |
| Sugar | 100 g |
| Salt | 1 tsp |
| Pandan leaf, knotted | 2 |
| Coconut cream (topping) + rice flour + salt | 150 ml + 1 tsp + ½ tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice, soaked 4 h–overnight | 300 g |
| Ripe mangoes (*nam dok mai*) | 2–3 |
| Crisp-fried yellow mung beans | 2 tbsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Steam:** Drain the soaked rice and steam it in the huad or steamer for **25–30 minutes**, turning halfway, until translucent.
2. **Moon:** Warm the coconut milk with the sugar, salt and pandan until dissolved (don't boil). Pour it over the hot rice, cover and leave for **20–30 minutes** until absorbed.
3. **Topping:** Simmer the coconut cream with salt and the rice flour slurry until just thickened.
4. **Mango:** Peel the mangoes and slice the cheeks.
5. **Serve:** Put rice and mango side by side, spoon the salted cream over the rice and scatter mung beans on top.
