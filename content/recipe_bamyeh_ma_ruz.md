# Bamyeh ma' Ruz / بامية مع رز

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** okra stew over rice is documented across Syria, Lebanon and Palestine. Syrian-specific details (tiny okra, pomegranate molasses, vermicelli rice) come from Syrian recipe media and a single home-cook "Aleppo style" recipe; the Aleppo attribution is flagged.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Syria (Damascus, Aleppo, Homs), with sister versions in Lebanon, Palestine and Iraq.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice with stew (*ruz w yakhneh*). Whole baby okra and lamb braised in a garlicky tomato sauce, sharpened with pomegranate molasses or lemon, spooned over vermicelli rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot (*tanjara*) on the stovetop; okra fried or seared first in a pan.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Bamyeh* (okra) is a summer staple of the Levant, and in Syria the preference is for the **smallest pods**, which stay whole and firm in the sauce. Syrian recipes cook it either as **bamyeh bil-zeit** (in olive oil, no meat, often eaten at room temperature) or as **bamyeh bil-lahmeh** with lamb on the bone, whose strained broth becomes the base of the red sauce. Both are served **with vermicelli rice** (*ruz bi-sh'ariyyeh*). Pomegranate molasses, coriander and garlic give the Syrian version its sweet-sour depth. In the Euphrates valley (Deir ez-Zor, Hasakah) okra appears instead in bread-and-broth *tharid*, a reminder that rice is not universal across Syria.

**Culinary science:** okra's mucilage is released mainly from cut surfaces. Using whole, very small pods, trimming only the stem end, and searing them in hot oil or adding acid (lemon, molasses) early keeps the sauce clear instead of slimy.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Damascus / standard Syrian:** Lamb on the bone, broth strained and reused, tomato paste, garlic, coriander, lemon; vermicelli rice.
*   **Aleppo ⚠️:** More red pepper, plus citric acid or pomegranate molasses and a little sugar for a sharper sweet-sour sauce (home-cook source).
*   **Bil-zeit (fasting, vegetarian):** Olive oil, onion, garlic, tomato, okra; cooked about 15 minutes; served with vermicelli rice or eaten cool.
*   **Deir ez-Zor / Jazira:** *Tharid bamyeh*: okra and broth poured over bread rather than rice.
*   **Lebanon / Palestine:** Very similar; Lebanon adds more coriander, Palestine sometimes cubes of beef.
*   **Lebanese style (Beirut / Mount Lebanon, *bamieh bi lahmeh*):** Small whole okra pods, caps on, fried briefly first; a fry of garlic and **fresh coriander** stirred in at the end, and a spoon of **pomegranate molasses** just before the heat goes off ("it makes a big difference"). Almost always served over buttery vermicelli rice. Anissa Helou includes it as *bamyeh bil-lahmeh* in *Cooking the Foods of My Homeland*.
*   **Lebanese variants:** A tomato-free family version (a minority style) and a meatless version with chickpeas.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Syrian bamyeh bil-lahmeh** with vermicelli rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb shoulder on the bone, in pieces | 800 g |
| Baby okra, stems trimmed | 700 g |
| Olive oil | 60 ml |
| Garlic, crushed | 8 cloves |
| Fresh coriander, chopped | 1 cup |
| Tomato paste | 2 tbsp |
| Crushed tomatoes | 400 g |
| Pomegranate molasses (or lemon juice) | 2 tbsp |
| Salt, black pepper, allspice | to taste / ½ tsp / ½ tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb broth for the sauce | ≈ 1 L |
| Vermicelli rice: rice + vermicelli | 300 g + 50 g |
| Rice water | ≈ 1.5 : 1 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Broth:** Simmer the lamb in 1.5 L water for 60–75 minutes; skim, strain and keep about 1 L of broth.
2. **Okra:** Sear the okra in the hot oil for 3–4 minutes, without stirring too much; lift out.
3. **Base:** In the same oil fry the garlic and coriander, then the tomato paste; add the crushed tomatoes and the broth.
4. **Braise:** Return the lamb and okra; simmer gently 25–30 minutes until the sauce thickens.
5. **Season:** Stir in the pomegranate molasses (or lemon), salt, pepper and allspice; simmer 5 minutes more.
6. **Rice:** Toast the vermicelli in butter, add the rice and boiling salted water, cover and cook 15 minutes; rest 5 minutes.
7. **Serve:** Rice on the plate, okra and lamb with plenty of sauce spooned over.
