# Fate de Velorio (Chilean-Palestinian Funeral Rice) / فتّة العزاء

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented by a single ethnographic source. A Chilean-Palestinian family from Beit Jala in La Calera described *fate* to the sociologist Nicholas Bascuñan-Wiley as "a rice-based dish with yogurt, cauliflower, and goat meat" served at wakes. No written recipe was found. The blueprint below is a reconstruction combining that description with Palestinian *fatteh* and yogurt-rice technique. Please verify with Chilean-Palestinian cooks.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Palestinian diaspora in Chile's Valparaíso region (La Calera, Quillota) among families from Beit Jala. It is likely shared more widely in the community ⚠️.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Diaspora mourning feast. Spiced rice layered with fried cauliflower and tender goat, under a warm garlicky yogurt sauce, eaten from shared platters.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Large stockpots and trays on gas burners, cooked in quantity at the family home of the deceased.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Palestinian tradition, the family of the deceased receives mourners for days. Bitter coffee (*qahwa sada*) is served, and neighbours and relatives cook rice-and-meat platters for the mourners. In Chile this custom survived three or four generations of migration. A La Calera restaurateur explained that at Chilean-Palestinian vigils people "drink bitter coffee before the ceremony then drink sweetened coffee afterward", and then "go to the house of the family to eat *fate*". She added: "Regardless if the person was well known or not, or whether it is a large or small vigil, they make the same food."

The name is the Chilean spelling of ***fatteh*** ("crumbled"). In Palestine and Lebanon that is a family of layered dishes of bread, rice, meat and yogurt; see the Gaza version, [fatteh ghazawiyya](recipe_fatteh_ghazawiyya.md). In Chile the dish keeps the core of yogurt, rice and meat, uses **goat** (more available in Chile than lamb in early migrant times; ethnographers note early substitutions of beef for goat or lamb), and adds **cauliflower**, a vegetable also central to [maqluba](recipe_maqluba.md). Cooking it is a collective, largely women's task. The Beit Jala families interviewed describe big events with hundreds of guests and many hours of preparation.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **La Calera / Quillota (Beit Jala families):** Rice + yogurt + cauliflower + goat, the same menu at every wake.
*   **Homeland comparison, Palestine (⚠️):** Mourning meals are typically [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md)-style rice-and-meat platters or [qidreh](recipe_qidreh.md) in the Hebron hills; *fatteh* is more a festive or breakfast dish there. The Chilean wake *fate* is therefore a diaspora specialisation.
*   **Other Chilean families:** ⚠️ Not documented. Lamb or beef may replace goat.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: reconstructed La Calera wake fate.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Goat (or lamb) on the bone, in pieces | 1.2 kg |
| Water | 2 L |
| Onion, halved | 1 |
| Bay leaves, cardamom pods, cinnamon stick | 2 / 4 / 1 |
| Cauliflower, in florets | 1 medium (≈ 700 g) |
| Oil for frying | 300 ml |
| Plain yogurt (or leben) | 750 g |
| Cornflour (to stabilise the yogurt) | 1 tbsp |
| Garlic, crushed | 3 cloves |
| Salt, allspice | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium-grain rice | 350 g |
| Strained goat broth for the rice | 650 ml |
| Yogurt sauce per plate | ≈ 180 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Broth:** Cover the goat with the water, skim, then add the onion, bay, cardamom and cinnamon. Simmer covered for 1.5–2 hours until tender. Strain and keep the broth.
2. **Cauliflower:** Fry the florets in hot oil until deep golden and drain them.
3. **Rice:** Rinse the rice, then cook it in 650 ml of hot broth with salt and a pinch of allspice, covered, on low for 18 minutes.
4. **Yogurt sauce:** Whisk the yogurt with the cornflour, garlic and salt. Warm it gently, stirring in one direction, until it just simmers.
5. **Assemble:** On a platter, spread the rice, lay the cauliflower and goat on top, and spoon the warm yogurt over just before serving.
