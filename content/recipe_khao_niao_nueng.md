# Khao Niao Nueng / ข้าวเหนียวนึ่ง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of northern Thailand (Lanna) and Isan, continuing across the Mekong into Laos. In the north it is often just *khao nueng* ("steamed rice").
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Steamed glutinous rice, the daily staple. The grains are chewy, glossy and cling together, so they can be pinched off and rolled into a ball by hand. They are never boiled.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A conical woven-bamboo steamer (*huad*, หวด) set over a narrow-necked pot of water (*mo nueng*) on a charcoal or wood stove. The cooked rice is kept warm in a lidded bamboo basket (*kratip* or *kong khao*).

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Glutinous rice is the defining grain of the Lao-Tai world. In Isan most people grew up in sticky-rice farming families, and meals centre on a shared *kratip* of rice with *som tam* and other dishes ([SBS Food](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/embracing-four-generations-of-isan-sticky-rice/9b7wl1hdh)). In Lanna, sticky rice is the main source of energy, eaten with chilli dips and wild vegetables ([Food Culture in Lanna Way of Life](https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/sdust/article/view/183834)). The basket itself is heritage: the Penn Museum holds an early-20th-century *kratip khao* / *kong khao* from Ban Bun Mi, Udon Thani ([Penn Museum](https://www.penn.museum/collections/object/126844)). Eating is done with the right hand: a pinch of rice is rolled into a ball and used to scoop dips, curries and grilled meat ([Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/travel/national/photos-of-thailand-northern-thai-food-khao-soi%20); [Table Journey](https://tablejourney.com/thailand/dish/sticky-rice/)).

Leftover rice feeds a whole family of other dishes: it is grilled into [khao jee](recipe_khao_jee.md), pounded into [khao puk nga](recipe_khao_puk_nga.md), and dried into crackers ([khao pong](recipe_khao_pong.md), [khao taen](recipe_khao_taen.md)). Raw grains are toasted into [khao kua](recipe_khao_kua.md) or pounded into [khao buea](recipe_khao_buea.md). Spanish and Levantine rices are cooked in liquid; this rice is **never** cooked in water at all, only in steam.

**Culinary science:** glutinous rice is almost pure amylopectin. Long soaking (6 hours to overnight) hydrates the grain through to the core, so steam alone gelatinises it without it bursting. The porous bamboo lets excess condensation drain away, which keeps the grains separate inside the mass rather than mushy.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Isan / Lao style:** Long overnight soak. The rice is flipped in the *huad* halfway through so the bottom layer becomes the top. Served in a *kratip* with *jaew* (dry chilli dip), som tam, larb and grilled chicken ([Michelin Guide – larb](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/what-is-larb-the-unsung-hero-of-thailand-s-isan-cuisine)).
*   **Chiang Mai / Lanna style:** Eaten with *nam phrik num* or *nam phrik ong*, pork crackling and boiled vegetables. Austin Bush's recipe calls for soaking 8 hours to overnight ([Eat Your Books – Khao nueng, *The Food of Northern Thailand* p. 54](https://www.eatyourbooks.com/library/recipes/2139369/sticky-rice-khao-nueng)).
*   **Purple sticky rice (*khao kam*):** Lanna's dark glutinous rice is steamed the same way, alone or mixed with white.
*   **Sick-day rice (*khao thuay*):** Isan families soften sticky rice by soaking it about an hour, then cook it with more water than rice for the ill ([Silpa Wattanatham](https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_153901)). ⚠️ Method from a secondary summary.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: everyday Isan / Lanna steamed sticky rice in a huad.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Thai glutinous rice (*khao niao*, e.g. RD6 or *khiao ngu*) | 500 g |
| Cold water (for soaking) | to cover by 5 cm |
| Water (in the steaming pot) | 1.5–2 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Soaking time | 6–12 h (overnight) |
| Steaming time | 25–35 min, flipped once |
| Yield | ≈ 1 kg cooked rice (≈ 250 g per person) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Wash and soak:** Rinse the rice until the water runs nearly clear, then cover it with cold water and soak overnight.
2. **Set up the huad:** Bring water to a rolling boil in the *mo nueng*. Wet the bamboo *huad* and fit it into the pot's neck so it doesn't touch the water.
3. **Steam:** Drain the rice and spread it in the *huad*. Cover with a lid or a damp cloth and steam over high heat for **15 min**.
4. **Flip:** Toss the *huad* with a sharp flick so the whole mass turns over, then steam **10–15 min** more, until the grains are translucent and chewy all through.
5. **Air and store:** Turn the rice onto a tray, spread it briefly to release steam, then pack it loosely into a *kratip*. It stays warm and soft for hours.
