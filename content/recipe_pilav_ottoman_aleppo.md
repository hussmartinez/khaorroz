# Ottoman Pilav (the Last Course of the Aleppine Table) / پلاو · رز مفلفل

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Two things are attested. (1) Ottoman Aleppo served "the last pilaw" as the closing dish of a meal (Alexander Russell, 1756). (2) The first printed Ottoman cookbook, Mehmed Kâmil's *Melceü't-Tabbâhîn* (Istanbul, 1844), has a 13-recipe pilaf chapter. Its English version by Turabi Efendi (London, 1864) indexes an "Aadi Pilaw — Ordinary pilaw", and an Arabic translation by Muhammad Sidqi circulated from 1887 to 1915. I could not read the recipe text: the scanned edition has no text layer. The method below is the standard Ottoman *sade pilav* (butter-absorption) technique, a reconstruction.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Ottoman Aleppo and Damascus, among the urban, Turkish-influenced households of the 18th–19th centuries.
*   **Historical Source:** *Melceü't-Tabbâhîn*, Mehmed Kâmil, 1844, Istanbul (Eng. tr. Turabi Efendi, *Turkish Cookery Book*, 1864; Ar. tr. Muhammad Sidqi, 1887); Aleppine course order from Alexander Russell, *The Natural History of Aleppo*, 1756
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Plain butter pilaf. Every grain separate, shiny and dry, the way Ottoman cooks judged pilav. It closes the meal as a neutral, buttery counterpoint.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A tinned-copper *tencere*, its lid wrapped in a cloth, over charcoal.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Russell's Aleppo the meal ran from soup to pilaw: "The first dish is generally a sort of broth, or soup, and the last pilaw." The large final pilaw was sometimes eaten with "a dish of sweet starch", a set pudding, then followed by *khushaf*. That order (soup, many stews and stuffed things, pilav, sweet) is the classic Ottoman sequence, which the Levant shared with Istanbul.

The 1844 *Melceü't-Tabbâhîn* was the first Ottoman cookbook to be printed. It was reprinted nine times by 1889, translated into English in 1864, and translated into Arabic in 1887, so it reached the Arabic-reading Levant directly. Its pilaf chapter fixes the rules of Ottoman pilav: good rice, salted soaking water, butter, a measured broth, and a cloth under the lid. Today's Levantine *ruz mfalfal* and [riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md) are its everyday heirs, and the medieval [Wusla pilaf](recipe_aruzz_mufalfal_wusla.md) is its ancestor.

**Culinary logic:** soaking in warm salted water lets the grains absorb some water before cooking. Toasting them in butter seals the surface, and a cloth-lined lid catches the steam drips, so the grains stay separate and dry.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Istanbul palace and Melceü't (1844):** 13 pilavs, including plain (*sade*/"ordinary"), and others not consulted here.
*   **Ottoman Aleppo (Russell, 1756):** A large pilaw as the final dish, sometimes with a sweet starch pudding.
*   **Aleppo Christians (Russell):** Pilaw served less often; bulgur used more.
*   **Modern Levant:** Vermicelli rice ([riz bi sh'ariyyeh](recipe_riz_bi_shariyyeh.md)) and *hashweh* rices.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern adaptation (Ottoman sade pilav technique).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Butter (or ghee) | 60 g |
| Hot chicken or lamb broth | 450 ml |
| Salt (for soaking water + broth) | 1 tbsp + to taste |
| Warm water (for soaking) | 1 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium-grain rice (e.g. Turkish baldo or Egyptian) | 300 g |
| Broth : rice | 1.5 : 1 by volume |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Soak:** Soak the rice in warm salted water for **30 min**, then rinse until the water runs clear and drain well.
2. **Toast:** Melt the butter, add the rice and stir over medium heat for **4–5 min**, until the grains turn translucent and crackle.
3. **Absorb:** Pour in the boiling, salted broth, cover and cook on low heat until all the liquid is absorbed (**12–15 min**).
4. **Rest:** Off the heat, lay a clean cloth under the lid and rest **15 min**. Fluff with a fork and serve as the last course, or with a stew.
