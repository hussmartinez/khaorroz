# Som Tam / ส้มตำ (Tam Mak Hung / ตำหมากหุ่ง)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a Lao origin is widely repeated but not firmly proven, and the date som tam reached Bangkok (mid-20th century) is approximate.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Isan and Laos; carried to Bangkok and the whole country by Isan migrants.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: shredded green papaya pounded in a mortar with chilli, garlic, lime, fish sauce or fermented fish, sour-hot-salty and crunchy; the classic trio is som tam, grilled chicken and sticky rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A tall clay mortar (*khrok din*) with a wooden pestle; no fire for the salad; sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Lao and Isan the dish is *tam mak hung*, "pounded papaya", or *tam som*, "pounded sour"; *som* means sour and *tam* means to pound ([Wikipedia – Green papaya salad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_papaya_salad); [Wikiwand – Tam mak hung](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Tam_mak_hung)). Reference works suggest the pounded sour salad predates papaya, a New World fruit that reached the Lao via the Khmer Loeu of southeastern Laos, and that som tam spread to Bangkok with Isan migrant workers in the mid-20th century ⚠️ ([Wikipedia – Green papaya salad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_papaya_salad)). Michelin describes it as a complete meal when eaten with sticky rice and grilled meat ([Michelin Guide – Decoding som tam](https://guide.michelin.com/en/article/features/decoding-the-delicious-som-tam)).

**Culinary science:** pounding bruises the papaya shreds so they absorb the dressing without becoming mushy; fermented fish (*pla ra*) adds glutamates and a funky depth that balances the lime's acidity.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Tam Lao / tam pla ra:** Isan-Lao style with *pla ra*, no sugar or peanuts, very hot ([Michelin](https://guide.michelin.com/en/article/features/decoding-the-delicious-som-tam)).
*   **Tam Thai:** Bangkok style, sweeter, with palm sugar, dried shrimp and peanuts.
*   **Tam pu:** with salted field crab.
*   **Mixed tams:** cucumber, long bean, corn or fruit instead of papaya.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Isan-Lao tam pla ra, served with sticky rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Green papaya, shredded | 400 g |
| Bird's-eye chillies | 4–8 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Pla ra (fermented fish sauce), boiled | 3 tbsp |
| Fish sauce | 1 tbsp |
| Lime juice | 3 tbsp |
| Cherry tomatoes, halved / long beans | 8 / 2 |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Papaya per batch in the mortar | ≈ 100 g (pound in 4 batches) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Base:** pound garlic and chillies to a coarse paste in the clay mortar.
3. **Pound:** add long beans and tomatoes, then a quarter of the papaya; pound lightly while turning with a spoon, adding pla ra, fish sauce and lime.
4. **Serve:** taste, repeat for each batch and serve with sticky rice, grilled chicken and raw vegetables.
