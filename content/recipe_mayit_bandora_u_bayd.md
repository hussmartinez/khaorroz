# Mayit Bandora u Bayd / مية بندورة وبيض

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** documented in *The Gaza Kitchen* (Laila El-Haddad & Maggie Schmitt) as "a popular and inexpensive meal from the rural villages in eastern Gaza". Only the headnote and the first steps were accessible, so the egg-poaching finish and quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The rural villages of eastern Gaza (the farming belt along the eastern edge of the Strip: Beit Hanoun, Shuja'iyya fields, Khuza'a, Bani Suhaila ⚠️).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Humble one-pot rice. Rice simmered in strained tomato "water" with cumin until just tender and still a little brothy, with eggs poached on top; torn bread is dipped in the sauce.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A high-rimmed pan or skillet on a gas ring or a wood fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Mayit bandora* means "tomato water". Ripe tomatoes are blended and strained into a thin red liquid that becomes the cooking medium. The Gaza farmlands produce tomatoes in abundance, and eggs come from the household's own hens, so the dish turns the cheapest farm staples into a full meal. *The Gaza Kitchen* places it in the countryside of **eastern Gaza**, whose villages have suffered repeatedly in successive wars. The book records it among the everyday dishes, alongside the festive [qidreh](recipe_qidreh.md) and [maqluba](recipe_maqluba.md).

Since 2023, reporting from Gaza describes families surviving on rice, lentils and whatever tomatoes and eggs can be found. That makes this old peasant dish a present-day necessity again.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Eastern Gaza villages (documented):** Onion in olive oil, strained tomato, rice, cumin and black pepper; eggs poached in the sauce; bread for dipping.
*   **Gaza City households (⚠️):** Often add green chilli or dill, the city's signature herbs.
*   **Comparison:** *Qalayet bandora* (fried tomatoes, sometimes with eggs) is the pan-Palestinian cousin without rice.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: eastern Gaza (reconstructed quantities).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 3 tbsp |
| Onion, chopped | 1 medium |
| Ripe tomatoes, blended and strained | 4 (≈ 600 g → ≈ 500 ml liquid) |
| Ground cumin | 1 tsp |
| Black pepper, salt | ½ tsp / 1 tsp |
| Eggs | 4 |
| Bread (taboon or pita), torn | to serve |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice, rinsed | 200 g |
| Tomato water + water | 500 ml + 250 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Onion:** Sauté the onion in the olive oil over medium heat until golden.
2. **Tomato and rice:** Add the strained tomato, water, rice, salt, cumin and pepper. Cook partially covered until the rice is *al dente* and some sauce remains (15–18 min).
3. **Eggs:** Make 4 hollows, crack in the eggs, cover and cook on low until the whites set (5–6 min).
4. **Serve:** Bring the pan to the table and dip torn bread into the tomato broth.
