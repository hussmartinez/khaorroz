# Khao Yam / ข้าวยำ

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The whole of southern Thailand. It is strongest in the Malay-Muslim provinces (Pattani, Yala, Narathiwat), Nakhon Si Thammarat, Songkhla and the Andaman coast. Across the border it is Kelantan's *nasi kerabu*.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** A rice salad. Cool, separate grains of jasmine rice (often tinted blue with butterfly-pea flower or yellow with gardenia) are tossed at the table with a heap of finely shredded raw herbs, sour fruit, toasted coconut and dried shrimp, and dressed with a thick, sweet-salty **budu** sauce.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot for the rice, a small saucepan (*mo*) for the budu dressing and a wok (*kratha*) to toast the coconut. The dish itself is assembled on a plate or banana leaf, with no further cooking.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao yam ("mixed rice") is the south's signature breakfast: rice eaten at room temperature with the morning's herbs. The Michelin Guide records a southern origin legend: a ruler asked a physician to put every healthy ingredient of the land onto one plate, including *bai yo* (Indian mulberry/noni leaf) from the gardens of respected households. It also notes that the dish more plausibly comes from Malay tradition. Pattani, Yala and Narathiwat were part of the **Sultanate of Pattani**, which shared a kitchen with Kelantan, where the same dish is called *nasi kerabu*. ⚠️ Claims of a 15th-century origin found online are unsourced.

The key is **nam budu**, a liquid made by fermenting small fish (anchovy, *pla sai tan*) with salt in the Muslim coastal villages of Pattani, Yala, Narathiwat and Songkhla. For khao yam it is simmered with palm sugar, lemongrass, galangal and makrut-lime leaf into a dark syrup. The dish is about balance: sour (green mango, pomelo), salty (budu, dried shrimp), herbal-bitter (*chaplu*, lemongrass, moringa flower) and rich (toasted coconut). Rice tinted with butterfly-pea flowers (*dok anchan*) gives the market version its striking blue.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Pattani and the deep south (budu school):** Rice dressed with budu syrup. Pattani home versions add minced fresh shrimp, *yeera* blossom, small Thai mackerel stir-fried with lemongrass, and noni-leaf rice (Nuraya Mahani's version, per the Michelin Guide).
*   **Nakhon Si Thammarat (curry-paste school):** Instead of budu, a **krueng gaeng** (lemongrass, makrut-lime zest, turmeric, galangal and chilli) is fried with coconut milk and stirred through the rice. This is *khao yam hua kha*, a dressing with no budu at all.
*   **Phang Nga, Chumphon, Ranong:** Eaten with pickled papaya and carrot and dressed with **nam koei** (shrimp-paste liquor) rather than fish budu.
*   **Satun:** Reported to omit budu.
*   **An old "roadside herb" khao yam** recorded in a century-old Thai cookbook, without budu, is described by *Silpa Wattanatham* ([silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=52687)). ⚠️ That recipe wasn't read in full.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Pattani / southern budu khao yam**, with Nakhon's curry-paste option.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Nam budu (fermented fish sauce) | 250 ml |
| Water | 250 ml |
| Palm sugar | 100 g |
| Lemongrass, bruised | 2 stalks |
| Galangal, sliced | 20 g |
| Makrut-lime leaves | 5 |
| Shallots, halved | 3 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (cooked with a handful of butterfly-pea flowers) | 300 g raw |
| Toasted grated coconut | 60 g |
| Dried shrimp, pounded | 40 g |
| Green mango, pomelo, long beans, bean sprouts, finely shredded | 300 g total |
| Herbs: lemongrass, makrut-lime leaf, *chaplu*, Vietnamese mint, finely sliced | 1 large handful |
| Dried chilli powder, lime wedges | to taste |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Blue rice:** Steep the butterfly-pea flowers in hot water for 10 minutes and use this water to cook the rice. Spread it out to cool.
2. **Budu syrup:** Simmer the budu, water, palm sugar, lemongrass, galangal, lime leaves and shallots for **20–30 minutes**, until the sauce coats a spoon. Strain and cool.
3. **Coconut:** Dry-toast the grated coconut in the wok until golden, then pound it lightly.
4. **Assemble:** Mound the rice and arrange the herbs, fruit, coconut, dried shrimp and chilli around it.
5. **Serve:** Each diner pours over the budu syrup and squeezes lime, then mixes everything together. [**Nakhon variant:** fry the curry paste in coconut cream until fragrant and toss it through the rice instead of the budu.]
