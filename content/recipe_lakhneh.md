# Lakhneh (Nablus Stuffed Leaves) / اللخنة

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a **disappearing dish**. The artist-researcher Mirna Bamieh (founder of the "Palestine Hosting Society", which researches forgotten Palestinian dishes) told *Asharq Al-Awsat* that *lakhneh* is still cooked **only in Nablus and its villages**. It consists of leaves of the plant called *dhakar al-qarnabit* ("male cauliflower") wrapped around minced meat, rice and spices, with **cumin** as the main seasoning. The botanical identity of the leaf, the cooking liquid and the quantities aren't documented, so the blueprint below is a reconstruction modelled on Nablus stuffed-leaf technique.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Nablus and the villages of Jabal Nablus.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed leaves (*mahashi*). Tender wild or field leaves rolled around rice and cumin-scented meat, simmered until soft.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot on a gas or wood stove, rolls weighted with a plate.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Nablus is known for its elaborate *madani* cooking, including knafeh, maqluba with cauliflower and seasonal *'akkoub*. Its villages kept dishes made with gathered and field plants. *Lakhneh* belongs to this second world. Bamieh describes it as surviving only in the Nablus area while "other Palestinians have largely stopped making it". Her long communal tables, the first held with the A. M. Qattan Foundation and five families, are an attempt to record such dishes before they are lost. Alongside *lakhneh* she lists other endangered dishes: Nablus *musluʿa* (lentils and rice; see [musluʿa](recipe_musluaa.md)), Jaffa *rummaniyyeh*, and Jerusalem cucumbers stuffed with tamarind.

The leaf name *dhakar al-qarnabit* ("male cauliflower") suggests the outer leaves of a non-heading or bolted cauliflower or brassica ⚠️. This would make *lakhneh* a thrifty cousin of [malfuf mahshi](recipe_malfouf_mehshi.md).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Nablus city and villages (documented):** Leaves of *dhakar al-qarnabit*, minced meat, rice and spices, with cumin dominant.
*   **Elsewhere in Palestine:** Said to be no longer cooked. Cabbage and vine-leaf rolls took its place.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: reconstructed Nablus lakhneh.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Large tender brassica leaves (outer cauliflower / collard-type) | 30–35 |
| Minced lamb or beef | 250 g |
| Short-grain rice, rinsed | 200 g |
| Ground cumin | 2 tsp |
| Allspice, salt, black pepper | ½ tsp / 1 tsp / ½ tsp |
| Olive oil | 3 tbsp |
| Garlic cloves, whole | 6 |
| Lemon juice | 3 tbsp |
| Water | ≈ 800 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Filling per leaf | 1 heaped tsp |
| Simmer | 40–50 min |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Leaves:** Blanch the leaves in salted boiling water for 1–2 minutes until pliable, then cut out the tough central ribs.
2. **Filling:** Mix the rice, meat, cumin, allspice, salt, pepper and oil.
3. **Roll:** Roll each leaf tightly around a line of filling, cigar-style.
4. **Pack and cook:** Pack the rolls in a pot with the garlic, add water and lemon juice to just cover, weight with a plate and simmer covered on low for 40–50 minutes.
5. **Serve:** Rest 5 minutes, then serve with yogurt and lemon.
