# Loubieh bi-Lahmeh / لوبية باللحمة

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Levant, best documented in Lebanon and Syria, cooked across Palestine and Jordan as *fasoulia khadra* (green beans) stew.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: flat or round green beans stewed with lamb or beef in a tomato and garlic sauce until silky, ladled over vermicelli rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy stew pot on the hob; a separate lidded pot for the rice.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Green beans (*loubieh*) appear on the Levantine table in two forms. In summer they are cooked as **loubieh bi-zeit**, braised in olive oil and eaten cold with bread. As soon as meat is added, the dish becomes **loubieh bi-lahmeh**, a hot stew for rice: "Loubieh bi Zeit becomes Loubieh bi Lahme when meat is added." Recipes generally describe green beans, lamb or beef and onions cooked in tomato and "typically served over rice", with vermicelli rice the usual pairing because it soaks up the sauce.

It belongs to the *ruz w yakhneh* family alongside [yakhnet batata](recipe_yakhnet_batata.md), [bazella w ruz](recipe_bazella_w_ruz.md) and [bamyeh ma' ruz](recipe_bamyeh_ma_ruz.md).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Lebanese beef version:** Beef cubes, garlic, coriander at the end, tomato paste; vermicelli rice.
*   **Lebanese lamb-chop version (Beirut family cookbook):** Lamb chops instead of cubes, as in "Loubieh bil lahme, green beans with lamb chops".
*   **Minced-meat version:** Quicker weekday stew with minced beef (*loubia bi lahme* with mince).
*   **Pomegranate molasses (⚠️ household variation):** Some cooks finish with pomegranate molasses for tartness; sources differ.
*   **Chicken or meatless:** Chicken pieces also work, and a vegetarian version is served the same way with rice on the side.
*   **Palestine / Jordan / Iraq-border cooking (⚠️):** Called *fasoulia khadra* or *loubia* stew; the same structure, often with more tomato juice and served with plain white rice.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Lebanese beef loubieh bi-lahmeh with vermicelli rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Beef or lamb, cubed (or lamb chops) | 500 g |
| Flat green beans, topped and cut in 4 cm pieces | 800 g |
| Onion, chopped | 1 large |
| Garlic, crushed | 5 cloves |
| Fresh coriander, chopped | 1 small bunch |
| Tomato paste + crushed tomatoes | 2 tbsp + 400 g |
| 7-spice, salt, pepper | 1 tsp each to taste |
| Olive oil | 50 ml |
| Water or meat stock | 600 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium-grain rice | 300 g |
| Vermicelli | 50 g |
| Water for the rice | 450 ml |
| Butter or ghee | 30 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Meat:** Brown the meat in the oil with the onion; add stock and spices and simmer **60 min** (lamb chops 45 min).
2. **Beans:** Sauté the garlic, coriander and beans in a little oil for 5 min, then add them to the meat with the tomato paste and crushed tomatoes.
3. **Stew:** Cover and simmer **30–40 min** until the beans are very tender and the sauce has thickened.
4. **Rice:** Toast the vermicelli in butter, add the rinsed rice and boiling salted water; cook covered on low **15 min**, rest 10 min.
5. **Serve:** Rice on the plate, stew ladled over, extra coriander on top.
