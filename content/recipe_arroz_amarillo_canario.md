# Arroz Amarillo Canario / Arroz Canario

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Canarias](provincial_canarias.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** I found **no well-documented traditional Canarian recipe** for arroz amarillo. The sources are user recipe sites (Cookpad), including an *arroz canario con picadillo*. Saffron as the yellow colouring fits Canarian cooking (it scents the *rancho canario*), but my searches did not confirm it for this rice. Everything below is a reconstruction. Verify it with Canarian cookbooks, such as the ULPGC digital collection.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Canary Islands, mainly Gran Canaria and Tenerife home cooking.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry). Loose and fluffy, cooked by absorption.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A covered pot (*caldero*) on a gas stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Canarian cooking sits between Castile, Andalucía, Africa and the Americas, and **rice reached the islands' everyday tables partly through the return of emigrants from Cuba and Venezuela**. Arroz amarillo is a side and base rice, coloured with **saffron** (or, today, *colorante* or turmeric) and cooked with onion, garlic and stock. It accompanies stews and *carne fiesta*, and with minced meat (**picadillo**) it becomes a one-pot meal. Its sibling, **arroz a la cubana** (white rice, tomato sauce, fried egg and fried banana), is described as typical of Canarias.

**Culinary science:** this is an absorption-method rice, unlike the open-pan rices of the Mediterranean. The pot is covered after boiling, so steam finishes the grains evenly. A blended onion-garlic liquid gives flavour without visible pieces.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — reconstructed)
*   **Basic home style (Cookpad):** Onion, garlic and chicken stock blended into the water, plus colouring.
*   **With picadillo:** Minced meat stewed with tomato and pepper, folded through or served on top.
*   **Festive:** With peas, roasted pepper and *chorizo canario*.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **saffron arroz amarillo** (reconstructed).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 40 ml |
| Onion | 1 small |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |
| Chicken stock | 750 ml |
| Saffron (or colouring) | 0.2 g |
| Bay leaf | 1 |
| Salt | to taste |

**Extraction:** Blend the onion and garlic with 200 ml of the stock. Fry the purée in the oil for **4 min**, then add the rest of the stock, the saffron and the bay.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Long or medium-grain rice | 350 g |
| Stock | ≈ 750 ml (≈ 1.8–2:1 volume) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Toasting:** Fry the rice in the base for 1 min.
2. **Liquid:** Add the boiling stock. When it boils again, **cover** and set the heat to minimum.
3. **Steam:** Cook **15–17 min** without lifting the lid.
4. **Finish:** Rest 5 min covered, then fluff with a fork.
