# Khanom Jak (Khanom Bai Jak) / ขนมจาก (ขนมใบจาก)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the method is documented in home and cultural sources; I found no dated history.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The nipa-palm coasts: Samut Prakan (Pak Nam), Chachoengsao (Bang Pakong), Samut Songkhram, and the southern mangrove provinces.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sticky rice snack: glutinous-rice dough with grated coconut and palm or cane sugar, flattened inside strips of nipa-palm leaf (*bai jak*) and grilled over charcoal. Chewy, smoky and lightly caramelised.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A charcoal grill; nipa-palm leaves pinned with coconut-rib skewers. Where there is no nipa, coconut leaves are used.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khanom jak is the snack of the nipa-palm (*jak*) estuaries. The traditional dough is soaked glutinous rice pounded fine, or ready-made glutinous rice flour, mixed with cane or palmyra sugar, grated coconut and egg. It is wrapped in two overlapping nipa leaves pinned with a coconut-rib skewer and grilled over charcoal. Cooks are advised to choose whole leaves with no tears or holes, and coconut leaves are used where nipa doesn't grow ([GotoKnow – khanom jak](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/516337)). Kneading the flour with coconut flesh and using coconut water makes the dough more fragrant ([TrueID Food](https://food.trueid.net/detail/OrBknZd6PvDN)). In Pak Nam, Samut Prakan, a sweet shop more than 100 years old still sells *khanom bai jak* and grilled sticky rice ([Wongnai – Lim Damrong](https://www.wongnai.com/restaurants/77440MP-%E0%B8%A5%E0%B8%B4%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%84%E0%B9%8C/reviews)).

**Culinary science:** the leaf steams the dough from inside while the outer leaf chars, so the sugar caramelises against the leaf and the dough takes on a smoky, slightly tannic aroma.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Pak Nam (Samut Prakan):** sold in century-old sweet shops alongside grilled sticky rice ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/restaurants/77440MP-%E0%B8%A5%E0%B8%B4%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%B3%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%84%E0%B9%8C/reviews)).

*   **Coconut-water dough:** more fragrant ([TrueID](https://food.trueid.net/detail/OrBknZd6PvDN)).
*   **Coconut-leaf wrap:** where nipa is absent ([GotoKnow](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/516337)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous rice flour | 300 g |
| Grated coconut (young-mature) | 200 g |
| Cane or palm sugar | 150 g |
| Coconut water | 100 ml |
| Egg | 1 |
| Salt | pinch |
| Nipa-palm leaves, cut in 25 cm strips | about 40 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dough per parcel | 1 heaped tbsp |
| Parcels | about 20 |
| Grilling | 3–4 min per side |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Dough:** Knead the flour, coconut, sugar, salt and egg, adding coconut water until soft and spreadable.
2. **Wrap:** Spread 1 tbsp along a nipa strip, cover with a second strip and pin both ends with coconut-rib skewers.
3. **Grill:** Grill over medium charcoal, turning, until the leaves char and the dough sets (6–8 min total).
4. **Serve:** Peel the leaf open and eat warm.
