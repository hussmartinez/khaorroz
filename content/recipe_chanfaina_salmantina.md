# Chanfaina Salmantina / Arroz de Chanfaina

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Castilla y León](provincial_castilla_y_leon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is well attested (Directo al Paladar, Wikipedia via es-academic, Salamanca food guides), but sources disagree on its texture and ingredients (semi-soupy or dry; with or without chorizo or blood). No municipal or official recipe was found. The quantities below are reconstructed from the documented ingredients.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Province of Salamanca (city bars, the Campo Charro).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). Sources range from "arroz semicaldoso" (Cookpad) to "arroz seco de matanza" (La Maleta Inquieta), so meloso is the middle ground.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay *cazuela* on the hearth.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Chanfaina* is a name shared across the Spanish-speaking world (from Extremadura to Peru, Ecuador and Colombia) for **offal stews**. The **Salamanca** version is distinct because it is a **rice**: lamb offal (*menudillos*, *asadura*: liver, lung, heart), sometimes **lamb's blood**, stewed with paprika and **plenty of cumin**, with rice cooked in, and finished with **chopped hard-boiled egg**. Infobae describes it as a humble shepherds' dish that used the least noble parts of the lamb. Directo al Paladar notes it is a common bar tapa in Salamanca, especially on holidays, and The Objective describes it as a dish for family celebrations. It sits alongside [arroz a la zamorana](recipe_arroz_a_la_zamorana.md) as the region's other offal rice.

**Culinary science:** offal is lean but strongly flavoured. Cumin and paprika balance its iron-rich, slightly bitter notes. When lamb's blood is used it coagulates in the stew and darkens and thickens the sauce. The rice absorbs the cooking broth from the pre-boiled offal.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Salamanca bar style (Directo al Paladar, Wikipedia):** Lamb offal + **blood** + rice + hard-boiled egg, with paprika and lots of cumin.
*   **Home-style semi-soupy (Cookpad):** Lamb *menudillos* stewed with onion and garlic in their pre-cooking broth, with rice added and chopped egg on top. No blood.
*   **Matanza "dry" style (La Maleta Inquieta):** Asadura plus **chorizo** and sometimes blood, cooked drier.
*   ⚠️ Town-level differences within the province are not documented.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Salamanca bar style** (reconstructed quantities).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb offal (liver, lung, heart), diced | 600 g |
| Lamb's blood, cooked and crumbled (optional) | 150 g |
| Onion | 1 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Olive oil or lard | 60 ml |
| Sweet paprika | 1 tsp |
| Ground cumin | 1–1.5 tsp |
| Bay leaf | 1 |
| Water (for pre-boiling the offal) | 1.5 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 300 g |
| Offal broth | ≈ 0.9 L (≈ 3 : 1, meloso) |
| Hard-boiled eggs, chopped | 2 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Pre-boil:** Blanch the lung and heart in the water with the bay leaf for 20 min. Keep the broth.
2. **Sofrito:** Fry the onion and garlic. Add the liver and the boiled offal and fry 5 min. Off the heat, add the paprika and cumin.
3. **Rice:** Add the rice and stir for 1 min. Pour in the hot broth.
4. **Cook:** Simmer **17–18 min**. Add the crumbled blood at minute 12.
5. **Finish:** Scatter the chopped egg over the top and serve in the cazuela.
