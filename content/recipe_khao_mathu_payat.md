# Khao Mathu Payat / ข้าวมธุปายาส (Khao Thip, Khao Yakhu / ข้าวทิพย์, ข้าวยาคู)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the rite is well documented (PPTV, Khaosod, a Thai academic study of the ceremony at Tha Tum, Surin). The published recipe lists the ingredients but no quantities, so the proportions here are reconstructed. In ceremonies the dish is made in huge communal batches.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Nationwide Theravada practice. Strong local ceremonies are held at **Nakhon Si Thammarat** (where it is called *khao yakhu* or *khao yakho*, made with milk pressed from green rice for the Maha Bucha "Hae Pha Khuen That"), in Mon communities of **Pathum Thani**, and in **Isan** (Surin).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** A sacred stirred rice: rice (white or glutinous) cooked down with coconut milk, cane juice, honey, milk, sugar, beans and sesame until it is a thick, glossy, fudge-like paste that can be cut or rolled.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Giant shallow brass or iron **kratha** pans over wood fires, stirred with long wooden paddles by teams in white.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Mathu payat* is the Pali *madhupāyāsa*, "honeyed milk-rice". It recalls **Sujata's** offering of rice cooked with honey and cane sugar to the Bodhisattva just before his enlightenment, when he gave up extreme asceticism for the Middle Way. Thai Buddhists therefore make it for **Visakha Bucha** and other holy days, believing it brings long life, health and a clear mind (PPTV). The rite has its own rules. A Thai academic study of Tha Tum (Surin) and press accounts describe young unmarried girls or women keeping the eight precepts adding the grains, with older women assisting. At Nakhon Si Thammarat, rice is cut from a "Buddha field" so that its **rice milk** can be pressed for the *khao yakhu* of the Maha Bucha procession to the Phra Borommathat.

It is a cousin of every milk-and-rice offering in the atlas, from the Levant's [riz bi haleeb](recipe_riz_bi_haleeb.md) to Spain's [arroz con leche](recipe_arroz_con_leche.md).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Nakhon Si Thammarat (*khao yakhu*):** Made with **nam nom khao**, milk pressed from unripe rice grains harvested in a dedicated field, for Maha Bucha.
*   **Pathum Thani (Mon tradition):** Stirred jointly by laypeople and monks, alongside old Mon rites.
*   **Isan (Surin, Tha Tum):** Girls keeping eight precepts pour the grains and cereals into the pan before stirring begins.
*   **Central Thailand (*khao thip*):** The general Visakha Bucha version: rice with beans, sesame, coconut milk, cane juice and honey.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **central khao thip**, at household scale (reconstructed quantities).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Coconut milk | 500 ml |
| Fresh cane juice | 250 ml |
| Palm sugar or sugar | 150 g |
| Honey | 60 ml |
| Milk (fresh or condensed) | 100 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Glutinous or white rice, soaked overnight | 200 g |
| Mixed beans (black, red, mung), cooked | 80 g |
| Black and white sesame, toasted | 40 g |
| Peanuts, roasted | 40 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the rice until swollen, then drain and steam it until cooked (**25 minutes**).
2. **Syrup:** Bring the coconut milk to a boil and add the cane juice, sugar, honey and milk, stirring until dissolved.
3. **Stir:** Add the rice and stir continuously over medium-low heat. Add the beans, sesame and peanuts and keep stirring so the bottom doesn't catch (**45–60 minutes**).
4. **Set:** When the mass pulls away from the pan as a thick, shiny paste, spread it into trays or roll it into balls.
5. **Offer:** Offer to monks first, then share.
