# Arroz de Pals a la Cazuela / Arròs de Pals a la Cassola (Empordà)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Catalunya](provincial_catalunya.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** "Arròs de Pals" is sold under the **Productes de l'Empordà** guarantee mark. I could **not confirm an IGP** (protected geographical indication) in any source. No canonical recipe was found either: the Pals tourism office names *arròs a la cassola* as the town's most traditional rice, and the recipe below is reconstructed from Catalan cassola practice.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Baix Empordà (Girona): Pals, Torroella de Montgrí, L'Estartit, Palamós. The paddies sit on the right bank of the Ter, beside the Montgrí natural park.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). Pals rice stays loose rather than sticky.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** An earthenware **cassola** on a wood or gas stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Rice has a long and interrupted history in Pals. A Catalan study dates the town's rice mill to **1452**. Cultivation expanded in the 16th and 17th centuries, but outbreaks of disease (malaria, from the flooded paddies) caused conflict, and growing was **abandoned in 1838**. Planting was allowed again around **1900**, and the landowner **Pere Coll i Rigau** drove its revival. Today roughly 640–710 ha are cultivated. The rice is sold as **"Arròs de Pals"**, extra quality, under the Productes de l'Empordà mark, and the town holds an annual **"Pals i la Cuina de l'Arròs"** campaign. According to the Pals tourism office, the town's most traditional rice dish is **arròs a la cassola**, an Empordà **mar i muntanya** (sea and mountain) rice.

**Culinary science:** sources describe Pals rice as holding its shape and staying loose, which suits the Empordà cassola. It absorbs the sofregit and picada without breaking into a porridge.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Pals (documented):** Arròs a la cassola as the town's signature dish.
*   **Palamós (cuina de barca, the fishermen's cooking):** A cassola made with the day's catch from the Palamós fish auction, demonstrated at the Espai del Peix and the Museu de la Pesca.
*   ⚠️ **Reconstructed Empordà mar i muntanya:** Chicken or rabbit + sausage (*botifarra*) + squid + prawns, with a picada of almond and chocolate is sometimes cited for Empordà sauces generally (unverified for rice).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: reconstructed Empordà mar i muntanya cassola.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Chicken thighs, pieces | 400 g |
| Botifarra (fresh pork sausage), chunks | 200 g |
| Squid, rings | 250 g |
| Onion, chopped | 2 |
| Tomato, grated | 100 g |
| Fish and chicken stock | 1.4 L |
| Prawns | 8 |
| **Picada:** garlic, parsley, 10 toasted almonds, saffron | — |

**Extraction:** Brown the chicken and botifarra (**12 min**), then the squid (**3 min**). Cook the onion slowly until dark (**20 min**), then add the tomato (**5 min**).

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Arròs de Pals | 320 g |
| Stock | ≈ 1.25 L (≈ 4:1) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Toast the rice in the sofregit for 1 min, then add the boiling stock.
2. Simmer **10 min**. Add the picada and lay the prawns on top.
3. Cook **6–8 min** more, shaking the cassola.
4. Rest 2 min.
