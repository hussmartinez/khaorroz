# Kibbeh Halabiyeh (Rice-Crust Kibbeh) / كبة حلبية (كبة حلب)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** contested origin. The name means "Aleppo kibbeh", but Wikipedia and an Iraqi cultural source describe it as **Iraqi**: "Aleppo kibbeh is the name the Iraqis give to kibbeh made of rice". In Aleppo itself, kibbeh is overwhelmingly made with bulgur. It is included here as a regional crossover, attributed to Iraqi cooks; the travel-food story below is folk explanation. Quantities are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Named after Aleppo; made mainly in Iraq (Baghdad, Mosul) and in Syrian and Iraqi diaspora kitchens.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice-crust kibbeh: a smooth dough of boiled, mashed short-grain rice (bound with a little meat or semolina) wrapped around spiced minced meat and pine nuts, then fried golden or simmered in broth. Crisp outside, soft and slightly sticky inside.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot to boil the rice, a mortar or grinder for the dough, a deep pan for frying; stovetop.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Across the Levant, *kibbeh* means a shell of bulgur and meat. In Iraq, rice-based shells developed instead, and one of them carries the name of Syria's great kibbeh city. Wikipedia's Syrian cuisine article describes kibbeh halabiyyeh as "kibbeh with a rice crust; though named after Aleppo, this recipe seems to be of Iraqi origin" ([Wikipedia – Syrian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_cuisine)). An Iraqi cultural site says "Aleppo kibbeh" is simply what Iraqis call rice kibbeh, and repeats the story that merchants travelling from Baghdad to Aleppo carried it because rice keeps well ([Our Iraq – culture & society](https://ouriraq.org/ku/node/90)). Rice-shell kibbeh is also the basis of Iraqi *kubbat hamudh* (sour soup), a cousin of the Aleppo-Jewish [kibbeh hamda](recipe_kibbeh_hamda.md).

Aleppo, by contrast, is famous for more than 17 kinds of bulgur kibbeh, made with sumac, yogurt, quince and lemon ([Enab Baladi – الكبّة الحلبية](https://www.enabbaladi.net/784164/الكبّة-الحلبية-طعم-المدينة-وذاكرة-أهل/)). The rice kibbeh's name is therefore best read as a tribute, not a statement of origin.

**Culinary science:** short-grain rice has a lot of amylopectin; boiled until very soft and pounded while warm, it forms a sticky, pliable dough that can be thinned into a shell. A little semolina or raw minced meat strengthens it so it does not crack when fried.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Iraqi (Baghdad) kubbat halab:** Rice dough shaped into flattened ovals or balls around meat and fried.
*   **Iraqi kubba hamudh:** The same rice shells simmered in a sour turnip-and-tomato broth (soup form).
*   **Syrian Aleppo:** ⚠️ No published Aleppine version was found; Aleppo's kibbeh are bulgur-based.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **fried rice-crust kibbeh**, reconstructed.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Minced lamb or beef (filling) | 300 g |
| Onion, finely chopped | 1 |
| Pine nuts or chopped almonds | 30 g |
| Raisins (optional) | 2 tbsp |
| Baharat (allspice blend) | 1 tsp |
| Salt, black pepper | to taste |
| Oil for frying | 1 L |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice (Egyptian) | 300 g |
| Water to boil the rice | 900 ml |
| Turmeric (for colour) | ½ tsp |
| Fine semolina (binder) | 3 tbsp |
| Raw lean minced meat (in the dough, optional) | 100 g |
| Salt | 1 tsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Boil the rice:** Cook the rice in plenty of water with the turmeric until very soft, about 25 min; drain well.
2. **Make the dough:** While warm, pound or grind the rice with the semolina, salt (and the raw meat) to a smooth, sticky dough; cool.
3. **Filling:** Brown the minced meat with the onion, spices and nuts (and raisins); cool.
4. **Shape:** With wet hands, flatten a walnut-sized piece of dough, fill with a spoonful of meat and close into a flattened oval.
5. **Fry:** Deep-fry at 175 °C until golden, about 4–5 min.
6. **Serve:** Hot, with pickles and salad; or simmer the unfried shells in a sour tomato broth for the soup version.
