# Arroz con Choco / Arroz con Choco Onubense

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Andalucía](provincial_andalucia.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Huelva's emblematic choco dish is **papas con choco**, and some cooks add rice to it. The arroz con choco recipe documented below (Universidad Pablo de Olavide's recipe series) is not explicitly tied to Huelva. Town variations are reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Huelva coast: Huelva city, Punta Umbría, Isla Cristina, Ayamonte, Lepe.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep *cazuela* or pot over gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
People from Huelva are nicknamed *choqueros* because of their attachment to the **choco**, the local cuttlefish: very tender, with short tentacles. *Papas con choco* is the province's signature fishermen's stew, and adding rice to it, or replacing the potato with rice, gives a heartier **arroz con choco**. The UPO recipe uses fish stock, choco, bomba rice, white wine, onion, garlic and a **slice of bread fried and pounded** to thicken the broth. That is an Andalusian *majado* technique.

**Culinary science:** the *majado* (fried bread, garlic and sometimes almond, pounded in a mortar) thickens the caldo with starch and emulsified oil, giving it body without flour. Choco needs either a quick sear or a long braise. Here it is braised for 30–40 min before the rice goes in.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial — reconstructed)
*   **Huelva city:** Choco + onion + green pepper + **majado** of bread and garlic. White wine.
*   **Isla Cristina / Ayamonte:** With **clams** or **langostinos**, and a looser broth. See also [Isla Cristina caldoso](recipe_arroz_caldoso_isla_cristina.md).
*   **Inland (Condado):** Made with **potato and rice** together, a hybrid of *papas con choco*.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Huelva city with majado** (after the UPO recipe structure).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Choco (cuttlefish), cleaned, in pieces | 800 g |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Onion | 1 large |
| Green pepper | 1 |
| Garlic | 4 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Dry white wine | 150 ml |
| Bread slice (for majado) | 1 (30 g) |
| Bay leaf | 1 |
| Fish stock | 1.8 L |

**Extraction:** Fry the bread slice and 2 garlic cloves and set them aside. Sweat the onion and pepper (10 min), then add the remaining garlic and the tomato (5 min). Add the choco and cook 5 min. Add the wine and reduce. Add the stock and bay. Simmer **30–40 min** until the choco is tender. Pound the fried bread and garlic with a ladle of stock and stir the *majado* in.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Bomba / round rice | 300 g |
| Stock in pot | ≈ 1.6 L (≈ 5:1) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Add the rice to the simmering choco stew.
2. **Fire:** **17 min** at a steady simmer. Adjust the liquid to taste; the broth level is a matter of preference.
3. **Finish:** Add parsley and serve immediately.
