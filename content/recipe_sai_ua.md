# Sai Ua / ไส้อั่ว

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** origins (northern Thailand vs Shan State/Burma vs Laos) are reported differently; the pairing with sticky rice and nam phrik num is documented by market practice rather than a historical source.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna (Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, Mae Hong Son) and the Shan State, where it is *sai long phik*.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a coiled pork sausage packed with red curry paste, lemongrass, makrut lime leaf and galangal, grilled slowly over charcoal until smoky and juicy; sliced and eaten with sticky rice, *nam phrik num* and crisp pork rind.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Grilled in a bamboo clamp over low charcoal; sticky rice from the *huad*, served in a *kratip* / *kong khao*.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Sai ua ("stuffed intestine") is a grilled pork sausage typical of Mae Hong Son, Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai, with place of origin given as northern Thailand and northeastern Burma; in the Shan State it is called *sai long phik* ([Wikipedia – Sai ua](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Sai_ua)). Some accounts trace it to Burma or Laos ([SBS Food](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/2018/08/24/thai-lao-pork-sausage-orchestra-flavours)). It is grilled over charcoal and sold at markets such as Ton Payom ([Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/sai-ua-chiang-mai-sausage)); at Kad Luang it is sold alongside *nam phrik num* ([Wongnai – Chiang Mai sai ua](https://www.wongnai.com/listings/northern-sausage-sai-oua-cm)), and it is a fixture of the *khantok* table ([Michelin – khantok](https://guide.michelin.com/th/th/article/features/on-the-culinary-trail-khan-tok-dining-in-the-north)).

**Culinary science:** slow grilling renders the fat in the coarse mince so the curry paste fries inside the casing; low heat avoids bursting.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Chiang Mai:** classic red-paste version ([Eating Thai Food](https://www.eatingthaifood.com/sai-ua-chiang-mai-sausage)).
*   **Sai ua het thop:** seasonal version with *het thop* mushrooms ([Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/Tuktid/episodes/115718)).
*   **Lao sai oua:** a related but distinct sausage ([Wikipedia – Sai oua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_oua)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Chiang Mai sai ua with sticky rice and nam phrik num.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Coarse minced pork with fat (≈ 25 %) | 600 g |
| Red curry paste (dried chilli, shallot, garlic, lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, shrimp paste) | 5 tbsp |
| Makrut lime leaves, finely shredded | 6 |
| Fish sauce / salt | 1 tbsp / ½ tsp |
| Pork casing | 1.5 m |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice | 300 g raw |
| Paste : pork | ≈ 1 tbsp : 120 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Mix & stuff:** mix pork, paste, lime leaf and seasoning; stuff loosely into the casing and coil; prick.
2. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
3. **Grill:** grill the coil in a bamboo clamp over low charcoal 25–30 min, turning, until browned and cooked through.
4. **Serve:** slice and serve with sticky rice, [nam phrik num](recipe_nam_phrik_num.md), crisp pork rind and fresh ginger.
