# Caldero de Santa Pola y Tabarca / Caldero Santapolero i Tabarquí

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** El Baix Vinalopó coast: Santa Pola and the **Illa de Tabarca** (the only inhabited island in the Comunitat Valenciana). The tradition extends south to Torrevieja (La Vega Baja coast) and to the Murcian Mar Menor.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). Wetter than arroz a banda: the rice is moist and loose, and it is served with a little broth that is still moving.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A cast-iron **caldero** (a round-bottomed, two-handled pot) over a wood or charcoal fire on the beach or boat. Today a butane ring is common.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The caldero is the "mother" of arroz a banda. It is a two-service fishermen's meal cooked in a single iron pot. The Tabarca tradition goes back to the Genoese fishing families from the Tunisian island of Tabarka, whom Carlos III resettled on the islet in 1770. They brought a Ligurian–North African fish-stew sensibility that merged with the local **ñora**-based cooking. First, rockfish (*gallina*/*rascacio*, scorpionfish, gurnard, *morralla*) is boiled with **potatoes** and a fried ñora-garlic picada. The fish and potatoes are served with **allioli**. Then the rice is cooked in that broth in the same caldero.

**Culinary science:** the iron caldero's high thermal mass and round bottom keep a steady rolling boil without hot spots, so the starch is released evenly and the rice turns creamy rather than forming socarrat. **Ñora** releases carotenoid pigments and a mildly bitter, raisin-like sweetness into oil. Frying the ñora for just seconds is crucial: a few seconds too long and it turns acrid.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **The Tabarca Style:** **Gallina** (*Scorpaena scrofa*, red scorpionfish) is the noble fish. **Potato** is always included, and the broth is generous (≈5 L for a large family caldero). Rice is cooked very moist. Served in **two services**: fish and potatoes first, then rice, both with allioli.
*   **The Santa Pola Style:** Built on **morralla** (1.5 kg of small mixed rockfish) plus 1 gallina, with **2 ñoras**, a **head of garlic**, a **bunch of parsley** and **pear tomatoes** (from the recipe of Mesón Moniato's, Santa Pola, documented in the *Revista Española de Nutrición Comunitaria*). The broth is strained before the rice goes in. Slightly drier, almost a seco-meloso. Allioli is again essential.
*   **The Torrevieja Style:** Often cooked with **salmonetes** (red mullet) and **dorada**, with more tomato. The picada includes **almond** in some families.
*   **The Mar Menor (Murcia, cross-border reference):** Ñora plus **saffron** and often **mújol** (grey mullet). Cooked in an iron caldero over a fire.

| Variable | Tabarca | Santa Pola | Torrevieja |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key fish | Gallina (scorpionfish) | Morralla + gallina | Mullet, bream + morralla |
| Potato | Yes, always | Optional | Yes |
| Ñora | Yes | 2 per caldero | Yes |
| Extra picada | Garlic + parsley | Garlic + parsley | ± almond |
| Texture | Very moist | Seco-meloso | Meloso |

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Santa Pola** (Mesón Moniato's proportions), with the Tabarca potato service included.*

**Vessel:** 6–8 L cast-iron caldero.

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 100 ml |
| Ñoras, seeded | 2 |
| Garlic | 1 head (≈ 10 cloves) |
| Flat parsley | 1 small bunch |
| Pear tomatoes, grated | 4 (≈ 350 g) |
| Morralla (small mixed rockfish), cleaned | 1.5 kg |
| Gallina / scorpionfish, gutted, in 2–3 pieces | 1 (≈ 600–800 g) |
| Potatoes, in thick slices (Tabarca service) | 3 medium (600 g) |
| Water | 2.5 L |
| Salt | to taste |

**Extraction:**
1. Heat the oil in the caldero. Fry the ñoras for **10 seconds**, then remove. Fry 6 garlic cloves until golden, then remove.
2. In a mortar, pound the ñora, the fried garlic, the raw parsley and salt into a **picada**. Loosen it with a splash of water.
3. In the same oil, fry the grated tomato until jammy (**6 min**).
4. Add the water and picada. Bring to a boil. Add the **morralla** and simmer for **20 min**.
5. Strain the morralla, pressing it hard through a sieve or *pasapurés* to extract everything. Return the broth to the caldero.
6. Add the **potatoes**, cook for **10 min**, then add the **gallina** and cook for **8–10 min** more. Lift out the fish and potatoes and keep them warm. They are the first course.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Broth for the rice | 1.6 L (≈ 4:1 by volume; meloso) |
| Allioli | 200 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Clean and scale the fish, seed the ñoras and make the allioli (garlic, salt and oil, mortar-emulsified).
2. **Fondo:** As above.
3. **First Service:** Serve the gallina and potatoes with a drizzle of broth and allioli.
4. **Rice Addition:** Measure **1.6 L** of broth back into the caldero and bring to a boil. Add the rice.
5. **Fire Manipulation:** Strong boil for **10 min**, then medium for **6–8 min**. Stir gently once or twice in the first 5 minutes; this is a meloso, so a little agitation helps release starch.
6. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Rest **2 min**. Serve in deep plates with allioli on the side.
