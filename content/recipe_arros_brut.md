# Arroz Sucio Mallorquín / Arròs Brut

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Illes Balears](provincial_baleares.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Mallorca, especially the rural interior (Es Pla) and the Serra de Tramuntana villages. Eivissa has a sibling dish, the [arròs de matances](recipe_arros_de_matances_eivissa.md).
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy). Served hot from the pot.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** An earthenware **greixonera** or *olla* on a wood stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
In Mallorcan Catalan, *brut* means **"dirty"**. The name refers to the **murky broth** coloured by the spices: cinnamon, pimentón, saffron, pepper, and in some versions clove and nutmeg. Originally it was a **hunter's and farmer's dish**. It was made with **hare and rabbit**, thrush or pigeon, whatever vegetables the field gave, and in older versions **offal and blood**, which made it a typical winter dish. Modern versions use chicken, pork ribs, *sobrassada* (spreadable cured sausage) or *botifarró*, mushrooms or snails, and a **picada** of liver. Some cooks insist an arròs brut **without chopped liver** doesn't deserve the name.

**Culinary science:** the picada of pounded **fried liver**, garlic and spices is added at the end. Liver proteins and blood thicken the broth slightly and darken it, which is why it looks "dirty". This is the opposite of the Valencian idea of a clear caldoso. The warm spices recall the medieval Catalan spice palette that survived on the islands.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
> ⚠️ Village-level variations are only loosely documented online. The patterns below come from the sources cited, and the town names are indicative, not confirmed.

*   **Classic interior (game):** Hare or rabbit + thrush or pigeon + snails + wild mushrooms + liver picada.
*   **Modern / urban (Palma):** Chicken + pork ribs + sobrassada + vegetables (green beans, artichoke, peas) + liver picada.
*   **Tramuntana:** ⚠️ Heavier on **mushrooms** (*esclata-sangs*, saffron milk caps) in autumn.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **modern Mallorcan** (chicken, pork, sobrassada, liver picada).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 70 ml |
| Chicken, pieces | 500 g |
| Pork ribs, short | 300 g |
| Chicken livers | 100 g |
| Onion, chopped | 1 |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Sobrassada | 50 g |
| Mushrooms (esclata-sangs, if in season) | 200 g |
| Green beans / artichoke / peas | 250 g total |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| Water | 2 L |
| **Spice picada:** cinnamon ⅓ tsp, black pepper, 2 cloves, nutmeg pinch, saffron 0.2 g, parsley, 2 garlic cloves, the fried livers | — |

**Extraction:** Fry the livers briefly and set them aside. Brown the chicken and ribs (**15 min**). Add the onion (10 min), then the garlic, tomato and pimentón (5 min), and the sobrassada (1 min). Add the water and simmer **30 min**. Add the vegetables and mushrooms (**10 min**). Pound the picada in a mortar.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 280 g |
| Broth | ≈ 1.8–2 L (≈ 6:1, caldoso) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Bring the broth to a boil and add the rice.
2. Simmer **10 min**, then stir in the **spice and liver picada**.
3. Cook **6–8 min** more.
4. Serve immediately. It must stay soupy.
