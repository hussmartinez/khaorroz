# Mahshi Batata (Gaza Stuffed Potatoes) / محشي بطاطا

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** *The Gaza Kitchen* lists a "Stuffed Potatoes (Mahshi Batata)" recipe, but only its title and metadata are accessible. The filling and method below follow the general Palestinian rule that any vegetable that can be hollowed is stuffed with rice and minced meat, plus generic Levantine recipes. Gaza-specific details are therefore reconstructed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Gaza Strip, and Palestinian home cooking generally.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vegetables (*mahashi*). Hollowed potatoes are filled with rice and spiced minced meat, then baked or simmered in a garlicky tomato sauce. The potato shell turns creamy and the rice soft and saucy.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A coring tool, a frying pan, and an oven tray or wide pot.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The *Rice Dishes and Stuffed Vegetables* chapter of *The Gaza Kitchen* runs "from simple everyday fare to splendid gala meals" and includes *mahshi batata* among Gaza's stuffed dishes ([ckbk – Stuffed Potatoes (Mahshi Batata)](https://app.ckbk.com/recipe/gaza70968c08s001r008/stuffed-potatoes); [ckbk – Rice Dishes and Stuffed Vegetables](https://app.ckbk.com/section/gaza70968c08s001/rice-dishes-and-stuffed-vegetables)). A Palestinian writer sums up the tradition: in Palestine, any vegetable that can be hollowed is stuffed with rice and minced meat, or with rice and minced vegetables ([Elena Dudum – Palestinian mahshi](https://elenadudum.substack.com/p/recipe-and-palestinian-mahshi)). Levantine *batata mahshi* is commonly hollowed potatoes filled with beef, rice and spices and baked in tomato sauce with garlic, lemon and dried mint ([Chef's Pencil](https://www.chefspencil.com/batata-mahshi/)). See also the summer [mahshi](recipe_mahshi.md) of zucchini and eggplant.

**Culinary science:** potatoes are cored and briefly fried so the shell sets and doesn't crumble. Their starch thickens the tomato sauce as they bake, while the rice inside steams in the potato's moisture.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Gaza (The Gaza Kitchen):** Listed. ⚠️ The filling details are not accessible. Gaza cooks typically add dill, garlic and chili to stuffings.
*   **General Levantine:** Beef or lamb, rice, baked in tomato with lemon and dried mint.
*   **Meatless version:** Rice and vegetables only.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: reconstructed Palestinian home style.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Tomato passata | 500 ml |
| Garlic, crushed | 4 cloves |
| Dried mint + lemon juice | 1 tsp + 2 tbsp |
| Olive oil (sauce + frying) | 2 tbsp + 100 ml |
| Water | 250 ml |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium potatoes, peeled and cored | 8 (≈ 1.2 kg) |
| Short-grain rice, soaked 20 min | 150 g |
| Minced beef or lamb | 250 g |
| Seven-spice, salt, pepper | 1 tsp / to taste |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Core the potatoes, leaving a 1 cm wall, and fry them briefly until lightly golden.
2. Mix the raw rice, meat, spices and 1 tbsp of oil, and fill the potatoes three-quarters full.
3. Simmer the passata with the garlic, mint, lemon, water and salt for 5 min.
4. Stand the potatoes in a tray, pour the sauce around them, cover with foil and bake at 190 °C for **50 min**.
5. Uncover and bake for 15 min more, until the tops colour and the rice is tender.
6. Rest for 10 min and serve with yogurt or salad.
