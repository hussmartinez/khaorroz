# Rabiʿiyya (Spring Fava-Bean Maqluba) / ربيعية – مقلوبة فول أخضر

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** recipe by the Palestinian cook **Muzna Bishara**, published by Asif, the culinary institute of Israel. Asif presents it as Palestinian and repeats the popular story that maqluba "originated in Jerusalem in 1187". That legend links the dish to Salah ad-Din's entry into Jerusalem and is not historically verified (see [maqluba](recipe_maqluba.md)). The village or town origin of the spring fava version isn't specified.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Palestine (Jerusalem region and Arab villages of the Galilee and Triangle ⚠️). A spring dish.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Upside-down rice (*maqluba*). Spiced, turmeric-tinted rice moulded over fresh green fava beans and shredded lamb, turned out onto a platter.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large non-stick or heavy pot, inverted onto a round tray.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Rabiʿ* means "spring": *rabiʿiyya* is a seasonal maqluba made when **fresh broad beans** (*ful akhdar*) arrive, replacing the fried eggplant or cauliflower of the winter versions. The recipe sets the shift from grain to rice in a proverb: ***"al-ʿizz lil-ruz wal-burghul shanaq halu"*** ("glory goes to rice, and bulgur hanged itself"). Rice was once reserved for the wealthy, and as trade made it cheaper it pushed bulgur out of everyday pots. Hyssop (*zaʿtar*) in the lamb broth gives it a Palestinian hill-country note.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Spring fava (Bishara/Asif):** Lamb shanks, hyssop broth, fresh fava, basmati or jasmine rice, baharat and turmeric.
*   **With peas:** Peas can replace fava (blanched in stock).
*   **Jerusalem / Hebron / Gaza winter maqluba:** See [maqluba](recipe_maqluba.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Muzna Bishara's rabiʿiyya (scaled to 4).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb shank pieces (ossobuco cut) | 1 kg |
| Butter | 40 g |
| Onion, sliced | 1 |
| Carrot, leek, garlic | 1 / ½ / 2 cloves |
| Fresh hyssop (za'atar) sprigs | 3 |
| Cardamom, bay, allspice, cinnamon stick, clove, peppercorns | 3 / 1 / 4 / 1 / 1 / 1 tsp |
| Water | 1.5 L |
| Fresh broad beans, shelled | 350 g |
| Olive oil | 4 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Basmati or jasmine rice | 380 g |
| Strained lamb stock | 700 ml |
| Baharat / turmeric / black pepper | 1 tsp / ½ tsp / 1 tsp |
| Toasted nuts, parsley | 4 tbsp / 2 tbsp |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Lamb:** Brown the shanks in the butter, add the onion and fry 3 minutes. Add the vegetables and spices, cover with the water, salt, and simmer covered for about 1.5 hours. Shred the meat and strain the stock.
2. **Beans:** Fry the broad beans in the olive oil for about 6 minutes, peeling the large ones.
3. **Season the rice:** Toss the rinsed rice with a little oil, the baharat, turmeric, pepper, salt and half the beans.
4. **Layer and cook:** Butter the pot and layer the remaining beans, then the meat, then the rice. Add the stock, boil, then cover and simmer on low for 20 minutes. Rest covered for 10 minutes.
5. **Flip:** Invert onto a platter and top with browned reserved meat, nuts and parsley.
