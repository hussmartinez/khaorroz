# Khua Kling / คั่วกลิ้ง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Southern Thailand](provincial_southern_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the origin of the name is discussed, not settled, in Silpa Watthanatham magazine. The quantities come from a home recipe.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Thai south, now on menus nationwide.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a "dry curry", minced pork or beef stir-fried in a fiery southern curry paste until every crumb is coated and the pan is dry. It is eaten with plenty of steamed rice (or sticky rice) and raw vegetables.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok (*kratha*) over a high flame; the paste is pounded in a stone mortar.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khua* means to dry-fry and *kling* to roll: the meat is "rolled" in the paste in a dry pan until no liquid remains. It is one of the hottest southern dishes, made from minced meat and a paste of several kinds of chili pounded with salt and spices, and eaten with steamed or sticky rice and fresh vegetables ([Grab Food blog](https://www.grab.com/th/en/food-blog/the-spicier-the-better-6-spicy-thai-dishes-you-must-try/)). Silpa Watthanatham magazine has investigated where the name comes from and compared versions, including a similar central-Thai dish in Jitsaman Komolthiti's recipe collection ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=7244)).

**Culinary science:** dry-frying the paste in a little oil "blooms" the fat-soluble aromatics (capsaicin, turmeric, lemongrass oils), and cooking off the meat's water concentrates the flavour, so a small spoonful seasons a lot of rice.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Pork (most common in the south):** minced pork, lemongrass and kaffir-lime leaf ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/ugc/4e70adcb56f949d098da930d7dca9c5c)).
*   **Beef:** The Silpa-mag author first ate a beef version in Bangkok about 30 years ago ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=7244)).
*   **Modern:** "Khua kling" pasta and other fusion dishes ([Phol Food Mafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=25339)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Khua kling paste: dried chilies, bird's-eye chilies, turmeric, lemongrass, galangal, garlic, shallot, black pepper, shrimp paste | 4 tbsp (≈ 60 g) |
| Minced pork | 500 g |
| Oil | 1 tbsp |
| Sugar | 1 tsp |
| Lemongrass, thinly sliced | 2 stalks |
| Kaffir-lime leaves, finely shredded | 8 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (or sticky rice) | 400 g raw |
| Khua kling per plate | 3–4 tbsp |
| Raw vegetables | cucumber, long beans, Thai basil |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Steam the rice (jasmine ≈ 18 min; sticky rice soaked overnight and steamed 25 min in a *huad*).
2. **Paste:** Fry the paste in the oil in a hot wok until fragrant (1–2 min).
3. **Meat:** Add the pork and keep tossing and pressing until it is dry and fully coated (8–10 min). Add no water.
4. **Season:** Add the sugar, lemongrass and lime leaves and toss 1 min. Serve with the rice and vegetables.
