# Kaeng Som (Central) / แกงส้ม

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand; distinct from the southern yellow *kaeng som* (*kaeng lueang*).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a thin, sour and spicy tamarind curry-soup with fish or prawns and vegetables (morning glory, papaya, cha-om omelette), no coconut milk, eaten with jasmine rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot; stone mortar (*khrok*) for the dried-chili and shallot paste.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Central *kaeng som* is a sour curry soured mainly with tamarind and rounded with palm sugar, usually with freshwater fish or prawns and vegetables ([Wikipedia – Kaeng som](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_som)). The Nation describes it as originally a rustic dish of freshwater fish and native vegetables, later refined ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/life/food/40052258)). In the south a turmeric-yellow sour curry shares the name, so elsewhere that one is called *kaeng lueang* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaeng_som)).

It is a standard *kap khao*: the sourness and heat are balanced by plain rice, and it often accompanies rich curries and salty dishes in the same meal ([Saveur – Thai curries](https://www.saveur.com/article/travels/Andy-Ricker-Thai-curries)).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Kaeng som kung cha-om:** prawns with a cha-om (acacia leaf) omelette cut into squares.
*   **Kaeng som phak ruam:** mixed vegetables (green papaya, long beans, cabbage) with fish.
*   **Kaeng som dok khae:** with sesbania flowers in season.
*   **Southern kaeng som (kaeng lueang):** turmeric, fresh chilies and *som khaek*; not covered here.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Dried red chilies (soaked), shallots, garlic, shrimp paste | 8, 5, 3 cloves, 1 tsp |
| Fish (snakehead or seabass) — part pounded into the paste | 300 g |
| Prawns | 200 g |
| Water or light stock | 1 L |
| Tamarind water | 4 tbsp |
| Fish sauce, palm sugar | 3 tbsp, 1 tbsp |
| Vegetables (morning glory, green papaya, long beans) | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) | 400 g, rinsed (+ 520 ml water, absorption method) |
| Soup per person | ≈ 300 ml |
| Rice per person | ≈ 180 g cooked |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Poach a little fish and pound it with the chilies, shallots, garlic and shrimp paste into the paste.
2. Bring the water to a boil, dissolve the paste, and add the hard vegetables.
3. Season with tamarind, fish sauce and palm sugar until sour-salty-sweet.
4. Add the fish pieces and prawns and cook 2–3 min; add leafy vegetables last.
5. Cook the rice: rinse 400 g jasmine rice until the water runs nearly clear, add 520 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 min, then rest 10 min covered (or use an electric rice cooker / *mo hung khao*).
6. Serve the soup in bowls with rice on plates.
