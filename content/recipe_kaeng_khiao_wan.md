# Kaeng Khiao Wan / แกงเขียวหวาน

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Dating is uncertain: the Thailand Foundation places its emergence around the reigns of Rama VI–VII (c. 1910–1935), while the Thai government notes there is no definite evidence of when it began.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand (Bangkok and the Chao Phraya plain); today eaten nationwide.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a coconut-milk curry of fresh green chilies, pale green and fragrant with Thai basil, ladled over jasmine rice (or eaten with *khanom chin* rice noodles). The 'sweet' (*wan*) in the name refers to the soft green colour, not to sugar.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A wok (*kratha*) or pot to fry the paste in separated coconut cream; a stone mortar (*khrok*) for the paste; rice in a pot or electric cooker.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Green curry is the best-known Central Thai coconut curry. Its paste is built on fresh green chilies with galangal, lemongrass, makrut lime zest, coriander root, shallot, garlic, shrimp paste and toasted spices, fried in coconut cream until the oil separates ([Wikipedia – Green curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_curry); [Thailand Foundation](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/kaeng-khiao-wan-thailands-iconic-green-curry/)). National Geographic points out that, for all its fame, the dish has a surprisingly short documented history ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/thai-green-curry-revealing-spicy-secrets-culinary-classic)).

At the Thai table it is a *kap khao*, something eaten with rice: a ladle of curry is spooned over a plate of jasmine rice, alongside other dishes, and it is a staple of the *khao kaeng* (rice-and-curry) shops ([Wikipedia – Khao kaeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_kaeng)). See [khao rad kaeng](recipe_khao_rad_kaeng.md).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Bangkok home style:** chicken or fish balls with Thai eggplant and pea eggplant; served with rice for lunch.
*   **Khao kaeng shops:** cooked in large batches and kept warm in trays; often beef (*nuea*) or fish balls.
*   **With *khanom chin*:** in the morning markets, green curry is poured over fermented rice noodles instead of steamed rice.
*   **Restaurant versions abroad:** sweeter and greener than home versions (⚠️ general observation, not sourced to a single study).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Green curry paste | 60 g |
| Coconut milk, cream and thin milk separated | 600 ml |
| Chicken thigh, sliced | 400 g |
| Thai eggplants, quartered | 200 g |
| Pea eggplants | 50 g |
| Fish sauce | 2 tbsp |
| Palm sugar | 1 tsp |
| Makrut lime leaves, torn | 4 |
| Thai sweet basil, red chili | 1 handful, 2 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) | 400 g, rinsed (+ 520 ml water, absorption method) |
| Curry per plate | ≈ 200 ml |
| Rice per plate | ≈ 180 g cooked |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Fry the coconut cream in the wok until it splits and the oil appears.
2. Add the paste and fry until fragrant (3–4 min).
3. Add the chicken and stir until coated, then the thin coconut milk; simmer 5 min.
4. Add the eggplants and lime leaves; simmer until tender (5 min). Season with fish sauce and palm sugar.
5. Finish with basil and chili, off the heat.
6. Cook the rice: rinse 400 g jasmine rice until the water runs nearly clear, add 520 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 min, then rest 10 min covered (or use an electric rice cooker / *mo hung khao*).
7. Serve: rice on each plate, curry ladled over or in a bowl alongside.
