# Khao Kan Chin / ข้าวกั๊นจิ๊น

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Northern Thailand (Lanna)](provincial_lanna.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lanna (Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, Lamphun markets), with Tai Yai (Shan) roots. Also called *khao ngiao* or *chin som ngiao*.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice steamed in a banana-leaf parcel with pork blood and minced pork. It sets into a soft, moist, dark-maroon block that is eaten warm with crisp garnishes.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** Banana-leaf parcels (*hor bai tong*) steamed in a tiered steamer over charcoal.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Khao kan chin began with the **Tai Yai** (Shan) and spread into the Lanna kingdom. The Tai Yai were the one group in the region who habitually ate non-glutinous rice, which makes this one of the few Lanna dishes made with ordinary rice. The alternative name *khao ngiao* comes from the Lanna term for the Tai Yai, which once carried a pejorative tone ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_kan_chin); [Khaosod – Technology Chaoban](https://www.khaosod.co.th/technologychaoban/?p=43764)). It is a market and breakfast food, and the spelling varies widely (กั๊นจิ๊น, กั้นจิ้น, กั๋นจิ้น).

**Culinary science:** pork blood coagulates around 70 °C. Folded into cooked rice and steamed, it sets as a binder, like a blood pudding, giving the parcel its sliceable texture. Squeezing the blood with lemongrass leaves first masks its iron smell ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_kan_chin)).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Chiang Mai market style:** Parcels drizzled with garlic-pork-fat oil and topped with cracklings, fried garlic, fried dried chilli, shallot, cucumber and coriander ([Phol Food Mafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=42450)).
*   **With nam ngiao:** Served as a side to *nam ngiao*, the Tai Yai-style pork-and-tomato noodle soup ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_kan_chin)).
*   **Fried version:** Some shops stir-fry rice with cooked pork blood instead of steaming it in parcels ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/photos/90c3a5a90dbe40a4abef3a60134f2aef)).
*   **Mae Hong Son / Tai Yai heartland:** ⚠️ Not documented specifically in the sources found.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Chiang Mai steamed parcels.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Cooked jasmine (non-glutinous) rice, cooled | 600 g |
| Fresh pork blood | 200 ml |
| Minced pork | 200 g |
| Lemongrass leaves (to squeeze with the blood) | 1 handful |
| Garlic + shallot, minced | 2 cloves + 2 |
| Salt / sugar / vegetable oil | 1 tsp / 1 tsp / 2 tbsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Parcels | 8, about 120 g each |
| Steaming | 20–30 min |
| Garnish per serving | fried garlic, fried dried chilli, sliced shallot, cucumber, coriander |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Prepare the blood:** Squeeze and knead the blood with crushed lemongrass leaves, then strain.
2. **Season the pork:** Fry the garlic and shallot in the oil, then add the pork, salt and sugar. Cook just until it loses its pink colour, and let it cool.
3. **Mix:** Combine the rice and pork, then add the blood gradually, mixing gently until evenly maroon.
4. **Wrap:** Spoon portions onto squares of banana leaf, fold the ends tightly to the middle, and pin or tie them.
5. **Steam and serve:** Steam **20–30 min** until set. Open the parcels, drizzle with the garlic oil and add the garnishes.
