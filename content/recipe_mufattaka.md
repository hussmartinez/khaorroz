# Mufattaka / مفتّقة

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** a rare, near-lost Beiruti sweet. It is documented by the Food Heritage Foundation, AFP reporting (2024) on the Makari family shop, and a handful of Beirut cooks. Sources describe it as a Sunni Beiruti tradition. The date of the Ayoub Wednesday festival is given as the last Wednesday of April; it is reported as a custom that has largely lapsed.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Beirut: the old Sunni neighbourhoods (Basta, Ras Beirut) and the beach at Ramlet al-Baida.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sweet rice (festival dessert). Soaked rice cooked yellow with **turmeric**, then stirred for hours with **tahini**, sugar and pine nuts into a dense, glossy, golden pudding with oil rising to the surface. Served at room temperature, decorated with pine nuts.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large heavy pot and a long wooden paddle; it needs continuous stirring over a steady flame.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Mufattaka* is Beirut's yellow rice pudding. It is tied to **Arbaa Ayoub** ("Job's Wednesday"), when Beiruti families went down to the sea at Ramlet al-Baida, according to tradition on the last Wednesday of April, to bathe as Job did and be cured. The pudding's endless stirring is said to require the patience of Job himself. A 2024 AFP report (carried by Al-Monitor, The New Arab, Emarat Al Youm and Al-Quds Al-Arabi) followed the Makari family. Hajj Abdallah Makari, the "King of Moufataka", first sold it from his shop in Basta, and his grandson Mohammad still makes it in Barbour, warning that "otherwise it will disappear". Taste of Beirut notes that it is little known even among Lebanese who did not grow up in old Beirut.

**Culinary science:** turmeric gives colour and a gentle bitterness that cuts through the very rich tahini. Tahini is sesame paste, about half fat, and the long stirring emulsifies it with the starchy rice until the oil separates out again as a sheen on top. That is the cooks' sign that it is done. With no milk or eggs, it is naturally vegan.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Makari family / Basta and Barbour (Beirut):** About four hours of work after soaking the rice overnight; served with *knefe kaak* (sesame bread) on the side.
*   **Ras Beirut home version (Taste of Beirut):** The rice is soaked overnight in water with turmeric; the tahini, sugar and pine nuts are mixed separately and stirred in.
*   **Anise touch:** One Beirut cook adds a little anise.
*   ⚠️ Whether it was ever made outside Beirut (e.g. in Saida or Tripoli) is not documented.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Food Heritage Foundation proportions (60 portions) scaled to ≈ 8 small portions.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice, soaked overnight | 135 g |
| Water | 470 ml |
| Turmeric powder | 7 g (≈ 2 tsp) |
| Tahini (good quality) | 135 g |
| Sugar | 270 g |
| Pine nuts (plus extra to decorate) | 40 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice : tahini : sugar (by weight) | 1 : 1 : 2 |
| Stirring after combining | ≈ 60–90 min |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Soak:** Soak the rice overnight, then rinse and drain it.
2. **Boil yellow:** Bring the water and turmeric to a boil, add the rice and cook until the water has evaporated. Cover and finish cooking over medium heat until very soft.
3. **Tahini mixture:** In another pot, mix the tahini, sugar and pine nuts.
4. **Stir:** Add the tahini mixture to the rice and stir continuously over medium heat for 60–90 min, until very thick and the oil rises to the surface.
5. **Serve:** Pour into dishes while hot, let it cool, and serve at room temperature decorated with pine nuts.
