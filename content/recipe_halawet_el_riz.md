# Halawet el-Riz / حلاوة الرز

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Tripoli (al-Mina and the old souks), North Lebanon; a Ramadan sweet of the city's pastry shops.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sweet rice (dessert): rice cooked to a smooth, stretchy paste with sugar and sweet white cheese, chilled, then rolled or layered around *ashta* (clotted cream); chewy, almost mochi-like.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large heavy pot for the rice paste; marble slabs or trays to spread and roll it.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Tripoli is Lebanon's capital of sweets, and *halawet el-riz* ("rice sweetness") is its rice counterpart to the better-known *halawet el-jibn* (semolina-cheese rolls). Shops such as Tom Sweets are known for both, and the Haddad family shop sells the rice dessert filled with cream under the name *halawet a'rozz bi ashta*, better known as *halawet shmayseh* ([Lebanon Traveler – Local Lebanese pastries & their makers](https://www.lebanontraveler.com/?p=2404); [No Garlic No Onions – Nouh al-Haddad, the one and only halawat el-shmeisseh](https://www.nogarlicnoonions.com/nouh-al-haddad-the-one-and-only-halawat-el-shmeisseh)). A home version from a Tripoli family, published by SBS, cooks rice to a paste, sweetens it, perfumes it with rosewater and melts in sweet cheese, then serves it cold with ashta, pistachios and rose petals ([SBS Food – Halawet el rez](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/recipe/halawet-el-rez-sweetened-rice/5pwt6i0vi)). Recipe writers describe the result as a "sweet cheesy rice pudding" ([Hadia's Lebanese Cuisine](https://hadiaslebanesecuisine.com/newsite/recipe-items/halawet-al-riz-lebanese-cheesy-rice-pudding)).

A close relative is *shmayseh*, rice paste with rosewater spread over ashta ([Taste of Beirut – Rice paste with rosewater and ashta](https://tasteofbeirut.com/rice-paste-with-rosewater-and-ashta-shmayseh)). Both sit in the Levantine rice-pudding family with [riz bi haleeb](recipe_riz_bi_haleeb.md) and [meghli](recipe_meghli.md).

**Culinary science:** long cooking in plenty of water fully gelatinises the starch, and blending breaks the grains, so the paste sets firm and elastic when chilled; melting the cheese into the very hot paste gives the chew.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Tripoli pastry shops:** rolled or layered around ashta, cut into pieces, served with sugar syrup (*atr*) and pistachio.
*   **Tripoli home version (SBS, Omran family):** rice-and-cheese paste set in a tray, topped with ashta, pistachio and rose petals; no syrup.
*   **Shmayseh (Haddad sweets, Tripoli):** the rice paste with rosewater spread over cream rather than mixed with cheese.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Tripoli home tray, scaled down from the SBS recipe (which serves 12–18).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium-grain white rice, rinsed | 100 g |
| Water | 500 ml (plus up to 80 ml) |
| Caster sugar | 160 g |
| Sweet white cheese (*jibneh helweh*) or fresh mozzarella, shredded | 170 g |
| Rosewater | ½ tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ashta (or clotted cream) | 150 g |
| Pistachios, crushed | 20 g |
| Rice : water | 1 : 5 |
| Sugar syrup (*atr*), optional | to serve |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Cook the rice:** bring the rice and water to a boil in a heavy non-stick pot, cover and simmer on the lowest heat about 1 hour, stirring now and then, until very soft; add a little boiling water if it dries.
2. **Sweeten and blend:** stir in the sugar until dissolved, then blend smooth with a stick blender over the lowest heat; add the rosewater.
3. **Dry the paste:** keep stirring 5–10 minutes until the mixture is thick and pulls from the pot.
4. **Cheese:** off the heat, while very hot, beat in the shredded cheese in batches until melted and stretchy.
5. **Set:** spread in a tray, cover and chill about 4 hours. Serve cold, topped with ashta and pistachios (and syrup if you like).
