# Canarias / Islas Canarias

> ⬅ [README](README.md)

## 1. Overview
The Canary Islands grow no rice, and their staple starches are **gofio**, potato (*papas*) and sweet potato. Rice arrived as an everyday food with trade and with **emigration to and return from Cuba and Venezuela**, which is why the islands' rice dishes look Caribbean: **arroz amarillo** (saffron- or colouring-tinted absorption rice, often with *picadillo*) and **arroz a la cubana** with *plátano de Canarias*. Fish is eaten mostly as *sancocho* (salted cherne with papas and gofio) rather than in rice. I found no documented traditional Canarian fish rice.

⚠️ Canarian rice dishes are poorly documented online; see each file's notes.

## 2. Cultural Sub-Regions
| Sub-region | Rice personality | Key places |
|---|---|---|
| **Gran Canaria** | Arroz amarillo, arroz con picadillo | Las Palmas, Telde |
| **Tenerife** | Arroz a la cubana with local banana | Santa Cruz, La Laguna |
| **Islas menores** | Home-style rices beside sancocho and potajes | La Palma, Lanzarote, Fuerteventura |

**Leads without a recipe file (too thin to document):**

*   **Arroz con lapas** (limpet rice): attested only anecdotally in the Canaries, where limpets are usually grilled with green mojo ([Diario de Avisos – Lapas canarias](https://diariodeavisos.elespanol.com/2026/01/lapas-canarias-gastronomia/)). The documented limpet rice is Madeira's and the Azores' *arroz de lapas* ([Ruralea – Arroz de lapas](https://ruralea.com/arroz-de-lapas)).
*   **Caldo de millo** (Lanzarote): a corn broth served *with* chickpeas, rice or wheat, so the rice is an accompaniment ([Capture the Atlas – Comidas canarias](https://capturetheatlas.com/es/comidas-canarias/)).
*   **Arroz seco de vaca canaria:** a **modern restaurant creation** (Arrocería Ardeola, Garachico, Tenerife) with native-breed Canarian beef, mushrooms and a salmorreta made with *pimienta palmera* ([RTVC – Como en casa](https://rtvc.es/receta-arroz-seco-con-vaca-canaria-como-en-casa-rtvc/)). It isn't traditional, so it's noted here rather than as a recipe.
