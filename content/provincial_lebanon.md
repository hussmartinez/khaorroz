# Lebanon / لبنان

> ⬅ [README](README.md)

## 1. Overview
Lebanon grows little rice, yet rice is at the centre of its table. It is the everyday base under the *yakhneh* stews, cooked with toasted vermicelli; the celebration platter of chicken, spiced minced meat and toasted nuts; the stuffing rolled in vine leaves; and, on the coast, the brown onion-and-fish rice of the fishermen. Alongside rice runs an older grain tradition of **bulgur** (cracked wheat), which in the South still replaces rice in *mujaddara*. Lebanese cooking is organised around home kitchens and village producers, a heritage championed by the farmers' market **Souk el Tayeb** (founded in Beirut in 2004 by Kamal Mouzawak) and its kitchen **Tawlet**, where village cooks serve their regional food, and recorded by cookbook authors such as **Anissa Helou**, whose 2026 book *Lebanon* travels village by village.

## 2. Cultural Sub-Regions
| Sub-region | Rice personality | Key towns |
|---|---|---|
| **Beirut** | Home and festive rices: riz a djej, riz bi sh'ariyyeh under stews | Beirut |
| **Tripoli & the North** | Coastal sayadieh, dark onion and cumin | Tripoli (Trablous), Batroun |
| **Mount Lebanon** | Vine-leaf rolls, festive chicken rice | Zgharta, Jbeil, Chouf villages |
| **Saida (Sidon) & the South** | Fishermen's sayadieh; *mujaddara janoubiyeh* with bulgur | Saida, Sour (Tyre), Nabatieh |
| **Bekaa & Zahle** | Vineyards (vine leaves), legumes, bulgur | Zahle, Baalbek |
