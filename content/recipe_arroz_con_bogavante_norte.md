# Arroz Caldoso con Bogavante del Cantábrico / Arroz con Lumbrigante (Rinlo y la Mariña)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Norte (Galicia, Asturias, Cantabria, País Vasco)](provincial_norte.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** A Mariña Lucense, especially **Rinlo** (Ribadeo, Lugo), plus the Galician Rías Altas and the Asturian and Cantabrian coast.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A cast-iron **pota** over gas or wood.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
**Rinlo**, a tiny former whaling port in Ribadeo, is described as the "capital of the *potas* of arroz caldoso". Its restaurant A Cofradía serves an arroz caldoso con bogavante cooked slowly in traditional iron *potas*; sources give conflicting founding dates (1992 vs 2010). Rinlo also has one of Spain's first natural **cetáreas** (sea-water shellfish pools), built in **1904**, which explains its long relationship with live lobster. The Galician-style lobster rice sometimes adds **pulpo, mejillones and chorizo**. The Galician lobster (*bogavante*, *lumbrigante*) is prized for its firm flesh and strong flavour.

**Culinary science:** the lobster's head and coral contain most of its flavour. They are fried and pressed into the sofrito, and the shell is simmered into the stock. Recipes use about **4:1 stock to rice by volume** for the caldoso. Iron potas hold a steady simmer.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Rinlo (Ribadeo):** Iron **pota**, slow simmer, very soupy, lobster cut in pieces with the shell on.
*   **"A la gallega":** Adds **pulpo, mejillones and chorizo**, a mar-e-terra mixed version.
*   **Cantabria / Asturias (reconstructed):** Lobster + *andarica* (velvet crab) or clams. Fish stock from *pixín* (monkfish) heads.
*   **Valencian echo:** The Mediterranean [arroz caldoso con bogavante](recipe_arroz_caldoso_con_bogavante.md) is a restaurant-era dish with ñora and fumet. Compare the two traditions.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Rinlo-style pota caldoso**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Live Galician lobsters | 2 × 600 g |
| Fish stock (rockfish/turbot bones) | 2 L |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Onion | 1 |
| Red pepper | 1 |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |
| Brandy or white wine | 60 ml |

**Extraction:** Split the lobsters. Keep the coral and liquid and cut the bodies into rings. Sear the pieces for 2 min and set them aside. Fry the heads, pressing them (3 min). Add the onion and pepper (10 min), garlic, pimentón and tomato (5 min), then the brandy (flambé or reduce). Add the stock and simmer **15 min**. Remove the heads (or blend and strain them back in).

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Round rice | 350 g |
| Stock | ≈ 1.6 L (≈ 4:1 volume) |
| Lobster | 1.2 kg |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Add the rice and saffron to the boiling broth in the pota.
2. **Fire:** Simmer **14 min**, then return the lobster pieces and coral and cook **4 min**.
3. **Finish:** Serve immediately in deep plates, without resting.
