# Meghli / مغلي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Lebanon, among families of every faith, for the birth of a child, and in Christian homes at **Christmas**.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Sweet rice (dessert). A smooth, set pudding of **rice flour** boiled with caraway, anise and cinnamon. Dark brown and spiced, chilled in cups and topped with coconut and a thick layer of soaked nuts (walnuts, almonds, pine nuts, pistachios).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy saucepan stirred constantly with a wooden spoon over a medium flame; small bowls or cups for setting.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Meghli* ("boiled") is Lebanon's birth pudding. It was traditionally made to celebrate the birth of a son, and is now made for any newborn: when a baby arrives, the family cooks large pots of it and offers a bowl to "every eager guest that comes to meet the baby", as Simply Lebanese puts it. A WGBH report calls it the pudding that "welcomes babies, including Jesus", because Christian families also make it for Christmas Eve. The spices are explained by folk tradition: caraway is believed to help the new mother's milk come in (a traditional belief, not a medical fact). Lebanese sources also call it *moghli*, *meghleh* or *karawiyah* (caraway).

**Culinary science:** rice flour gelatinises at around 70–80 °C. It thickens smoothly without eggs or milk, but sticks and lumps unless it is whisked into cold water and stirred continuously. The long stirring gives the dish its name. The nuts are soaked for hours or overnight to soften them and make the skins easy to remove, which matches the pudding's soft texture.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Birth celebration (all communities):** Made in big batches when a baby is born and served to visitors. Historically linked especially to the birth of a son.
*   **Christmas Eve (Christian homes):** Served as part of the Christmas Eve table, as the pudding of the Nativity.
*   **Topping styles:** Desiccated coconut plus mixed soaked nuts (common today); older or simpler versions use walnuts and almonds only.
*   ⚠️ One online entry ties meghli to the Virgin Mary's birthday (8 September); this is not corroborated elsewhere.
*   **Related sweets:** [Mufattaka](recipe_mufattaka.md) (Beirut's turmeric-tahini rice) and the [riz bi haleeb](recipe_riz_bi_haleeb.md) family.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: modern Lebanese birth meghli, 6–8 small cups.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice flour | 100 g |
| Sugar | 150–180 g |
| Water | 1 L |
| Ground caraway | 1½ tsp |
| Ground anise | ½ tsp |
| Ground cinnamon | 1 tsp |
| Walnuts, almonds, pine nuts, pistachios (soaked overnight, peeled) | 150 g total |
| Desiccated coconut | 30 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice flour : water | 1 : 10 by weight |
| Cooking time after it starts to thicken | ≈ 15–20 min of stirring |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Mix cold:** Whisk the rice flour, sugar and spices into the cold water in a heavy pan until smooth.
2. **Cook:** Bring to a boil over medium heat, stirring **constantly** with a wooden spoon.
3. **Thicken:** Keep stirring for 15–20 min after it thickens, until it coats the spoon heavily and the raw flour taste is gone.
4. **Set:** Pour into small bowls, cool, then chill for at least 3 h.
5. **Garnish:** Before serving, cover with desiccated coconut and a generous layer of the soaked, peeled nuts.
