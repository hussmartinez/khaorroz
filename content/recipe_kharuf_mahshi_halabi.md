# Kharuf Mahshi of Ottoman Aleppo (Whole Lamb Stuffed with Rice) / خاروف محشي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The dish is attested as an 18th-century Aleppine feast dish by Alexander Russell, physician to the English factory in Aleppo, in *The Natural History of Aleppo* (London, 1756): "A whole lamb, stuffed with rice, almonds, raisins, pistaches, &c. and stewed, is a favourite dish with them." Russell gives **no recipe**. The spicing, proportions and method are a modern adaptation for a lamb shoulder, guided by today's Levantine stuffed-lamb rices ([ouzi](recipe_ouzi.md), [riz a djej](recipe_riz_a_djej.md)).

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Ottoman Aleppo, among the households of "the great people" Russell observed. The dish is related to festive stuffed lamb across Syria, Lebanon, Palestine and Jordan.
*   **Historical Source:** *The Natural History of Aleppo*, Alexander Russell, 1756, London (observations made in Aleppo; 2nd ed. by Patrick Russell, 1794)
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Feast rice stuffed into meat. Spiced rice with almonds, raisins and pistachios, cooked inside the lamb so it soaks up the juices; tender, rich and lightly sweet.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A large tinned-copper cauldron for stewing, or the neighbourhood baker's oven.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Russell's chapter on Aleppine dining is one of the most precise 18th-century portraits of a Levantine table. The meal was laid on a low round tray. It began with "a sort of broth, or soup" and ended with "a large pilaw", sometimes eaten with "a dish of sweet starch", followed by *khushaf*, a thin syrup with dried fruit. In between came mutton stewed with herbs and chickpeas, birds "commonly stuffed with rice and spices", and the whole stuffed lamb. Rice and minced meat in vine leaves or in vegetables he records as *mahshee* (Turkish *dolmah*). He also notes that Aleppo's Christians ate "more *burgle*, and less rice" than the Turks, and used oil where the Turks used butter. For the common people, "bread, dibbs, leban, butter, rice, and a very little mutton" were the staples.

The stuffed lamb is the ancestor of today's festive *ouzi*/*qouzi* and of the stuffed-lamb platters of Gulf and Levantine weddings.

**Culinary logic:** half-cooked rice inside the cavity finishes in the meat's steam and fat, so it ends up richer than any rice cooked in a pot.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Ottoman Aleppo (Russell, 1756):** Whole lamb stuffed with rice, almonds, raisins and pistachios, then stewed.
*   **Damascus and Lebanon today:** *Ouzi*, the rice-and-meat filling baked in pastry or under lamb (see [ouzi](recipe_ouzi.md)).
*   **Jordan and Gulf weddings:** Whole lamb on spiced rice; compare [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md) and [zarb](recipe_zarb.md).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Modern adaptation for a boned lamb shoulder.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Lamb shoulder, boned (pocket for stuffing) | 1.8 kg |
| Ghee | 4 tbsp |
| Onion, chopped | 1 |
| Seven-spice (baharat) | 2 tsp |
| Cinnamon stick, bay leaf | 1 + 2 |
| Water | 1.5 L |
| Salt | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium-grain rice, soaked 30 min | 300 g |
| Blanched almonds, toasted | 60 g |
| Raisins | 50 g |
| Pistachios | 40 g |
| Stock for the rice | 450 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Filling:** Fry the onion in half the ghee, add the drained rice and 1 tsp of the spice, then 450 ml of stock. Cook covered **8 min** (half-cooked). Fold in the nuts and raisins.
2. **Stuff:** Season the lamb inside and out. Fill the pocket loosely with rice (the rest goes around the meat), then sew or skewer it shut.
3. **Stew:** Brown the lamb in the remaining ghee. Add the water, cinnamon and bay, cover and stew gently for **2–2½ h** (or bake at 160 °C), adding the extra rice for the last 30 min.
4. **Serve:** Rest **15 min**, slice, and serve on the rice with extra toasted nuts and yogurt.
