# Shishbarak / شيشبرك

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** the dish is pan-Levantine and its national attribution is contested: Palestinian, Jordanian and Lebanese sources each present it as their own. The "ancient" origin stories (Persian *shush barah*) are folk etymology and are not verified here.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Lebanon, Syria, Palestine and Jordan.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: small meat-filled dumplings simmered in a hot garlic-and-mint yogurt (or jameed) sauce, served with vermicelli rice; some cooks thicken the yogurt with a spoonful of rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot for the yogurt, stirred in one direction over gentle heat so it doesn't split; the oven or a frying pan to set the dumplings first.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Shishbarak are tiny half-moon dumplings, the dough pinched around spiced minced lamb or beef, often with pine nuts, and the ends joined into a ring. They are baked or fried to firm them, then dropped into **cooked yogurt** (*laban matbukh*), the same sauce as [kibbeh labanieh](recipe_kibbeh_labanieh.md) and [shakriyeh](recipe_shakriyeh.md). Sources agree that rice goes with it: one Lebanese source serves the dumplings "smothered in a yoghurt sauce ... over rice", another notes the sauce has "a tad of rice mixed in", and vermicelli rice is "customarily served along with it".

Attribution varies. A Palestinian site calls it "one of the most popular Palestinian traditional recipes, ... also popular in Levant countries, Syria, Lebanon, and Jordan", while Jordanian and Lebanese sources present it as theirs. It is best understood as a shared Levantine dish.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Lebanon:** Cow's-milk yogurt stabilised with cornstarch or a little rice, garlic fried with dried mint (*taqliyeh*) on top; vermicelli rice on the side.
*   **Palestine:** Filling of lamb or beef; sauce of **jameed or regular yogurt**; served with rice.
*   **Jordan:** Jameed-based sauce linking it to [mansaf](recipe_mansaf.md); presented as a Jordanian specialty.
*   **Syria (⚠️):** Damascene versions add coriander and garlic to the yogurt; less documented online.
*   **Crisp-dumpling school:** Some cooks keep the dumplings apart from the sauce until serving so they stay crisp.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Lebanese-Palestinian shishbarak in cooked yogurt, with vermicelli rice.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Flour (dough) | 300 g |
| Water + oil + salt (dough) | 160 ml + 2 tbsp + 1 tsp |
| Minced lamb or beef | 300 g |
| Onion, finely chopped + pine nuts | 1 + 30 g |
| 7-spice, salt | 1 tsp each |
| Plain yogurt (full fat) or diluted jameed | 1.5 kg |
| Cornstarch (or 1 tbsp short-grain rice) + 1 egg white | 1 tbsp + 1 |
| Garlic + dried mint + butter (taqliyeh) | 4 cloves + 1 tbsp + 30 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium-grain rice | 300 g |
| Vermicelli | 50 g |
| Water for the rice | 450 ml |
| Dumplings | about 60–70 small |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Filling and dough:** Fry the meat with onion, spices and pine nuts. Knead the dough, rest 30 min, roll thin, cut 5 cm rounds, fill, fold into half-moons and join the ends.
2. **Firm the dumplings:** Bake at 200 °C for 10–12 min until pale gold (or fry briefly).
3. **Cook the yogurt:** Whisk the yogurt with the cornstarch and egg white (or add the rice), bring slowly to a simmer, stirring in one direction, about 15 min.
4. **Combine:** Add the dumplings and simmer gently **10 min**.
5. **Rice:** Toast the vermicelli in butter, add the rinsed rice and boiling salted water, cook covered on low 15 min, rest 10 min.
6. **Finish:** Fry garlic and mint in butter, pour over the pot; serve with the rice.
