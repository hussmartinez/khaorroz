# Arroz Caldero del Mar Menor / Caldero Murciano

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Región de Murcia](provincial_murcia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Campo de Cartagena and the Mar Menor shore: San Pedro del Pinatar, San Javier, Los Alcázares, Cabo de Palos, Cartagena.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). The rice is cooked in about four times its weight of broth and served loose, with allioli.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A cast-iron **caldero** (round-bottomed pot) over a wood fire, originally on the fishing boats.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
According to the San Pedro del Pinatar town council, the caldero goes back to the **19th century**, when Mar Menor fishermen cooked the fish that wouldn't sell at market in iron pots on board. From the boats it moved ashore to the bars and *ventas* of Cabo de Palos and San Pedro, and it is now the signature dish of the whole Campo de Cartagena. It is served in **two courses**: the broth's fish (often *mújol* or grey mullet, *gallineta*, *dorada*) with boiled potatoes and allioli, and then the rice cooked in that broth.

The defining element is the **ñora**. The council's leaflet stresses that, properly fried, the ñora gives a very characteristic flavour, but that frying it too long or using too much turns the dish bitter. The ñoras are fried with garlic, then pounded and returned to the pot with tomato. **Culinary science:** the ñora's carotenoids are fat-soluble, so frying moves the colour into the oil. Its sugars caramelise within seconds and burn soon after, which is the reason for the short fry. The iron caldero's mass keeps a steady boil, which favours a creamy rice over a socarrat.

The caldero is the Murcian sibling of the [Santa Pola and Tabarca caldero](recipe_caldero_santa_pola_tabarca.md) and of the Alicante [arroz a banda](recipe_arroz_a_banda.md).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **San Pedro del Pinatar:** The municipal recipe uses about 2 kg of mixed fish (*morralla*, *gallineta*, **mújol** or *dorada*) plus **langostino del Mar Menor** (or fresh *quisquilla*, small shrimp). Mújol is the typical fish here.
*   **Cabo de Palos:** Rockfish-heavy, using *gallineta* and *morralla*. Restaurant tradition since the dish came ashore.
*   **Cartagena city:** Many households use **dorada** or **lubina** as the noble fish and add **saffron** alongside the ñora. A Directo al Paladar recipe gives 5 ñoras, 1 head of garlic, 3 tomatoes, 400 g bomba and 2 L of fumet.
*   **Contrast with Santa Pola:** The Murcian caldero uses **more ñoras** (up to about 7 in some versions) and a darker, redder broth.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **San Pedro del Pinatar**, scaled to 4 people.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Olive oil | 120 ml |
| Ñoras, stems and seeds removed | 5 |
| Garlic | 1 head (8–10 cloves) |
| Ripe tomato, crushed | 300 g |
| Morralla (small rockfish) | 600 g |
| Mújol (grey mullet) or dorada, in steaks | 800 g |
| Langostinos del Mar Menor (or quisquilla) | 8 |
| Potatoes, thick slices (first course) | 500 g |
| Water | 2.2 L |
| Saffron (optional) | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:**
1. Heat the oil in the caldero. Fry the ñoras for **10–15 s** and remove them. Fry the garlic until golden and remove it. Pound both with salt in a mortar.
2. Fry the tomato in the same oil (**6 min**). Add the ñora and garlic paste, then the water and the morralla. Simmer for **20 min** and strain, pressing the fish hard.
3. Poach the potatoes in the broth (**12 min**), then the mújol steaks (**8 min**). Lift them out for the first course. Keep about **1.4 L** of broth.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Bomba or Calasparra rice | 320 g |
| Broth | 1.3–1.4 L (≈ 4:1, meloso) |
| Allioli | 200 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **First course:** Serve the fish and potatoes with allioli.
2. **Rice:** Bring the broth to a boil in the caldero, add the rice and the saffron, and stir once.
3. **Fire:** Strong boil for **10 min**, then medium for **7–8 min**. Lay the langostinos on top at minute 13.
4. **Finish:** Rest **2 min** and serve with allioli.
