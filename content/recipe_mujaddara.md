# Mujaddara / مجدرة (Mudardara / مدردرة)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Lebanon](provincial_lebanon.md) · [Palestine](provincial_palestine.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The whole of Bilad al-Sham (Lebanon, Syria, Palestine, Jordan). The bulgur version is associated with South Lebanon.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice and lentils (*mujaddara*). In the *mudardara* form the grains of rice and lentils stay separate under crisp fried onions; the Lebanese puréed *mjaddara* is closer to a soft porridge (see §3).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot (*tanjara*) on the stove, with a frying pan for the onions.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Mujaddara is the region's most famous **rice-and-lentil** dish and the archetypal food of the poor and of fast days. It needs nothing but lentils, a grain, onions and oil. The first recorded recipe for *mujaddara* appears in the *Kitab al-Tabikh*, compiled by al-Baghdadi in Iraq in **1226**. Several writers note that the older form was lentils with **bulgur**, the grain of the poor, before rice became common. The dish keeps a strong place in Christian Lenten eating and on Fridays, and it is commonly eaten with yoghurt, raw onion, radishes and a cabbage or tomato salad.

The naming is famously contested. In Lebanon, ***mjaddara*** usually refers to the **puréed** version, with a consistency like rice pudding. ***Mudardara*** is the version where rice and lentils stay whole and distinct, topped with fried onions. Taste of Beirut notes that mudardara uses roughly **equal rice and lentils**, while mjaddara uses far less rice. Other sources say Syria and Egypt use *mudardara* for the whole family. Syrian writers have joked about the "great war of mujaddara" over these names.

It is the Levantine cousin of the Vega Baja's Lenten [arroz de los tres puñaos](recipe_arroz_de_los_tres_punaos.md), with the same logic of legumes plus a cereal, cooked in staggered stages, meatless and filling.

**Culinary science:** brown or green lentils hold their shape after 15–20 min of simmering, which is when the rice goes in. Rice and lentils then finish together in the lentils' own cooking water. Onions fried to a deep brown supply both flavour (caramelisation) and fat. Part of the onion oil goes into the pot, and the crisp onions go on top. In the puréed *mjaddara* the lentils are cooked soft and passed through a mouli, and the rice thickens the purée.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Lebanese *mudardara*:** Whole grains, rice:lentils ≈ 1:1, lots of brown onion on top. Served warm or at room temperature.
*   **Lebanese puréed *mjaddara*:** Lentils cooked very soft and puréed, with only 1/4–1/3 cup of rice per batch. It sets as it cools and is eaten cold with salad and raw onion.
*   **South Lebanon, *mujaddara hamra* or *janoubiyeh* ("red" or "southern"):** **Bulgur instead of rice**. The colour comes from very dark onions. Some sources say the "red" refers to the onions rather than the grain, so the naming isn't settled.
*   **Palestine:** Usually called *mujaddara*, made with rice (or freekeh/bulgur in villages), and eaten with yoghurt and *salata*. Common in Lent and among farming families.
*   **Syria:** *Mudardara* is the usual name for the whole family. Often cumin-spiced, sometimes with bulgur.
*   **Jordan:** Rice and lentils, onions on top, served with yoghurt.

| Variable | Mudardara | Puréed mjaddara | Janoubiyeh / hamra |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grain | Rice | A little rice | Coarse bulgur |
| Lentils | Whole | Puréed | Whole |
| Rice : lentils | ≈ 1 : 1 | ≈ 1 : 4 | Bulgur ≈ 1 : 1 |
| Served | Warm | Cold, set | Warm |

*   **Mjaddara safra ("yellow" mujaddara):** made with split yellow lentils instead of whole brown ones, giving a softer, paler dish; Arabic sources distinguish the yellow version (peeled yellow lentils) from the "black" one (whole brown lentils) ([Areq – مجدرة](https://areq.net/m/مجدرة.html); [Cooking with Alia – Lebanese-style mujadarra](https://old.cookingwithalia.com/?p=15375)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Lebanese mudardara** (whole grains, onion-crowned).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Brown or green lentils, rinsed | 200 g |
| Water | 1.2 L |
| Onions, thinly sliced | 4 large (≈ 800 g) |
| Olive oil | 150 ml |
| Ground cumin | 1½ tsp |
| Salt | 2 tsp |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Medium- or long-grain rice, rinsed (or coarse bulgur for janoubiyeh) | 200 g |
| Lentil cooking water at rice-in | ≈ 500 ml (enough to stand 1 cm above the grains) |
| Lentils : rice | 1 : 1 by weight |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Onions:** Fry the onions in the oil over medium heat until **deep brown and crisp at the edges** (25–30 min). Lift out half to drain for the topping.
2. **Lentils:** Simmer the lentils in the water for **15–18 min**, until tender but whole.
3. **Combine:** Stir the rice, cumin, salt and the remaining onions with their oil into the pot. Adjust the water to about 1 cm above the surface.
4. **Fire Manipulation:** Bring to a boil, cover and cook on the **lowest heat for 18–20 min** (bulgur: 15 min).
5. **Rest:** Off the heat, covered, **10 min**. Fluff.
6. **Serve:** Top with the crisp onions. Serve with yoghurt or laban, raw onion wedges, radishes and a tomato-cucumber salad.
