# Paella de Verduras / Paella de Verdures (Paella d'Estiu i Paella d'Hivern)

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** L'Horta de València, La Safor (Oliva, Gandia), La Ribera, and the Alicante huertas.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A steel paella over orange wood or vine shoots.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Before tourism and refrigeration, the Valencian paella followed the **huerta calendar**. Valencia Plaza, drawing on the Safor writers Chelo Peiró and Josep Piera, contrasts two seasonal paellas:
*   **Paella d'estiu (summer):** made "from Sant Jaume to Sant Miquel" (late July to late September) with *garrofó*, *tavella*, *roget* or flat green beans, good tomatoes, red pepper strips, and the prized local **fesol morú**. It may include chicken and rabbit, but Valencia Bonita notes that the summer version could be made **entirely from huerta produce, without meat**.
*   **Paella d'hivern (winter):** **"Faves, carxofes i alls tendres"** (broad beans, artichokes and garlic shoots), in Piera's words. The artichoke is "the queen". Some households add a little meat from the *matança* (pig slaughter). Valencia Bonita calls it the *paella negra*, because the artichokes and habas darken the rice. It was the winter answer to the lack of fresh bajoqueta and garrofó.

A meat-free vegetable paella is therefore a **traditional seasonal form**, not a modern vegetarian invention. Some Valencian vegetable varieties (*garrofó*, *tavella*) are grown almost exclusively for paella.

**Culinary science:** without meat there is no gelatin or browned-protein fond, so flavour has to come from **Maillard browning of the vegetables** (pepper strips, artichoke edges), a **dark tomato sofrito**, and a vegetable stock simmered from trimmings. Green pepper or onion in the stock base (as in Pakus's method) adds sweetness.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **Oliva (La Safor):** The town clearly separates a winter paella from a summer one. Some summer versions add **tiny meatballs** (*pilotetes*).
*   **La Safor generally:** Summer means *fesol morú*, tavella and tomato. Winter means artichoke, habas and ajos tiernos.
*   **L'Horta de València:** *Ferraura* (flat bean) is more typical here. In winter, artichokes and peas.
*   **Vega Baja (Alicante):** See [arroz de verduras de la Vega Baja](recipe_arroz_de_verduras_vega_baja.md), with ñora, artichoke, habas and cauliflower.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Two seasonal columns: **summer** (huerta, no meat) and **winter** (paella negra).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Summer (paella d'estiu) | Winter (paella d'hivern) |
|---|---|---|
| Olive oil | 100 ml | 100 ml |
| Red pepper, in strips | 1 | — |
| Flat green beans / tavella / garrofó | 200 g / 100 g / 150 g | — |
| Fesol morú (if available) | 100 g | — |
| Artichokes, in eighths | — | 6 |
| Broad beans, shelled | — | 250 g |
| Ajos tiernos (garlic shoots), in pieces | — | 1 bunch |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 200 g | 150 g |
| Pimentón dulce | 3 g | 3 g |
| Vegetable stock (trimmings + green pepper + onion, 40 min) | 1.3 L | 1.3 L |
| Saffron | 0.3 g | 0.3 g |

**Extraction:** Brown the pepper strips (or artichokes) and set them aside for the top. Fry the beans and legumes (or habas and ajos tiernos) for 6 min. Add the pimentón (5 s), then the tomato, and cook until jammy (6 min). Add the stock and simmer for 10 min.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Stock in the pan | ≈ 1.2 L |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Scatter the rice and level it. Don't stir after this.
2. **Fire Manipulation:** High heat for 8 min, then medium-low for 8–9 min. Lay the reserved pepper strips or artichokes on top at minute 12. Finish with 1 min on high heat.
3. **Resting Phase:** Rest 5 min.
