# Arroz con Bacalao y Coliflor / Arròs amb Bacallà i Coliflor

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Alicante city and L'Alacantí. It is also cooked in La Safor (Gandia, Oliva) as the *paella de Setmana Santa*, and in Castellón as a Lent rice.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Seco** (dry) when made in the paella, which is the Alicante and Safor habit. A caldoso version exists in Catalonia and in some Valencian homes (see §3).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A steel paella over wood or gas, or a clay *cassola* for the caldoso version.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
This is a **Lent and Holy Week** rice (*Quaresma*, *Setmana Santa*). Meat was forbidden on vigil days, so households used **salt cod**, the inland Mediterranean's preserved fasting fish, and **cauliflower**, which is at its best in late winter. Writing about La Safor, Valencia Plaza calls the dish "the Holy Week paella, made of cauliflower and cod". Alicante city's **Jornadas Gastronómicas de Cuaresma** (Lent food days) list a cod rice with huerta vegetables among their main dishes. The dish is one of a family of Lenten rices with [arròs amb bledes](recipe_arroz_con_acelgas.md), [arròs empedrat](recipe_arroz_empedrado.md) and the Vega Baja [arroz de los tres puñaos](recipe_arroz_de_los_tres_punaos.md).

**Culinary science:** cauliflower florets fried hard in oil caramelise, which turns their sulphurous notes nutty and sweet. Salt cod has been cured and dried. Once it has been desalted for 36–48 h, a brief fry turns its gelatinous skin into a binder. The cod is still salty, so the broth must be **salted at the end**, after tasting.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **Alicante city:** Seco in the paella. Often a **ñora** or salmorreta base. Cauliflower, cod, and sometimes **garlic shoots** (*ajos tiernos*) and **peas**. Fish or vegetable broth.
*   **La Safor (Oliva, Gandia):** Seco, as the "paella de Setmana Santa". Cauliflower and cod, with pebre roig and saffron. Cooked over wood at family Easter gatherings.
*   **Castellón:** Lent "vigil" versions with desalted cod and mixed vegetables, with no trace of meat. Often caldoso.
*   **Catalan/Valencian caldoso:** Caldoso, with green pepper and tomato. Some home versions include **panceta**, which goes against the vigil purpose.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Alicante seco** in the paella.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Salt cod, desalted 36–48 h, in chunks or crumbled | 300 g |
| Cauliflower, in small florets | 500 g |
| Olive oil | 90 ml |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ñora (or 3 g pimentón dulce) | 1 |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Ajos tiernos (optional) | 1 bunch |
| Light fish or vegetable broth (unsalted) | 1.3 L |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:** Fry the ñora for 10 s, take it out, and pound it with 2 garlic cloves. Fry the cauliflower until the edges brown (8 min). Add the cod for 1–2 min. Add the ñora-garlic paste and the tomato (5 min).

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Broth | ≈ 1.2 L |
| Cod (desalted weight) | 300 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Desalt the cod in the fridge, changing the water 4–6 times.
2. **Toasting:** Add the rice to the sofrito and toast for 1–2 min.
3. **Liquid Addition:** Add the boiling broth and saffron. **Taste before salting.**
4. **Fire Manipulation:** High heat for 8 min, then medium-low for 8–9 min, then a brief high flame for the socarrat.
5. **Resting Phase:** Rest 4 min.
