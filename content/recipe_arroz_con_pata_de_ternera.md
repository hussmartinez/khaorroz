# Arroz con Pata de Ternera / Arròs amb Pota de Vedella

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** L'Alacantí (Alicante city, Sant Vicent del Raspeig, Mutxamel), with echoes in L'Alcoià and El Vinalopó.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Meloso** (creamy). The gelatin from the trotter gives a sticky, glossy grain. Some homes cook it drier in the paella.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep paella or *cassola* over gas or wood.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
This is a working-class Alicante city classic and a **winter** rice. It uses the cheapest, most gelatin-rich cut, the **calf's foot**, together with **chickpeas**, **botifarra de ceba** (onion sausage) and **chorizo**. The Alicante city tourism board's "Ciudad del Arroz" campaign lists it among the city's emblematic rices. Its structure is a cocido turned into rice: the trotter is pre-cooked until tender, and **its cooking liquid becomes the rice stock**.

**Culinary science:** calf's foot is almost pure collagen and connective tissue. Over 2–3 h of simmering (or 45 min under pressure) it gives up a large amount of gelatin, which coats the rice, makes the grain glossy and shortens the time to a meloso texture. The chickpeas add a second source of starch.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **The Alicante City Style:** Pata + chickpeas + **botifarra de ceba** + chorizo + tomato. Often a **salmorreta** or ñora base. Meloso.
*   **The Sant Vicent / Mutxamel (Horta d'Alacant) Style:** Adds **cardo** (cardoon) and **penca** in winter. Cooked slightly more caldoso.
*   **The Alcoi / interior Style:** Pata combined with **pork trotter** and *pericana*-style dried peppers. Drier, paella-cooked.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Alicante city** (Turismo de Alicante proportions: 400 g rice, 1 pata, 250 g chickpeas, 2 botifarras, 50 g chorizo).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Calf's foot (*pota de vedella*), split by the butcher | 1 (≈ 1 kg) |
| Water (for the pata) | 3 L |
| Bay leaf, onion, salt | 1 / ½ / to taste |
| Cooked chickpeas | 250 g |
| Botifarra de ceba | 2 |
| Chorizo | 50 g |
| Olive oil | 60 ml |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Garlic | 2 cloves |
| Pimentón dulce (or 40 g salmorreta) | 3 g |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:**
1. Blanch the pata for 5 min and discard the water. Cover with 3 L of fresh water, add the bay leaf and onion and simmer for **2.5 h** (or 45 min in a pressure cooker) until the meat comes off the bone.
2. Bone it and cut the meat and skin into bite-size pieces. Strain the cooking liquid and keep **1.4 L**.
3. In the paella, fry the chorizo and botifarra slices for 2 min. Add the garlic, then the pimentón (5 s), then the tomato (5 min).
4. Add the pata pieces and chickpeas **before** the stock. Stir to coat.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice | 400 g |
| Pata stock (boiling) | 1.3–1.4 L |
| Pata meat (boned) | ≈ 400 g |
| Chickpeas | 250 g |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Preparation:** Cook the pata the day before. The stock will set like jelly in the fridge, which shows it is right.
2. **Fondo:** As above.
3. **Liquid & Rice:** Pour in the boiling pata stock. When it returns to a boil, scatter the rice and saffron evenly.
4. **Fire Manipulation:** Strong for 8 min → medium for 9–10 min. Stir lightly once or twice for a meloso finish.
5. **Finish/Resting Phase:** Rest **3 min**. Serve hot; it stiffens as it cools.
