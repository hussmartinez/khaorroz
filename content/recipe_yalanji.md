# Yalanji / يالنجي

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Syria](provincial_syria.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** yalanji is well documented as the Syrian name for **vegetarian, oil-cooked stuffed vine leaves**, served cold as mezze. Published recipes come mostly from Syrian restaurant chefs (one Aleppo-trained) and diaspora cooks. Town-level differences beyond Aleppo vs. Damascus are reconstructed from those recipes. The meat-and-rice vine-leaf dish of the wider Levant is covered separately in [warak enab](recipe_warak_enab.md).

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Syria (Aleppo and Damascus restaurant and home tables), and Syrian kitchens in the diaspora (Gulf, Australia, Europe).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Stuffed vine leaves cooked in oil (*mahashi bil-zeit*). Small, tight rolls with a tender, tangy rice-and-herb filling, eaten **cold** as mezze.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot (*tanjara*) lined with potato or tomato slices, the rolls packed in circles and weighted with an inverted plate; stovetop, long slow simmer.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
The name *yalanji* comes from Turkish *yalancı* ("liar", "false"). In Ottoman kitchens a *yalancı dolma* was a "false" stuffed vegetable: one with no meat. Syrians kept the word for their **meatless vine leaves**, filled with raw short-grain rice, tomato, onion, parsley and mint, and cooked slowly in olive oil, lemon and often **pomegranate molasses**. Because they contain no meat, they belong to the oil-cooked (*bil-zeit*) repertoire eaten cold, on fast days and in summer.

An Aleppo-trained chef's published recipe uses Egyptian rice, tomato, parsley, mint, red onion, sweet pepper, olive oil, lemon juice and pomegranate molasses. The rolls simmer for 30 minutes on a high flame and then about 2.5 hours very low, and are chilled before serving. Egyptian and Damascene-style recipes describe the same dish: the filling is sautéed or mixed raw, the rolls are laid over sliced potatoes, and they are finished with a lemon–oil–molasses dressing.

**Culinary science:** the rice goes into the leaves **raw** and cooks entirely by absorbing the acidic, oily liquor. Acid (lemon, molasses) slows starch breakdown, so the grains stay distinct inside the tight roll. The weight on top keeps the rolls from unrolling as the rice swells. Serving cold sets the starch slightly, which is why yalanji slices cleanly and holds as mezze.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Aleppo:** More **pomegranate molasses** and **Aleppo/sweet pepper**; a sharper, redder sauce. Some Aleppine families add dried or fresh fruit notes, consistent with Aleppo's sweet-sour cooking ⚠️.
*   **Damascus:** Lighter: lemon, olive oil and mint dominate; molasses used sparingly ⚠️.
*   **Shami restaurant style (Egypt, Gulf):** Filling fried first with garlic, onion and tomato, then mixed with rice and molasses; potatoes under the rolls; served cold with pomegranate seeds.
*   **Diaspora:** Australian and North American Syrian cooks sometimes add chickpeas or bulgur; some lay chicken pieces under the rolls, which turns the dish into a meat variant and no longer yalanji in the strict sense ⚠️.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Aleppo restaurant style** (after chef Hasan Al Obaid), scaled to 4 as a main mezze.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Vine leaves (fresh blanched, or brined and rinsed) | 50–60 leaves (≈ 250 g) |
| Egyptian short-grain rice, rinsed | 250 g |
| Ripe tomatoes, finely chopped | 300 g |
| Flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped | 1 large bunch (≈ 150 g) |
| Fresh mint, finely chopped | 30 g |
| Red onion, finely chopped | 1 medium |
| Sweet red pepper, finely chopped | 1 small |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | 150 ml |
| Lemon juice | 100 ml |
| Pomegranate molasses | 3 tbsp |
| Salt, allspice, black pepper | 2 tsp / ½ tsp / ½ tsp |
| Potatoes, sliced 1 cm (pot lining) | 2 |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Rice : chopped vegetables | ≈ 1 : 2.5 by weight |
| Cooking liquid (water + half the oil, lemon, molasses) | ≈ 600 ml, just covering the rolls |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Leaves:** Blanch fresh leaves for 1 minute (or rinse brined leaves well) and cut off the stems.
2. **Filling:** Mix the raw rice with tomato, parsley, mint, onion, pepper, half the oil, half the lemon juice, 1 tbsp molasses and the spices.
3. **Rolling:** Place a teaspoon of filling at the stem end of each leaf (vein side up), fold in the sides and roll tightly into a finger-thick cylinder.
4. **Packing:** Line the pot with potato slices. Pack the rolls seam-side down in tight circles, layer upon layer.
5. **Liquid:** Whisk the remaining oil, lemon and molasses with hot water and salt, pour over to just cover, and weight with an inverted plate.
6. **Cooking:** Simmer 30 minutes on medium-high, then 2–2½ hours on the lowest heat, adding a splash of water if it dries.
7. **Finish:** Cool in the pot, then refrigerate. Serve cold, drizzled with molasses and oil, with lemon wedges.
