# Arroz Caldoso con Bogavante / Arròs Caldós amb Llamàntol

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Província d'Alacant](provincial_alicante.md) · [Província de València](provincial_valencia.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Alicante coast (Alicante city, El Campello, Dénia, Xàbia) and Valencia's beach restaurants. A festive dish along the whole coast.
*   **Liquid & Texture Profile:** **Caldoso** (soupy).
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A deep clay *cassola*, a wide pot or a deep paella, over gas.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Arroz caldoso con bogavante is the coast's **celebration rice**. It is ordered for Sunday lunches, Christmas and family gatherings, and one Alicante blog describes it as a festive staple of the comarca. It belongs to the Valencian caldoso tradition, which runs from the humble [fesols i naps](recipe_arroz_con_fesols_i_naps.md) to the refined [Vinaròs llagostins](recipe_arroz_caldoso_langostinos_vinaros.md). The European lobster (*Homarus gammarus*) was once caught along the Mediterranean rocky coast and is now often imported. Alicante's tourism board stresses that **fish stock, never water**, is essential.

**Culinary science:** the lobster's head holds its tomalley and coral, which give the broth its sweetness and its colour. Splitting the head lengthways and searing it **cut-side down**, then pressing it as it cooks, emulsifies that richness into the oil. The tail and claw meat need only 4–6 min at a simmer, so they are seared briefly and added back at the end. For a caldoso the rice must be fully cooked but still swimming. The broth-to-rice ratio is about **4–6:1 by volume**, and the dish has to be **eaten immediately**, because it keeps absorbing liquid.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)

*   **Alicante city:** Salmorreta base (ñora). Caldoso. Often with **sepia** and **prawns** as well as the lobster.
*   **Dénia / Xàbia:** Lighter tomato. A strong rockfish and shellfish fumet. Sometimes with **gamba roja de Dénia**.
*   **Valencia city restaurants:** Classic garlic, tomato and pebre roig sofrito. Some kitchens add a splash of **brandy** to deglaze after searing the lobster, which is a restaurant habit rather than a traditional one.
*   **Meloso variant:** The same base with less broth (about 3.5:1). Alicante Turismo publishes a *meloso* ("creamy rice with lobster") version.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Alicante caldoso** (salmorreta + fumet).*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Live or very fresh lobster (*bogavante*) | 1 × 800–1,000 g |
| Rockfish fumet | 2 L |
| Olive oil | 80 ml |
| Salmorreta (see [arroz a banda](recipe_arroz_a_banda.md)) | 60 g |
| Red pepper, diced | ½ |
| Garlic | 3 cloves |
| Ripe tomato, grated | 150 g |
| Saffron | 0.2 g |

**Extraction:** Split the lobster: separate the tail into medallions, crack the claws and halve the head lengthways. Sear the medallions and claws for 1 min per side and set them aside. Sear the head cut-side down, pressing it, for 3 min. Fry the pepper and garlic, then the salmorreta (1 min), then the tomato (5 min). Add the fumet and simmer for 10 min, then take out the head, pressing it into the pot.

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain rice (Bomba or Senia) | 320 g |
| Broth | ≈ 1.6–1.8 L (≈ 5:1 by volume) |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice Addition:** Bring the broth to a lively boil, add the rice and saffron, and check the salt.
2. **Fire Manipulation:** Keep it at a steady simmer for **16 min**, stirring every few minutes.
3. **Finish:** Add the lobster medallions and claws at **minute 13**. If the rice has absorbed too much, top it up with hot fumet so it stays soupy.
4. **Resting Phase:** **None.** Serve at once in deep bowls.
