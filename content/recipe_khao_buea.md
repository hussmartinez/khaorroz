# Khao Buea (Pounded Rice for Curries) / ข้าวเบือ

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Isan and Laos. It is essential in bamboo-shoot curry (*kaeng nor mai*, called *kaeng poe* in central Thailand) and other Isan soups.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Rice used as a thickener. Soaked raw sticky rice is pounded to a wet paste and stirred into a herb broth, turning it opaque and gently creamy.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A clay mortar for the paste and a clay or metal pot over a wood or charcoal fire.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Khao buea* is soaked glutinous rice pounded smooth, and it is what gives Isan bamboo-shoot and *yanang*-leaf curries their body ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2932457)). A typical recipe uses **2 tablespoons** of khao buea for 400 g of boiled bamboo shoots in 800 ml of *yanang* juice, seasoned with *pla ra* (fermented fish) and chilli ([Wongnai – kaeng prer](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/kaeng-prer)). Even the naming is debated: some Isan groups simply say "bamboo-shoot curry", others call it *kaeng poe*, and some Isan cookbooks insist "Isan has no *kaeng poe*" ([Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/prachachuen/news_5124456)). Together with [khao kua](recipe_khao_kua.md), it shows rice used as an ingredient rather than as the meal.

**Culinary science:** raw glutinous rice is almost all amylopectin. Pounded wet and heated in broth, its starch granules swell and gelatinise at about 60–70 °C and thicken the liquid much as a roux or cornflour slurry would, without any fat.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Kaeng nor mai sai bai yanang (bamboo shoot with *yanang* leaf):** The classic use; khao buea, *pla ra*, chilli, sometimes holy basil ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2932457)).
*   **With mushrooms and cha-om:** Straw mushrooms, *cha-om* and *maenglak* (lemon basil) added ([Wongnai](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/kaeng-prer)).
*   **Lao *or* stews:** ⚠️ Lao *or lam*-type stews use a similar rice thickener, but no source documents the link in detail.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: Isan bamboo-shoot and yanang curry thickened with khao buea.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Raw glutinous rice, soaked 1–2 h | 2 tbsp (≈ 30 g) |
| Boiled bamboo shoots (de-bittered) | 400 g |
| *Yanang* leaf juice (or water) | 800 ml |
| *Pla ra* (fermented fish sauce) | 2–3 tbsp |
| Bird's-eye chillies, bruised | 5 |
| Lemon basil (*maenglak*) or holy basil | 1 handful |
| Salt / fish sauce | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Khao buea : liquid | ≈ 30 g : 800 ml |
| Simmer after adding the paste | 5–10 min |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Make the khao buea:** Drain the soaked rice and pound it in a mortar with a splash of water to a smooth, milky paste.
2. **Broth:** Bring the *yanang* juice to a boil and add the bamboo shoots. Simmer **10 min**.
3. **Thicken:** Stir in the khao buea and keep stirring for **5–10 min** as the broth turns opaque and thickens.
4. **Season:** Add the *pla ra*, chillies, salt or fish sauce.
5. **Finish:** Off the heat, add the basil. Serve with [sticky rice](recipe_khao_niao_nueng.md).
