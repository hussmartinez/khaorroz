# Kaeng Liang / แกงเลียง

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** The postpartum association is traditional belief; the Suphanburi link comes from one academic paper citing a provincial culture office.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Central Thailand (associated with Suphanburi in a Thai academic source).
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a peppery vegetable soup without coconut milk, thickened lightly by pumpkin and gourds, with prawns and lemon basil (*maenglak*), eaten with rice.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A pot; stone mortar (*khrok*) for the paste of shallot, white pepper, dried shrimp and shrimp paste.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
*Kaeng liang* is one of the oldest-feeling Central Thai soups: a paste of shallots, white peppercorns, dried shrimp and shrimp paste dissolved in water with seasonal vegetables (pumpkin, angled gourd, baby corn, banana blossom) and finished with *maenglak*. A Thai study on a ready-to-cook kaeng liang paste describes it as a healthy everyday dish rich in herbs and links it to Bang Pla Ma district in Suphanburi ([JTTAM / ThaiJO](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JTTAM/article/view/262479)). It is traditionally given to new mothers, believed to help milk production, a belief no study has confirmed ([Hot Thai Kitchen](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/kaeng-liang)).

It is eaten with rice as the gentle, herbal counterpoint to spicier dishes ([She Simmers](https://shesimmers.com/2011/08/spicy-thai-mixed-vegetable-soup-kaeng.html)).

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **With prawns:** the common version.
*   **With dried or smoked fish:** country style.
*   **Postpartum version:** with banana blossom (*hua plee*) for new mothers ([JTTAM](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JTTAM/article/view/262479)).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Shallots, white peppercorns, dried shrimp, shrimp paste (paste) | 5, 1 tsp, 2 tbsp, 1 tsp |
| Water or stock | 1 L |
| Pumpkin, cubed | 200 g |
| Angled gourd / baby corn / banana blossom | 300 g |
| Prawns | 200 g |
| Fish sauce | 2 tbsp |
| Lemon basil (*maenglak*) | 1 handful |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice (*khao hom mali*) | 400 g, rinsed (+ 520 ml water, absorption method) |
| Soup per person | ≈ 300 ml |
| Rice per person | ≈ 180 g cooked |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. Pound the paste ingredients smooth.
2. Dissolve the paste in boiling water; add pumpkin and cook 5 min.
3. Add the other vegetables and cook until just tender.
4. Add prawns and fish sauce; cook 2 min; add lemon basil off the heat.
5. Cook the rice: rinse 400 g jasmine rice until the water runs nearly clear, add 520 ml water, bring to a boil, cover and cook on the lowest heat for 15 min, then rest 10 min covered (or use an electric rice cooker / *mo hung khao*).
6. Serve with rice.
