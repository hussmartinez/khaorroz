# Ships w Pasta / شبس وباسته

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Jordan](provincial_jordan.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** this is the signature dish of Jordan's **Circassian** (Adyghe) community. The Jordanian version is documented mainly by an Atlas Obscura report on Circassian women cooks in Amman and by general sources on Circassian cuisine. Quantities below are reconstructed from that report's description (equal parts rice and bulgur, cooked like rice, pressed and cut into squares, under a walnut gravy). Town-level variations are not documented; the community notes in §3 come from the settlement history, not from food sources.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** The Circassian towns and quarters of Jordan: Amman (the old Circassian core around the Roman theatre and Jabal Amman), Wadi al-Seer, Sweileh, Na'ur, Jerash, al-Rusaifa and Zarqa.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Pressed grain "pasta" with gravy. Rice and bulgur cooked together until soft, packed into a firm, sliceable block, cut into squares and drenched in a thick, garlicky walnut-and-chicken gravy (*ships*), with the chicken served alongside.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A heavy pot for the grain, a flat tray to press and set it, and a separate pot for the chicken and gravy, on the household stove.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Circassians are North Caucasian Muslims expelled by the Russian Empire after the Caucasian War. After the 1878 Treaty of Berlin the Ottoman authorities resettled tens of thousands in the Levant. In Transjordan they founded or refounded **Amman (1878)**, **Wadi al-Seer (1880)**, **Jerash (1884)**, **Na'ur (1901)** and **al-Rusaifa (1904)**; the historian Vladimir Hamed-Troyansky calls Amman a Circassian refugee settlement before it became a capital. Chechens followed from 1903 and founded Zarqa. The Circassian Charity Association in Amman has served the community since 1932.

At home in the Caucasus, *pasta* (p'aste) is a stiff porridge of **millet or maize**, cut in slices like polenta and eaten with *ships*, a gravy of chicken stock, spices, butter or flour and walnuts. In Jordan the cooks could not find the right corn, so, as the Atlas Obscura report puts it, they adapted **ships w pasta** to the grains of their new country: **bulgur and rice**, cooked together like rice, pressed into blocks and cut into squares. That makes this one of the most interesting rice dishes in the atlas: a Caucasian dish whose grain changed when its people crossed into Bilad al-Sham. It is a cousin of the Levantine and Egyptian *sharkasiyya* ("the Circassian one"), chicken in walnut sauce served over rice.

**Culinary science:** short-grain rice cooked with bulgur releases enough starch to bind the cracked wheat. Pressed while hot and left to cool, the mixture retrograde-sets into a firm block that slices cleanly (the same physics as polenta or Spanish [arroz con costra](recipe_arroz_con_costra.md) leftovers). The gravy is thickened by **ground walnuts** (fat and protein) and a little toasted flour, so it clings to the squares rather than soaking in.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Amman (Circassian Charity Association and community kitchens):** The documented form. Equal parts rice and bulgur, pressed squares, walnut *ships* with garlic and chili, boiled chicken alongside. Served at community gatherings and in Circassian restaurants such as Samawer.
*   **The Caucasus original (for comparison):** Millet or maize *pasta*, not rice. Some diaspora households elsewhere (Turkey, Syria) keep maize; the Jordanian switch to rice and bulgur is a documented local adaptation.
*   **Wadi al-Seer, Sweileh, Na'ur, Jerash:** ⚠️ Not documented separately. These are the historic Circassian villages; any household differences there are unrecorded.
*   **The Arab-Levantine cousin, *sharkasiyya*:** Chicken in a walnut-garlic sauce served over plain rice, known in Egypt, Syria and Jordan. It shares the sauce idea but not the pressed grain.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Amman Circassian household**, reconstructed proportions.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Whole chicken, in 8 pieces | 1.3 kg |
| Water (for the chicken stock) | 2 L |
| Onion, halved | 1 |
| Bay leaf, black peppercorns | 2 / 6 |
| Walnuts, finely ground | 150 g |
| Butter | 40 g |
| Flour | 1 tbsp (10 g) |
| Garlic, crushed | 4 cloves |
| Red chili flakes or sweet paprika | 1 tsp |
| Salt | to taste |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Short-grain (Egyptian/Calrose) rice | 200 g |
| Coarse bulgur | 200 g |
| Water for the grain | ≈ 1 L (2.5 : 1) |
| Chicken stock for the gravy | ≈ 750 ml |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Stock:** Simmer the chicken with the onion, bay, peppercorns and salt in 2 L of water for **45 min**, skimming. Lift out the chicken and keep it warm; strain the stock.
2. **Grain:** Combine the rinsed rice, bulgur, salt and ≈ 1 L of water in a pot. Bring to a boil, then lower and cook covered as you would rice, **20–25 min**, until soft and all the water is absorbed. Beat it with a wooden spoon so it binds.
3. **Press:** Spread the hot grain in an oiled tray about 3 cm thick, press firmly and leave to set for **20–30 min**. Cut into squares.
4. **Ships (gravy):** Melt the butter, toast the flour for 1 min, add the garlic and chili, then the ground walnuts. Whisk in ≈ 750 ml of hot stock and simmer **10 min**, stirring, until thick enough to coat a spoon.
5. **Serve:** Arrange the squares on a platter, pour the hot *ships* over them, and serve the chicken pieces alongside.
