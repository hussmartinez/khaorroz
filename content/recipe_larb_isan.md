# Larb Isan / ลาบอีสาน

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Isan (Northeast)](provincial_isan.md)

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** All of Isan and across the Mekong in Laos, where larb is widely called the national (if "unofficial") dish. Sold in every Isan *ran som tam* and Bangkok's Isan eateries.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Eaten with rice: a warm minced-meat "salad" of pork, chicken, duck, beef or fish, sharp with lime, fish sauce and chilli, fragrant with mint and shallot, and given its nutty crunch by [khao kua](recipe_khao_kua.md) (toasted ground sticky rice). Eaten by hand with balls of sticky rice and raw vegetables.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A cleaver and chopping block for mincing, a small pot or wok to poach the meat in a splash of water over charcoal, a stone mortar (*khrok*) for the toasted rice; sticky rice from the *huad* steamer, served in a *kratip* basket.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
Larb is the emblematic dish of the Lao-speaking world. English-language reference works describe it as a Lao meat salad and the national dish of Laos, eaten equally in Isan, whose majority population is ethnically Lao ([Wikipedia – Larb](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Larb); [Wikipedia mirror – Laap](https://ipfs.aleph.im/ipfs/QmXoypizjW3WknFiJnKLwHCnL72vedxjQkDDP1mXWo6uco/wiki/Laap.html)). Thai sources define Isan larb by its bold balance of sour (lime), salty (fish sauce or *pla ra*), hot (ground dried chilli) and *nua* (savoury), with the "unique aroma" of *khao kua* as its signature ([Wongnai – Isan larb recipe](https://www.wongnai.com/recipes/spicy-minced-pork); [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/food/2892951)). It is always eaten with sticky rice and a basket of fresh herbs and vegetables ([GotoKnow](https://www.gotoknow.org/posts/716045)).

The rice in larb works twice: as the meal (sticky rice) and inside the dish (toasted rice powder), a link to the Isan habit of using rice as a seasoning and thickener (see [khao buea](recipe_khao_buea.md)). Larb also exists in a raw form (*larb dip*), made with raw minced meat; health authorities warn against raw freshwater fish and meat, so this file gives the cooked (*larb suk*) version.

**Culinary science:** poaching minced pork in a little water (*rua*) cooks it gently without browning, so the meat stays tender and absorbs the lime-fish-sauce dressing; the khao kua is added at the last moment so it stays crunchy and its toasted-rice aroma (Maillard pyrazines) stays bright.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Isan larb vs Lanna larb mueang:** Isan larb is sour and lime-forward with khao kua and ground chilli; the northern [larb mueang](recipe_larb_mueang.md) uses a dry-spice *phrik larb* paste and no lime ([Sanook – northern vs Isan larb](https://sanook.com/news/9908290)).
*   **By meat:** pork (*larb mu*), chicken, duck (*larb pet*), beef (*larb nuea*), catfish (*larb pla duk*) and mushroom versions are all common ([Wikipedia – Larb](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Larb)).
*   **Larb vs koi:** *koi* is the coarser, often raw, chopped variant ([Wongnai – larb vs koi](https://www.wongnai.com/news/differences-between-larb-and-koi)). ⚠️ Raw versions carry a liver-fluke risk with freshwater fish.

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: cooked Isan pork larb (larb mu), after the Wongnai recipe.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Minced pork | 300 g |
| Water (to poach) | 3 tbsp |
| Fish sauce | 2 tbsp |
| Lime juice | 2–3 tbsp |
| Ground dried chilli | 1–2 tsp |
| [Khao kua](recipe_khao_kua.md) (toasted ground sticky rice) | 2 tbsp |
| Shallots, sliced | 3 |
| Spring onion, sawtooth coriander, mint | 1 handful |
| Glutinous (sticky) rice, soaked 6–8 h | 300 g |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sticky rice (served alongside) | 300 g raw (≈ 75 g per person) |
| Meat : khao kua | ≈ 150 : 1 by weight |
| Raw vegetables (cabbage, long bean, cucumber) | 1 basket |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Rice:** Soak the sticky rice 6–8 h, drain, and steam it in a woven bamboo *huad* over a pot of boiling water for 20–25 min, flipping the mass once halfway; keep it warm in a lidded *kratip* basket.
2. **Poach:** heat the water in a small pot, add the pork and stir until just cooked and crumbly (3–4 min); take off the heat.
3. **Season:** stir in fish sauce, lime juice and ground chilli; taste for a sour-salty-hot balance.
4. **Finish:** fold in shallots, herbs and the khao kua; serve at once with sticky rice and raw vegetables, eaten by hand.
