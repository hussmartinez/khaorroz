# Khao Op Mo Din / ข้าวอบหม้อดิน

> ⬅ [README](README.md) · [Central Thailand](provincial_central_thailand.md)

> ⚠️ **Source-confidence note:** Its history in Thailand is undocumented; sources are recipes and museum records of Thai rice pots, and the link to Cantonese clay-pot rice is general.

## 1. Geographic & Texture Profile
*   **Primary Geographic Zone:** Bangkok's Thai-Chinese restaurants; clay-pot cooking is also strong in Isan.
*   **Dish Type & Texture:** Clay-pot baked rice: raw rice cooked with seasoned chicken (or prawns, Chinese sausage), ginger and mushrooms in a sealed earthenware pot, forming a fragrant crust at the base.
*   **Traditional Cooking Vessel & Fuel:** A lidded earthenware clay pot (*mo din*), heated before the rice goes in, over gas or charcoal.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context
ImportFood presents *khao op mo din* as a baked rice in an earthenware pot, typical of Thai-Chinese restaurants ([ImportFood](https://importfood.com/recipes/recipe/175-baked-rice-in-earthenware-pot-khao-op-mor-din)); Thai recipe sites give a clay-pot chicken rice ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=33585)). Clay pots are an old Thai rice vessel — the Penn Museum holds a hand-made Isan rice-steaming pot ([Penn Museum](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/162748)) — and before electric cookers Thais cooked rice in earthenware ([Silpa-mag](https://www.silpa-mag.com/?p=50277)). Clay-pot rice also has deep Chinese roots ([Foodicles](https://foodicles.com/clay-pot-rice-history)).

**Culinary science:** earthenware heats slowly and holds heat, so the rice steams evenly and a golden crust (*khao tang*) forms against the base; the sealed lid traps aromas.

## 3. Local Town-by-Town Variations (Crucial)
*   **Chicken (*op kai*):** marinated chicken, ginger, shiitake ([Pholfoodmafia](https://www.pholfoodmafia.com/?p=33585)).
*   **Prawn or crab (*op kung / pu*):** a Thai-Chinese seafood-restaurant version, often with glass noodles instead of rice.
*   **With Chinese sausage (*kun chiang*):** Cantonese-style ⚠️ (general practice).

## 4. The Master Recipe (4-Person Batch Blueprint)
*Variant represented: **Chicken and shiitake**.*

### The Flavor Base (El Fondo)
| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Chicken thighs, bite-size | 400 g |
| Oyster sauce / soy / sesame oil / sugar | 2 tbsp / 1 tbsp / 1 tsp / 1 tsp |
| Ginger, julienned | 30 g |
| Dried shiitake, soaked and sliced | 6 |
| Garlic | 4 cloves |
| Chicken stock | ≈ 450 ml |
| Spring onion, coriander | to finish |

### Assembly Ratios
| Element | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 300 g raw, rinsed and soaked 20 min |
| Rice : stock | 1 : 1.5 |

### Step-by-Step Cooking Blueprint
1. **Marinate:** chicken with sauces, sugar and sesame oil, 30 min.
2. **Heat the pot:** warm the clay pot slowly on low, then medium; add oil and fry garlic and ginger.
3. **Rice:** add drained rice and stock; bring to a simmer, cover.
4. **Bake:** after 8 min lay the chicken and mushrooms on top; cover and cook on low 15 min, then 2 min higher to crisp the base.
5. **Finish:** rest 5 min, scatter spring onion and coriander, serve from the pot.
